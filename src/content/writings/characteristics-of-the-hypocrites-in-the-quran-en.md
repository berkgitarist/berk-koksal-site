# [2:8] Characteristics of the Hypocrites in the Quran: Professed Faith, Hidden Intentions, and Responsibility

**Author:** Berk KÖKSAL  
**Date:** November 03, 2026  
**Place:** Mersin

The purpose of this reflection is to consider 2:8 alongside other related verses; to ask how hypocrisy is revealed not merely through outward behavior but through a distance between professed faith and the inner direction of a human being, and what bearing witness to the oneness of GOD requires of us in knowledge, justice, truthfulness, and submission.

Peace. Peace be upon you.

A person can speak beautiful words, declare that they are on the right side, and appear trustworthy in the eyes of others. But what happens when no one is watching? What happens when power changes hands? Do the principles a person praises survive the moment those principles become inconvenient? These questions are uncomfortable, partly because it is tempting to ask them only about people we already distrust.

As I read the Quran's descriptions of the hypocrites, I find something more troubling than a recognizable villain. Can a statement be true while the person who speaks it is insincere? Can an act of worship become an occasion for self-display? Can someone promise solidarity and disappear as soon as keeping that promise has a cost? The issue is not only deceiving other people. In 2:9, the deception turns back upon the person carrying it out.

I do not want this article to become a way of placing a permanent label on another person's forehead. In 9:101, even the messenger is told that there are hypocrites he does not know, while GOD knows them. This places a limit on what I can claim. I may examine speech, decisions, patterns of conduct, and harm. That is different from claiming complete access to someone's hidden intentions. If I forget the distinction, an article about truthfulness could become an excuse for false testimony.

In my earlier reflection, [The Steps of Satan](https://berkkoksal.com/writings/the_steps_of_satan_en/), I considered how a human being may move toward wrongdoing step by step. In [Who Are 'Those in Charge Among You' in 4:59?](https://berkkoksal.com/writings/who-are-those-in-charge-among-you-4-59_en/), I questioned the limits of human authority. The two questions meet here. What happens when a person begins to decorate wrongdoing with convincing language? And when I scrutinize the sincerity of others, do I forget how little I can actually know of their hearts?

## 1. Does Saying 'I Believe' Make a Person a Believer?

### Verse

> **2:8**
>
> وَمِنَ ٱلنَّاسِ مَن يَقُولُ ءَامَنَّا بِٱللَّهِ وَبِٱلْيَوْمِ ٱلْـَٔاخِرِ وَمَا هُم بِمُؤْمِنِينَ
>
> Then there are those who say, “We believe in GOD and the Last Day,” while they are not believers.
>
> Sonra inanan olmadıkları hâlde “Biz TANRI’ya ve Son Gün’e inanıyoruz” diyenler vardır.

Source: <a href="https://kuranteyit.com/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">kuranteyit.com</a>

> **63:1** — When  the  hypocrites  come  to  you they  say,  “We  bear  witness  that  you are  the  messenger  of  GOD.”*  GOD knows  that  you  are  His  messenger, and  GOD  bears  witness  that  the hypocrites are liars.

### Reflection on the Verse

The opening of 2:8 is ordinary enough to be unsettling. People declare belief in GOD and the Last Day. On the surface, this is a confession of faith. Yet the verse distinguishes the declaration from the reality of the people making it. I cannot ignore the gap. A statement about belief is not presented here as the final proof of belief.

Verse 63:1 makes the question sharper. The content of a testimony is affirmed by GOD, and yet those making the testimony are called liars. What strikes me is the distinction between **speaking a true proposition and bearing truthful witness from within oneself**. A person may repeat words that are correct while treating them as a convenient means of appearing loyal. The sentence remains true; the witness may not be sincere.

This turns my attention back to myself. When I declare faith, what authority do I allow that declaration to have over my choices? Do I use the words to describe a direction I am trying to live, or as protection against every question about my conduct? These verses do not grant me sight into another person's heart. They do, however, make it difficult to treat my own declarations as certificates beyond examination.

**Verse references:** 2:8, 63:1.

## 2. The First Person Deceived May Be the Deceiver

### Verse

> **2:9** — In trying to deceive GOD and those who  believe,  they  only  deceive themselves without perceiving.

> **2:10** — In  their  minds  there  is  a  disease. Consequently,  GOD  augments  their disease. They have incurred a painful retribution for their lying.

> **2:16** — It is they who bought the straying, at the expense of guidance. Such trade never prospers,  nor  do  they  receive any guidance.

### Reflection on the Verse

In 2:9, the attempt to deceive turns upon its makers. They seem to be directing deception toward GOD and the believers, but the verse says that they deceive themselves without perceiving it. I find that especially disturbing. It suggests that a false appearance may eventually become so familiar that its author no longer notices the distance between the appearance and reality.

Verse 2:10 connects lying with an affliction within the person. I do not read this as a medical diagnosis or as a description of people who have mental health conditions. The passage speaks about deception and lies. It makes me consider the moral harm of repeatedly defending something untrue. Perhaps a person can become increasingly skilled at explaining away a contradiction while becoming less willing to see it.

A dangerous form of confidence is the declaration that I know myself perfectly. Knowing myself should make me more accountable, not less open to correction. If the story that protects my ego becomes the only story I am willing to hear, I may cease to recognize the falsehood I originally chose to tell.

Verse 2:16 then brings choice into view: guidance is traded away for straying. The people described are not only maintaining a misleading image; they are presented as having exchanged one direction for another. I cannot use this to declare everyone who makes a mistake a hypocrite. But I can ask what happens when I repeatedly give up what I recognize as right because another path seems to serve me better.

**Verse references:** 2:9-10, 2:16.

## 3. Calling Corruption 'Doing What Is Right'

### Verse

> **2:11** — When they are told, “Do not commit  evil,”  they  say,  “But  we  are righteous!”

> **2:12** — In fact, they are evildoers, but they do not perceive.

### Reflection on the Verse

The people described in 2:11-12 do not merely hide their wrongdoing. When challenged, they claim to be acting rightly. The verse then contradicts that claim and says that they do not perceive what they are doing. This is a particularly difficult kind of failure: conduct that brings harm while the person responsible believes the name they have given it.

Human beings are remarkably capable of choosing noble titles for their own actions. We speak of restoring order, protecting others, standing for truth, or keeping people together. Those words can describe real goodness. They can also become a screen that prevents us from examining the damage that follows. What matters is not simply what I call an act, but whether my claim stands before the measure of righteousness.

I must be careful not to turn this verse into a weapon against everyone who disagrees with me. Disagreement itself is not the evil identified here. The warning concerns wrongdoing that is defended as goodness. If I judge the people around me without evidence while congratulating myself on being a reformer, I might be repeating the very pattern I thought I was opposing.

**Verse references:** 2:11-12.

## 4. Looking Down on Believers While Seeking Their Acceptance

### Verse

> **2:13** — When they are told,  “Believe like the people who believed,” they say, “Shall we believe like the fools who believed?” In fact, it is they who are fools, but they do not know.

> **2:14** — When they meet the believers, they say, “We believe,” but when alone with their devils, they say, “We are with you; we were only mocking.”

### Reflection on the Verse

In 2:13, contempt appears in the way believers are described. In 2:14, people speak one way in front of believers and another way when they are alone with their own circle. Reading these together, I see how disdain can coexist with the performance of belonging. The outward gesture invites trust; the hidden conversation mocks those who extended it.

Questioning a belief is not the same thing as mocking a believer. Nor is reconsidering an argument a sign of hypocrisy. What troubles me here is the ability to seek acceptance from people while treating them as contemptible behind their backs. That is no longer an honest difference of opinion. It is a betrayal of the trust made possible by the words spoken face to face.

This asks something concrete of me. Would I stand by my words if the person I am speaking about entered the room? Am I offering one image of myself to gain approval and another to gain status in a different group? The question is difficult precisely because it reaches into ordinary conversation, not only public religious life.

**Verse references:** 2:13-14.

## 5. Two Rooms, Two Accounts of Loyalty

### Verse

> **4:81** — They  pledge  obedience,  but  as  soon as they leave you, some of them harbor  intentions  contrary  to  what  they say.  GOD  records  their  innermost intentions.  You  shall  disregard them,  and  put  your  trust  in  GOD. GOD suffices as an advocate. 

> **3:119** — Here  you are  loving  them,  while they do not love you, and you believe in all the scripture. When they meet you they say, “We believe,” but as soon as they leave, they bite their fingers out of rage towards you. Say, “Die in your rage.” GOD is fully aware of the innermost thoughts.

> **3:167** — And  to  expose  the  hypocrites  who were  told,  “Come  fight  in  the  cause of  GOD,  or  contribute.”  They  said, “If we knew how to  fight, we would have  joined  you.”  They  were  closer to  disbelief  then  than  they  were to  belief.  They  uttered  with  their mouths  what  was  not  in  their  hearts. GOD knows what they conceal. 

### Reflection on the Verse

Verse 4:81 describes an outward pledge followed by intentions contrary to what was said. Verse 3:119 describes a profession of belief during an encounter and anger concealed after separation. In both, a public statement and a private direction stand against each other. It is not simply that a person speaks differently in different circumstances. It is that the appearance of trust hides a conflicting intention.

There is a place for privacy and restraint in human relationships. We do not owe every person our every thought, and silence need not be dishonest. But there is a difference between protecting something private and giving someone assurance that we intend to betray. That distinction matters if the subject is to remain morally serious rather than becoming a suspicion directed at every reserved person.

The verses also remind me of the limits of what I know. GOD records hidden intentions. I do not. I can compare words with subsequent actions and take reasonable precautions where trust has been broken. Yet the first clear responsibility is my own: when I promise loyalty or cooperation, will the promise still mean something after the meeting ends?

Verse 3:167 expresses the contradiction even more explicitly: they say with their mouths what is not in their hearts. Yet this statement occurs within a particular situation of conflict. It cannot become my license to claim knowledge of the inner lives of people I have never truly known.

**Verse references:** 4:81, 3:119, 3:167.

## 6. Eloquence, Impressive Appearances, and the Conduct Behind Them

### Verse

> **63:4** — When  you  see  them,  you  may  be impressed  by  their  looks.  And  when they speak, you may listen to their eloquence.  They  are  like  standing logs.  They  think  that  every  call  is intended  against  them.  These  are the  real  enemies;  beware  of  them. GOD  condemns  them;  they  have deviated. 

> **2:204** — Among  the  people,  one  may  impress you with his utterances concerning  this  life,  and  may  even  call upon  GOD  to  witness  his  innermost thoughts,  while  he  is  a  most  ardent opponent.

> **2:205** — As  soon  as  he  leaves,  he  roams  the earth  corruptingly,  destroying  properties  and  lives.  GOD  does  not  love corruption.

> **2:206** — When  he  is  told,  “Observe  GOD,” he  becomes  arrogantly  indignant. Consequently,  his  only  destiny  is Hell; what a miserable abode.

### Reflection on the Verse

Verse 63:4 notes how a person's appearance and speech can be impressive. Verse 2:204 goes further: a persuasive speaker even invokes GOD as witness to his inner thoughts. But the following verses reveal destructive conduct and arrogant resistance to correction. The movement from convincing presentation to harmful action is what gives these passages their force.

A composed voice can be helpful. Eloquence can communicate truth. Neither beauty nor intelligence is condemned merely for existing. Yet neither is proof of sincerity. A religious speaker, community leader, or ordinary person may use correct vocabulary and still need to be judged by what is actually done. I cannot conclude that every polished speaker is secretly corrupt; I can conclude that being impressed is not the same thing as having verified a claim.

There is a personal warning here too. If I grow accustomed to being heard, I may begin to mistake the quality of my explanation for the quality of my conduct. If correction feels like an attack on my identity, I may defend my image before examining the harm. The verses invite me to look beyond the stage and ask what happens when the speech is finished.

**Verse references:** 63:4, 2:204-206.

## 7. When Oaths Become a Shield

### Verse

> **63:2** — Under  the  guise  of  their  apparent faith,  they  repel  the  people  from  the path  of  GOD.  Miserable  indeed  is what they do.

> **63:3** — This  is  because  they  believed,  then disbelieved.  Hence,  their  minds  are blocked; they do not understand.

> **9:56** — They  swear  by  GOD  that  they  belong  with  you,  while  they  do  not belong  with  you;  they  are  divisive people.

### Reflection on the Verse

Verse 63:2 describes people who use an outward profession as cover while directing others away from GOD's path. Verse 9:56 describes solemn claims of belonging that do not reflect the actual state of those making them. What concerns me is the use of the language of faith not as an honest confession but as a tool for acquiring trust and influence.

Every community depends on words that carry promises: we stand together, we believe, we will keep faith with one another. Those words can sustain people. But if they become a disguise for an opposing purpose, the damage spreads beyond one individual. Others make decisions on the basis of trust that was deliberately made unreliable.

These passages do not suggest that everyone who takes an oath is suspect. The problem is the contradiction between what is claimed and what is sought. I want my use of GOD's name to make my responsibility heavier, never to make my statements immune to scrutiny. Sacred language must not become a barrier protecting an ordinary human interest.

**Verse references:** 63:2-3, 9:56.

## 8. Can Worship Become a Performance for an Audience?

### Verse

> **4:142** — The  hypocrites  think  that  they  are deceiving  GOD,  but  He  is  the  One who  leads  them  on.  When  they  get up  for  the  Contact  Prayer  (Salat), they  get  up  lazily.  That  is  because they  only  show  off  in  front  of  the people,  and  rarely  do  they  think  of GOD.

> **107:4** — And woe to those who observe the contact prayers (Salat)— 

> **107:5** — who  are  totally  heedless  of  their prayers. 

> **107:6** — They only show off. 

> **107:7** — And they forbid charity. 

### Reflection on the Verse

Verse 4:142 speaks of the hypocrites rising lazily for the Contact Prayer, displaying themselves before people, and remembering GOD only rarely. Verses 107:4-7 bring negligence, showing off, and hindering charity into the discussion of prayer. The presence of an outward religious act does not, by itself, settle the question of inward orientation.

I must not misuse these verses to diagnose someone who is tired, ill, distracted, or struggling to keep up a practice. The description in 4:142 is not simply a report of slow movement. Showing off and the rare remembrance of GOD are part of the same passage. Those are not things a bystander can reliably identify from one difficult morning.

The question I can apply to myself is more demanding. Would I still turn toward GOD if nobody knew I had prayed? Would I still want to do the right thing if no one praised me? The point is not to reject visible worship. It is to remember that its purpose cannot be replaced by the audience gathered around it.

**Verse references:** 4:142, 107:4-7.

## 9. Seeking Credit from Whoever Prevails

### Verse

> **4:141** — They  watch  you  and  wait;  if  you  attain  victory  from  GOD,  they  say  (to you),  “Were  we  not  with  you?”  But if  the  disbelievers  get  a  turn,  they  say (to  them),  “Did  we  not  side  with  you, and  protect  you  from  the  believers?”  GOD  will  judge  between  you on  the  Day  of  Resurrection.  GOD will  never  permit  the  disbelievers  to prevail over the believers.

> **4:143** — They  waver  in  between,  neither belonging to this group, nor that group.  Whomever  GOD  sends astray, you will never find a way to guide him.

### Reflection on the Verse

In 4:141, people present themselves as friends to whichever side has gained an advantage. In 4:143, the wavering between groups is described. Reading both, I see the danger of turning belonging into a calculation: not where truth is, but which association is likely to reward me.

A person can change their mind for good reasons. New evidence may correct an old opinion, and abandoning a mistake can be a sign of integrity. That is different from repeatedly presenting oneself as loyal to whichever group now offers victory. The issue is not change itself but whether principle is quietly replaced by the pursuit of benefit.

I wonder what happens to my convictions when holding them costs something. Do I revise them because I have learned more, or because I no longer want to be associated with those who are losing? These verses do not entitle me to assign the hidden intentions of every undecided person. They do make me examine the price I place on my own commitments.

**Verse references:** 4:141, 4:143.

## 10. Accepting Justice Only When It Favors Me

### Verse

> **24:47** — They  say,  “We  believe  in  GOD  and in  the  messenger,  and  we  obey,”  but then  some  of  them  slide  back  afterwards. These are not believers.

> **24:48** — When  they  are  invited  to  GOD  and His  messenger  to  judge  among them, some of them get upset.

> **24:49** — However,  if  the  judgment  is  in  their favor, they readily accept it!

> **24:50** — Is  there  a  disease  in  their  hearts? Are  they  doubtful?  Are  they  afraid that  GOD  and  His  messenger  may treat  them  unfairly?  In  fact,  it  is  they who are unjust.

> **4:60** — Have  you  noted  those  who  claim that  they  believe  in  what  was  revealed  to  you,  and  in  what  was revealed  before  you,  then  uphold the  unjust  laws  of  their  idols?  They were  commanded  to  reject  such laws.  Indeed, it  is the devil’s  wish  to lead them far astray.

> **4:61** — When  they  are  told,  “Come  to  what GOD  has  revealed,  and  to  the  messenger,”  you  see  the  hypocrites shunning you completely.

### Reflection on the Verse

The sequence of 24:47-50 is remarkably concrete. Belief and obedience are declared; then some turn away from a judgment they dislike. When a decision favors them, they accept it immediately. The question raised is not whether they can speak about justice. It is whether they recognize justice as binding when it does not reward them.

The same temptation can appear in small matters: dividing work, settling a disagreement, discussing money, or deciding how to treat someone we do not like. It is easy to praise fairness while receiving the larger share. What do I do when the same standard establishes that I owe something or that I was wrong? This is where a beautiful principle becomes a real burden.

Verse 24:50 raises the issue of injustice directly. That keeps this reflection from becoming a discussion of appearances alone. Speech, prayer, and affiliation do not replace a person's obligation to deal justly. Still, I have to know the relevant facts before using any particular dispute as evidence about a person's hidden faith.

Verses 4:60-61 extend the question to religious authority. People who claim to believe in revelation are described as turning to the unjust laws of their idols and avoiding what GOD has revealed when called toward it. Does the faith I declare shape where I seek guidance, or only what I am willing to say in public? This passage is not a ready-made label for every disagreement over law today. It is a warning about the divide between a claim of belief and the authority a person actually chooses.

**Verse references:** 24:47-50, 4:60-61.

## 11. Promoting Wrongdoing and Blocking What Is Right

### Verse

> **9:67** — The  hypocrite  men  and  the  hypocrite  women  belong  with  each other  − they advocate evil  and prohibit  righteousness,  and  they  are stingy. They forgot GOD, so He forgot them. The hypocrites are truly wicked.

> **63:7** — They  are  the  ones  who  say,  “Do  not give  any  money  to  those  who  followed  the  messenger  of  GOD,  perhaps  they  abandon  him!”  However, GOD  possesses  the  treasures  of  the heavens  and  the  earth,  but  the hypocrites do not comprehend.

### Reflection on the Verse

Verse 9:67 describes hypocritical men and women as supporting wrongdoing and obstructing what is right. Verse 63:7 shows a sharper practical method: calling for financial support to be withheld so that people will abandon a messenger. The issue is no longer confined to a misleading personal image. It becomes an effort to direct the lives of others through pressure.

I need to distinguish such pressure from the ordinary fact that resources are limited. Someone who cannot afford to give is not automatically one of the people described here. The conduct in these verses involves stopping good or deploying resources to redirect the choices of others. A genuine inability to help and a deliberate attempt to coerce are not interchangeable.

The collective dimension matters. When people encourage one another to maintain an injustice, wrongdoing acquires social protection. The question is not merely whether a community feels united, but what that unity makes possible. Does it protect the vulnerable and open the way to what is right, or does it silence those who refuse to cooperate with harm?

**Verse references:** 9:67, 63:7.

## 12. Reluctant Giving and Contempt for the Generous

### Verse

> **9:54** — What  prevented  the  acceptance  of their  spending  is  that  they  disbelieved  in  GOD  and  His  messenger, and  when  they  observed  the  Contact  Prayers  (Salat),*  they  observed them  lazily,  and  when  they  gave  to charity, they did so grudgingly.

> **9:79** — Those  who  criticize  the  generous believers for giving too much, and ridicule the poor believers for giving too little, GOD despises them. They have incurred a painful retribution.

### Reflection on the Verse

Verse 9:54 connects reluctant spending with a broader pattern of disbelief and lazy observance of the Contact Prayer. Verse 9:79 describes people who criticize generous believers for giving much and mock poorer believers for giving little. Together, they expose an attitude that can corrupt giving from two directions: unwillingness to participate in good, and contempt toward those who do.

The amount given is a poor substitute for understanding a human being. Greater wealth does not automatically mean greater sincerity, and small means do not indicate a small heart. Verse 9:79 is particularly important here because the less wealthy are mocked for offering what they can. It would be a distortion to turn this material into a simple ranking of donors.

I would rather ask what I expect when I give. Is the help directed toward a need or toward the image I want others to have of me? Do I judge another person's generosity without knowing their circumstances? The verses make financial conduct part of the moral question, while also warning me against making money itself the measure of a soul.

**Verse references:** 9:54, 9:79.

## 13. A Promise Forgotten Once the Blessing Arrives

### Verse

> **9:75** — Some  of  them  even  pledged:  “If GOD showered us with His grace, we would be charitable, and would lead a righteous life.”

> **9:76** — But when He did shower them with His provisions, they became stingy, and turned away in aversion.

> **9:77** — Consequently,  He  plagued  them with hypocrisy in their hearts, till the day they meet Him. This is because they broke their promises to GOD, and because of their lying.

### Reflection on the Verse

The movement in 9:75-77 is painfully recognizable. A promise is made: if blessings arrive, righteousness and generosity will follow. The blessings do arrive, and the promise is abandoned. The passage then connects the resulting hypocrisy to breaking the promise made to GOD and to lying. The sequence matters. It is a story of choices sustained over time.

During hardship, people may make fervent promises about who they will become if life changes. Not every forgotten intention carries the same spiritual judgment as the people addressed in these verses. But the passage still asks me something precise. What becomes of my promises once I am no longer desperate? Does comfort release me from responsibilities that I embraced when I needed help?

There is a danger in treating a promise to GOD as a temporary bargain. If the only purpose of the words was to obtain what I wanted, the promise may seem unnecessary once the benefit has been received. I want to take my own commitments seriously before making them, and even more seriously after I no longer have an immediate reason to remember them.

**Verse references:** 9:75-77.

## 14. Mocking Revelation and Then Calling It a Joke

### Verse

> **9:64** — The  hypocrites  worry  that  a  sura may  be  revealed  exposing  what  is inside  their  hearts.  Say,  “Go  ahead and  mock.  GOD  will  expose  exactly what you are afraid of.”

> **9:65** — If  you  ask  them,  they  would  say, “We were only  mocking  and kidding.” Say, “Do you realize that you are mocking GOD, and His revelations, and His messenger?”

> **4:140** — He  has  instructed  you  in  the  scripture  that:  if  you  hear  GOD’s  revelations  being  mocked  and  ridiculed, you  shall  not  sit  with  them,  unless they  delve  into  another  subject. Otherwise,  you  will  be  as  guilty as  they  are.  GOD  will  gather  the hypocrites  and  the  disbelievers together in Hell. 

### Reflection on the Verse

In 9:64-65, the people described fear that what they have concealed may be exposed. When questioned, they try to explain their conduct as joking. Verse 4:140 gives an instruction about remaining in a gathering where GOD's revelations are being mocked. In these passages, ridicule is not simply a personality trait; it is tied to a person's treatment of revelation.

I do not want to confuse sincere questioning with mockery. People can challenge an interpretation, acknowledge that they do not understand a verse, and ask difficult questions. Inquiry is not the same as ridicule. To use these verses as a way of silencing every uncomfortable question would be to add a claim that the passages do not themselves establish.

Yet humor can also be used to avoid accountability. A person may insult what another holds sacred and then say that nothing serious was meant. Not every joke is a religious violation, and not every hurtful sentence establishes hypocrisy. But I should still be willing to ask whether I have used laughter to escape responsibility for words I chose to speak.

**Verse references:** 9:64-65, 4:140.

## 15. Withdrawing in Hardship and Appearing When There Is Gain

### Verse

> **4:72** — Surely, there are those among you who would drag their feet, then, if a setback afflicts you, they would say, “GOD has blessed me that I was not martyred with them.”

> **4:73** — But if you attain a blessing from GOD, they would say, as if no friendship ever existed between you and them, “I wish I was with them, so I could share in such a great victory.”

> **33:12** — The  hypocrites  and  those  with doubts  in  their  hearts  said,  “What GOD  and  His  messenger  promised us was no more than an illusion!”

> **33:13** — A group of them said, “O people of Yathrib, you cannot attain victory; go back.” Others made up excuses to the prophet: “Our homes are vulnerable,” when they were not vulnerable. They just wanted to flee. 

> **59:11** — Have  you  noted  those  who  are plagued  with  hypocrisy,  and  how they  said  to  their  companions  in  disbelief  among  the  people  of  the scripture,  “If  you  are  evicted  we  will go out with you, and will never obey anyone  who  opposes  you.  If  anyone fights  you,  we  will  fight  on  your side.”  GOD  bears  witness  that  they are liars.

> **59:12** — In  fact,  if  they  were  evicted,  they would  not  have  gone  out  with  them, and  if  anyone  fought  them,  they would  not  have  supported  them. Even  if  they  supported  them,  they would  have  turned  around  and  fled. They could never win.

### Reflection on the Verse

These passages describe different events, not one interchangeable story. In 4:72-73, participation is viewed through the lens of personal loss or gain. In 33:12-13, hardship brings despairing speech and excuses for retreat. In 59:11-12, assurances of solidarity are exposed as empty when support would have a cost. What connects them for me is the strain placed on professed loyalty by difficult circumstances.

Fear is human. A person may lack the strength, health, or means to do what someone else demands. The verses have particular settings, and I cannot flatten those settings into a condemnation of every frightened or hesitant person. The moral question is more specific: Was a promise offered to secure a reputation, only to be set aside when it became inconvenient to keep it?

Sometimes the most honest statement is that I cannot provide the help being asked for. That may be disappointing, but it is not the same as making a grand promise with no intention of bearing its cost. When I tell someone that I will stand with them, these verses challenge me to consider what those words will require after the comfortable conversation ends.

**Verse references:** 4:72-73, 33:12-13, 59:11-12.

## 16. Who Knows the Hidden Intention? Is There a Path Back?

### Verse

> **9:101** — Among  the  Arabs  around  you,  there are  hypocrites.  Also,  among  the  city dwellers,  there  are  those  who  are accustomed  to  hypocrisy.  You  do not  know  them,  but  we  know  them. We  will  double  the  retribution  for them,  then  they  end  up  committed to a terrible retribution.

> **4:145** — The hypocrites will be committed to the lowest pit of Hell, and you will find no one to help them.

> **4:146** — Only  those  who  repent,  reform, hold fast to GOD, and devote their religion absolutely to GOD alone, will be counted with the believers. GOD will bless the believers with a great recompense.

> **4:63** — GOD  is  fully  aware  of  their  innermost  intentions.  You  shall  ignore them,  enlighten  them,  and  give  them good  advice  that  may  save  their souls.

### Reflection on the Verse

Verse 9:101 gives me a necessary boundary. The messenger is told that there are hypocrites unknown to him, while GOD knows them. If that is so, I cannot pretend that observing a few actions gives me complete knowledge of a person's inner life. I can examine evidence, respond to deception, and take wrongdoing seriously. But judging conduct is not the same thing as possessing the unseen record of someone's heart.

Verse 4:145 speaks with great severity about the fate of the hypocrites. Then comes 4:146, describing repentance, reform, holding fast to GOD, and devoting religion absolutely to GOD alone. The order matters. If I speak only of condemnation and ignore the next verse, I leave out the possibility of return that the text itself includes. I can recognize signs of reform where conduct supports that recognition without claiming perfect certainty about someone's inner state.

Verse 4:63 also mentions instructing and offering good advice. That matters for the tone of a reflection like this one. It is possible to name wrongdoing without indulging in reckless accusations. I need neither excuse deception nor claim a power of judgment that belongs to GOD. The work is to remain truthful about what can be known, careful about what cannot, and serious about the possibility of change.

**Verse references:** 9:101, 4:145-146, 4:63.

## 17. Arabic Word Roots: Declaration, Deception, Corruption, and Showing Off

### Verse

> **2:8** — Then there are those who say, “We believe in GOD and the Last Day,” while they are not believers.

> **2:9** — In trying to deceive GOD and those who  believe,  they  only  deceive themselves without perceiving.

> **4:142** — The  hypocrites  think  that  they  are deceiving  GOD,  but  He  is  the  One who  leads  them  on.  When  they  get up  for  the  Contact  Prayer  (Salat), they  get  up  lazily.  That  is  because they  only  show  off  in  front  of  the people,  and  rarely  do  they  think  of GOD.

> **9:77** — Consequently,  He  plagued  them with hypocrisy in their hearts, till the day they meet Him. This is because they broke their promises to GOD, and because of their lying.

### Reflection on the Verse

Looking at Arabic roots does not authorize me to invent a ruling that the verses do not express. A root's broad semantic field cannot replace the particular sentence in which a word appears. My purpose here is narrower: to listen more closely to the relationships already present in Rashad Khalifa’s Authorized English Translation and, where relevant, in the Turkish translation of the Authorized English Translation.

**ن ف ق (n-f-q):** The form *al-munāfiqīn* in 4:142 refers to the hypocrites. It is the specific word used for those addressed in the passage. The root does not allow me to declare that everyone who behaves inconsistently belongs to the same condemned category. The surrounding verse mentions showing off and rarely remembering GOD, and those details must not disappear.

**خ د ع (kh-d-ʿ):** The form *yukhādiʿūna* in 2:9 belongs to the field of deception. The remarkable movement of that verse is that the attempted deception returns upon the deceivers themselves. The word helps me attend to the moral action described, but it cannot supply secret information about someone I observe today.

**ك ذ ب (k-dh-b):** In 9:77, *yakdhibūn* concerns lying. It appears alongside a broken promise made to GOD. A careless mistake and a deliberate lie are not the same thing. Studying the word should make me more attentive to the passage, not more eager to accuse.

**ف س د (f-s-d) and ص ل ح (ṣ-l-ḥ):** In 2:11-12, corruption and the claim to be doing what is right stand in tension. A person may describe an action as reform while the passage presents that action as evil. The verbal contrast cautions me against taking a self-applied title as proof of what a person has actually done.

**ر أ ي (r-ʾ-y):** The form *yurāʾūna* in 4:142 relates to showing off, with the semantic field of seeing and being seen. The Authorized English Translation's wording and its Turkish translation both direct attention to performance before other people. That does not mean all visible worship is performed for an audience.

**ذ ك ر (dh-k-r):** The form *yadhkurūna* also appears in 4:142 and concerns remembering. The connection between outward display and the rarity of remembering GOD is already part of the verse. It raises a question more important than visibility alone: whom am I thinking of as I act?

**ش ه د (sh-h-d):** The testimony vocabulary of 63:1 opens another side of the declaration in 2:8. A person can utter words of witness without having the inward truthfulness those words appear to claim. Yet definitive knowledge of that inward reality remains with GOD.

Taken together, these words do not amount to a new method for identifying hypocrites. They are a series of challenges: speaking, deceiving, lying, claiming reform, appearing before others, remembering, and bearing witness. They help only when their limits remain within those of the verses themselves.

**Verse references:** 2:8-12, 4:142, 9:77, 63:1.

## 18. Other Claims

### Verse

> **9:101** — Among  the  Arabs  around  you,  there are  hypocrites.  Also,  among  the  city dwellers,  there  are  those  who  are accustomed  to  hypocrisy.  You  do not  know  them,  but  we  know  them. We  will  double  the  retribution  for them,  then  they  end  up  committed to a terrible retribution.

> **4:146** — Only  those  who  repent,  reform, hold fast to GOD, and devote their religion absolutely to GOD alone, will be counted with the believers. GOD will bless the believers with a great recompense.

### Reflection on the Verse

In traditional interpretation, hypocrisy is sometimes discussed on two levels: a concealed lack of belief and an inconsistency between a person's stated values and actions. Some readers find this distinction useful because inward conviction and outward conduct are not identical. But a scheme becomes misleading when it is treated as an independent license to assign a hidden spiritual identity to anyone who behaves badly. I do not want a later category to override the actual language of the verses.

Some approaches emphasize the collective dangers of hypocrisy and call for firm responses. Others place greater weight on personal moral examination, reform, and the possibility of repentance. I cannot turn either emphasis into a universal method for dealing with every person in every circumstance. Verse 9:101 limits what humans can know about hidden intentions, while 4:146 includes a path of reform. Any broader interpretive claim needs to remain clearly distinct from those statements.

In everyday speech, we may call someone a hypocrite simply because we dislike them, because they are uncertain, or because they changed a position. Such ordinary uses may have their own social explanations. But they should not be silently equated with the deliberate deception, showing off, or pursuit of advantage depicted in the verses. These external categories remain a separate discussion, not the foundation of the reflection.

**Verse references:** 9:101, 4:145-146, 2:8-9, 4:142.

## 19. Conclusion: Looking for Another Person's Mask Without Forgetting My Own Face

### Verse

> **2:8** — Then there are those who say, “We believe in GOD and the Last Day,” while they are not believers.

> **4:142** — The  hypocrites  think  that  they  are deceiving  GOD,  but  He  is  the  One who  leads  them  on.  When  they  get up  for  the  Contact  Prayer  (Salat), they  get  up  lazily.  That  is  because they  only  show  off  in  front  of  the people,  and  rarely  do  they  think  of GOD.

> **9:101** — Among  the  Arabs  around  you,  there are  hypocrites.  Also,  among  the  city dwellers,  there  are  those  who  are accustomed  to  hypocrisy.  You  do not  know  them,  but  we  know  them. We  will  double  the  retribution  for them,  then  they  end  up  committed to a terrible retribution.

> **4:146** — Only  those  who  repent,  reform, hold fast to GOD, and devote their religion absolutely to GOD alone, will be counted with the believers. GOD will bless the believers with a great recompense.

### Reflection on the Verse

I began by asking, 'What are the characteristics of the hypocrites?' What I have found is not merely a label for a group of people. It is a connected examination of speaking and believing, claiming to do good and actually doing good, performing worship and remembering GOD, giving and withholding, keeping promises, dealing justly, and changing allegiance for advantage. Each issue asks whether the outward life and the inward direction of a person still belong together.

The warnings are severe, and their severity should not be softened. But I cannot read them only as an invitation to identify the faults of others. If I turn the whole subject into a search for another person's mask, I may neglect my responsibility to examine my own words. Verse 2:8 makes a declaration of faith insufficient by itself. Verse 4:142 makes the purpose of worship impossible to dismiss. Verse 9:101 reminds me that the hidden record of another person's heart is not mine.

The boundary of knowledge does not require me to overlook wrongdoing. False promises can be challenged; damage can be documented; fair precautions can be taken. Still, a decision about conduct is not a pronouncement about someone's final standing before GOD. Verse 4:146 keeps repentance and reform before me. This path must matter when I consider others, and it must remain open when I examine myself.

The list below gathers characteristics described across several passages. It is a reading guide, not a checklist that entitles anyone to pass a final verdict on a person. **No single listed behavior, observed from the outside, gives me complete knowledge of another person's heart.** Each point returns the question to the one place where I have the clearest responsibility: my own conduct.

**Verse references:** 2:8-14, 2:204-206, 3:119, 4:72-73, 4:81, 4:141-143, 4:145-146, 9:54, 9:56, 9:64-65, 9:67, 9:75-77, 9:79, 9:101, 24:47-50, 33:12-13, 59:11-12, 63:1-4, 63:7, 107:4-6.

### Characteristics of the Hypocrites in the Quran — Verse-Referenced List

- **Declaring faith without genuine belief:** A gap between outward profession and actual belief (2:8; 63:1).
- **Using true words for insincere testimony:** Treating a true statement as proof of the speaker's inward sincerity (63:1).
- **Attempting to deceive others:** Pursuing deception toward believers (2:9).
- **Deceiving themselves:** Failing to perceive the falsehood in the image they maintain (2:9-10).
- **Trading guidance for straying:** Treating departure from the right path as a gain (2:16).
- **Persisting in lies:** Enlarging the distance between speech and reality (2:10; 9:77).
- **Presenting wrongdoing as reform:** Giving the name of righteousness to harmful action (2:11-12).
- **Looking down on believers:** Mocking and dismissing those who believe (2:13-14).
- **Changing faces with the audience:** Speaking one way with believers and another way among a different circle (2:14; 3:119).
- **Professing obedience while harboring contrary intentions:** Allowing private purposes to contradict public assurances (4:81).
- **Using eloquence and appearance to build misleading trust:** Speaking impressively while acting destructively (2:204-206; 63:4).
- **Making oaths and religious appearances a shield:** Employing professions of belonging as a cover for misleading others (9:56; 63:2-3).
- **Showing off in worship:** Performing Contact Prayers for an audience (4:142; 107:4-6).
- **Rarely remembering GOD:** Allowing the outward act to become detached from remembrance (4:142).
- **Taking sides according to advantage:** Claiming affiliation with whoever appears to be winning (4:141-143).
- **Accepting judgment only when it favors them:** Turning justice into a matter of self-interest (24:47-50).
- **Professing belief while turning to unjust authorities:** Separating a declaration of faith from the standards actually consulted (4:60-61).
- **Promoting wrongdoing and obstructing right action:** Sustaining evil through social support (9:67).
- **Using money to pressure others:** Encouraging support to be withheld in order to change people's direction (63:7).
- **Being miserly or reluctant in giving:** Withholding or giving without sincere willingness in the patterns described (9:54, 9:67).
- **Mocking charitable people for the amount they give:** Ridiculing both generous and poorer believers (9:79).
- **Abandoning promises when blessings arrive:** Turning away from pledged righteousness after receiving provision (9:75-77).
- **Mocking revelations:** Retreating behind the claim that it was only a joke (9:64-65; 4:140).
- **Withdrawing when solidarity carries a cost:** Seeking advantage in success and distance in hardship (4:72-73; 33:12-13).
- **Promising support that is never delivered:** Offering assurances of solidarity and abandoning them when tested (59:11-12).
