# [35:18] Who Are the Only People Who Heed Warnings? Reverence in Privacy, Prayer, and Responsibility

**Author:** Berk KÖKSAL
**Date:** November 23, 2026
**Place:** Mersin

Peace. Peace be upon you.

When I warn someone repeatedly and see no change, I begin to examine my delivery. Perhaps my words were too harsh. Perhaps I was not clear enough. Perhaps another example would have made the difference. These questions can be useful, but 35:18 turns my attention toward something I cannot control: the person who receives a warning. Before speaking of who responds, the verse declares that one soul cannot carry another soul’s sins. Its answer about listening is inseparable from responsibility.

I find this unsettling because it prevents several easy conclusions. It does not allow me to decide that my social circle, my religious label, or my writing qualifies me as a person who truly heeds GOD’s warnings. Nor does it allow me to treat people who disagree with me as spiritually deaf. The verse speaks about reverence in privacy, the Contact Prayers, the purification of one’s own soul, and the final return to GOD. Those are not credentials to display; they are questions to live with.

In <a href="https://www.berkkoksal.com/writings/43-40-can-you-make-the-deaf-hear-en/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">“Can You Make the Deaf Hear?”</a> I considered the limits of bringing another person to understanding. In <a href="https://www.berkkoksal.com/writings/deep-reverence-for-god-ego-disrespect-and-the-inner-reality-of-crossing-the-boundary/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">“Deep Reverence for GOD”</a> I examined the distance between public devotion and private decisions. Verse 35:18 brings the questions together. What happens to a warning after it reaches me? Does it remain a sentence I can repeat, or does it reshape the choices for which I alone will answer?

The purpose of this reflection is to consider 35:18 alongside its related verses, exploring what witnessing the oneness of GOD means in the daily practice of knowledge, justice, truthfulness, submission, and personal responsibility.

## Summary

> **35:18** — No soul can carry the sins of another soul. If a soul that is loaded with sins implores another to bear part of its load, no other soul can carry any part of it, even if they were related. The only people to heed your warnings are those who reverence their Lord, even when alone in their privacy, and observe the Contact Prayers (Salat). Whoever purifies his soul, does so for his own good. To GOD is the final destiny.

> **36:11** — You will be heeded only by those who uphold this message, and reverence the Most Gracious—even when alone in their privacy. Give them good news of forgiveness and a generous recompense.

> **35:23** — You are no more than a warner.

> **39:18** — They are the ones who examine all words, then follow the best. These are the ones whom GOD has guided; these are the ones who possess intelligence.

> **50:33** — They reverenced the Most Gracious, in their privacy, and came wholeheartedly.

> **51:55** — And remind, for the reminder benefits the believers.

> **6:48** — We do not send the messengers except as deliverers of good news, as well as warners. Those who believe and reform have nothing to fear, nor will they grieve.

### Reflection on the Verse

There is something unsettling about the way 35:18 answers the question. I might expect an account of people who have heard enough sermons, who belong to the right community, or who know how to defend their beliefs. Instead, the verse begins with a burden no one else can carry for me. It then describes those who heed warnings through what happens where an audience cannot follow them: reverence for their Lord even in privacy, and the observance of the Contact Prayers. Finally, it returns to purification and to GOD as the final destination.

I find the sequence difficult to escape. A warning is not merely information arriving from outside. It concerns a person who will have to answer for his or her own choices. I can be surrounded by people who agree with me and still fail to examine my conduct. I can repeat an accurate warning to someone else while leaving it untouched in my own life. The verse gives me no reason to mistake the sound of a warning for obedience to it.

The word “only” demands care. I cannot casually weaken what 35:18 says, but neither can I turn it into a license to name the individuals whose inner state I supposedly know. Its description asks me first to look at my own life. If I were left completely alone with a choice, what would guide me? And when I return from that solitude, does the prayer I claim to observe have any bearing on the person I become?

**Verse references:** 35:18.

## 2. What It Means to Carry One’s Own Responsibility

### Verse

> **6:164** — Say, "Shall I seek other than GOD as a lord, when He is the Lord of all things? No soul benefits except from its own works, and none bears the burden of another. Ultimately, you return to your Lord, then He informs you regarding all your disputes."

### Reflection on the Verse

6:164 makes the responsibility of the soul difficult to outsource. No one benefits except from his or her own works, and nobody bears another person's burden. The verse concludes by returning disputed matters to the Lord. This touches the beginning and the end of 35:18: the personal burden, and the final destination that cannot be delegated to somebody else.

I do not read this as an argument against helping a person in trouble. The texts concern responsibility for one's deeds; they do not tell me to abandon compassion. There is a crucial difference between helping someone face a wrong and pretending that my loyalty can make that person's wrong vanish. A parent cannot manufacture the sincerity of a child. A respected teacher cannot carry a student's choices. Nor can I hide my own conduct behind the status of someone I admire.

Sometimes I want another person to reassure me that I am already right. That reassurance may feel like relief, but it does not replace the examination of my actions. If 35:18 is read together with 6:164, the question becomes personal: Am I seeking guidance, or merely someone willing to speak in my favor? The warning may be the moment when I stop searching for a substitute conscience and accept that the burden is mine.

**Verse references:** 6:164.

## 3. Hearing a Warning or Upholding the Message?

### Verse

> **36:11** — You will be heeded only by those who uphold this message, and reverence the Most Gracious—even when alone in their privacy. Give them good news of forgiveness and a generous recompense.

### Reflection on the Verse

36:11 identifies people who uphold the message and reverence the Most Gracious even in privacy. It brings the question raised by 35:18 into sharper focus. The issue is not whether the warning reached an ear. A message can be heard, repeated, debated, and even memorized without being allowed to direct a decision. To uphold a message is a more demanding matter.

I know that distinction from ordinary life. A person can understand that an action is unjust and still carry it out for convenience. I can acknowledge a truthful criticism and still spend the evening inventing a defense for myself. Such experiences do not allow me to pronounce judgment on the hidden faith of others. They do, however, expose how easily I confuse understanding with following.

Both verses connect the reception of a warning to reverence that survives solitude. This is not a badge I can verify by watching someone for a few hours. It is a measure by which I can question myself. 36:11 also speaks of forgiveness and generous recompense, so the encounter with a warning is not described only in the language of threat. There is good news for those who respond. I want to remember that when correction feels uncomfortable: hearing what is true can be the beginning of a different life, rather than simply another occasion to feel accused.

**Verse references:** 36:11.

## 4. Seeing and Hearing: Similar Senses, Different Responses

### Verse

> **35:19** — The blind and the seer are not equal.

> **35:20** — Nor are the darkness and the light.

> **35:21** — Nor are the coolness of the shade and the heat of the sun.

> **35:22** — Nor are the living and the dead; GOD causes whomever He wills to hear. You cannot make hearers out of those in the graves.

### Reflection on the Verse

In 35:19–22, a series of contrasts follows the account of those who heed warnings: the blind and the seer, darkness and light, shade and heat, the living and the dead. I do not want to seize those comparisons as insults to throw at people who disagree with me. They first ask me to consider whether merely possessing the organs of sight and hearing means that I have responded to what was shown or said.

Two people may encounter exactly the same words and move in different directions. One asks what needs to change; another immediately searches for an excuse. I cannot infer from that difference everything that is happening within either person. The point I take from these verses is more modest and more serious: reception is not a mechanical result of exposure. The fact that I can quote something does not establish that I have allowed it to illuminate my behavior.

The end of 35:22, about the limits of making others hear, is especially sobering. I might care deeply about a person and want to persuade them with one more argument. Yet care does not give me control over another will. The contrasts lead toward 35:23's description of a warner, not an owner of human hearts. Perhaps the fairest place to apply them first is the moment I refuse to see what is inconvenient in myself.

**Verse references:** 35:19, 35:20, 35:21, 35:22.

## 5. Delivering a Warning Is Not Controlling Its Reception

### Verse

> **35:23** — You are no more than a warner.

> **35:24** — We have sent you* with the truth, a bearer of good news, as well as a warner. Every community must receive a warner.

### Reflection on the Verse

The messenger is addressed in 35:23 as no more than a warner. The following verse presents the mission as bringing truth, good news, and warning. I read these statements beside 35:18 because they stop me from measuring the value of a truthful warning solely by the reaction it produces. Delivering a message and determining another person's inner response are not the same act.

This matters to me whenever I speak with someone I love about a difficult subject. It is tempting to believe that care entitles me to an outcome. If my words fail to change the other person, I may grow harsher or begin to treat the person as a problem to be conquered. But these verses do not make human coercion an additional duty. They describe the warning and its limits, and they preserve the distinction between speaking the truth and commanding another's inward choice.

That does not excuse careless speech. If I misrepresent a verse, speak without knowledge, or insult the listener, I cannot hide behind the word “warner.” Truthfulness remains my responsibility. Yet after speaking carefully, I must leave space for a decision that does not belong to me. I have no need to pretend that persuasion gives me a share in GOD's authority. The limits of warning can make a person more patient, rather than less concerned.

**Verse references:** 35:23, 35:24.

## 6. Why Do Some Warnings Appear Not to Work?

### Verse

> **2:6** — As for those who disbelieve, it is the same for them; whether you warn them, or not warn them, they cannot believe.*

> **36:10** — It is the same whether you warn them or not, they cannot believe.*

### Reflection on the Verse

2:6 and 36:10 describe people for whom warning and not warning lead to the same result. These are difficult statements, particularly for someone who hopes that one clear explanation will be enough to convince everyone. Together with 35:18, they make me question the assumption that truth must always succeed through repetition.

I do not think these verses authorize me to identify a particular person and declare, “Nothing can ever reach you.” The texts report something serious about disbelief and the limits of warning; they do not give me an instrument for reading any individual's future. To use them as a personal weapon would be especially dangerous because it could let me evade responsibility for my own manner of speaking.

There is another danger as well: I could hear these verses and conclude that trying to communicate is pointless. But 35:23–24 still describe the sending of warning and good news. Holding these passages together restrains both extremes. I cannot force belief, but I need not give up honest communication. The harder task is to know my limits without turning those limits into contempt. And when a warning makes me uncomfortable, perhaps I should avoid hurriedly assigning myself to the group that understands while assigning everyone else to those who cannot.

**Verse references:** 2:6, 36:10.

## 7. When a Reminder Finds Its Listener

### Verse

> **51:55** — And remind, for the reminder benefits the believers.

> **50:45** — We are fully aware of everything they utter, while you have no power over them. Therefore, remind with this Quran, those who reverence My warnings.

### Reflection on the Verse

51:55 tells the reader to remind because reminders benefit believers. 50:45, while acknowledging what people say and the lack of power to control them, directs a reminder through the Quran to those who reverence GOD's warnings. I hear in these verses a distinction between the responsibility to convey and the possibility of benefit. A reminder is neither a guarantee of change nor an empty gesture.

I sometimes think of reminders as new information. Yet many of the corrections that matter most to me concern things I already know. I know not to distort a fact because it threatens my pride. I know that justice is not reserved for people who please me. I know that the way I behave in private matters. What I may need is not a more elaborate explanation but the willingness to let a familiar truth interrupt an established habit.

If a reminder benefits someone, I should be grateful rather than claim ownership of that person's change. If it does not seem to do so, I cannot know the entire inward story. The verses keep the focus on the message and the limits of human power. They also confront me with a direct question: When the Quran reminds me of something I would rather overlook, am I still willing to hear it?

**Verse references:** 51:55, 50:45.

## 8. Can Knowledge Become Reverence?

### Verse

> **35:28** — Also, the people, the animals, and the livestock come in various colors. This is why the people who truly reverence GOD are those who are knowledgeable. GOD is Almighty, Forgiving.

### Reflection on the Verse

35:28 makes a strong connection between knowledge and reverence for GOD. Its examples of varied colors in people, animals, and livestock appear in the same verse. I find that setting important: the call to knowledge does not have to be reduced to titles, ranks, or the authority of one specialized group. The verse speaks of the people who truly reverence GOD as knowledgeable; it does not provide a roster of approved personalities.

Knowledge can certainly make me more careful. It can also make me proud. I may know the citation for a passage without applying the justice that passage requires. I may be excellent at identifying an error in another person's reasoning while being unable to acknowledge one in my own. The verse makes me ask what my learning is doing to me. Does it deepen my awareness of responsibility, or merely enlarge the collection of arguments with which I defend myself?

I do not want to pretend that reverence replaces inquiry or that inquiry automatically proves reverence. 35:28 makes a connection; it does not settle every question about how expertise should be assessed. The most credible response for me is to keep studying while resisting the idea that knowledge gives me ownership of truth. If learning leaves me less willing to be corrected, I should examine whether I have understood its purpose.

**Verse references:** 35:28.

## 9. Examining Words and Then Following the Best

### Verse

> **39:18** — They are the ones who examine all words, then follow the best. These are the ones whom GOD has guided; these are the ones who possess intelligence.

> **17:36** — You shall not accept any information, unless you verify it for yourself. I have given you the hearing, the eyesight, and the brain, and you are responsible for using them.

### Reflection on the Verse

39:18 describes people who examine words and then follow the best. 17:36 commands verification and speaks of responsibility for hearing, eyesight, and the brain. These verses challenge a quiet but common habit of mine: accepting a statement because I like its source, or rejecting one because I dislike the person who speaks it.

A warning is not made true by the number of times it is repeated. Nor does an uncomfortable statement become false merely because it unsettles me. The commitment to examine requires work. I need to distinguish the wording of a verse from the claim made about it; a witnessed fact from a rumor; a carefully reasoned inference from something I merely hope to be correct. 17:36 leaves me no permission to treat the tools of understanding as ornaments.

This is also why I hesitate to confuse reverence with blind agreement. I can treat a speaker respectfully while still checking an assertion. I can examine a disagreement without turning it into hostility. The most difficult test arrives when evidence contradicts the conclusion I wanted. If I care more about being right than about following what is true, a warning may become another occasion for self-defense. Yet 39:18 speaks not only of examining words but of following. The work is incomplete until the conclusion is allowed to affect a choice.

**Verse references:** 39:18, 17:36.

## 10. From Trembling to Softening: Emotion and Change

### Verse

> **39:23** — GOD has revealed herein the best Hadith; a book that is consistent, and points out both ways (to Heaven and Hell). The skins of those who reverence their Lord cringe therefrom, then their skins and their hearts soften up for GOD's message. Such is GOD's guidance; He bestows it upon whomever He wills. As for those sent astray by GOD, nothing can guide them.

### Reflection on the Verse

39:23 describes bodily and inward responses to GOD's message: trembling, followed by a softening of skin and heart. I am struck by the movement between the two. It makes me cautious about using a single emotion as proof that a warning has done its work. Fear can be intense and still fade without changing a decision. Calmness can be genuine, but it can also conceal indifference. The verse gives me a scene of response, not a method for measuring another person's soul.

I know that a text may move me deeply. At times I may be silent for a while after reading it. Yet if I then return to the very injustice the text confronted, what has that feeling achieved? This is not to dismiss emotion. It is to ask what follows it. 35:18 brings the inquiry back to purification and personal responsibility, while 39:23 makes space for a heart that is affected rather than merely argumentative.

I want to be able to receive a difficult correction without treating it as humiliation. There may be a form of softness that is harder than anger: admitting that I did not see clearly, that I injured someone, or that a cherished belief needs re-examination. I cannot manufacture such a response on command. But when a warning reaches me, I can resist the immediate impulse to turn it into a contest.

**Verse references:** 39:23.

## 11. Who Am I When No One Is Watching?

### Verse

> **50:33** — They reverenced the Most Gracious, in their privacy, and came wholeheartedly.

### Reflection on the Verse

50:33 speaks of people who reverence the Most Gracious in privacy and come wholeheartedly. Its closeness to 35:18 is striking. Both make the unseen part of a human life relevant to the question of receiving a warning. Public reputation, however impressive, does not settle what a person chooses when there is no audience to reward the choice.

I can control how I present myself much more easily than I can control the motives that arise when I am alone. Perhaps I do the right thing because people are watching, because a habit is expected of me, or because I hope someone will praise the result. Those motives need careful examination. Neither verse asks me to disclose another person's private life, nor does either grant me a right to invent hidden faults. I can start with the private decisions that are already mine to know.

The phrase about coming wholeheartedly reminds me that solitude is not the whole subject. A person's direction matters, too. Am I turning toward GOD only when I have run out of other support, or do I seek to keep that direction in ordinary hours? I cannot claim that a moment of sincerity proves permanent faithfulness. But it may be the place where the difference between outward performance and inward commitment becomes visible to me.

**Verse references:** 50:33.

## 12. Remembering the Meeting with GOD Without Seeking Another Lord

### Verse

> **6:51** — And preach with this (Quran) to those who reverence the summoning before their Lord—they have none beside Him as a Lord and Master, nor an intercessor—that they may attain salvation.

### Reflection on the Verse

6:51 urges warning through the Quran to people mindful of being summoned before their Lord, and it denies them another Lord and Master or an intercessor beside Him. Beside 35:18, this changes the meaning of dependence. I may receive guidance from other people, but the final responsibility described in the verses cannot be handed to a human authority.

That distinction is easy to state and difficult to practice. When I admire a teacher, writer, or leader, I may begin to defend that person's every judgment as though my own faith depends on doing so. I may even feel safer repeating an inherited answer than returning to the text and facing a question for myself. Yet 6:51 reminds me of the one before whom I must ultimately stand. It leaves little room for treating a human voice as the final owner of my conscience.

I do not take this as a prohibition on learning from people. It calls me to preserve the difference between help and lordship. Another person's knowledge may assist my search; it does not erase my duty to examine. A genuine warning may therefore unsettle not only my behavior but the authorities I have installed within my thinking. The question is whether I can listen without allowing either admiration or fear of a group to decide the answer in advance.

**Verse references:** 6:51.

## 13. Prayer, Business, and the Real Order of Priorities

### Verse

> **24:37** — People who are not distracted by business or trade from commemorating GOD; they observe the Contact Prayers (Salat), and give the obligatory charity (Zakat), and they are conscious of the day when the minds and the eyes will be horrified.

> **29:45** — You shall recite what is revealed to you of the scripture, and observe the Contact Prayers (Salat), for the Contact Prayers prohibit evil and vice. But the remembrance of GOD (through Salat) is the most important objective.* GOD knows everything you do.

### Reflection on the Verse

24:37 describes people whose business and trade do not distract them from commemorating GOD, observing the Contact Prayers, and giving the obligatory charity. 29:45 speaks of the Contact Prayers in relation to the avoidance of evil and vice and calls the remembrance of GOD their most important objective. These verses keep me from separating prayer from the conduct of an ordinary day.

I spend much of life making things, meeting obligations, solving problems, and earning a living. None of that is dismissed as worthless in 24:37. What matters in the example is that business does not take possession of the center. A flourishing project can become an excuse to postpone prayer, overlook a colleague's rights, or conceal a fact that threatens a commercial advantage. If so, the problem is not merely a crowded calendar. It is a confused order of priorities.

I cannot turn the mention of prayer in 35:18 into a system for judging people by what I happen to see of their worship. Nor should I make the opposite mistake and treat an explicitly named practice as incidental. The more immediate question concerns my own consistency. What changes in my conduct after I have prayed? If remembrance leaves the way I treat people untouched, I need to examine my understanding of what it means to observe the prayer rather than merely finish it.

**Verse references:** 24:37, 29:45.

## 14. Can a Person Heeding Warnings Bear Unjust Witness?

### Verse

> **4:135** — O you who believe, you shall be absolutely equitable, and observe GOD, when you serve as witnesses, even against yourselves, or your parents, or your relatives. Whether the accused is rich or poor, GOD takes care of both. Therefore, do not be biased by your personal wishes. If you deviate or disregard (this commandment), then GOD is fully Cognizant of everything you do.

> **5:8** — O you who believe, you shall be absolutely equitable, and observe GOD, when you serve as witnesses. Do not be provoked by your conflicts with some people into committing injustice. You shall be absolutely equitable, for it is more righteous. You shall observe GOD. GOD is fully Cognizant of everything you do.

### Reflection on the Verse

4:135 is demanding in a way that cannot be softened by eloquence: justice in testimony must stand even against myself, my parents, or relatives. 5:8 adds that conflict with others must not push me toward injustice. Reading them alongside 35:18, I encounter an uncomfortable test. Can I claim to heed warnings while protecting a wrong simply because the person responsible belongs to my side?

If I notice dishonesty only when it harms people I like, I may be guided by loyalty rather than truth. If I invent evidence to defend a friend, or ignore relevant facts because an opponent deserves no sympathy in my eyes, I have reason to question the depth of my reverence. This is not a mandate to invade private lives or pronounce judgment on every dispute. It concerns the responsibility of a witness when a matter is genuinely before him or her.

I find the test hardest when the truth would cost me something. Am I prepared to acknowledge a fact that weakens my position? Can I describe the conduct of someone I dislike without exaggeration? Can I admit that my own side has caused harm? These questions give the idea of heeding a warning a practical form. Reverence cannot remain merely a warm sentiment if I refuse the justice the verses explicitly demand.

**Verse references:** 4:135, 5:8.

## 15. Verifying Reports and Testing Inherited Authority

### Verse

> **49:6** — O you who believe, if a wicked person brings any news to you, you shall first investigate, lest you commit injustice towards some people, out of ignorance, then become sorry and remorseful for what you have done.

> **2:170** — When they are told, "Follow what GOD has revealed herein," they say, "We follow only what we found our parents doing." What if their parents did not understand, and were not guided?

> **9:31** — They have set up their religious leaders and scholars as lords,* instead of GOD. Others deified the Messiah, son of Mary. They were all commanded to worship only one god. There is no god except He. Be He glorified, high above having any partners.

### Reflection on the Verse

49:6 directs the examination of news brought by a wicked person, warning that ignorance can produce injustice and remorse. 2:170 questions the refusal to depart from inherited practice even when parents lacked understanding or guidance. 9:31 criticizes taking religious leaders and scholars as lords instead of GOD. These are not identical situations, but together they warn against granting the people I trust automatic ownership of what is true.

I need to say this about my own work, too. Using a translation carefully is not the same as declaring every explanation of its translator beyond question. Learning from a scholar is not the same as placing the scholar's word over GOD's message. Loving a community does not establish the truth of every report that travels through it. Sometimes the warning I most need to hear comes from an unexpected direction; sometimes the comforting claim most in need of checking comes from my own side.

In my earlier essay <a href="https://www.berkkoksal.com/writings/we_found_our_parents_doing_this_en/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">“We Found Our Parents Doing This”</a>, I considered the need to examine inherited beliefs. Now I see its connection with heeding warnings. If the authority of a familiar person becomes a substitute for the measure revealed by GOD, a correction can be dismissed before it is heard. None of this justifies contempt for teachers or the fantasy that I can learn everything alone. It asks me to remain responsible for the truthfulness of what I accept.

**Verse references:** 49:6, 2:170, 9:31.

## 16. How Do I Respond While There Is Still Time?

### Verse

> **3:135** — If they fall in sin or wrong their souls, they remember GOD and ask forgiveness for their sins—and who forgives the sins except GOD—and they do not persist in sins, knowingly.

> **35:37** — They will scream therein, "Our Lord, if you get us out of here, we will work righteousness, instead of the works we used to do." Did we not give you a life-long chance, with continuous reminders for those who would take heed? Did you not receive the warner? Therefore, taste (the consequences). The transgressors will have no one to help them.

> **6:48** — We do not send the messengers except as deliverers of good news, as well as warners. Those who believe and reform have nothing to fear, nor will they grieve.

### Reflection on the Verse

3:135 describes people who remember GOD after wrongdoing, seek forgiveness, and do not knowingly persist in sin. 35:37 presents a scene in which a lifetime of opportunity and reminders has passed and the request to return comes too late. Together they make the time of warning feel serious. While I can still listen and act, postponement is not a harmless habit.

I know how quickly I can adapt to a wrong. The first time I do it, I may be ashamed; later I supply a reasonable-sounding explanation; eventually I hardly notice. When someone challenges me, my first reaction may be to explain why the criticism was badly delivered rather than ask whether it was true. The sequence in 3:135 does not describe mere emotional regret. It places remembrance and the request for forgiveness beside the refusal to persist knowingly. Still, I cannot look at another person and measure the completeness of their repentance.

6:48 places good news beside warning and mentions belief and reform. That keeps me from hearing every warning as a sentence without hope. While life continues, correction may be an opening. The question that remains with me is not how convincingly I can explain these verses to other people. It is which excuse I am willing to surrender today, and what I am prepared to repair. The last words of 35:18 are still waiting for my attention: to GOD is the final destiny.

**Verse references:** 3:135, 35:37, 6:48.

## 17. Arabic Roots: Warning, Reverence, and Purification

### Verse

> **35:18** — No soul can carry the sins of another soul. If a soul that is loaded with sins implores another to bear part of its load, no other soul can carry any part of it, even if they were related. The only people to heed your warnings are those who reverence their Lord, even when alone in their privacy, and observe the Contact Prayers (Salat). Whoever purifies his soul, does so for his own good. To GOD is the final destiny.

> **36:11** — You will be heeded only by those who uphold this message, and reverence the Most Gracious—even when alone in their privacy. Give them good news of forgiveness and a generous recompense.

### Reflection on the Verse

I want to examine the Arabic of 35:18 without pretending that the root of a word is a substitute for the meaning of a whole sentence. Roots suggest related fields of meaning; grammar, context, and translation choices still matter. They help me read more attentively, not manufacture a new revelation behind the verse.

**وِزْرَ / وَازِرَةٌ — و ز ر (w-z-r):** A field associated with a burden and the bearing of it. The closely related forms at the beginning of 35:18 make the non-transferability of personal responsibility hard to miss. They do not authorize a general refusal to help others.

**تُنذِرُ — ن ذ ر (n-dh-r):** The field of warning. A warning can be delivered; the speaker does not thereby acquire command over the listener's conscience. Read this alongside the limit expressed in 35:23.

**يَخْشَوْنَ — خ ش ي (kh-sh-y):** A field joining fear with deep reverence. Rashad Khalifa’s Authorized English Translation says “reverence their Lord,” and the Turkish translation of the Authorized English Translation renders it as “Rablerine derin saygı duyanlar.” I hesitate to reduce that to a moment of panic.

**بِٱلْغَيْبِ — غ ي ب (gh-y-b):** The unseen or what is hidden from ordinary perception. The Arabic semantic field should be distinguished from the phrasing “even when alone in their privacy” in the Authorized English Translation. The latter conveys an interpretive emphasis; it should not be presented as the word's sole literal dictionary definition (35:18; 36:11).

**وَأَقَامُوا۟ — ق و م (q-w-m):** Standing, establishing, or maintaining. This helps me notice that the Contact Prayers are spoken of as an observed commitment, without inventing procedural details that this verse does not state (35:18).

**تَزَكَّىٰ / يَتَزَكَّىٰ — ز ك و (z-k-w):** Two forms connected with purification. They return the consequence of purification to the person concerned. They do not make me a judge of someone else's inward state (35:18).

**ٱلْمَصِيرُ — ص ي ر (ṣ-y-r):** The destination or final return. The closing word brings the verse back to accountability before GOD. The roots do not identify a secret list of worthy people; they sharpen the ethical shape already visible in the verse.

**Verse references:** 35:18, 36:11.

## 18. Other Claims

### Verse

> **35:18** — No soul can carry the sins of another soul. If a soul that is loaded with sins implores another to bear part of its load, no other soul can carry any part of it, even if they were related. The only people to heed your warnings are those who reverence their Lord, even when alone in their privacy, and observe the Contact Prayers (Salat). Whoever purifies his soul, does so for his own good. To GOD is the final destiny.

> **35:28** — Also, the people, the animals, and the livestock come in various colors. This is why the people who truly reverence GOD are those who are knowledgeable. GOD is Almighty, Forgiving.

### Reflection on the Verse

Some traditional readings emphasize that reverence “in the unseen” means respect for GOD without seeing Him. Others attend especially to inward devotion when nobody can observe or reward the believer's conduct. The Arabic **bil-ghayb** allows a discussion of the unseen, while the Authorized English Translation expressly uses the language of privacy. I do not regard either emphasis as a warrant to erase the wording of the translation used in this study, or to declare the historical debate settled by a single observation.

Some explanations may give priority to the outward observance of prayer as evidence of faith. Others may emphasize the sincerity of the heart. I see risks on both sides when either element is isolated from 35:18: a person might claim certainty about another's standing merely from appearances, or speak as though the verse had not explicitly mentioned the Contact Prayers at all. The verse places the elements together, and I want to respect that fact before importing a system built around one of them.

35:28 can also be read as giving special standing to a class of religious experts. Yet the verse itself does not supply a list of professions, schools, or authorized individuals. Earlier interpretations may offer questions worth considering, but they should not become an independent power to classify every person's final condition. I can listen to interpretations without allowing any interpreter to occupy the place reserved for GOD.

**Verse references:** 35:18, 35:28.

## 19. Conclusion

### Verse

> **35:18** — No soul can carry the sins of another soul. If a soul that is loaded with sins implores another to bear part of its load, no other soul can carry any part of it, even if they were related. The only people to heed your warnings are those who reverence their Lord, even when alone in their privacy, and observe the Contact Prayers (Salat). Whoever purifies his soul, does so for his own good. To GOD is the final destiny.

> **36:11** — You will be heeded only by those who uphold this message, and reverence the Most Gracious—even when alone in their privacy. Give them good news of forgiveness and a generous recompense.

> **39:18** — They are the ones who examine all words, then follow the best. These are the ones whom GOD has guided; these are the ones who possess intelligence.

> **6:48** — We do not send the messengers except as deliverers of good news, as well as warners. Those who believe and reform have nothing to fear, nor will they grieve.

### Reflection on the Verse

I began with the question of who the only people to heed warnings are. After reading these passages together, I cannot answer with the name of a movement, a profession, or a group I happen to admire. 35:18 names reverence for the Lord even in privacy and the observance of the Contact Prayers. But it begins with responsibility no one else can bear for me and ends with purification and the return to GOD. Those are not decorative details around an isolated label; they are part of the verse's argument.

36:11 joins fidelity to the message with reverence in privacy. 39:18 describes people who examine words and follow the best, while 6:48 places reform and good news alongside warning. None of these verses hands me a key to another person's hidden inner life. They ask more of my own. I may speak about the conduct the verses commend; I cannot announce the final destiny of individuals as though that judgment belongs to me.

For me, heeding a warning cannot simply mean the relief of believing that I am already on the right side. It means being willing to choose truth when self-defense would be easier; preserving the same measure when nobody watches; refusing to turn prayer into a performance; correcting a wrong when it is exposed; and not installing human beings as lords over my conscience. I will not pretend I have fulfilled all of that perfectly. The question I want to carry forward is smaller, and more demanding: When the next warning reaches me, will I rush to decide who else needs it—or will I examine my own soul first?

**Verse references:** 35:18, 36:11, 39:18, 6:48.
