# [60:13] How Should We Choose Our Friends? Those with Whom GOD Is Angry, Friendship, and Justice

**Author:** Berk KÖKSAL  
**Date:** November 08, 2026  
**Place:** Mersin

The central purpose of this article is to reflect on 60:13 together with related verses, and to examine what bearing witness to the oneness of GOD means in friendship, knowledge, justice, truthfulness, and submission. In particular, I want to take the warning against befriending people with whom GOD is angry seriously, without detaching it from the Quran's equally clear commands concerning justice and kindness.

Peace. Peace be upon you.

What should I look for when I let someone become a friend? Their kindness toward me? The ease with which we speak? Whether they are there when life becomes difficult? These things matter. Yet they are not enough to answer a harder question: **Does this closeness support a life in which I remember GOD and act truthfully, or does it encourage me to move away from His message?**

I find myself stopping at 60:13. Its warning is serious: believers are told not to befriend people with whom GOD is angry. But the seriousness of the statement forces me to ask another question. What happens if I begin to decide, on the basis of my prejudices, exactly whom GOD must be angry with? Would I then turn a warning meant to guide my own conduct into an excuse to wrong other people? In the same sura, 60:8 explicitly permits friendship and equitable treatment toward those who do not fight believers because of religion or evict them from their homes.

In my earlier reflection <a href="https://berkkoksal.com/writings/loving-god-alone-en/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Loving GOD Alone</a>, I considered how love can become a loyalty that belongs to GOD alone. In <a href="https://berkkoksal.com/writings/responding_to_evil_with_what_is_best_en/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Responding to Evil with What Is Best</a>, I examined how to remain just when confronted with wrongdoing. Friendship brings those questions into everyday life. We may absorb not only the words of those around us but also their habits and ways of seeing the world. Perhaps choosing friends is therefore not merely about who enters my life, but about **who I become within those relationships**.

## 1. Friendship Is Not Merely a Label; It Is a Direction

### Verse

> **60:13**
>
> يَٰٓأَيُّهَا ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوا۟ لَا تَتَوَلَّوْا۟ قَوْمًا غَضِبَ ٱللَّهُ عَلَيْهِمْ قَدْ يَئِسُوا۟ مِنَ ٱلْـَٔاخِرَةِ كَمَا يَئِسَ ٱلْكُفَّارُ مِنْ أَصْحَٰبِ ٱلْقُبُورِ
>
> O  you  who  believe,  do  not  befriend people  with  whom  GOD  is  angry, and  who  are  hopelessly  stuck  in  disbelief;  they  are  just  as  hopeless  as the  disbelievers  who  are  already  in the graves.
>
> Ey inananlar, TANRI’nın kızgın olduğu ve umutsuzca inkâra saplanmışlar ile arkadaşlık etmeyin; onlar, hâlihazırda kabirlerde olan inkârcılar kadar umutsuzdurlar.

### Reflection on the Verse

The address in 60:13 is directed to believers. Friendship is not presented here as a trivial social preference. When I allow someone near me, I may choose to trust them, stand beside them, defend them, and listen to their advice. The verse speaks of people with whom GOD is angry and who are hopelessly entrenched in disbelief. It is understandable that a deep allegiance to such a direction would conflict with the submission a believer seeks to practice.

Yet the verse does not grant me the ability to read another person's heart. I cannot take every mistake, disagreement, or difference in belief and turn it into a verdict that someone belongs among those described in 60:13. The verse gives a grave description; I ordinarily see only fragments of another person's words and actions. My first task, then, is to examine the allegiances I choose. Whose approval am I afraid to lose? Which truths do I suppress to keep someone's affection?

Friendship is not just a name stored in a phone. It is also revealed by the voices I follow when I am uncertain and the side I take when integrity becomes costly. This is why the warning strikes me so deeply. But if I use it to feel spiritually superior to others, I have neglected the self-examination that such a warning should inspire.

**Verse reference:** 60:13.

## 2. Those with Whom GOD Is Angry: What Do 58:14 and 5:80 Add?

### Verse

> **58:14** — Have  you  noted  those  who  befriended  people  with  whom  GOD  is angry?  They  neither  belong  with you,  nor  with  them.  They  deliberately swear lies!

> **5:80** — You would see many of them allying themselves  with  those  who  disbelieve. Miserable indeed is what their hands have sent forth on behalf of their souls. GOD is angry with them and, consequently, they will abide forever in retribution.

> **5:81** — Had they believed in GOD, and the prophet, and in what was revealed to him herein, they would not have befriended them. But many of them are evil.

### Reflection on the Verse

The scene described in 58:14 troubles me. It speaks of friendship with people with whom GOD is angry and, in the same verse, of deliberately swearing lies. The problem is larger than being seen in the wrong company. There is a moral fracture: someone moves between sides without truthfulness and disguises their allegiance through false oaths. Friendship becomes entangled with deceit.

In 5:80-81, alliances and GOD's anger appear together once again. The wording matters. The passage speaks of many among a particular group; it does not give me permission to apply the same judgment to every member of an entire people, past or present. I must respect the limits of the text. What GOD says concerning those described in the verse should be taken seriously, but I cannot assign that judgment to a neighbor or colleague based solely on identity, appearance, or disagreement.

The question I bring into my own life is this: **If remaining someone's friend requires me to lie knowingly, what exactly am I being loyal to?** Can a relationship still claim moral goodness if I must bear false witness, hide wrongdoing, or betray what I know to be true? I do not need to pronounce another person's ultimate standing before GOD to recognize my own responsibility when I participate in a lie.

**Verse references:** 58:14, 5:80-81; read with 60:13.

## 3. Can Someone Who Wishes Me Harm Be a Trusted Friend?

### Verse

> **3:118** — O you who believe, do not befriend outsiders who never cease to wish you harm; they even wish to see you suffer. Hatred  flows out of their mouths and what they hide in their chests is far worse. We thus clarify the revelations for you, if you understand.

> **60:1** — O  you  who  believe,  you  shall  not befriend My enemies and your enemies, extending love and friendship to them, even though they have disbelieved in the truth that has come to  you.  They  persecute  the  messenger,  and  you,  just  because  you believe in GOD, your Lord. If you mobilize  to  struggle  in  My  cause, seeking My blessings, how can you secretly love them? I am fully aware of everything you conceal, and everything you declare. Those among you who do this have indeed strayed off the right path.

> **60:2** — Whenever they encounter you, they treat you as enemies, and hurt you with their hands and tongues. They want you to disbelieve.

### Reflection on the Verse

The warning in 3:118 reminds me that outward warmth does not settle the question of trust. It describes people who continually wish harm upon believers, who want them to suffer, and whose hostility is expressed in their words. In 60:1-2, the warning concerns people who persecute others because of their faith and harm them through words and deeds. Those are not merely differences of taste or opinion. They are patterns of hostility.

I should still resist a dangerous shortcut: not everyone who challenges me wishes me harm. A friend may disagree strongly, point out an error, or tell me a truth I do not want to hear. Honest correction can be one of friendship's greatest gifts. The question is not whether someone constantly agrees with me, but how they relate to truth, fairness, and my well-being.

I cannot know another person's hidden motives with certainty. But I can notice repeated deception, threats, contempt, and deliberate injury. Being kind to someone does not require placing every part of my life under that person's influence. Maintaining boundaries and refusing unjust treatment can coexist with the refusal to hate or slander.

**Verse references:** 3:118, 60:1-2.

## 4. Friendship with Those Who Do Not Fight Us: Why 60:8 Matters

### Verse

> **60:8** — GOD  does  not  enjoin  you  from befriending  those  who  do  not  fight you  because  of  religion,  and  do  not evict  you  from  your  homes.  You  may befriend  them  and  be  equitable towards  them.  GOD  loves  the  equitable.

> **49:13** — O people, we created you from the same  male  and  female,  and  rendered you distinct peoples and tribes, that you may recognize one another. The best among you in the sight of GOD is the most righteous. GOD is Omniscient, Cognizant. 

### Reflection on the Verse

I would make a serious mistake if I read 60:13 and ignored 60:8. I might convince myself that believers must avoid every meaningful friendship with anyone who follows a different religion or was raised in another culture. But 60:8 explicitly states that friendship and equitable treatment are not forbidden toward those who do not fight believers because of religion and do not evict them from their homes. It is an important boundary against extending a warning beyond its proper reach.

In 49:13, human differences among peoples and tribes are connected with recognizing one another. That makes me examine my own fear of the unfamiliar. Listening to a person is not the same as surrendering to every claim they make. Working together, helping a neighbor, speaking respectfully, and developing an honest friendship are not automatically acts of worship or allegiance to something other than GOD.

Choosing friends does not give me permission to strip other people of dignity. If I take what the verse permits and declare it forbidden because of my personal suspicions, I have confused my own preference with divine instruction. I want to honor the Quran's warnings without turning them into an excuse for injustice.

**Verse references:** 60:8, 49:13; compared with 60:13.

## 5. The Boundary in 60:9: Supporting Oppression Is Not Kindness

### Verse

> **60:9** — GOD  enjoins  you  only  from  befriending  those  who  fight  you  because  of  religion,  evict  you  from your  homes,  and  band  together  with others  to  banish  you.  You  shall  not befriend  them.  Those  who  befriend them are the transgressors.

> **5:8** — O  you  who  believe,  you  shall  be  absolutely  equitable,  and  observe GOD,  when  you  serve  as  witnesses. Do  not  be  provoked  by  your  conflicts  with  some  people  into  committing  injustice.  You  shall  be  absolutely  equitable,  for  it  is  more  righteous. You  shall  observe  GOD.  GOD  is fully  Cognizant  of  everything  you do.

### Reflection on the Verse

The next verse specifies a different situation: people who fight believers because of religion, drive them from their homes, and join forces to banish them. These are concrete acts of persecution. When I detach the prohibition in 60:9 from those actions, I risk converting it into a general command to be hostile toward anyone different from me. But I would also be wrong to ignore the actual warning and support oppression merely because its perpetrators offer me comfort or influence.

Then 5:8 introduces another responsibility. Conflict must not provoke me into injustice. Even when another person has wronged me, I cannot justify false accusations, dishonesty, or violations of their rights. I may need to withdraw my trust or refuse cooperation. Such boundaries do not mean I have acquired the right to mistreat them.

Friendship can tempt me to overlook cruelty when I am not its victim. I might accept protection or advantage from someone while pretending not to see what they are doing to others. These two verses ask me to face both sides: do not ally myself with oppression, and do not become oppressive in my response to it.

**Verse references:** 60:8-9, 5:8.

## 6. Could Yesterday's Enemy Become Tomorrow's Friend?

### Verse

> **60:7** — GOD  may  change  the  animosity  between  you  and  them  into  love.  GOD is  Omnipotent.  GOD  is  Forgiver, Most Merciful.

> **41:34** — Not  equal  is  the  good  response  and the  bad  response.  You  shall  resort to  the  nicest  possible  response. Thus,  the  one  who  used  to  be  your enemy,  may  become  your  best friend.

> **41:35** — None  can  attain  this  except  those who  steadfastly  persevere.  None can  attain  this  except  those  who  are extremely fortunate.

### Reflection on the Verse

Amid the serious warnings of this sura, 60:7 opens an unexpected possibility: GOD may transform enmity into love. That stops me from treating every present conflict as a final statement about a person's entire life. I may need distance from what someone is doing today. Still, I do not know their entire future, and I cannot close the door on change by pretending to possess divine knowledge.

The language of 41:34-35 also matters. Responding to wrongdoing in the best possible way can turn a former enemy into a close friend. I do not read that as a demand to ignore danger, accept endless harm, or restore trust without evidence. The verse presents a possibility grounded in perseverance and goodness. Sometimes the best response is a calm answer; sometimes it is to refuse retaliation and remain fair while maintaining a boundary.

There is no necessary contradiction between recognizing wrongdoing and remaining open to transformation. I can be careful about trust without committing myself to bitterness. GOD knows where people may turn. I am responsible for how I conduct myself before that future unfolds.

**Verse references:** 60:7, 41:34-35.

## 7. Friend, Ally, and Lord: Not Every Kind of Closeness Is the Same

### Verse

> **5:55** — Your  real  allies  are  GOD  and  His messenger,  and  the  believers  who observe  the  Contact  Prayers  (Salat), and  give  the  obligatory  charity (Zakat), and they bow down.

> **5:56** — Those  who  ally  themselves  with GOD  and  His  messenger,  and  those who  believed,  belong  in  the  party  of GOD;  absolutely,  they  are  the  victors.

> **4:139** — They  are  the  ones  who  ally  themselves  with  disbelievers  instead  of believers.  Are  they  seeking  dignity with  them?  All  dignity  belongs  with GOD alone.

> **3:64** — Say, “O followers of the scripture, let us come to a logical agreement between us and you: that we shall not worship except GOD; that we never set up any idols besides Him, nor set up any human beings as lords beside GOD.” If they turn away, say, “Bear witness that we are submitters.”

### Reflection on the Verse

The Quran speaks in different ways about friends, allies, and the danger of taking human beings as lords beside GOD. In 5:55-56, allegiance is linked with faith and practice. In 4:139, the question is where people seek dignity. In 3:64, human beings must not be set up as lords beside GOD. Treating all these relationships as identical would blur important distinctions. Having a friend is not automatically making that person an object of worship.

The danger becomes more personal when I transfer my moral responsibility to someone whose approval I need. A friend may be intelligent, persuasive, respected, or successful. I can value their knowledge and listen carefully without treating their words as infallible. If I cannot tell them when they are wrong, or if I cannot refuse a request to participate in wrongdoing, our relationship has begun to demand more than friendship should.

The question about dignity is especially uncomfortable. How much of my conduct changes when I want to belong to a fashionable or powerful circle? Would I soften the truth or abandon someone treated unjustly to preserve my place? A friendship should allow us to remain honest, even when honesty is inconvenient.

**Verse references:** 5:55-56, 4:139, 3:64.

## 8. Appearances, Eloquence, and False Oaths: How Do We Test Trust?

### Verse

> **58:14** — Have  you  noted  those  who  befriended  people  with  whom  GOD  is angry?  They  neither  belong  with you,  nor  with  them.  They  deliberately swear lies!

> **63:4** — When  you  see  them,  you  may  be impressed  by  their  looks.  And  when they speak, you may listen to their eloquence.  They  are  like  standing logs.  They  think  that  every  call  is intended  against  them.  These  are the  real  enemies;  beware  of  them. GOD  condemns  them;  they  have deviated. 

> **49:6** — O you who believe, if a wicked person brings any news to you, you shall first investigate, lest you commit injustice towards some people, out of ignorance, then become sorry and remorseful for what you have done.

### Reflection on the Verse

In 58:14, knowingly false oaths stand alongside misplaced allegiance. In 63:4, impressive appearances and eloquent speech are described as things that can mislead. That makes me think of how easily confidence of manner can be mistaken for truth. Someone speaks convincingly, appears generous, or gathers an admiring audience, and I am tempted to treat their statements as reliable before examining them.

The instruction in 49:6 is simple but demanding: investigate the news that comes to you. A friend's word may deserve attention, but emotional closeness does not eliminate the need to verify facts. Spreading a damaging story without checking it can harm not only a friendship but also the dignity and rights of someone who is absent.

Yet verification must not become an excuse for spying. The goal is not to gather secrets or interpret every silence as proof of guilt. Sometimes it is enough to ask where a claim came from, hear the other person's account, and admit that I do not know. I do not want the search for trustworthy friends to make me an untrustworthy person.

**Verse references:** 58:14, 63:4, 49:6; see also 49:12.

## 9. A Good Friend Does Not Ask Me to Stop Thinking

### Verse

> **17:36** — You  shall  not  accept  any  information,  unless  you  verify  it  for  yourself. I  have  given  you  the  hearing,  the eyesight,  and  the  brain,  and  you  are responsible for using them.

> **39:18** — They  are  the  ones  who  examine  all words,  then  follow  the  best.  These are  the  ones  whom  GOD  has  guided;  these  are  the  ones  who  possess intelligence.

> **5:77** — Say, “O people of the scripture, do not  transgress  the  limits  of  your religion beyond the truth, and do not  follow  the  opinions  of  people who  have  gone  astray,  and  have misled  multitudes  of  people;  they are far astray from the right path.”

### Reflection on the Verse

Another danger of closeness is adopting someone else's conclusions as though they were my own verified knowledge. In 17:36, I am told not to accept information without verification. In 39:18, people examine words and follow the best. In 5:77, there is a warning against following the opinions of those who have gone astray and misled others. These verses remind me that affection does not remove responsibility for thought.

I want a friendship in which I can ask, “Are we certain about this?” without being accused of disloyalty. I also want my friend to be able to question me. It is possible to be surrounded by people who agree with everything I say and still have almost no one with whom I can speak honestly. Agreement is pleasant, but it is not proof.

How does this person respond when a fact is corrected? Do they welcome investigation, or insist that loyalty means never questioning them? Do they care more about what is true, or about winning an argument? These are not rigid tests quoted word for word from the Quran. They are questions I can reasonably ask when trying to put 17:36, 39:18, and 5:77 into practice.

**Verse references:** 17:36, 39:18, 5:77.

## 10. What Does Silence Mean When Revelation Is Ridiculed?

### Verse

> **4:140** — He  has  instructed  you  in  the  scripture  that:  if  you  hear  GOD’s  revelations  being  mocked  and  ridiculed, you  shall  not  sit  with  them,  unless they  delve  into  another  subject. Otherwise,  you  will  be  as  guilty as  they  are.  GOD  will  gather  the hypocrites  and  the  disbelievers together in Hell. 

> **6:68** — If  you  see  those  who  mock  our revelations, you shall avoid them until they delve into another subject. If the devil causes you to forget, then, as soon as you remember, do not sit with such evil people.

> **5:57** — O  you  who  believe,  do  not  befriend those  among  the  recipients  of  previous  scripture  who  mock  and  ridicule  your  religion,  nor  shall  you befriend  the  disbelievers.  You  shall reverence  GOD,  if  you  are  really believers.

### Reflection on the Verse

Verses 4:140 and 6:68 address gatherings in which GOD's revelations are mocked, telling believers to avoid such gatherings until the subject changes. I find this significant because repeated exposure can normalize a form of contempt. I might not join in the mockery, yet stay silent merely to avoid losing my place in a group. The verses make me examine what my presence permits and what it slowly teaches me to accept.

A sincere question, however, is not the same as ridicule. Someone may disagree, ask something difficult, or challenge my understanding in good faith. It would be wrong to label every uncomfortable conversation as mockery. The passages refer specifically to ridiculing revelations, and 60:8 still stands as a permission for friendship and justice with those who are not hostile.

Sometimes leaving a conversation is better than trying to win it. I am not required to turn every argument into a public contest. A calm withdrawal may honor the seriousness of the subject while avoiding insult and needless escalation.

**Verse references:** 4:140, 6:68, 5:57; compared with 60:8.

## 11. “I Wish I Did Not Take That Person as a Friend”

### Verse

> **25:27** — The  day  will  come  when  the  transgressor  will  bite  his  hands  (in  anguish)  and  say,  “Alas,  I  wish  I  had followed  the  path  with  the  messenger.

> **25:28** — “Alas,  woe  to  me,  I  wish  I  did  not take that person as a friend.

> **25:29** — “He  has  led  me  away  from  the  message  after  it  came  to  me.  Indeed,  the devil lets down his human victims.”

> **43:67** — The close friends on that day will become enemies of one another, except for the righteous.

### Reflection on the Verse

The regret in 25:27-29 is haunting. This is not the disappointment of a friendship that simply became inconvenient. It is the cry of someone who says a friend led them away from the message after it had reached them. The words make companionship a matter of profound moral consequence. In 43:67, close friends, except for the righteous, become enemies of one another on that day. Today's comfort may conceal a direction that becomes painfully clear later.

Still, the regret expressed in the passage does not allow me to transfer every part of my responsibility to another person. A friend may influence me, invite me, and normalize a path. Yet I must also examine why I accepted that influence and what I did with the guidance before me. Blame cannot replace self-examination.

I read these verses not merely as a frightening picture of the future, but as a reason to take present choices seriously. There is a real blessing in being close to people who encourage truthfulness and sound judgment. The blessing is not a guarantee attached to someone's name; I too must remain receptive to what is right.

**Verse references:** 25:27-29, 43:67.

## 12. Parents and Siblings: Does Blood Justify Every Allegiance?

### Verse

> **9:23** — O you who believe, do not ally yourselves even with your parents and your siblings, if they prefer disbelieving over believing. Those among you who ally themselves with them are transgressing.

> **58:22** — You will not find people who believe in GOD and the Last Day befriending those who oppose GOD and His messenger, even if they were their parents, or their children, or their siblings, or their tribe. For these, He decrees faith into their hearts, and supports them with inspiration from Him, and admits them into gardens with flowing streams wherein they abide forever. GOD is pleased with them,  and  they  are  pleased  with Him. These are the party of GOD. Most assuredly, GOD’s party are the winners.

> **31:15** — If  they  try  to  force  you  to  set  up any idols beside Me, do not obey them. But  continue  to  treat  them  amicably in this world. You shall follow only the  path  of  those  who  have  submitted  to  Me.  Ultimately,  you  all return to Me, then I will inform you of  everything you have done.

### Reflection on the Verse

Friendship cannot be examined without thinking about family. In 9:23 and 58:22, even the closest family relationships do not override allegiance to GOD. These are serious statements. Yet when I read 31:15 beside them, another essential distinction appears. If parents pressure someone toward idolatry, that person must not obey them; nevertheless, they are told to continue treating their parents amicably in this world.

Obedience, alliance, and kind treatment are not identical. Disagreeing with a parent's belief does not authorize me to treat that parent unjustly. Refusing a wrongful demand is not a command to humiliate them or neglect every humane obligation. Love may require boundaries, while justice and respect remain.

I would not want to use these passages as a weapon in a family argument, announcing with certainty that someone I resent is an enemy of GOD. I would rather ask whether I am abandoning truth to keep a relative's approval—or using the language of truth to excuse needless cruelty toward them. Both possibilities require reflection.

**Verse references:** 9:23, 58:22, 31:15.

## 13. Justice and Dignity Inside Friendship

### Verse

> **5:8** — O  you  who  believe,  you  shall  be  absolutely  equitable,  and  observe GOD,  when  you  serve  as  witnesses. Do  not  be  provoked  by  your  conflicts  with  some  people  into  committing  injustice.  You  shall  be  absolutely  equitable,  for  it  is  more  righteous. You  shall  observe  GOD.  GOD  is fully  Cognizant  of  everything  you do.

> **49:11** — O you who believe, no people shall ridicule other people, for they may be better than they. Nor shall any women ridicule other women, for they may be better than they. Nor shall you mock one another, or make fun of your names. Evil indeed is the reversion to wickedness after attaining faith. Anyone who does not repent after this, these are the transgressors.

> **49:12** — O you who believe, you shall avoid any suspicion, for even a little bit of suspicion is sinful. You shall not spy on one another, nor shall you backbite one another; this is as abominable as eating the flesh of your dead brother.  You  certainly  abhor  this. You  shall  observe  GOD.  GOD  is Redeemer, Most Merciful.

### Reflection on the Verse

I must pay attention not only to whom I choose, but also to how I treat the people I have chosen. Verse 5:8 warns against letting conflict lead to injustice. Verse 49:11 forbids ridicule and degrading names. Verse 49:12 prohibits suspicion, spying, and backbiting. These boundaries matter within friendship just as much as they matter in relations between strangers.

Closeness can make violations easier. Someone confides a private matter to me, and later I may feel tempted to reveal it during an argument. Or I may defend a friend even when they have wronged someone else, as though loyalty required me to deny the facts. Neither behavior is faithful to the principles in these verses. Justice does not oppose love; it helps prevent love from turning into favoritism or control.

I can ask myself a few unsettling questions. How do I speak about my friends when they are not present? What happens when someone falsely accuses them? What happens when they themselves mistreat another person? I want to apply these questions to my own conduct before making them an instrument for investigating everyone else's faults.

**Verse references:** 5:8, 49:11-12.

## 14. Being on the Same Side: Together in Good or Together in Wrongdoing?

### Verse

> **5:2** — O  you  who  believe,  do  not  violate the  rites  instituted  by  GOD,  nor  the Sacred  Months,  nor  the  animals  to be  offered,  nor  the  garlands  marking  them,  nor  the  people  who  head for  the  Sacred  Shrine  (Ka‘bah) seeking  blessings  from  their  Lord and  approval.  Once  you  complete the  pilgrimage,  you  may  hunt.*  Do not  be  provoked  into  aggression  by your  hatred  of  people  who  once prevented  you  from  going  to  the Sacred  Masjid.  You  shall  cooperate in  matters  of  righteousness  and piety;  do  not  cooperate  in  matters that  are  sinful  and  evil.  You  shall observe  GOD.  GOD  is  strict  in  enforcing retribution.

> **9:71** — The believing men and women are allies of one another. They advocate righteousness and forbid evil, they observe  the Contact  Prayers  (Salat) and give the obligatory charity (Zakat), and they obey GOD and His messenger. These will be showered by GOD’s mercy. GOD is Almighty, Most Wise.

> **9:67** — The  hypocrite  men  and  the  hypocrite  women  belong  with  each other  − they advocate evil  and prohibit  righteousness,  and  they  are stingy. They forgot GOD, so He forgot them. The hypocrites are truly wicked.

### Reflection on the Verse

Verse 5:2 draws a line between cooperation in righteousness and cooperation in sin and evil. Verse 9:71 describes believing men and women as allies who advocate righteousness and oppose evil. In contrast, 9:67 speaks of hypocrites who advocate evil and prohibit righteousness. What defines an alliance, then, is not merely how warmly its members treat each other. It also matters what they are doing together.

A group may welcome me and make me feel protected. But what if acceptance requires me to help conceal dishonesty, participate in gossip, take advantage of the vulnerable, or support a false accusation? A friendship cannot obligate me to cooperate in wrongdoing. When saying “no” is treated as betrayal, perhaps the relationship has confused loyalty with compliance.

Cooperation in good does not need to be limited to people who share every opinion I hold. The permission in 60:8 also allows us to imagine fair and beneficial relationships across differences. The direction of our shared conduct matters more than the ease of attaching a label to the group.

**Verse references:** 5:2, 9:71, 9:67, 60:8.

## 15. Friendship's Positive Face: Help, Hospitality, and Responsibility

### Verse

> **4:36** — You shall worship GOD alone—do not associate anything with Him. You shall regard the parents, the relatives, the orphans, the poor, the related neighbor, the unrelated neighbor, the close associate, the traveling alien, and your servants. GOD  does  not  like  the  arrogant show-offs.

> **8:74** — Those  who  believed  and  emigrated, and  strove  in  the  cause  of  GOD,  as well  as  those  who  hosted  them  and gave  them  refuge,  and  supported them,  these  are  the  true  believers. They  have  deserved  forgiveness  and a generous recompense.

> **42:38** — They  respond  to  their  Lord  by  observing  the  Contact  Prayers  (Salat). Their  affairs  are  decided  after  due consultation  among  themselves, and  from  our  provisions  to  them they give (to charity).

### Reflection on the Verse

Warnings are important, but I do not want to reduce friendship to a list of dangers. Verse 4:36 includes the close associate among people whose rights must be regarded. Verse 8:74 recalls those who welcomed others, provided refuge, and supported them. Verse 42:38 connects the believers' affairs with consultation and sharing. Friendship has a practical, generous side.

It is more than repeating the same beliefs aloud. It can mean noticing a need, lifting someone's burden, safeguarding their rights when they are absent, and listening seriously when decisions affect us both. If I reduce friendship to “What does this person do for me?”, I overlook half of the relationship. I must also ask whether others can rely on me.

These verses do not give an unlimited claim on another person's time, money, or personal boundaries. Still, they make it difficult to treat help, mercy, and mutual responsibility as optional decorations. Good companionship gives faith a way to appear in ordinary actions, not merely in conversations about faith.

**Verse references:** 4:36, 8:74, 42:38.

## 16. Before Judging a Friend: What Kind of Friend Am I?

### Verse

> **49:10** — The believers are members of one family; you shall keep the peace within your family and reverence GOD, that you may attain mercy.

> **3:159** — It was mercy from GOD that you became  compassionate  towards them.  Had  you  been  harsh  and mean-hearted, they would have abandoned you. Therefore, you shall pardon them and ask forgiveness for them, and consult them. Once you make a decision, carry out your plan, and trust in GOD. GOD loves those who trust in Him.*

> **16:125** — You shall invite to the path of your Lord  with  wisdom  and  kind  enlightenment, and debate with them in the best possible manner. Your Lord knows best who has strayed from His path, and He knows best who are the guided ones.

### Reflection on the Verse

At the beginning, I was asking whom I should choose. As this reflection reaches its later chapters, the question returns to me: am I a trustworthy friend? Verse 49:10 describes believers as one family and urges them to maintain peace. Verse 3:159 emphasizes compassion, pardon, and consultation. Verse 16:125 instructs that even invitation and debate be conducted with wisdom and kindness. I cannot simply wait to meet the perfect companion while neglecting these responsibilities in myself.

How do I speak to a friend when I am angry? Do I allow them to finish a sentence? Does their success bring me joy, or does it secretly become a competition? The verses do not provide ready-made answers about my inner life, but they give me reasons to ask more demanding questions.

I also do not believe friendship must be limited to flawless people. We are capable of error. What matters is whether we are willing to correct it. Apologizing, reconsidering a judgment, and remaining fair during disagreement help preserve trust. I do not want to become someone who rigorously tests every potential friend while never examining his own conduct.

**Verse references:** 49:10, 3:159, 16:125.

## 17. Arabic Word Roots: Allegiance, Anger, Despair, and Justice

### Verse

> **60:13** — O  you  who  believe,  do  not  befriend people  with  whom  GOD  is  angry, and  who  are  hopelessly  stuck  in  disbelief;  they  are  just  as  hopeless  as the  disbelievers  who  are  already  in the graves.

> **58:14** — Have  you  noted  those  who  befriended  people  with  whom  GOD  is angry?  They  neither  belong  with you,  nor  with  them.  They  deliberately swear lies!

> **60:8** — GOD  does  not  enjoin  you  from befriending  those  who  do  not  fight you  because  of  religion,  and  do  not evict  you  from  your  homes.  You  may befriend  them  and  be  equitable towards  them.  GOD  loves  the  equitable.

> **60:9** — GOD  enjoins  you  only  from  befriending  those  who  fight  you  because  of  religion,  evict  you  from your  homes,  and  band  together  with others  to  banish  you.  You  shall  not befriend  them.  Those  who  befriend them are the transgressors.

### Reflection on the Verse

The form **تَتَوَلَّوْا۟** (*tatawallaw*) in 60:13, **تَوَلَّوْا۟** (*tawallaw*) in 58:14, and **تَوَلَّوْهُمْ** (*tawallawhum*) in 60:9 are connected with the Arabic root **و-ل-ي** (*w-l-y*). That root opens a field of association involving closeness, attachment, and allegiance. Yet this observation does not authorize me to replace the words used in Rashad Khalifa's Authorized English Translation, or to erase the differences between the passages. The translation's choices—such as “befriend” and “allies”—must remain intact.

**غَضِبَ** (*ghaḍiba*) comes from **غ-ض-ب** (*gh-ḍ-b*), associated with anger. The description of GOD's anger in 60:13 and 58:14 deserves its full force. The form **يَئِسُوا۟** (*yaʾisū*) in 60:13 is associated with **ي-أ-س** (*y-ʾ-s*), the field of despair. These terms invite solemn reflection; they do not authorize me to assign GOD's final judgment to particular living people on the basis of suspicion.

In 60:8, **تَبَرُّوهُمْ** (*tabarrūhum*) is related to **ب-ر-ر** (*b-r-r*), associated with goodness, and **تُقْسِطُوٓا۟** (*tuqsiṭū*) to **ق-س-ط** (*q-s-ṭ*), associated with fairness. In 60:9, the actions of fighting and expelling are named explicitly. I do not want to force every dictionary meaning of a root onto an individual verse. Word roots are useful here because they draw attention to the Quran's simultaneous concern for responsible allegiance and for kindness and justice.

**Verse references:** 60:8-9, 60:13, 58:14; compare 5:55-56.

## 18. Other Claims

### Verse

> **60:13** — O  you  who  believe,  do  not  befriend people  with  whom  GOD  is  angry, and  who  are  hopelessly  stuck  in  disbelief;  they  are  just  as  hopeless  as the  disbelievers  who  are  already  in the graves.

> **60:8** — GOD  does  not  enjoin  you  from befriending  those  who  do  not  fight you  because  of  religion,  and  do  not evict  you  from  your  homes.  You  may befriend  them  and  be  equitable towards  them.  GOD  loves  the  equitable.

> **60:9** — GOD  enjoins  you  only  from  befriending  those  who  fight  you  because  of  religion,  evict  you  from your  homes,  and  band  together  with others  to  banish  you.  You  shall  not befriend  them.  Those  who  befriend them are the transgressors.

> **5:51** — O  you  who  believe,  do  not  take  certain  Jews  and  Christians  as  allies; these  are  allies  of  one  another. Those  among  you  who  ally  themselves  with  these  belong  with  them. GOD  does  not  guide  the  transgressors. 

### Reflection on the Verse

Some traditional readings extend the warnings about allegiance into a broad prohibition on social friendship with members of other religions. Other readings narrow the passages almost entirely to historical political alliances. Each approach may be presented with its own arguments. Yet I find it difficult to justify a categorical conclusion without keeping the wording of 60:8-9 in view: the text speaks both of permissible friendship with those who do not persecute believers and of prohibited closeness to those involved in warfare and expulsion.

The word “certain” in the Authorized English Translation of 5:51 is significant. The Turkish translation of the Authorized English Translation likewise says “belirli.” Ignoring that qualification and treating every Jew or every Christian as belonging to one moral category would extend the translation's claim beyond what it says. At the same time, that observation must not become an excuse to disregard the real warnings found in 60:13 and 58:14. Both caution about allegiance and concern for justice matter.

Another claim sometimes made is that these warnings permit believers to decide with certainty which living individuals have earned GOD's anger and what their ultimate fate will be. I am cautious about that conclusion. Revelation communicates GOD's judgment concerning the people it describes; it does not give me knowledge of every hidden intention, repentance, or final outcome. These external interpretive positions do not control the reflection developed in earlier sections; I include them here only to clarify where competing approaches draw their boundaries.

**Verse references:** 60:8-9, 60:13, 58:14, 5:51, 5:8.

## 19. Conclusion: Whose Friend Will I Be, and Whose Path Will I Follow?

### Verse

> **60:13** — O  you  who  believe,  do  not  befriend people  with  whom  GOD  is  angry, and  who  are  hopelessly  stuck  in  disbelief;  they  are  just  as  hopeless  as the  disbelievers  who  are  already  in the graves.

> **60:8** — GOD  does  not  enjoin  you  from befriending  those  who  do  not  fight you  because  of  religion,  and  do  not evict  you  from  your  homes.  You  may befriend  them  and  be  equitable towards  them.  GOD  loves  the  equitable.

> **5:2** — O  you  who  believe,  do  not  violate the  rites  instituted  by  GOD,  nor  the Sacred  Months,  nor  the  animals  to be  offered,  nor  the  garlands  marking  them,  nor  the  people  who  head for  the  Sacred  Shrine  (Ka‘bah) seeking  blessings  from  their  Lord and  approval.  Once  you  complete the  pilgrimage,  you  may  hunt.*  Do not  be  provoked  into  aggression  by your  hatred  of  people  who  once prevented  you  from  going  to  the Sacred  Masjid.  You  shall  cooperate in  matters  of  righteousness  and piety;  do  not  cooperate  in  matters that  are  sinful  and  evil.  You  shall observe  GOD.  GOD  is  strict  in  enforcing retribution.

> **25:28** — “Alas,  woe  to  me,  I  wish  I  did  not take that person as a friend.

> **41:34** — Not  equal  is  the  good  response  and the  bad  response.  You  shall  resort to  the  nicest  possible  response. Thus,  the  one  who  used  to  be  your enemy,  may  become  your  best friend.

### Reflection on the Verse

At first, choosing friends might seem like making a list of people I enjoy. But the more I read these verses together, the more I see friendship as a question of direction. Close companions influence how I approach truth, wrongdoing, knowledge, and my responsibility before GOD. The warning of 60:13 is therefore not a minor warning. I am asked not to treat allegiance to those described as objects of GOD's anger as something harmless.

Yet I cannot ignore 60:8 in the same Quran. Difference alone is not a license for hostility. I may be just and friendly with those who do not fight me because of religion or drive me from my home. Verse 41:34 even holds out the possibility that a former enemy may become a dear friend. If I take only the verses that support rejecting people I already dislike, I am no longer letting the whole text question me; I am using it to protect my own anger.

The false oaths of 58:14, the principle of cooperation in 5:2, the regret in 25:28, and the warning against suspicion in 49:12 lead me toward a difficult balance. **Friendship should be neither blind allegiance nor universal suspicion.** I must not participate in wrongdoing merely because I care about someone. I must not deny another person's rights merely because they are different from me. Nor should I let a group's approval become more precious to me than acting truthfully before GOD.

Perhaps the hardest test comes when I decide a relationship needs distance. Can I set a boundary without cruelty, mockery, or invented accusations? Can I refuse a harmful influence without pretending to know someone's final standing before GOD? Can I hope for change while paying attention to what is happening now?

I want to choose friends not only by asking who makes me happy, but by asking **with whom I become more truthful, more just, more merciful, and more conscious of GOD**. And I must ask the same question of myself: when I enter another person's life, in which direction do I invite them? That may be the most enduring question of this reflection.

**Verse references:** 60:7-9, 60:13, 58:14, 5:2, 5:8, 25:27-29, 41:34, 49:12.

### Choosing Friends — A Verse-Referenced Checklist

The points below combine principles stated directly in the verses with questions I draw from them for practical life. They are not a method for classifying another person's ultimate standing before GOD.

1. **Take seriously the warning about people described as objects of GOD's anger and entrenched in disbelief.** Do not extend that judgment beyond what the verses establish. (60:13; 58:14)
2. **Do not knowingly swear falsely or participate in deception to preserve a friendship.** (58:14)
3. **Do not give unlimited trust to those who continually wish you harm and desire your suffering.** (3:118)
4. **Be cautious of allegiance to those who persecute people for their faith and harm them through words or actions.** (60:1-2)
5. **Do not declare friendship with peaceful people of different beliefs forbidden merely because they are different.** (60:8)
6. **Remain equitable even toward those with whom you disagree or have a conflict.** (60:8; 5:8)
7. **Examine alliances connected with persecution, expulsion, and oppression.** (60:9)
8. **Allow for the possibility that people can change.** Enmity can become love. (60:7; 41:34)
9. **Do not sacrifice truth merely to gain prestige or acceptance from a group.** (4:139)
10. **Do not turn a friend, teacher, or leader into an unquestionable lord beside GOD.** (3:64)
11. **Do not assume that impressive speech proves a claim true.** (63:4; 17:36)
12. **Investigate reports before accepting or repeating something that may harm another person.** (49:6)
13. **Value companions who examine words and are willing to follow what is best.** (39:18)
14. **Be wary of influences that ask you to follow opinions without verification.** (5:77; 17:36)
15. **Do not join gatherings that ridicule GOD's revelations; withdraw when appropriate.** (4:140; 6:68)
16. **Do not confuse sincere questioning with deliberate mockery.** Debate with wisdom and kindness. (16:125; 5:57)
17. **Ask whether a close friendship is drawing you away from the message.** (25:27-29)
18. **Consider the moral direction of companionship, not only its present comfort.** (43:67)
19. **Honor family without obeying pressure to set up idols beside GOD.** (31:15; 9:23)
20. **Do not violate other people's rights in order to defend a friend.** (5:8)
21. **Seek and offer friendship without ridicule or degrading names.** (49:11)
22. **Do not normalize suspicion, spying, or backbiting within friendship.** (49:12)
23. **Cooperate in what is good and refuse to cooperate in what is wrong.** (5:2)
24. **Value solidarity with people who advocate righteousness and resist evil.** (9:71)
25. **Examine relationships that encourage wrongdoing and obstruct what is right.** (9:67)
26. **Remember the practical dimensions of friendship: help, hospitality, and responsibility.** (4:36; 8:74)
27. **Practice consultation, listening, and generosity when making shared decisions.** (42:38)
28. **In conflict, consider justice, pardon, and the best possible response.** (3:159; 41:34)
29. **Finally ask yourself: Am I a trustworthy, truthful, and just friend to others?** (49:10-12; 5:8; 17:36)
