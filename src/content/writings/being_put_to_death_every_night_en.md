# Being Put to Death Every Night: Sleep, the Soul, and Morning as a Resurrection

**Author:** Berk KÖKSAL  
**Date:** September 30, 2026  
**Place:** Mersin

The central purpose of this article is to contemplate the verses together with other related verses, and to consider sincerely and deeply what bearing witness to the oneness of GOD means in human life in terms of knowledge, justice, truthfulness, and submission.

Peace. Peace be upon you.

Sleep is easy to treat as an ordinary pause inside life. We become tired, we sleep, we wake in the morning, and we continue from where we left off. Because this cycle repeats every day, it can become almost invisible. We close our eyes without thinking much about what we are surrendering, and we open them without asking what has just been returned to us.

39:42 interrupts that familiarity. The verse places death and sleep in the same sentence. It says that GOD puts the souls to death when their lives end and also at the time of sleep; some are taken back, while others are allowed to continue living until the end of their predetermined interim.

That makes sleep difficult for me to regard as a neutral gap between two days. When I go to bed, I am not merely resting my body. I am releasing the control I imagine I possess over my life. And when I wake, I am not merely completing a biological cycle. In the language of 6:60 and 25:47, I have been resurrected again and placed into another day.

So the question of this article is not only, “What happens to the soul during sleep?” A more demanding question comes first: **If every night I experience something that shows me that life is not under my absolute control, why do I spend the day as though it belongs entirely to me?**

## 1. Being Put to Death Every Night: Why Do Sleep and Death Meet in the Same Verse?

### Verse

> **39:42**
>
> ٱللَّهُ يَتَوَفَّى ٱلْأَنفُسَ حِينَ مَوْتِهَا وَٱلَّتِى لَمْ تَمُتْ فِى مَنَامِهَا فَيُمْسِكُ ٱلَّتِى قَضَىٰ عَلَيْهَا ٱلْمَوْتَ وَيُرْسِلُ ٱلْأُخْرَىٰٓ إِلَىٰٓ أَجَلٍ مُّسَمًّى إِنَّ فِى ذَٰلِكَ لَـَٔايَٰتٍ لِّقَوْمٍ يَتَفَكَّرُونَ
>
> GOD puts the souls to death when the end of their life comes, and also at the time of sleep. Thus, He takes some back during their sleep, while others are allowed to continue living until the end of their predetermined interim. This should provide lessons for people who reflect.
>
> TANRI, hayatlarının sonu geldiğinde ve bir de uyku anında nefisleri ölüme koyar. Böylece O, bazılarını uykuları sırasında geri alır, diğerlerinin ise yaşamaya devam etmelerine izin verilir, ta ki önceden belirlenmiş geçici sürelerinin sonuna kadar. Bu, derinlemesine düşünen insanlar için dersler sağlamalıdır.

### Reflection on the Verse

39:42 moves sleep beyond an ordinary biological habit. The verse mentions the souls whose lives have reached their end and the souls of those who are asleep within the same statement. It then distinguishes between them: some are taken back, while others are permitted to continue living until their predetermined interim is complete.

Even without inventing details that the verse does not give, something profound becomes visible. When a person goes to bed, he cannot guarantee by his own power that morning will come for him. Sleep is a threshold in which conscious control loosens, contact with the surrounding world largely disappears, and tomorrow cannot be opened by human will.

One idea that strikes me strongly here is the idea of being “allowed to continue living.” Morning is not merely the result of an alarm clock. According to 39:42, continued life occurs within an interim whose limit is already determined. This can change the way one sees the morning. Instead of treating it as something that was owed to me, I can see it as another granted opportunity.

The verse ends by saying that this should provide lessons for people who reflect. It is not only delivering information about death. It is asking us to think. My first reflection is simple: if every night I release the reins of life, why am I so possessive during the day? My plans, work, body, influence, and expectations about tomorrow can easily make me feel like an owner. Yet every night I enter an experience that exposes the limits of that ownership.

Sleep, from this perspective, is not merely frightening. It is instructive. A human being can learn his limits every night. Tawhid is not only saying that GOD is one; it is also recognizing that the continuation of life remains under His power.

**Verse references:** 39:42.

## 2. “He Resurrects You Every Morning”: Is Morning More Than Waking Up?

### Verse

> **6:60** — He is the One who puts you to death during the night, and knows even the smallest of your actions during the day. He resurrects you every morning, until your life span is fulfilled, then to Him is your ultimate return. He will then inform you of everything you had done.
> 
> **6:61** — He is Supreme over His creatures, and He appoints guards to protect you. When the appointed time of death comes to any of you, our messengers put him to death without delay.
> 
> **6:62** — Then everyone is returned to GOD, their rightful Lord and Master. Absolutely, He is the ultimate judge; He is the most accurate reckoner. 

### Reflection on the Verse

6:60 completes the thought of 39:42 with a remarkably direct expression: “He resurrects you every morning.” Because waking up is one of the most familiar events in my life, it is easy to miss the weight of those words. The verse renames the ordinary with the language of resurrection.

The moral dimension of the day immediately appears as well. The same verse that speaks of being put to death at night says that GOD knows even the smallest actions of the day. Morning resurrection, the completion of the life span, ultimate return, and the disclosure of what we have done are joined in one movement. A day is not an isolated piece of time. It belongs to a limited life that has a destination.

This changes the first question of the morning. Before “What do I have to do today?” comes another question: “What does it mean that today has been given to me at all?” I may go to the same workplace, meet the same people, and face the same obligations. But viewed through this verse, none of those things are automatic. Another day has been opened.

6:62 calls GOD the rightful Lord and Master. This makes tawhid concrete. If life were truly my property, I would determine its length. I would open the morning myself. I would postpone death at will. The verses show the opposite: my claim of ownership is temporary; real ownership belongs to GOD.

In my earlier article *When the Mouth Is Sealed*, I reflected on deeds becoming witnesses against the person who performed them. 6:60 extends that responsibility across the ordinary day. To be resurrected in the morning is not only to receive another opportunity; it is to receive another portion of responsibility.

**Verse references:** 6:60-62; 39:42.

## 3. If the Day Is a Resurrection, What Is the Night?

### Verse

> **25:47** — He  is  the  One  who  designed  the night to be a cover, and for you to sleep and rest. And He made the day a resurrection.

### Reflection on the Verse

25:47 places three ideas together in a very small space: the night as a cover, sleep as rest, and the day as a resurrection. The night is not presented as an empty darkness. It is designed as a cover and a field of rest. The day is explicitly called a resurrection.

This makes me think about the rhythm of human life. We can begin to live as though we were created for uninterrupted production, visibility, activity, and control. Modern habits can even make sleep look like lost time: less sleep, more work, more output, more achievement. Yet the verse places rest inside the design.

Rest, then, can be an acceptance of limit rather than laziness. The body has to stop. The mind has to withdraw from the world. For part of every day, control is released. This reminds me that I am created. GOD does not need rest; the human being does. GOD’s power does not weaken; human power does.

The expression “He made the day a resurrection” also prevents sleep from becoming meaningless interruption. Night and day form a meaningful pair. One brings withdrawal and rest; the other returns us to visible life and responsibility.

Seen in this way, night and day are not independent natural mechanisms. They are two sides of the same order of mercy and power. One stops me. The other raises me. One covers me. The other returns me to action.

**Verse references:** 25:47.

## 4. If Sleep Is Called a Proof, What Are We Expected to Notice?

### Verse

> **30:23** — Among  His  proofs  is  your  sleeping during  the  night  or  the  day,  and  your working  in  pursuit  of  His  provisions. In  this,  there  are  sufficient  proofs for people who can hear.

### Reflection on the Verse

30:23 presents sleep not merely as a need but as one of GOD’s proofs. That arrests my attention because people often search for signs only in what appears extraordinary while overlooking what repeats every day.

Habit can dull perception. Because we sleep every night, we assume sleep needs no contemplation. Because we wake so often, we begin to treat waking as a right. Because we work for provision every day, the ability to move, think, plan, and produce becomes invisible.

The verse speaks of “people who can hear.” Hearing may mean more than registering sound. It can suggest receiving the message carried by recurring realities. Sleep says, “You are limited.” Waking says, “You have been given another opportunity.” Working for provision says, “Use the capacities you have been given.” But hearing these messages requires attention.

There is also a question of knowledge here. Human beings can study the biology of sleep in great detail, and that knowledge is valuable. Yet the verse opens another dimension: knowing how something works does not exhaust what it means for human existence.

30:23 reminds me that contemplation does not have to begin in distant galaxies. My own bed, my own sleep, and my own morning can be enough to begin.

**Verse references:** 30:23.

## 5. Retiring at Night, Rising in the Morning, and Glorifying GOD

### Verse

> **30:17** — Therefore,  you  shall  glorify  GOD when  you  retire  at  night,  and  when you rise in the morning.
> 
> **30:18** — All  praise  is  due  to  Him  in  the heavens  and  the  earth,  throughout the evening, as  well  as  in the  middle of your day.
> 
> **30:19** — He  produces  the  live  from  the  dead, and  produces  the  dead  from  the live,  and  He  revives  the  land  after  it had  died;  you  are  similarly  resurrected.

### Reflection on the Verse

30:17-19 connects the transition between night and morning directly to glorifying GOD. Retiring at night and rising in the morning become more than changes on a clock. They become thresholds at which awareness can be renewed.

What matters to me here is that glorification is not added to life as an isolated religious sentence. The night and morning themselves become reminders. The day ends: did my control not reach its limit? I enter sleep: is my ability to protect myself not limited? I rise again: did I do this by my own power? Questions like these can make glorification emerge from lived reality rather than habit alone.

30:19 then speaks of bringing the living from the dead, the dead from the living, and reviving the land after its death; “you are similarly resurrected.” Personal morning experience is therefore not left disconnected from the larger reality of resurrection.

These verses do not make the thought of death a form of despair. They make the morning more meaningful. If resurrection awaits me, then the small “resurrections” of this life are not empty. Every morning can become another opportunity to repair, repent, act justly, speak truthfully, and be grateful.

Believing in resurrection is not only affirming a future event. It may also mean taking seriously the fact that I have been raised again today.

**Verse references:** 30:17-19.

> **78:11** — And the day to seek provisions.

### Reflection on the Verse

78:9-11 establishes a simple balance: sleep, night, and day. Sleep is for rest, night is a cover, and day is for seeking provision. Both withdrawal and work have a place in the design.

Religious seriousness can sometimes become confused with constant visible activity. More speaking, more producing, more appearing, more doing. Yet the verse treats rest as part of creation’s order. To accept the body’s need for rest and to prepare honestly for another day is not a meaningless interval.

At the same time, the day is not emptied of responsibility. It is for seeking provision. Trust in GOD is not passivity. The person releases control in sleep and takes responsibility again when awake.

This balance corrects two opposite illusions. One is to imagine that everything depends on me, to the point that I refuse rest and live as though I were self-sufficient. The other is to use trust as an excuse for neglecting effort. The verses support neither extreme.

Perhaps healthy submission is this: to do what I can with integrity during the day, and to release what I cannot control when night comes.

**Verse references:** 78:9-11; 30:23.

## 7. Why Are Death and Life Mentioned Together as a Test?

### Verse

> **67:1** — Most exalted is the One in whose hands is all kingship, and He is Omnipotent.
> 
> **67:2** — The One who created death and life for the purpose of distinguishing those among you who would do better.* He is the Almighty, the Forgiving.

### Reflection on the Verse

67:2 does not treat death and life as unrelated opposites. They are mentioned together within one purpose: distinguishing those who would do better. Death therefore becomes not only the end of life but also the boundary that gives moral weight to the time before it.

If life had no limit, postponement could become endless. “One day I will apologize. One day I will correct this. One day I will become more just. One day I will turn back to GOD.” A predetermined interim makes those sentences serious.

I also notice that the verse speaks about those who “would do better,” not merely those who would do more. Quantity can deceive. Visibility, religious activity, speech, and productivity are not automatically signs of what is better. The question of quality remains: Is the act truthful? Is it just? Is it based on knowledge? Is it directed toward GOD? Does it repair or harm?

67:1 reminds us whose test this is: all kingship belongs to GOD. The human being is not the final sovereign of his own life.

Every morning that I wake, the test continues. As long as my eyes open, there remains an opportunity to answer differently. That makes the morning both mercy and responsibility.

**Verse references:** 67:1-2; 39:42.

## 8. Adversity and Prosperity Are Both Part of the Test While We Are Awake

### Verse

> **21:35** — Every  soul  will  taste  death,  after  we put  you  to  the  test  through  adversity and  prosperity,  then  to  us  you  ultimately return.

### Reflection on the Verse

21:35 places two very different experiences under the same category of test: adversity and prosperity. We often recognize hardship as a test, while treating comfort as a reward that requires no examination. The verse does not make that distinction.

This adds another dimension to morning. We do not all wake into the same conditions. Some mornings come with health, security, work, companionship, and ease. Others come with illness, debt, grief, uncertainty, or loneliness. In both cases, the human being still moves toward the same return.

Prosperity can test gratitude; hardship can test endurance. I do not want to turn this into a rigid formula beyond what the verse says. The safe conclusion is that both conditions belong to the field in which our moral response is revealed.

Sleep reduces everyone to a striking vulnerability. Morning returns us to very different circumstances. Instead of asking only why someone else has what I do not, the verse redirects attention toward my own answer: What will I do with the condition in which I have been placed today?

Since death is common to all, prosperity is not proof of superiority and hardship is not proof of worthlessness. Both belong to the temporary life.

**Verse references:** 21:35.

## 9. If Death Is Certain, Where Does Anxiety About Provision Belong?

### Verse

> **29:57** — Every soul will taste death, then to us you will be ultimately returned.
> 
> **29:58** — Those  who  believe  and  lead  a righteous life, we will surely settle them  in  Paradise,  with  mansions and flowing streams. Eternally they abide  therein.  What  a  beautiful reward for the workers.
> 
> **29:59** — They  are  the  ones  who  steadfastly persevere, and trust in their Lord.
> 
> **29:60** — Many a creature that does not carry its provision, GOD provides for it, as well as for you. He is the Hearer, the Omniscient.

### Reflection on the Verse

29:57-60 gathers death, righteous living, perseverance, trust, and provision into one passage. It speaks directly to the anxieties that can arrive with the morning. Work, money, health, responsibilities, and the future can fill the mind the moment we wake.

29:60 says that many creatures do not carry their own provision, yet GOD provides for them and for us. This does not cancel effort. 30:23 and 78:11 explicitly place the pursuit of provision within human life. The balance is important: work is real, but I must not imagine that I am the ultimate source of what sustains me.

Part of anxiety may come from the illusion of total control. I feel that I must secure every possibility, determine every result, and guarantee tomorrow. Yet every night I lose even the ordinary control of consciousness. Morning itself is not mine to manufacture.

This awareness need not make me irresponsible. I still work, plan, endure, and seek what is right. But I do not turn my own capacity into an idol.

Trust may not mean the absence of anxiety. It may mean remembering, even inside anxiety, who the rightful Lord and Master is.

**Verse references:** 29:57-60; 30:23; 78:11.

## 10. If This Worldly Life Is an Illusion, Does Morning Become Less Valuable or More?

### Verse

> **3:185** — Every person tastes death, then you receive  your  recompense  on  the Day of Resurrection. Whoever misses Hell, barely, and makes it to Paradise, has attained a great triumph. The life of this world is no more than an illusion.
> 
> **3:186** — You  will  certainly  be  tested, through your money and your lives, and you will hear from those who received the scripture, and from the idol worshipers, a lot of insult. If you steadfastly persevere and lead a righteous life, this will prove the strength of your faith.

### Reflection on the Verse

“The life of this world is no more than an illusion” can be misunderstood if detached from the surrounding verses. The passage does not make this life morally irrelevant. It speaks of death, recompense, resurrection, testing, perseverance, and righteous living. The temporary nature of life makes what we do here more serious, not less.

The illusion is not that nothing here exists. The danger is treating what is temporary as though it were permanent, and building one’s identity as if this life had no end.

From that perspective, morning becomes more valuable. A truthful word, a repaired injustice, an apology, a debt repaid, a false claim withdrawn, gratitude shown, or a duty fulfilled all occur within limited time.

3:186 reinforces this point. The world’s temporary nature does not excuse withdrawal from ethical responsibility. Money, life, insult, endurance, and righteous conduct remain part of the test.

If I have been resurrected for another morning, I have received another limited day in which choices can have consequences beyond the day itself.

**Verse references:** 3:185-186; 6:60.

## 11. Dead Land, Night, and Day: Is Resurrection Shown Only Inside the Human Being?

### Verse

> **36:33** — One  sign  for  them  is  the  dead  land: we  revive  it  and  produce  from  it grains for their food.
> 
> **36:35** — This  is  to  provide  them  with  fruits, and to let them manufacture with their  own  hands  whatever  they need. Would they be thankful?
> 
> **36:37** — Another  sign  for  them  is  the  night: we  remove  the  daylight  therefrom, whereupon they are in darkness.
> 
> **36:40** — The  sun  is  never  to  catch  up  with  the moon—the  night  and  the  day  never deviate—each  of  them  is  floating  in its own orbit.

### Reflection on the Verse

Sura 36 speaks of dead land being revived and of the rhythm of night and day using the same language of signs. Resurrection is therefore not left only as an abstract doctrine about a distant future. The reader is directed toward patterns already visible in creation.

The land appears dead and then becomes alive. Daylight is withdrawn and darkness comes, then light returns. Sun and moon move in their ordered courses. The human being sleeps and rises. It would be careless to say these are all identical events, but there is a shared rhythm: disappearance and return, darkness and visibility, lifelessness and renewed life.

36:35 asks, “Would they be thankful?” That question turns observation into moral responsibility. A sign is not only something to understand intellectually; it can call for gratitude.

The more I treat morning as something I deserve, the less visible its gift becomes. The same can happen with food, work, movement, health, and the ability to produce with my own hands.

One purpose of contemplation may be to make ordinary gifts visible again.

**Verse references:** 36:33, 36:35, 36:37, 36:40.

## 12. If GOD Puts to Death and Resurrects, Why Does Tawhid Enter This Subject So Directly?

### Verse

> **30:40** — GOD is the One who created you He is the One who provides for you. He is the One who puts you to death. He is the One who resurrects you. Can any of your idols do any of these things? Be He glorified. He is much too exalted to have any partners.
> 
> **39:43** — Have  they  invented  intercessors  to mediate between them and GOD? Say, “What if they do not possess any power, nor understanding?”
> 
> **39:44** — Say,  “All  intercession  belongs  to GOD.”  To  Him  belongs  all  sovereignty  of  the  heavens  and the earth,  then  to  Him  you  will  be returned.

### Reflection on the Verse

The placement of 39:43-44 immediately after 39:42 matters to me. After speaking of the souls, sleep, death, and continued life, the passage turns to intercessors. Tawhid is therefore not an abstract doctrine detached from the experience of life and death.

30:40 is even more explicit. GOD creates, provides, puts to death, and resurrects. Then the verse asks whether any idol can do any of these things.

This also places religious authority within a proper boundary. A teacher can teach me. A knowledgeable person can show me a verse. A messenger can deliver a message. But none of them puts me to death at night, raises me in the morning, grants provision independently, or controls my final return.

The danger of turning human beings into lords becomes clearer here. To place the word of a leader, scholar, or institution above GOD’s authority can assign to a human being a status that the verses do not grant.

If every night I sleep directly under GOD’s sovereignty, then my submission should also remain directly oriented toward Him. Respect is not worship. Learning from someone is not making that person a lord.

**Verse references:** 30:40; 39:42-44.

## 13. If Not Even a Leaf Falls Outside His Knowledge, What Does Safety Mean at Night?

### Verse

> **6:59** — With Him are the keys to all secrets; none  knows  them  except  He.  He knows everything  on  land  and  in  the sea.  Not  a  leaf  falls  without  His knowledge.  Nor  is  there  a  grain  in the depths of the soil. Nor is there anything  wet  or  dry,  that  is  not recorded in a profound record.
> 
> **6:60** — He is the One who puts you to death during the night, and knows even the smallest of your actions during the day. He resurrects you every morning, until your life span is fulfilled, then to Him is your ultimate return. He will then inform you of everything you had done.

### Reflection on the Verse

Read together, 6:59 and 6:60 make sleep not only a matter of power but also a matter of knowledge. Immediately after saying that not even a leaf falls outside GOD’s knowledge, the passage says that He puts us to death at night and knows even the smallest actions of the day.

In sleep, human awareness of the outside world diminishes greatly. I do not consciously supervise the passing of time or the detailed functioning of my body. Yet life continues. This makes me reconsider what trust means.

Trust cannot mean assuming that GOD will always produce the outcome I prefer. 39:42 explicitly says that some souls are taken back during sleep. So the verses do not support the claim that “nothing can happen to me at night.” A more careful conclusion is that nothing happens outside GOD’s knowledge and sovereignty.

That can change fear. Instead of panicking because I lack control, I can remember who actually possesses control. At the same time, the same knowledge that comforts me also makes me responsible: even the smallest actions of the day are known.

The One who receives me at night and the One who knows what I do in daylight is the same GOD.

**Verse references:** 6:59-60; 39:42.

## 14. Sleep Does Not Erase Responsibility: The Record Continues When We Wake

### Verse

> **50:16** — We  created  the  human,  and  we know what he whispers to himself. We are closer to him than his jugular vein.
> 
> **50:17** — Two recording (angels), at right and at left, are constantly recording.
> 
> **50:18** — Not  an  utterance  does  he  utter without an alert witness.

### Reflection on the Verse

Sleep can psychologically feel like a reset. A difficult day ends; morning brings a sense of beginning again. That can be valuable. Yet 50:16-18 reminds me that a new day does not automatically erase previous responsibility.

The verses say that GOD knows what the human being whispers to himself, that recording angels record, and that not a single utterance is made without an alert witness. These verses do not tell us technical details about what is recorded during sleep, and I do not want to invent them. What they clearly establish is the seriousness of conscious life, speech, and responsibility.

So morning should not mean only, “Yesterday is gone.” It may also mean, “What from yesterday still needs to be repaired?” An apology, an unpaid obligation, a false statement, an injustice, a broken promise, a person I harmed—sleep does not transform these into non-events.

In *When the Mouth Is Sealed*, I reflected on testimony and record. The same issue returns here from another direction: forgetting something does not make it unreal. Morning can give me another opportunity to address what remains unfinished.

Resurrection in the morning is therefore not merely feeling refreshed. It can be an invitation to correction.

**Verse references:** 50:16-18; 6:60.

## 15. Night Meditation: Where Does the Mind Turn Before Sleep?

### Verse

> **73:5** — We will give you a heavy message.
> 
> **73:6** — The  meditation  at  night  is  more  effective, and more righteous.
> 
> **73:7** — You have a lot of time during the day for other matters.
> 
> **73:8** — You  shall  commemorate  the  name of  your  Lord,  to  come  ever  closer and closer to Him.

### Reflection on the Verse

73:6 suggests that the night can be more than a time for sleep. It can also be a special field for reflection. During the day the mind is divided among work, people, responsibilities, news, conversations, and urgency. When the outer noise falls, the inner direction can become more visible.

That gives the time before sleep moral value. Before the day closes, I can ask: What did I do today? Where was I truthful? Where did I fail? Did I wrong someone? Did I speak confidently about something I did not know? What gift did I take for granted?

73:8 connects remembering the Lord with drawing closer to Him. This makes remembrance more than repetition. It can become a reorientation. What occupied my mind today—money, fear, status, anger, people, reputation? Night can become the moment when those things are measured again before GOD.

Read beside 39:42, this becomes even more serious. Sleep is an entry into a threshold from which the next morning is not guaranteed. Not to produce fear, but to reduce unnecessary postponement.

Night can be a time when the body rests and the direction of the soul is examined.

**Verse references:** 73:5-8; 39:42.

## 16. If No Soul Knows What Will Happen Tomorrow, What Is the Ethics of Planning?

### Verse

> **31:34** — With GOD is the knowledge regarding  the  Hour  (end  of  the  world).*  He is the One who sends down the rain, and  He  knows  the  contents  of  the womb. No soul knows what will happen  to  it  tomorrow,  and  no  one knows in which land he or she will die. GOD is Omniscient, Cognizant.

### Reflection on the Verse

31:34 draws a clear boundary around human knowledge. No soul knows what will happen to it tomorrow, and no one knows the land in which he or she will die. This does not forbid planning. It forbids confusing planning with ownership of the future.

We arrange calendars, make appointments, invest, study, travel, and build projects. These are ordinary and often necessary. The problem begins when the plan becomes a claim of certainty: when I act as though tomorrow were already mine.

39:42 makes that limit experiential. Sleep places a boundary between me and tomorrow that I cannot cross by my own power. I do not open the morning. 31:34 states the same limit in terms of knowledge: I do not know what tomorrow contains.

This need not make a person passive. It can make today more honest. If tomorrow is uncertain, then endlessly postponing a needed apology, a just act, a debt, a correction, or gratitude becomes more difficult to justify.

It also places limits on how confidently we speak about the future of other people. If I do not know my own tomorrow, I should be cautious about claiming certainty over someone else’s destiny.

**Verse references:** 31:34; 39:42.

## 17. When We Say “I Will Do It Tomorrow,” Whose Will Are We Forgetting?

### Verse

> **18:23** — You  shall  not  say  that  you  will  do anything in the future,
> 
> **18:24** — without  saying,  “GOD  willing.”*  If you forget to do this, you must immediately  remember  your  Lord  and say, “May my Lord guide me to do better next time.”

### Reflection on the Verse

18:23-24 brings the limit of 31:34 into everyday speech. When speaking about what we will do in the future, we are called to remember GOD’s will. This makes “GOD willing” much deeper than a habitual phrase.

If the words remain only on the tongue, they can become empty. Their substance is the recognition that the future is not under my sovereignty. I make plans but I am not the god of the plan. I work but I do not create the outcome. I sleep but I do not manufacture the morning.

18:24 also contains an ethic of correction. If we forget, we are told to remember our Lord immediately and ask to be guided to do better next time. Human beings forget; the point is not to turn forgetfulness into a permanent direction.

This can enter every area of life: work, family, health, travel, study, religious projects. Serious planning and humility about the future are not opposites.

Every night makes the word “tomorrow” more serious. If reaching tomorrow itself depends on permission to continue living, then speaking about the future should contain humility.

**Verse references:** 18:23-24; 31:34; 39:42.

## 18. If Morning Has Been Given Back to Us, What Should Change That Day?

### Verse

> **6:60** — He is the One who puts you to death during the night, and knows even the smallest of your actions during the day. He resurrects you every morning, until your life span is fulfilled, then to Him is your ultimate return. He will then inform you of everything you had done.
> 
> **36:35** — This  is  to  provide  them  with  fruits, and to let them manufacture with their  own  hands  whatever  they need. Would they be thankful?
> 
> **30:17** — Therefore,  you  shall  glorify  GOD when  you  retire  at  night,  and  when you rise in the morning.

### Reflection on the Verse

After all these verses, the most practical question begins here: if morning really is a resurrection, what should change that day?

The first answer may be gratitude. 36:35 asks, “Would they be thankful?” If gratitude is more than a sentence, then what I do with the day belongs to gratitude. How I use time, speak to people, perform my work, protect another person’s rights, handle knowledge, and respond to correction can all become parts of grateful living.

The second answer may be repair. Yesterday’s mistake does not have to become today’s habit. As long as life continues, repentance, correction, learning, and better action remain possible.

The third answer may be reduced possessiveness. If today is not something I was entitled to receive, I may become less harsh and more just toward others. They too are living within a temporary interim.

And perhaps most important is how the day begins. 30:17 connects rising in the morning with glorifying GOD. That can set the center of the day before other demands occupy it.

One morning will eventually not come. I do not know which one. But if this morning did come, it seems more meaningful to use it truthfully than to consume it in fear.

Every morning can sound like a quiet question: **You have been given another day. What will you do with it?**

**Verse references:** 6:60; 36:35; 30:17; 39:42.

## 19. Arabic Word Roots: What Does the Language of 39:42 Invite Us to Notice?

### Verse

> **39:42** — GOD puts the souls to death when the end of their life comes, and also at the time of sleep. Thus, He takes some back during their sleep, while others are allowed to continue living until the end of their predetermined interim. This should provide lessons for people who reflect.

### Reflection on the Verse

Looking at the Arabic words of 39:42 can be useful not to replace the authorized translation, but to notice the internal movement of the verse more carefully.

The word **يَتَوَفَّى — yatawaffā** belongs to the **و ف ي (w-f-y)** root family, associated with completion and taking in full. The authorized translation renders it here as “puts the souls to death.” The same verb appears in the statement about the end of life and the state of sleep, which is one reason the verse binds the two conditions so closely.

**ٱلْأَنفُس — al-anfus** belongs to the **ن ف س (n-f-s)** root family and is translated here as “the souls.” The verse’s subject is therefore not merely the visible body. At the same time, it does not give enough detail to construct a technical theory about exactly how the soul relates to the body during sleep.

**مَوْت — mawt** belongs to the **م و ت (m-w-t)** root family and carries the field of death. **مَنَام — manām** belongs to the **ن و م (n-w-m)** root family and concerns sleep. Their presence inside the same sentence is one of the most striking features of the verse.

**فَيُمْسِكُ — fa-yumsiku** belongs to the **م س ك (m-s-k)** root family, carrying the sense of holding or retaining. The authorized English translation says, “He takes some back.” **وَيُرْسِلُ — wa-yursilu** belongs to the **ر س ل (r-s-l)** root family, associated with sending or releasing; in context, the others are allowed to continue living.

**أَجَلٍ مُّسَمًّى — ajalin musammā** expresses a predetermined interim. The **أ ج ل (ʾ-j-l)** root family is associated with term or appointed time. Human life may feel open-ended from inside the day, but the verse describes it as limited.

Finally, **يَتَفَكَّرُونَ — yatafakkarūn** belongs to the **ف ك ر (f-k-r)** root family, the field of reflection and thought. The verse does not end with a mechanism. It ends with an invitation to contemplate.

The roots do not replace the verse. They help us notice its sequence: soul, death, sleep, retention, release, appointed term, reflection. At the center of that sequence is not human control, but GOD’s sovereignty.

**Verse references:** 39:42.

## Other Claims

Across religious and philosophical traditions, many explanations have been proposed for the relationship between sleep and death. Some traditions describe sleep as a “small death.” Others claim that the soul separates from the body in a particular manner during sleep, or that dreams directly reveal what the soul experiences in that state. Still others describe sleep through different theories about consciousness, spirit, and body.

39:42 clearly connects sleep and death. It says that GOD puts the souls to death both at the end of life and during sleep, takes some back, and allows others to continue until their predetermined interim is complete. But the verse does not explain the physical or metaphysical mechanism in detail. It does not establish with certainty where the soul “goes” during sleep, the exact ontological status of dreams, or a technical map of the body-soul relationship.

Such interpretations may be considered as separate claims, but they should not control the main reflection. What the verses clearly support is simpler and, in my view, more powerful: sleep is a daily sign that human beings do not possess absolute control over life; continued life depends on GOD’s permission, power, and appointed term.

## Conclusion

When I return to 39:42 after reflecting on the related verses, sleep no longer feels entirely ordinary.

Every night I place myself in bed. I close my eyes. For a few hours I release my plans, work, fears, achievements, arguments, titles, and nearly everything I imagine I control. I assume morning will come, but I do not open it.

The Quran rebuilds this daily event with the language of death, soul, appointed term, resurrection, and reflection. 6:60 says, “He resurrects you every morning.” 25:47 calls the day “a resurrection.” 30:23 counts sleep among GOD’s proofs. 31:34 reminds us that no soul knows what will happen to it tomorrow.

Taken together, these verses give me three major lessons.

The first is **tawhid**: the continuation of life is not in my hands. GOD puts to death, permits continued life, and resurrects. No other being shares that power.

The second is **submission**: I cannot control everything. Every night I accept that physically. During the day I must learn to accept the same truth morally.

The third is **responsibility**: if I have risen again, my appointed time is still running. That time is not empty. It can be used for justice, truthfulness, gratitude, repentance, fidelity to knowledge, and righteous action.

Thinking about death does not have to darken life. Keeping death in its proper place can make life more truthful. A person can stop living as though mornings were endless and begin living as though each one had been given.

Perhaps the question before sleep is this:

**If I were taken back tonight, what kind of life did I leave behind today?**

And when our eyes open in the morning, another question follows:

**Since I have been raised again, what will I do more truthfully today?**

May we remember the limit shown to us every night, the opportunity given to us every morning, and our ultimate return to GOD.
