## 1. Before the Wings, There Is One Who Sends

### Verse

**35:1**

**Arabic**

<blockquote data-verse="35:1" data-language="ar">
<p lang="ar" dir="rtl">ٱلْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ فَاطِرِ ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَٱلْأَرْضِ جَاعِلِ ٱلْمَلَٰٓئِكَةِ رُسُلًا أُو۟لِىٓ أَجْنِحَةٍ مَّثْنَىٰ وَثُلَٰثَ وَرُبَٰعَ يَزِيدُ فِى ٱلْخَلْقِ مَا يَشَآءُ إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ عَلَىٰ كُلِّ شَىْءٍ قَدِيرٌ</p>

### Reflection on the Verse

When I think of angels, my mind immediately reaches for an image. What do they look like? Where are they? How close are they to us? This curiosity is understandable. Yet when I read 35:1, I notice that the verse directs my attention first not to an image but to the One to whom praise belongs. I need to begin learning about angels without forgetting GOD, who appoints them.

The verse mentions two, three, and four wings. I cannot disregard that. At the same time, it does not explain what the wings are made of, how movement takes place, or every detail of appearance. Accepting the description I read is different from treating the picture I complete in my imagination as the verse itself. A gap filled by my curiosity does not become a detail disclosed by revelation.

The relationship that most draws my attention is this: being assigned a task is not the same as being its source. The existence of a messenger does not place a second absolute authority beside the One who sends. Here the diversity of creation and GOD’s power appear together; the capacities of angels are not presented as an independent domain of power.

Thinking about the unseen world, then, should not mean claiming to know every unseen detail. As I investigate the tasks of angels, I also want to examine the center of my own life. From whom do I expect support? To whom do I attribute judgment? Toward whom do I ultimately direct praise?

35:1 invites me not to belittle the existence of angels, but to avoid displacing GOD while magnifying their tasks. The questions of this essay unfold around that distinction.

**Verse references:** 35:1.

## 2. Glorification and Stepping Out of the Center

### Verse

<blockquote data-verse="21:19" data-language="en">
<p lang="en"><strong>21:19</strong> — To  Him  belongs  everyone  in  the heavens  and  the  earth,  and  those  at Him  are  never  too  arrogant  to  worship Him, nor do they ever waver.</p>
</blockquote>

<blockquote data-verse="21:20" data-language="en">
<p lang="en"><strong>21:20</strong> — They  glorify  night  and  day,  without ever tiring.</p>
</blockquote>

<blockquote data-verse="21:26" data-language="en">
<p lang="en"><strong>21:26</strong> — Yet,  they  said,  “The  Most  Gracious has  begotten  a  son!”  Glory  be  to Him.  All  (messengers)  are  (His) honored servants.</p>
</blockquote>

<blockquote data-verse="21:27" data-language="en">
<p lang="en"><strong>21:27</strong> — They  never  speak  on  their  own,  and they strictly follow His commands.</p>
</blockquote>

### Reflection on the Verse

When the unceasing glorification in 21:19–20 is considered alongside the servanthood and obedience of 21:26–27, I encounter a relationship in which nearness does not generate arrogance. Having worth and claiming ownership of everything are kept apart. Being honored does not become a reason to establish a new sovereignty in one’s own name.

For a human being, the examination may begin somewhere much more ordinary. When I perform a task well, I can begin to place myself above everyone involved. As my knowledge grows, my willingness to listen can shrink. An opportunity to serve can gradually become an expectation of being served. Before these verses, the question I want to ask is not how impressive I appear, but how far the responsibility I carry keeps me from boasting.

In <a href="https://berkkoksal.com/writings/you_do_not_understand_their_glorification_en/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">my earlier reflection on “You Do Not Understand Their Glorification”</a>, I considered how non-human glorification also brings the limits of human understanding into view. Here I continue that thought from another angle: glorification should be considered not only through the beauty of a sound I hear, but alongside the question of devotion.

Still, I cannot conclude from this account that I know every detail of the inner experience of angels. Their obedience does not, by itself, establish a separate theory that they are unconscious or function like machines. The faithfulness that is actually described gives me enough to contemplate.

The personal implication is not that I should compete with angels in capacity. It is that, while saying I glorify GOD, I should notice how much I also want to magnify my own name.

**Verse references:** 21:19; 21:20; 21:26; 21:27.

## 3. There Are Things the Angels Do Not Know

### Verse

<blockquote data-verse="2:31" data-language="en">
<p lang="en"><strong>2:31</strong> — He  taught  Adam  all  the  names* then presented  them to the angels, saying, “Give me the names of these, if you are right.”</p>
</blockquote>

<blockquote data-verse="2:32" data-language="en">
<p lang="en"><strong>2:32</strong> — They  said,  “Be  You  glorified,  we have  no  knowledge,  except  that which  You  have  taught  us.  You  are the Omniscient, Most Wise.”</p>
</blockquote>

<blockquote data-verse="2:33" data-language="en">
<p lang="en"><strong>2:33</strong> — He  said,  “O  Adam,  tell  them  their names.”  When  he  told  them  their names,  He  said,  “Did  I  not  tell  you that  I  know  the  secrets  of  the  heavens  and  the  earth?  I  know  what  you declare, and what you conceal.”</p>
</blockquote>

### Reflection on the Verse

2:31–33 interrupts one of the easiest assumptions about unseen beings: I cannot move from being an angel to knowing everything. In the encounter involving the teaching of names, the knowledge of angels and GOD’s knowledge are not presented on the same plane. What is taught is real knowledge, but the knowledge of its recipient is not unlimited.

What most affects me is that the angels do not invent an answer where they do not know. Faced with the test before them, they do not seek refuge in conjecture to protect their standing. Their acknowledgment of the source of their knowledge does not appear as a deficiency outside their servanthood. It is expressed together with their recognition of GOD’s wisdom.

In <a href="https://berkkoksal.com/writings/the_teaching_of_names_en/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">“The Teaching of Names: Does Naming a Thing Mean Knowing It?”</a>, I explored the distance between naming and knowing. The same distance is present in an inquiry into angels. Memorizing the name of a task does not mean learning its entire operation. Giving a being a name does not show that I have gained access to all its secrets.

This limit also matters to me when I encounter claims of religious authority. How could I conclude that a person claiming a special relationship with angels therefore knows everything? If the angels’ own words acknowledge that knowledge does not originate with them, I cannot uncritically accept unlimited authority from someone speaking in their name.

I do not want to read 2:32 merely as a beautiful closing sentence. I need the same disposition in the middle of an investigation, when I reach a detail I do not know. Admitting ignorance does not diminish my trust in GOD; it diminishes my excessive trust in myself.

**Verse references:** 2:31; 2:32; 2:33.

## 4. Carrying Revelation Is Not Owning Revelation

### Verse

<blockquote data-verse="22:75" data-language="en">
<p lang="en"><strong>22:75</strong> — GOD  chooses  from  among  the  angels  messengers,  as  well  as  from among  the  people.  GOD  is  Hearer, Seer.</p>
</blockquote>

<blockquote data-verse="16:2" data-language="en">
<p lang="en"><strong>16:2</strong> — He sends down the angels with the revelations,  carrying  His  commands,  to  whomever  He  chooses from  among  His  servants:  “You shall preach that there is no other god beside Me; You shall reverence Me.”</p>
</blockquote>

<blockquote data-verse="2:97" data-language="en">
<p lang="en"><strong>2:97</strong> — Say, “Anyone who opposes Gabriel should know that he has brought down this (Quran) into your heart, in accordance with GOD’s will, confirming  previous  scriptures,  and providing guidance and good news for the believers.”</p>
</blockquote>

<blockquote data-verse="19:64" data-language="en">
<p lang="en"><strong>19:64</strong> — We  do  not  come  down  except  by  the command  of  your  Lord.  To  Him belongs  our  past,  our  future,  and everything  between  them.  Your Lord is never forgetful.</p>
</blockquote>

### Reflection on the Verse

In 22:75, the choosing of messengers from among both angels and human beings places the task within a relationship of appointment. In 16:2, monotheism is at the center of the message carried by angels. In 2:97, Gabriel’s bringing the Quran into the heart is connected with GOD’s will. Reading these statements together reminds me not to confuse the bearer with the source of the message.

Gabriel’s name is connected with his role in conveying the message, not with claiming it in his own name. The revelation carried by angels does not call people to worship an angel. The meaning of being sent becomes apparent here: the appointed bearer fulfills the task by carrying the message, rather than by retaining attention for himself.

The descent subject to command in 19:64 is also important in this context. I cannot turn the idea of unseen assistance into a service I may summon and direct whenever I choose. This verse does not describe the unseen world as an unlimited sphere of access placed under human command.

The implication for my own research is quite concrete. Accurately transmitting a text and giving my interpretation the same authority are different acts. Making a verse accessible to a reader does not place me above that verse. My explanation may be mistaken; that possibility makes it necessary to distinguish my explanation from the text I convey.

The faithfulness I observe in the carrying of revelation becomes a measure for my own speech. Whose words am I transmitting? Where am I interpreting? Where am I merely guessing? Attending to the task of messengership means refusing to trivialize these distinctions.

**Verse references:** 22:75; 16:2; 2:97; 19:64.

## 5. Good News: Distinguishing Its Bearer from Its Giver

### Verse

<blockquote data-verse="3:39" data-language="en">
<p lang="en"><strong>3:39</strong> — The  angels  called  him  when  he  was praying  in  the  sanctuary:  “GOD gives  you  good  news  of  John;  a believer  in  the  word  of  GOD,  honorable,  moral,  and  a  righteous  prophet.”</p>
</blockquote>

<blockquote data-verse="3:45" data-language="en">
<p lang="en"><strong>3:45</strong> — The  angels  said,  “O  Mary,  GOD gives  you  good  news:  a  Word  from Him  whose  name  is  ‘The  Messiah, Jesus  the  son  of  Mary.  He  will  be prominent  in  this  life  and  in  the Hereafter,  and  one  of  those  closest to Me.’</p>
</blockquote>

<blockquote data-verse="3:46" data-language="en">
<p lang="en"><strong>3:46</strong> — “He  will  speak  to  the  people  from the  crib,  as  well  as  an  adult;  he  will be one of the righteous.”</p>
</blockquote>

### Reflection on the Verse

In the announcements addressed to Zachariah and Mary, the tasks of angels do not appear limited to distant, vast events. The birth of a child, a change in the direction of a life, and a promise confronting familiar limits are also part of the account. Angelic tasks therefore cannot be considered solely through fear or punishment.

In 3:39 and 3:45–46, the voices carrying the good news and the source of that news are distinguished. That angels announce a blessing does not mean the blessing belongs to them. GOD’s choosing and giving stand at the center of the message. The value attributed to its bearers does not push its true source into the background.

This raises a subtle question for my own life. While feeling grateful to someone through whom something good reaches me, I may bind all my hope to that person’s presence. I can experience the disappearance of a means as though goodness itself were no longer possible. Without treating these verses as a direct description of every human relationship, I draw a question of self-examination from them: can I distinguish the means through which a blessing comes from its source?

Another limit should be preserved here. I cannot read these announcements to particular people as a promise that every piece of news I await will arrive in the same way and with the same certainty. The truth of an account is not a guarantee granted to all my personal expectations.

Contemplating good news is not simply believing that my desired outcome will happen. It can also mean noticing whether a message directs me toward its bearer or toward GOD.

**Verse references:** 3:39; 3:45; 3:46.

## 6. Wind, Clouds, and Provisions: A Diversity of Tasks

### Verse

<blockquote data-verse="77:1" data-language="en">
<p lang="en"><strong>77:1</strong> — (Angels) dispatched in succession.</p>
</blockquote>

<blockquote data-verse="77:2" data-language="en">
<p lang="en"><strong>77:2</strong> — To drive the wind.</p>
</blockquote>

<blockquote data-verse="77:3" data-language="en">
<p lang="en"><strong>77:3</strong> — Stir up clouds.</p>
</blockquote>

<blockquote data-verse="77:4" data-language="en">
<p lang="en"><strong>77:4</strong> — Distribute the provisions.</p>
</blockquote>

<blockquote data-verse="77:5" data-language="en">
<p lang="en"><strong>77:5</strong> — Deliver messages.</p>
</blockquote>

<blockquote data-verse="77:6" data-language="en">
<p lang="en"><strong>77:6</strong> — Good news, as well as warnings.</p>
</blockquote>

### Reflection on the Verse

In Rashad Khalifa’s Authorized English Translation, 77:1–6 places driving the wind, stirring up clouds, distributing provisions, and delivering messages within the same sequence. This reminds me not to limit the tasks of angels to conveying spoken announcements to human beings. The appointment in 35:1 can be considered here alongside different kinds of work.

Before this breadth, I want to avoid two distinct kinds of haste. One is removing the tasks from the text simply because they do not resemble the forms of explanation I am accustomed to. The other is claiming to know from these statements which angel moves each cloud and by what method. The task disclosed by the verses and a detailed operational diagram of my own construction are not the same thing.

Nor do I find grounds for making this passage a substitute for investigating how a weather event develops. Examining a physical process and considering its religious meaning involve different questions. These verses do not give us laboratory measurements or an angelic signature that an instrument could detect. Adding such details myself would not make the investigation deeper.

For my part, this diversity of tasks challenges my ingratitude. Do I regard provision only as the thing that finally reaches my hand, forgetting the wider order that makes it possible? How readily do I treat everything sustaining my life as entirely the product of my own ability?

In reflecting on 77:1–6, I want less to multiply unknown details of tasks than to stop attributing to myself a sovereignty I do not possess.

**Verse references:** 77:1; 77:2; 77:3; 77:4; 77:5; 77:6; 35:1.

## 7. Protection Is Not Immunity

### Verse

<blockquote data-verse="13:11" data-language="en">
<p lang="en"><strong>13:11</strong> — Shifts  (of  angels)  take  turns,  staying with each one of you—they are in front  of  you  and  behind  you.  They stay with you, and guard you in accordance  with  GOD’s  commands. Thus,  GOD  does  not  change  the condition of  any  people  unless  they themselves  make  the  decision  to change.  If  GOD  wills  any  hardship for any people, no force can stop it. For  they  have  none  beside  Him  as Lord and Master.</p>
</blockquote>

<blockquote data-verse="6:61" data-language="en">
<p lang="en"><strong>6:61</strong> — He is Supreme over His creatures, and He appoints guards to protect you. When the appointed time of death comes to any of you, our messengers put him to death without delay.</p>
</blockquote>

### Reflection on the Verse

If I reduce 13:11 to a statement that protective beings exist, I lose the rest of the verse. Protection is mentioned in accordance with GOD’s commands. The same verse also speaks about people deciding to change and about the absence of any force capable of preventing GOD’s will. The idea of being protected does not exempt a person from making decisions.

6:61 is especially striking here. Guardians and the occurrence of death appear in the same verse. I therefore cannot infer from the task of protection that I will remain on earth forever. Nor am I being promised that no harm will ever reach me or that everything I want will be preserved.

The distinction explored in <a href="https://berkkoksal.com/writings/do-you-really-trust-god-the-word-of-trust-the-obsession-with-control-and-the-hard-test-of-submission/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">my essay on trusting GOD and trying to control the outcome</a> returns here. Does trust mean requiring events to unfold as I want? Or does it mean continuing to do my part while acknowledging GOD’s power?

I cannot treat someone’s suffering as evidence that angels have abandoned that person or that the person lacks worth. These two verses do not authorize me to diagnose the unseen circumstances of anyone’s protection. Such certainty about another person’s pain would carry my judgment into an area I do not know.

Making protection an excuse for neglect is equally problematic. With the responsibility to change present in the verse, I cannot continually hide my own decision behind unseen support. Believing that a guardian has been appointed does not mean that my responsibility has also been transferred to that guardian.

**Verse references:** 13:11; 6:61.

## 8. Angels Provide Support; They Do Not Own Victory

### Verse

<blockquote data-verse="8:9" data-language="en">
<p lang="en"><strong>8:9</strong> — Thus,  when  you  implored  your Lord to come to the rescue, He responded to you: “I am supporting you with one thousand angels in succession.”</p>
</blockquote>

<blockquote data-verse="8:10" data-language="en">
<p lang="en"><strong>8:10</strong> — GOD gave you this good news to strengthen  your  hearts.  Victory comes only from GOD. GOD is Almighty, Most Wise.</p>
</blockquote>

### Reflection on the Verse

In 8:9, human pleading, GOD’s answer, and support through angels appear together. Then 8:10 connects the strengthening of hearts with the source of victory. These verses need to be read together. While the first describes the form of support, the second explicitly identifies its source.

The number one thousand may attract attention. Yet if I devote all my attention to that number, I can miss the relationship between those asking for help and the true source of help. The multitude of angels is not an independent power added to GOD’s power. The support given is no reason to turn toward another owner of victory beside GOD.

Looking from here into my own days, I notice something. When support comes, I feel relieved; once relieved, I may turn that support into a certificate of my superiority. I can interpret an opened door as though all doors now owe me entry. Yet the assistance in this account is not a document of privilege produced for human boasting. It is presented as an answer to pleading in need.

At the same time, this particular support should not be automatically carried into every group’s own struggle. I cannot say that angels stand with me simply because I stand with myself. Such a claim would require knowledge beyond the event these verses describe.

What most concerns me is whether I preserve my direction when help arrives. Recognizing assistance with gratitude is one thing; appropriating it and imagining myself invincible is another.

**Verse references:** 8:9; 8:10.

## 9. The Recorders: What I Say When Nobody Is Watching

### Verse

<blockquote data-verse="50:16" data-language="en">
<p lang="en"><strong>50:16</strong> — We  created  the  human,  and  we know what he whispers to himself. We are closer to him than his jugular vein.</p>
</blockquote>

<blockquote data-verse="50:17" data-language="en">
<p lang="en"><strong>50:17</strong> — Two recording (angels), at right and at left, are constantly recording.</p>
</blockquote>

<blockquote data-verse="50:18" data-language="en">
<p lang="en"><strong>50:18</strong> — Not  an  utterance  does  he  utter without an alert witness.</p>
</blockquote>

<blockquote data-verse="82:10" data-language="en">
<p lang="en"><strong>82:10</strong> — Oblivious to the fact that there are (invisible) keepers around you.</p>
</blockquote>

<blockquote data-verse="82:11" data-language="en">
<p lang="en"><strong>82:11</strong> — They are honest recorders.</p>
</blockquote>

<blockquote data-verse="82:12" data-language="en">
<p lang="en"><strong>82:12</strong> — They record everything you do.</p>
</blockquote>

### Reflection on the Verse

50:16–18 speaks about what a person whispers to himself, the two recorders, and the spoken word. 82:10–12 turns that attention toward deeds. Before all this, I think first not of spectacular crimes but of actions I easily trivialize in ordinary life: needlessly damaging someone’s name, repeating uncertain news as established fact, or concealing the part of my mistake that would make me look bad.

That a statement is recorded does not mean the recorder said it. This simple distinction matters. While discussing the task of angels, I cannot transfer responsibility for the words that leave my mouth to them or to an unseen order. The first thing the idea of recording addresses is my own manner of speaking.

Nor can the attention described in these verses be reduced to conduct I can display to others. The nearness in 50:16 gives rise to an examination in which I cannot detach the image I present from what I know within myself. Those applauding me may have heard an incomplete story. I know what I left out.

Deriving authority to investigate other people’s privacy or constantly monitor them would be a different claim. Being told of a task assigned to angels does not mean I may appoint myself to it. What I want these verses to awaken in me is not curiosity about another person’s secrets, but honesty in my own speech.

Once a screen is closed, a sentence can seem to have disappeared. Yet the fact that I said it remains. Thinking about the record brings responsibility for speaking into view before the possibility of deleting.

**Verse references:** 50:16; 50:17; 50:18; 82:10; 82:11; 82:12.

## 10. Witnessing: Monotheism and Justice Give the Tasks Their Direction

### Verse

<blockquote data-verse="3:18" data-language="en">
<p lang="en"><strong>3:18</strong> — GOD  bears  witness  that  there  is  no god except He, and so do the angels and  those  who  possess  knowledge. Truthfully  and  equitably,  He  is  the absolute  god;  there  is  no  god  but He, the Almighty, Most Wise.</p>
</blockquote>

<blockquote data-verse="4:166" data-language="en">
<p lang="en"><strong>4:166</strong> — But  GOD  bears  witness  concerning what  He  has  revealed  to  you;  He  has revealed  it  with  His  knowledge.  And the  angels  bear  witness  as  well,  but GOD suffices as witness.</p>
</blockquote>

### Reflection on the Verse

While investigating angelic tasks, I cannot pass over 3:18 as merely another piece of information. Angels are named among those who bear witness to GOD’s oneness. Those possessing knowledge also appear in the verse, while truth and justice are not detached from that witness. An inquiry into unseen tasks eventually touches the question of what a human being bears witness to.

In 4:166, revelation’s relationship with GOD’s knowledge, the witness of angels, and GOD’s sufficiency as a witness appear together. The angels’ witness is real, but it is not described as indispensable assistance that fills a deficiency in GOD’s knowledge. The presence of witnesses does not mean that He is insufficient alone.

This distinction also helps me consider the task of recording. I cannot claim to know every detail of why recorders exist. Yet neither can I conclude from these verses that GOD needs independent informants in order to learn what has happened. The existence of assigned tasks and the existence of need are not the same concept.

My own witness becomes something heavier than a declaration of affiliation. Can I claim to defend GOD’s oneness while lying in favor of my own group? If I deliberately give an incomplete account of a truth because it is inconvenient, what am I doing to the witness I claim to carry? The emphasis on justice in 3:18 does not let me separate these questions.

Learning about angels should not mean only learning who has been assigned where. I should also examine how far the truth to which they bear witness changes my own understanding of knowledge and justice.

**Verse references:** 3:18; 4:166.

## 11. Those Who Ask Forgiveness: Mercy in the Unseen World

### Verse

<blockquote data-verse="40:7" data-language="en">
<p lang="en"><strong>40:7</strong> — Those  who  serve  the  throne  and  all those  around  it  glorify  and  praise their  Lord,  and  believe  in  Him.  And they  ask  forgiveness  for  those  who believe:  “Our  Lord,  Your  mercy and Your knowledge encompass all things.  Forgive  those  who  repent and  follow  Your  path,  and  spare them the retribution of Hell.</p>
</blockquote>

<blockquote data-verse="40:8" data-language="en">
<p lang="en"><strong>40:8</strong> — “Our  Lord,  and  admit  them  into  the gardens  of  Eden  that  You  promised for  them  and  for  the  righteous among  their  parents,  spouses,  and children.  You  are  the  Almighty, Most Wise.</p>
</blockquote>

<blockquote data-verse="40:9" data-language="en">
<p lang="en"><strong>40:9</strong> — “And  protect  them  from  falling  in sin.  Whomever  You  protect  from falling  in  sin,  on  that  day,  has  attained  mercy  from  You.  This  is  the greatest triumph.”</p>
</blockquote>

<blockquote data-verse="42:5" data-language="en">
<p lang="en"><strong>42:5</strong> — The  heavens  above  them  almost shatter, out of reverence for Him, and the angels praise and glorify their Lord, and they ask forgiveness for those on earth. Absolutely, GOD is the Forgiver, Most Merciful.</p>
</blockquote>

### Reflection on the Verse

The language of 40:7–9 does not leave my thinking about angels confined to monitoring and enforcement. Forgiveness is sought for believers; repentance, a right direction, families, and protection from evil appear within the same prayer. Mercy and knowledge are not separated. Human beings are not described as enclosed by their mistakes alone.

In 42:5, asking forgiveness for those on earth further opens this horizon of mercy. The majesty of the heavens and the vulnerability of earthly life meet in a single verse. The angels’ praise of GOD is not presented as a remote activity indifferent to human beings.

Yet here too I must distinguish those who pray from the One who forgives. Asking for forgiveness does not give angels ownership of forgiveness. In 40:7–9, the address is to our Lord. At the end of 42:5, forgiveness and mercy belong to GOD. Prayer does not become an office that issues judgment independently.

These verses make me consider how to be honest with myself without becoming merciless toward others. I can be quick to notice someone’s wrongdoing and slow to wish for that person’s good return. When the mistake is mine, I seek forgiveness; when it belongs to someone else, I can want that person to remain defined by the mistake. Reading the angels’ prayer brings me face to face with this double standard.

Still, I cannot derive an automatic promise of forgiveness detached from conduct. In 40:7, those who repent and follow the path are specifically mentioned. Hope is not an alternative to responsibility; it reminds me that returning is not meaningless.

**Verse references:** 40:7; 40:8; 40:9; 42:5.

## 12. Allies: Not Being Alone While Passing Through Fear

### Verse

<blockquote data-verse="41:30" data-language="en">
<p lang="en"><strong>41:30</strong> — Those who proclaim: “Our Lord is GOD,” then lead a righteous life, the angels  descend  upon  them:  “You shall have no fear, nor shall you grieve. Rejoice in the good news that Paradise has been reserved for you.</p>
</blockquote>

<blockquote data-verse="41:31" data-language="en">
<p lang="en"><strong>41:31</strong> — “We  are  your  allies  in  this  life,  and in  the  Hereafter.  You  will  have  in  it anything  you  wish  for;  you  will  have anything you want.</p>
</blockquote>

<blockquote data-verse="41:32" data-language="en">
<p lang="en"><strong>41:32</strong> — “(Such  is  your)  ultimate  abode, from a Forgiver, Most Merciful.”</p>
</blockquote>

<blockquote data-verse="33:43" data-language="en">
<p lang="en"><strong>33:43</strong> — He  is  the  One  who  helps  you,  together  with  His  angels,  to  lead  you out  of  darkness  into  the  light.  He  is Most  Merciful  towards  the  believers.</p>
</blockquote>

### Reflection on the Verse

In 41:30–32, the address is associated with people who do not leave belief as a sentence alone. Their proclamation of their Lord is followed by righteous living. The angels’ good news is not presented as a consolation service independent of that direction. There is a relationship between what is said and what is lived.

Reading about angelic alliance can awaken a deep sense of nearness. But a verse awakening that feeling is not the same as my being able to identify the source of every nearness I feel. These passages do not give me a criterion for saying that every intuition, dream, or inner conversation comes from an angel. Trusting the promised support does not require me to grant myself the authority of an unverifiable message.

In 33:43, the direction of assistance is connected with coming out of darkness into light. It is GOD who helps, together with His angels. In 41:32, the ultimate dwelling is also connected with a Forgiver, Most Merciful. I should not forget these endings and turn alliance into independent salvation.

As I read, I ask myself: do I seek support only so that my fears will settle, or do I also want the direction of my life to become right? Sometimes I only want relief; I do not want to change the conduct that troubles me. The connection between good news and righteous living disturbs that easy position.

May the nearness described here awaken in me not a curiosity about summoning unseen beings, but a desire to continue turning toward GOD in the way I live. Considered this way, my curiosity about the unseen stops being an escape from my visible conduct.

**Verse references:** 41:30; 41:31; 41:32; 33:43.

## 13. The Task of Death: The Final Return Is to the Lord

### Verse

<blockquote data-verse="32:11" data-language="en">
<p lang="en"><strong>32:11</strong> — Say,  “You  will  be  put  to  death  by the  angel  in  whose  charge  you  are placed,  then  to  your  Lord  you  will be returned.”</p>
</blockquote>

<blockquote data-verse="16:32" data-language="en">
<p lang="en"><strong>16:32</strong> — The angels terminate their lives in a  state  of  righteousness,  saying, “Peace be upon you. Enter Paradise (now) as a reward for your works.”*</p>
</blockquote>

### Reflection on the Verse

In 32:11, the existence of an angel appointed to the task of death does not change the end of the sentence: the return is to the Lord. Curiosity about the name or form of the appointed bearer should not eclipse this direction. The one who carries out a task of death is not the same as the ultimate owner of my life.

In 16:32, death is described together with peace and news of Paradise addressed to those who are in a state of righteousness. If I turn this entirely into a frightening scene, I lose the good news in the verse. The task of death is connected here with human deeds and the destination to be encountered.

I cannot therefore assess someone’s final moments from the outside and announce that I know that person’s ultimate fate. These verses do not supply a method for decoding the appearance of every last breath and declaring a final judgment. Telling someone who has lost a loved one a certain unseen story based on an observed detail may seem as easy as offering consolation. Its ease does not make it knowledge.

The question I turn toward myself is simpler and heavier: while considering the inevitability of return, what am I still postponing today? An apology, the correction of an injustice, a truth that needs to be spoken? Learning about the angel of death should not coexist comfortably with endlessly delaying my own responsibilities.

The point on which my attention should ultimately rest is not living in fear of the bearer of death, but working toward righteousness because I take returning to my Lord seriously. It is within that seriousness that I also hear the announcement of peace.

**Verse references:** 32:11; 16:32.

## 14. The Guardians of Hell: Severity Is Not Arbitrariness

### Verse

<blockquote data-verse="66:6" data-language="en">
<p lang="en"><strong>66:6</strong> — O you who believe, protect yourselves and your families from the Hellfire whose fuel is people and rocks. Guarding it are stern  and powerful angels who never disobey GOD; they do whatever they are commanded to do.</p>
</blockquote>

<blockquote data-verse="74:30" data-language="en">
<p lang="en"><strong>74:30</strong> — Over it is nineteen.*</p>
</blockquote>

<blockquote data-verse="74:31" data-language="en">
<p lang="en"><strong>74:31</strong> — We appointed angels to be guardians of Hell, and we assigned their number (19) (1) to disturb the disbelievers, (2)  to  convince the  Christians and Jews (that this is a divine scripture), (3) to  strengthen  the  faith  of  the faithful, (4) to  remove  all  traces  of  doubt from the hearts of Christians, Jews, as well as the believers, and (5)  to  expose  those  who  harbor doubt in their hearts, and the disbelievers; they will say, “What did GOD mean by this allegory?” GOD thus  sends  astray  whomever  He wills,  and  guides  whomever  He wills. None knows the soldiers of your Lord except He. This is a reminder for the people.</p>
</blockquote>

### Reflection on the Verse

The strength and sternness of the angels described in 66:6 are not separated from their obedience to command. The verse does not portray independent beings acting to gratify their own anger. At the same time, it would be wrong to soften the warning into a merely pleasant symbol. The seriousness of the text must remain.

The initial address is to human beings: to take responsibility for themselves and their families. This warning does not authorize disproportionate pressure within a family or a heavenly warrant for my own desire to punish. The task disclosed about the guardians of Hell is not permission for me to place my anger in that office.

The number in 74:30 and the explanation in 74:31 should also be considered together. Nineteen is part of the account, followed by an explanation of its relationship with different recipients. Yet the same verse also states that no one except the Lord knows His soldiers. From a particular number being given, I cannot claim to possess the number of all angels.

The limit awakens an interesting thought in me: something I can count should not make me forget the whole I cannot count. Knowing one detail does not mean I have completed an exhaustive map of the unseen world. The verse’s explanation is extensive; my desire to complete every detail can be more extensive still.

The part I reserve for myself is not to construct a regime of fear. It is to take the warning seriously for myself, rather than enlarge it to gain power over others. Even while investigating the severity of a task, I should not forget who assigned it.

**Verse references:** 66:6; 74:30; 74:31.

## 15. Peace at Every Door and Equitable Judgment

### Verse

<blockquote data-verse="13:23" data-language="en">
<p lang="en"><strong>13:23</strong> — They  enter  the  gardens  of  Eden, together  with  the  righteous  among their  parents,  their  spouses,  and their  children.  The  angels  will  enter to them from every door.</p>
</blockquote>

<blockquote data-verse="13:24" data-language="en">
<p lang="en"><strong>13:24</strong> — “Peace  be  upon  you,  because  you steadfastly  persevered.  What  a  joyous destiny.”</p>
</blockquote>

<blockquote data-verse="39:75" data-language="en">
<p lang="en"><strong>39:75</strong> — You  will  see  the  angels  floating around  the  throne,  glorifying  and praising  their  Lord.  After  the  equitable  judgment  is  issued  to  all,  it  will be  proclaimed:  “Praise  be  to  GOD, Lord of the universe.”</p>
</blockquote>

### Reflection on the Verse

In 13:23–24, the encounter with angels is described through peace and steadfast perseverance. As doors open in this scene, attention turns toward the joy of arrival. When I imagine the unseen world of angels solely as a source of anxiety, I do not adequately hear this greeting.

The reference to family is not presented as unconditional privilege by ancestry; the qualification of righteousness remains. For me, the joy here is deeper than simply wanting to be among familiar people. It involves wishing for the good of those I love, for their growth in righteousness, and for my own life to move in that direction too.

In 39:75, equitable judgment and praise meet while angels glorify their Lord. Their conjunction matters. Imagining only a reassuring ending without remembering justice, or considering only the weight of judgment without mercy and praise, narrows one side of the account.

These verses do not require me to turn the gates of Paradise into a detailed plan in my mind. I do not have the knowledge to describe each gate’s shape, the arrangement of its attendants, or every sensory detail of the encounter. Yet its direction is clear enough: peace, perseverance, righteousness, and praise of GOD.

I cannot help thinking about the doors in my own life. When I enter a place, do I bring peace or tension? Beyond wanting goodness from those who receive me, can I receive another person well? This is not an identification of tasks, but an ethical self-examination awakened by that greeting.

Longing for peace at the destination should not become an excuse to dismiss the harm I cause along the way.

**Verse references:** 13:23; 13:24; 39:75.

## 16. Believing in Angels Does Not Mean Taking Them as Lords

### Verse

<blockquote data-verse="3:80" data-language="en">
<p lang="en"><strong>3:80</strong> — Nor would he command you to idolize the angels and the prophets as lords. Would he exhort you to disbelieve after becoming submitters? </p>
</blockquote>

<blockquote data-verse="53:26" data-language="en">
<p lang="en"><strong>53:26</strong> — Not even the angels in heaven possess authority to intercede. The only ones permitted by GOD are those who act in accordance with His will and His approval.</p>
</blockquote>

<blockquote data-verse="34:40" data-language="en">
<p lang="en"><strong>34:40</strong> — On  the  day  when  He  summons  them all,  He  will  say  to  the  angels,  “Did these people worship you?”</p>
</blockquote>

<blockquote data-verse="34:41" data-language="en">
<p lang="en"><strong>34:41</strong> — They  will  answer,  “Be  You  glorified.  You  are  our  Lord  and  Master, not them. Instead, they were worshiping  the  jinns;  most  of  them  were believers therein.”</p>
</blockquote>

### Reflection on the Verse

Here I encounter the sharpest boundary concerning monotheism in discussing the tasks of angels. 3:80 rejects the idolization of angels and prophets as lords. 53:26 states that even the angels in heaven do not possess independent authority over intercession. If I remove the emphasis on permission and approval, I reshape the sentence to suit my expectations.

In 34:40–41, the question put to the angels concerning worship, and their answer, make this boundary clearer still. Acknowledging their servanthood and making them recipients of worship are not the same act. While claiming to honor them, I may assign them a station they do not claim for themselves.

The danger for me is not limited to standing before a statue. I may imagine myself too distant to reach GOD and place an indispensable gatekeeper before Him. Or someone may tell me that they possess such access. These verses do not present the tasks attributed to angels as a privilege that human beings can sell or transfer.

That angels ask for forgiveness does not let me build a mechanism in which I pray to them to change GOD’s judgment. The direction of the prayer in 40:7–9 and the limit of permission in 53:26 must be held together. The multiplicity of tasks does not mean that ultimate authority has been divided.

If learning more about angels makes my turning toward GOD more circuitous, I may have lost the direction somewhere. What I want to carry from this investigation is not a faith dependent on more intermediaries, but a clarity that recognizes those appointed while directing my worship to GOD alone.

**Verse references:** 3:80; 53:26; 34:40; 34:41; 40:7; 40:8; 40:9.

## 17. Arabic Word Roots: Appointment, Recording, and Forgiveness

### Verse

<blockquote data-verse="35:1" data-language="en">
<p lang="en"><strong>35:1</strong> — Praise be to GOD, Initiator of the heavens  and  the  earth,  and  appointer of the angels to be messengers  with  wings—two,  three,  and four (wings). He increases the creation  as  He  wills.  GOD  is  Omnipotent.</p>
</blockquote>

<blockquote data-verse="82:10" data-language="en">
<p lang="en"><strong>82:10</strong> — Oblivious to the fact that there are (invisible) keepers around you.</p>
</blockquote>

<blockquote data-verse="82:11" data-language="en">
<p lang="en"><strong>82:11</strong> — They are honest recorders.</p>
</blockquote>

<blockquote data-verse="42:5" data-language="en">
<p lang="en"><strong>42:5</strong> — The  heavens  above  them  almost shatter, out of reverence for Him, and the angels praise and glorify their Lord, and they ask forgiveness for those on earth. Absolutely, GOD is the Forgiver, Most Merciful.</p>
</blockquote>

### Reflection on the Verse

Looking closely at words helps me notice the distinctions in the account more carefully. Yet filling every verse with all the associations of a root can turn word study into arbitrary interpretation. Here I consider the roots not to produce a new translation, but to follow the language of tasks within these verses.

**ف ط ر — f-ṭ-r:** The form *fāṭir* in 35:1 is rendered “Initiator” in Rashad Khalifa’s Authorized English Translation and “Başlatan” in the Turkish translation of the Authorized English Translation. Grammatically it is an active participial form. The distinction within the verse between the Initiator and those appointed as messengers matters when considering the source of a task. <a href="https://corpus.quran.com/qurandictionary.jsp?q=fTr" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Root and form: f-ṭ-r.</a>

**ر س ل — r-s-l:** *Rusulan* in 35:1 is a plural form referring to messengers. The root’s relationship with sending and messengership can be considered alongside the choosing of messengers from angels and human beings in 22:75. The use of the same task word does not establish that angels and humans are the same kind of being. <a href="https://corpus.quran.com/qurandictionary.jsp?q=rsl" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Root and form: r-s-l.</a>

**ح ف ظ — ḥ-f-ẓ:** *Ḥāfiẓīn* in 82:10 and *yaḥfaẓūnahu* in 13:11 are forms in the field of guarding and protecting. A shared root does not show that every detail of the task is identical in every context, or that protection means unlimited immunity. The relationship with command must remain in view. <a href="https://corpus.quran.com/qurandictionary.jsp?q=HfZ" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Root and form: ḥ-f-ẓ.</a>

**ك ت ب — k-t-b:** *Kātibīn* in 82:11 belongs to the field of writing and recording. The task of recording is not the same as bringing a deed into existence or assuming moral responsibility for that deed. <a href="https://corpus.quran.com/qurandictionary.jsp?q=ktb" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Root and form: k-t-b.</a>

**س ب ح — s-b-ḥ:** *Yusabbiḥūna* in 42:5 is the verb associated with glorification. In the verse, glorification is directed to the Lord. I cannot import other uses of the root into this context to construct a theory about angelic movement or physical composition. <a href="https://corpus.quran.com/qurandictionary.jsp?q=sbH" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Root and form: s-b-ḥ.</a>

**غ ف ر — gh-f-r:** *Yastaghfirūna* in the same verse is a form associated with asking for forgiveness. Distinguishing the angels who ask from GOD who forgives is not merely a conceptual precaution; the distinction also appears in the way the sentence distributes the actions. <a href="https://corpus.quran.com/qurandictionary.jsp?q=gfr" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Root and form: gh-f-r.</a>

This inquiry leaves me with no code that unlocks the unseen world. It does help me distinguish actions I sometimes confuse: initiating, sending, guarding, recording, glorifying, and asking forgiveness are not the same action.

**Verse references:** 35:1; 82:10; 82:11; 42:5; 22:75; 13:11.

## 18. Other Claims

### Verse

<blockquote data-verse="2:98" data-language="en">
<p lang="en"><strong>2:98</strong> — Anyone  who  opposes  GOD,  and His angels, and His messengers, and Gabriel and Michael, should know that GOD opposes the disbelievers.</p>
</blockquote>

<blockquote data-verse="43:19" data-language="en">
<p lang="en"><strong>43:19</strong> — They  claimed  that  the  angels,  who are  servants  of  the  Most  Gracious, are  females!  Have  they  witnessed their  creation?  Their  claims  are recorded, and they will be asked.</p>
</blockquote>

<blockquote data-verse="18:50" data-language="en">
<p lang="en"><strong>18:50</strong> — We said to the angels, “Fall prostrate before Adam.” They fell prostrate, except Satan. He became a jinn, for he disobeyed the order of His Lord.* Will you choose him and his descendants as lords instead of Me, even though they are your enemies? What a miserable substitute!</p>
</blockquote>

### Reflection on the Verse

Familiar angelic names and the schemes of tasks associated with those names should not be treated as having the same source status. 2:97 describes Gabriel’s role in bringing revelation; 2:98 also names Michael. But the detailed assignment of rain and provision to Michael is not stated in that second verse. <a href="https://islamansiklopedisi.org.tr/mikail" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">The TDV Encyclopedia of Islam’s entry on Michael</a> presents related reports and interpretations. Their familiarity does not require their insertion into the verse itself.

The name Azrael used for the angel of death, and the name Israfil associated with the trumpet, also require separate consideration. The entries on <a href="https://islamansiklopedisi.org.tr/azrail" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Azrael</a> and <a href="https://islamansiklopedisi.org.tr/israfil" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Israfil</a> examine the place of these names in transmitted reports. We can distinguish the description of a task in 32:11 from the proper names and details supplied by later accounts. This is not a single judgment on every report, but a distinction about where each claim is made.

Creation from light, the interpretation of wings, and detailed hierarchies among beings have also been debated in interpretive history. <a href="https://islamansiklopedisi.org.tr/melek#2-islam-inancinda-melek" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">The TDV entry on angels</a> records different explanations. Equating the wings entirely with human anatomy, or declaring them certainly nothing but a symbol of power, are readings that require further grounds. None of these views replaces the text of 35:1 with which this essay began.

Concerning Satan’s relationship with angels, a familiar account should not be silently inserted into the translation either. In 18:50, Rashad Khalifa’s Authorized English Translation contains the wording “He became a jinn”. Khalifa’s explanatory note on the verse relates the classification of angels, jinns, and humans to the great feud in the heavenly society. This is the interpretive framework of the note; the verse and its accompanying explanation are not the same text. <a href="https://kuranteyit.com/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">18:50 and its explanatory note: KuranTeyit.</a>

Likewise, the explanatory note on 2:3 connects plural expression with the participation of other entities, usually angels. I cannot turn that approach into a claim that I know every participant in every plural expression throughout the Quran. <a href="https://kuranteyit.com/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Explanatory note on 2:3: KuranTeyit.</a>

Finally, any approach claiming to explain the essence of angels definitively through energy, frequency, or dimensions must provide grounds for that identification. These verses do not supply those measurements. The questioning of an unseen-world claim in terms of witnessing in 43:19 is especially noteworthy here. A claim’s familiarity or striking character does not establish that it rests on knowledge.

**Verse references:** 2:98; 43:19; 18:50; 2:97; 32:11; 35:1; 2:3.

## 19. Conclusion

### Verse

<blockquote data-verse="35:2" data-language="en">
<p lang="en"><strong>35:2</strong> — When  GOD  showers  the  people with mercy, no force can stop it. And if He withholds it, no force, other than He, can send it. He is the Almighty, Most Wise.</p>
</blockquote>

<blockquote data-verse="3:18" data-language="en">
<p lang="en"><strong>3:18</strong> — GOD  bears  witness  that  there  is  no god except He, and so do the angels and  those  who  possess  knowledge. Truthfully  and  equitably,  He  is  the absolute  god;  there  is  no  god  but He, the Almighty, Most Wise.</p>
</blockquote>

### Reflection on the Verse

As I consider the unseen world of angels, I no longer ask only where or how they exist. A closer question emerges: in what direction does learning about them take my relationship with GOD?

In 35:1, I encountered those who are appointed; in 35:2, the source of mercy and the inability of any independent force to take its place. Angels who carry revelation, bring good news, guard, record, ask forgiveness, carry out the task of death, and receive human beings are described through different tasks. Yet this diversity is not a multitude of competing sovereignties. The tasks differ; their dependence on GOD remains.

The angels’ acknowledgment of limited knowledge and GOD’s wisdom makes me consider how honestly I admit my own ignorance. Their recording makes me examine my own words before seeking to expose someone else’s secrets. Their asking forgiveness for believers reminds me not to lose mercy while defending truth. Their greetings of peace remind me that responsibility is not sustained by fear alone.

3:18 gathers the direction of this inquiry once more. The oneness to which angels bear witness and my own witness are not disconnected matters. If I claim to bear witness to GOD’s oneness, I should be honest toward knowledge, just toward people, and attentive to the limits of my authority. Discussing unseen tasks does not lighten this responsibility in my visible life.

I can trust without claiming to know every detail. I do not need to imagine that I can control unseen beings in order to hope for mercy. Nor do I need to give angels GOD’s place in order to acknowledge their tasks.

The life I want to reach is not one dependent on ever more hidden powers. It is a life in which, as I turn toward GOD, I do not turn servants into lords, confuse what I know with what I do not, or abandon responsibility for my deeds while hoping for mercy.

While investigating the world of angels, I do not want to forget my own servanthood.
