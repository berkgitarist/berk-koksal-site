# Forgetting GOD, Forgetting Ourselves: The Spiritual Cost of Memory Loss

**Author:** Berk KÖKSAL  
**Date:** September 19, 2026  
**Place:** Mersin

The central purpose of this article is to contemplate the verses together with other related verses, and to consider sincerely and deeply what bearing witness to the oneness of GOD means in human life in terms of knowledge, justice, truthfulness, and submission.

Peace. Peace be upon you.

Forgetting seems ordinary. We forget a name, an appointment, a task, or something we intended to say. Yet the Quran presents some forms of forgetting as more than a failure of memory. Forgetting can become a spiritual direction that changes how a person understands himself, his responsibilities, and his return.

59:19 makes a startling connection: those who forgot GOD were made to forget themselves. That means remembrance of GOD is not a decorative addition to an otherwise complete human identity. Something about understanding the self is tied to remembering the One who created it.

The verse does not give us permission to invent a psychological mechanism beyond the text. But it does force a question: if I forget where I came from, to whom I return, and before whom I am accountable, how accurately can I understand who I am?

## 1. How Can Forgetting GOD Become Forgetting Ourselves?

### Verse

> **59:19**
>
> وَلَا تَكُونُوا۟ كَٱلَّذِينَ نَسُوا۟ ٱللَّهَ فَأَنسَىٰهُمْ أَنفُسَهُمْ أُو۟لَٰٓئِكَ هُمُ ٱلْفَٰسِقُونَ
>
> Do  not  be  like  those  who  forgot GOD,  so  He  made  them  forget themselves. These are the wicked.
>
> TANRI’yı unutanlar gibi olmayın; bunun üzerine O da onlara kendilerini unutturdu. İşte bunlar kötülerdir.

Source: <a href="https://kuranteyit.com/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">kuranteyit.com</a>

### Reflection on the Verse

59:19 ties two forms of forgetting together: forgetting GOD and forgetting oneself. The verse therefore breaks the idea that a person can understand the self perfectly while treating GOD as irrelevant.

The precise psychology is not explained, so certainty beyond the verse would be irresponsible. Yet the surrounding context helps. 59:18 tells every soul to examine what it has sent ahead for tomorrow. Self-forgetting can therefore be contemplated in relation to losing sight of direction, accountability, and what one is becoming through one’s own deeds.

Remembering GOD does not erase individuality. It may instead place the self in its true scale: created, limited, responsible, and returning.

**Verse references:** 59:19.

## 2. Remembrance Begins With Examining What the Self Sends Ahead

### Verse

> **59:18** — O  you  who  believe,  you  shall  reverence  GOD,  and  let  every  soul  examine  what  it  has  sent  ahead  for tomorrow.  You  shall  reverence GOD;  GOD  is  fully  Cognizant  of everything you do.

### Reflection on the Verse

59:18 comes immediately before 59:19. First every soul is told to examine what it has sent ahead for tomorrow; then comes the warning not to become like those who forgot GOD and were made to forget themselves.

This sequence makes remembrance practical. It is not only an inner feeling or a repeated word. It includes moral review.

In my earlier article *What Did You Send Ahead for Tomorrow?*, I reflected on this verse as a call to responsibility. Here the same verse becomes a form of memory: I remember myself by looking honestly at what my life is producing.

A person who never examines his direction can remain busy while gradually becoming a stranger to his own stated values.

**Verse references:** 59:18-19.

## 3. If a Mountain Would Tremble, How Does a Human Heart Become Numb?

### Verse

> **59:21** — If we revealed this Quran to a mountain, you would see it trembling, crumbling,  out  of  reverence  for GOD. We cite these examples for the people, that they may reflect.

### Reflection on the Verse

59:21 presents the Quran as something that, if revealed to a mountain, would make it tremble and crumble out of reverence for GOD. The examples are given so that people may reflect.

Forgetting is therefore not always the absence of information. A person may know verses, repeat them, and even teach them, while allowing their moral force to disappear from daily life.

In *The Heavy Message*, I reflected on the weight of revelation. Here the same weight becomes the opposite of spiritual forgetfulness. Revelation truly remembered should disturb complacency, expose arrogance, and return the person to reflection.

Memory is not only storage. It is allowing truth to remain active.

**Verse references:** 59:21.

## 4. When Worldly Life Occupies the Whole Mind

### Verse

> **7:51** — Those who do not take their religion seriously,  and  are  totally preoccupied with this worldly life, we forget them on that day, because they forgot that day, and because they spurned our revelations.

### Reflection on the Verse

7:51 describes people whose minds are totally preoccupied with worldly life and who forgot the Day. The problem is not the existence of worldly activity. The problem is total occupation.

Work, family, learning, provision, rest, and ordinary pleasure all belong to life. But when the temporary world consumes the entire field of attention, ultimate return can disappear from practical consciousness.

One does not need to verbally deny GOD in order to live as though He were absent. Constant attention to status, money, entertainment, fear, or social approval can gradually make remembrance irrelevant to actual decisions.

Forgetting can therefore be a problem of attention before it becomes a problem of doctrine.

**Verse references:** 7:51.

## 5. The Ethical Signs of Forgetting GOD

### Verse

> **9:67** — The  hypocrite  men  and  the  hypocrite  women  belong  with  each other  − they advocate evil  and prohibit  righteousness,  and  they  are stingy. They forgot GOD, so He forgot them. The hypocrites are truly wicked.

### Reflection on the Verse

9:67 places forgetting GOD beside concrete behavior: advocating evil, prohibiting righteousness, and stinginess. That means remembrance is not only verbal.

A person may frequently use the name of GOD and still organize his life around injustice or selfishness. The verse does not let remembrance become a slogan detached from conduct.

Forgetting GOD can make the self appear absolute: my interest, my wealth, my group, my fear. Remembrance restores another center and another measure.

Tawhid is therefore remembered not only with the tongue but in what we permit, prevent, give, and withhold.

**Verse references:** 9:67.

## 6. Adam Forgot

### Verse

> **20:115** — We  tested  Adam  in  the  past,  but  he forgot, and we found him indecisive.

### Reflection on the Verse

20:115 shows forgetting inside the human story at a very early point: Adam forgot. That makes forgetfulness part of human vulnerability.

Yet the Quran’s many commands to remember show that forgetfulness is not an excuse for permanent surrender. A weakness can be acknowledged without being worshiped.

The verse also joins forgetting with indecisiveness. That suggests a powerful reflection: remembering purpose, promise, and destination may strengthen moral resolve.

Many failures begin not with a grand ideological rebellion but with a small forgotten principle—justice during anger, gratitude during success, humility during praise.

**Verse references:** 20:115.

## 7. What Should Be Done After Forgetting?

### Verse

> **18:24** — without  saying,  “GOD  willing.”*  If you forget to do this, you must immediately  remember  your  Lord  and say, “May my Lord guide me to do better next time.”

### Reflection on the Verse

18:24 gives a hopeful response to ordinary forgetting: remember your Lord immediately and ask for guidance to do better next time.

This means spiritual life is not built on flawless memory. A person may forget. What matters is the direction taken when the forgetting becomes visible.

The prayer in the verse looks forward. Remembrance is not merely feeling guilty about the past; it asks for better conduct in the future.

Forgetting can become a turning point when it leads back to GOD.

**Verse references:** 18:24.

## 8. “We Will Recite to You; Do Not Forget”

### Verse

> **87:6** — We will recite to you; do not forget. 

### Reflection on the Verse

87:6 is brief but powerful. Revelation and remembrance are brought together: recitation should not disappear into forgetfulness.

The verse should not be stretched into claims it does not make, but it does make the preservation of what is recited significant.

For my own reflection, this raises a practical question: does a verse remain only in memory, or does it remain in conduct? It is possible to remember words while forgetting their demand.

Returning to the Quran repeatedly is therefore not unnecessary repetition. Human attention leaks. Remembrance needs renewal.

**Verse references:** 87:6.

## 9. Worship as Remembrance

### Verse

> **20:14** — “I  am  GOD;  there  is  no  other  god beside  Me.  You  shall  worship  Me alone,  and  observe  the  Contact Prayers (Salat) to remember Me.

### Reflection on the Verse

20:14 joins tawhid, worship, and remembrance. GOD declares that there is no other god beside Him, commands worship of Him alone, and connects the Contact Prayers with remembering Him.

This gives worship a function inside human attention. The day fragments the mind. Prayer repeatedly returns the person to the center.

If prayer becomes purely mechanical, the body may perform while the mind remains elsewhere. The words “to remember Me” call the act back to its purpose.

Tawhid is therefore not merely a proposition believed once. It is a truth repeatedly remembered against the many things that compete for the center of life.

**Verse references:** 20:14.

## 10. Hearts Rejoice in Remembering GOD

### Verse

> **13:28** — They  are  the  ones  whose  hearts rejoice  in  remembering  GOD.  Absolutely,  by  remembering  GOD,  the hearts rejoice.

### Reflection on the Verse

13:28 connects remembrance with the condition of the heart. Remembering GOD is described as a source of rejoicing.

That prevents spiritual memory from becoming only fear. To remember GOD is also to remember the real Lord, the source of mercy, power, provision, and return.

A modern person can be constantly stimulated and still internally scattered. More information does not necessarily produce more remembrance. Attention can be full while the heart remains directionless.

13:28 asks what truly centers the heart.

**Verse references:** 13:28.

## 11. “Remember Me, That I May Remember You”

### Verse

> **2:152** — You  shall  remember  Me,  that  I  may remember  you,  and  be  thankful  to Me; do not be unappreciative.

### Reflection on the Verse

2:152 places remembrance inside relationship. Human beings remember GOD; GOD says He will remember them. The same verse joins remembrance with appreciation.

This connection with gratitude is important. When the source of a blessing is forgotten, the blessing begins to feel like an entitlement. Remembering restores dependence without denying human effort.

I can work hard and still recognize that ability, time, health, opportunity, and result are not under my absolute ownership.

Gratitude may therefore be one of the moral fruits of remembrance.

**Verse references:** 2:152.

## 12. Money and Children Can Become Distractions

### Verse

> **63:9** — O  you  who  believe,  do  not  be  distracted  by  your  money  and  your children  from  remembering  GOD. Those who do this are the losers. 

### Reflection on the Verse

63:9 names two deeply important parts of human life: wealth and children. They are not condemned in themselves. The warning is against allowing them to distract from remembering GOD.

This is realistic. People work for family, plan for the future, and seek security. But something valuable can become spiritually dangerous when it occupies the center.

Money can begin to look like the final source of safety. Children can become the continuation through which a person seeks permanence. Remembrance places both loves inside a larger truth.

Love, provide, and plan—but do not turn the gift into the Lord.

**Verse references:** 63:9.

## 13. Remembering GOD in the Middle of Business

### Verse

> **24:37** — People  who  are  not  distracted  by business  or  trade  from  commemorating  GOD;  they  observe  the  Contact  Prayers  (Salat),  and  give  the obligatory charity (Zakat), and they are  conscious  of  the  day  when  the minds and the eyes will be horrified.

### Reflection on the Verse

24:37 shows that remembrance is not escape from economic life. Business and trade continue, but they do not distract these people from remembering GOD, observing the Contact Prayers, giving Zakat, and remaining conscious of the Day.

This balance is important. Spirituality is not defined here as abandoning worldly responsibility. It is refusing to let responsibility become forgetfulness.

Commerce itself becomes an ethical field. Earning, giving, prayer, and awareness of judgment remain connected.

The remembering person remembers in the marketplace as well as in private worship.

**Verse references:** 24:37.

## 14. Mockery Can Make a Person Forget GOD

### Verse

> **23:110** — “But  you  mocked  and  ridiculed them,  to  the  extent  that  you  forgot Me. You used to laugh at them.

### Reflection on the Verse

23:110 gives an unexpected picture of forgetfulness: people became so absorbed in mocking and ridiculing others that they forgot GOD.

This exposes the moral direction of attention. A person who constantly examines other people’s faults can lose sight of his own accountability.

Ridicule creates a feeling of superiority. Remembrance reverses it: I also will answer; I also can be wrong; I also depend on mercy.

What occupies attention gradually shapes the self.

**Verse references:** 23:110; 53:32.

## 15. Forgetting the Day Has Consequences

### Verse

> **32:14** — Taste  the  consequences  of  your  forgetting  this  day;  now  we  forget  you. You  have  incurred  eternal  retribution in return for your own works.

### Reflection on the Verse

32:14 treats forgetting the Day as something whose consequences are tasted. This cannot be reduced to an ordinary lapse of memory, because the verse ties the outcome to people’s own works.

Remembering judgment does not require living in constant panic. It can mean refusing to treat today’s actions as though they vanish without significance.

Private honesty, restraint from unlawful gain, keeping a promise, apologizing, or refusing injustice become connected to a larger horizon.

The Day gives weight to the present.

**Verse references:** 32:14.

## 16. Forgetting the Meeting

### Verse

> **45:34** — It  will  be  proclaimed:  “Today  we forget you, just as you forgot the meeting of this day. Your abode is the hellfire, and you will have no helpers.

### Reflection on the Verse

45:34 speaks of forgetting “the meeting of this day.” The word “meeting” makes the future personal. Death is not merely disappearance; the Quranic frame includes return and judgment.

Human beings often harm their future selves for immediate comfort. Spiritual forgetfulness can work similarly: the present becomes so dominant that the final consequence loses reality.

Remembrance creates a bridge between the person I am now and the person who will stand before the result of what I did.

**Verse references:** 45:34.

## 17. GOD Does Not Forget

### Verse

> **19:64** — We  do  not  come  down  except  by  the command  of  your  Lord.  To  Him belongs  our  past,  our  future,  and everything  between  them.  Your Lord is never forgetful.
> 
> **20:52** — He  said,  “The  knowledge  thereof  is with  my  Lord  in  a  record.  My  Lord never errs, nor does He forget.”

### Reflection on the Verse

19:64 and 20:52 are essential for interpreting this theme. Human beings forget; GOD is explicitly described as not forgetful and as never erring or forgetting.

Therefore, verses that speak in the language of GOD “forgetting” people should not be flattened into human memory failure. The wider Quranic language prevents that reading.

This also strengthens the meaning of justice. Forgotten kindness, hidden wrong, and details no human remembers do not leave GOD’s knowledge.

I may forget what I did. My forgetting does not erase the record.

**Verse references:** 19:64; 20:52.

## 18. “Your Lord Never Abandoned You, Nor Did He Forget”

### Verse

> **93:3** — Your  Lord  never  abandoned  you, nor did He forget. 

### Reflection on the Verse

93:3 gives the theme of memory a deeply consoling dimension. Human beings may feel abandoned, unseen, or forgotten. The verse says the Lord did not abandon or forget.

This means spiritual remembrance is not a relationship held together only by human performance. The human side is fragile; GOD’s knowledge is not.

When I realize that I have forgotten, I can return. 18:24 already gives that movement: remember your Lord immediately and ask to do better next time.

The theology of remembrance therefore contains warning and hope together.

**Verse references:** 93:3; 18:24.

## 19. Arabic Word Roots: Forgetting, Remembering, and the Self

### Verse

> **59:19** — Do  not  be  like  those  who  forgot GOD,  so  He  made  them  forget themselves. These are the wicked.
> 
> **20:14** — “I  am  GOD;  there  is  no  other  god beside  Me.  You  shall  worship  Me alone,  and  observe  the  Contact Prayers (Salat) to remember Me.
> 
> **59:18** — O  you  who  believe,  you  shall  reverence  GOD,  and  let  every  soul  examine  what  it  has  sent  ahead  for tomorrow.  You  shall  reverence GOD;  GOD  is  fully  Cognizant  of everything you do.

### Reflection on the Verse

The Arabic field of forgetting in 59:19 is associated with the root **ن س ي (n-s-y)**. The same field appears in Adam’s forgetting in 20:115 and in the instruction of 18:24 to remember the Lord immediately after forgetting. The contexts show that not every instance of forgetting carries the same moral weight.

The field of remembering is strongly associated with **ذ ك ر (dh-k-r)**. 20:14 links the Contact Prayers to remembering GOD; 13:28 links remembrance to the heart’s rejoicing. Remembrance is therefore cognitive, devotional, and moral.

The word **ن ف س (n-f-s)**, self/soul, is also central. In 59:18 every soul examines what it has sent ahead; in 59:19 those who forgot GOD are made to forget themselves. That proximity invites reflection on memory as part of moral selfhood.

Roots do not replace context, but together they illuminate an important movement: remembering GOD helps the self remember its place, while forgetfulness can become loss of direction and responsibility.

**Verse references:** 59:18-19; 20:14; 20:115; 18:24; 13:28.

## Other Claims

Mystical, philosophical, and psychological traditions have interpreted “forgetting GOD and forgetting oneself” in many ways: alienation from one’s true nature, loss of moral consciousness, or the veiling of the spiritual self.

Those ideas can be studied, but 59:19 does not itself specify a modern psychological mechanism. The main reflection here remains with the explicit Quranic relationships: forgetting GOD is tied to forgetting oneself, while 59:18 calls the self to examine what it has sent ahead.

Likewise, contemporary theories of attention, identity, habit, and memory may provide useful comparisons, but they should not be presented as if the verse directly taught a particular scientific theory.

## Conclusion

The Quranic language of forgetting shows me that memory has a spiritual dimension. Some forgetting belongs to ordinary human weakness; 18:24 teaches immediate remembrance and correction. Other forms of forgetting become a direction of life: forgetting GOD, forgetting the meeting of the Day, becoming totally preoccupied with the world, and allowing responsibilities to disappear from consciousness.

The most unsettling statement remains 59:19: forgetting GOD can turn into forgetting oneself. If I forget creation, accountability, mortality, and return, the story I tell about myself becomes incomplete.

Yet remembrance does not require leaving ordinary life. 24:37 shows remembrance in commerce, 63:9 amid money and children, 20:14 in worship, and 13:28 in the heart. The task is not to escape the world but to live in it without losing the center.

The question I am left with is: **What occupies my mind most of the day, and does that occupation help me remember who I am before GOD—or make me forget?**

GOD knows best.
