# What Does “Right” Mean? The People of Paradise, Light, and Darkness

**Author:** Berk KÖKSAL  
**Date:** October 02, 2026  
**Place:** Mersin

The main purpose of this article is to contemplate the verses together with other relevant verses and to explore, in a sincere and deep way, what bearing witness to the oneness of GOD means for human life in terms of knowledge, justice, truthfulness, and submission.

Why does the Quran describe people of Paradise as those on the right? Is “right” simply a direction, or a sign of an accepted final state? When the believers’ light radiates “ahead of them and to their right,” does that reveal a deeper relationship between **right and light**? And can we then say **left means darkness**? This article follows those questions without claiming more than the verses allow.

## 1. Those on the Right: A Direction or an Identity?

### Verse

**56:27**

**Arabic:** وَأَصْحَٰبُ ٱلْيَمِينِ مَآ أَصْحَٰبُ ٱلْيَمِينِ

**English:** Those of the right side, will be on the right side.

**Turkish:** Sağ taraftakiler, sağ tarafta olacaklar.

Source: <a href="https://kuranteyit.com/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">kuranteyit.com</a>

### Reflection on the Verse

At first, 56:27 almost sounds like a repetition: “Those of the right side, will be on the right side.” Yet the repetition is precisely what makes me stop. The verse does not merely give a coordinate; it identifies a people.

The surrounding verses connect them with Paradise, shade, water, fruit, peace, and reward. Elsewhere the record is received in the right hand, and the believers’ light radiates in front of them and to their right. So “right” appears within a network of acceptance and a good final outcome.

Still, caution comes first. The verse does not say, “right means light.” It says “those of the right side.” The task is therefore not to force a preferred symbolism into the word, but to watch how the Quran itself places “right” beside accountability, mercy, guidance, light, and Paradise.

## 2. Three Classes: More Than Geography

### Verse

> **56:7** — You  will  be  stratified  into  three kinds.

> **56:8** — Those who deserved bliss will be in bliss.

> **56:9** — Those who deserved misery will be in misery.

### Reflection on the Verse

56:7 says humanity will be divided into three kinds. Rashad Khalifa’s authorized translation renders 56:8 as those who deserved bliss being in bliss, and 56:9 as those who deserved misery being in misery. The Arabic uses *ashab al-maymanah* and *ashab al-mash'amah*.

That is our first reason not to reduce the passage to physical positioning. The authorized translation itself foregrounds bliss and misery here, while later in the same sura 56:27 and 56:41 use explicit right-side and left-side language.

The sura therefore allows two layers to appear together: direction language and destiny language. It would be premature either to separate them completely or to declare them identical.

## 3. Right and Left as Outcomes

### Verse

> **56:38** — For those on the right side.

> **56:41** — Those  of  the  left,  will  be  on  the left.

### Reflection on the Verse

56:38 links the blessings of Paradise to those on the right side, while 56:41 introduces those on the left. The directional language sits inside two radically different outcomes.

But the direction itself is not presented as the moral cause. The people of the right are described through what they receive; the people of the left are described through the lives and denials that brought them to loss.

So “right” is better read as an eschatological sign of acceptance than as a mystical property of the ordinary compass direction.

## 4. Receiving the Record in the Right Hand: A Life Ready for Accountability

### Verse

> **69:19** — As  for  the  one  who  receives  his record  with  his  right  hand,  he  will say, “Come read my record. 

> **69:20** — “I  did  believe  that  I  was  going  to  be held accountable.”

> **69:21** — He has deserved a happy life.

> **69:22** — In an exalted Paradise.

> **69:23** — Its fruits are within reach.

> **69:24** — Eat  and  drink  happily  in  return  for your works in days past.

### Reflection on the Verse

The person who receives the record in the right hand does not hide it. He says, “Come read my record” (69:19). The next verse explains his confidence: he truly believed he would be held accountable.

The right hand is therefore joined to an interior moral fact—accountability was already real to him before the Day of Judgment. Paradise follows that consciousness.

This suggests that being “on the right” is prepared in the present by living a life one is willing to have exposed before GOD.

## 5. The Left Hand: A Life Before It Becomes a Symbol

### Verse

> **69:25** — As for him who is given his record in his left hand, he will say, “Oh, I wish I never received my record.

> **69:26** — “I wish I never knew my account.

> **69:27** — “I wish my death was eternal.

> **69:28** — “My money cannot help me.

> **69:29** — “All my power is gone.” 

> **69:33** — For he did not believe in GOD, Most Great.

> **69:34** — Nor  did  he  advocate  the  feeding  of the poor.

### Reflection on the Verse

The person given the record in the left hand wishes he had never received it. Wealth and power have disappeared as defenses. The passage then gives concrete moral causes: he did not believe in GOD, Most Great, and did not advocate feeding the poor.

This matters. He is not lost because “left” possesses an evil substance. The left-hand scene exposes the outcome of disbelief and neglected mercy.

So the Quran gives moral content before we construct symbolism. Left and darkness may converge within the same lost condition, but they are not simply the same word or concept.

## 6. Why Does 84:10 Say 'Behind His Back' Instead of 'Left'?

### Verse

> **84:7** — As  for  the  one  who  receives  his record in his right hand, 

> **84:8** — His reckoning will be easy. 

> **84:9** — He will return to his people joyfully. 

> **84:10** — As  for  the  one  who  receives  his record behind his back, 

> **84:11** — He will be ridden with remorse. 

> **84:12** — And will burn in Hell. 

### Reflection on the Verse

84:7 again connects the right hand with an easy account and joyful return. Yet the contrasting image changes: the other person receives the record from behind his back, not explicitly in the left hand.

That variation is important. If the Quran were offering nothing more than a rigid geometric code—right good, left evil—we might expect every negative scene to require the left side. It does not.

The moral reality remains; the visual image changes. That keeps us from turning the ordinary right-hand direction into a magical property.

## 7. Who Are the People of the Right? The Difficult Path Answers

### Verse

> **90:12** — Which one is the difficult path? 

> **90:13** — The freeing of slaves. 

> **90:14** — Feeding,  during  the  time  of  hardship. 

> **90:15** — Orphans who are related.

> **90:16** — Or the poor who is in need.

> **90:17** — And being one of those who believe, and  exhorting  one  another  to  be steadfast,  and  exhorting  one  another to be kind. 

> **90:18** — These have deserved happiness. 

### Reflection on the Verse

90:12-18 may be the most decisive passage for this question. It asks what the difficult path is, then answers with liberation, feeding in times of hardship, care for orphans and the poor, faith, steadfastness, and mercy. Only after these qualities does 90:18 say, “These have deserved happiness.”

The people of the right are therefore not defined by a spatial preference. Their identity is filled with ethical substance.

If I want to understand the spiritual meaning of “right,” this passage tells me to look less at abstract geometry and more at whom I free, whom I feed, whether I persevere, and whether I cultivate mercy.

## 8. What Marks the Opposite Group? Rejection of Revelation

### Verse

> **90:19** — As for those who disbelieved in our revelations,  they  have  incurred  misery. 

> **90:20** — They will be confined in the Hellfire.

### Reflection on the Verse

90:19 defines the opposite group by rejection of GOD’s revelations and 90:20 gives the consequence.

Again the decisive issue is not the physical direction. It is the response to revelation. The contrast is between a life of faith, patience and mercy and a life that rejects the signs.

Thus the right-left language is anchored in moral and spiritual orientation, not in superstition about physical sides.

## 9. 'Except Those on the Right': Released from the Trap of Sin

### Verse

> **74:38** — Every soul is trapped by its sins.

> **74:39** — Except for those on the right.

> **74:40** — While in Paradise, they will ask.

> **74:41** — About the guilty.

> **74:42** — “What brought you to this retribution?”

> **74:43** — They will say, “We did not observe the contact prayers (Salat).

> **74:44** — “We did not feed the poor.

> **74:45** — “We blundered with the blunderers.

> **74:46** — “We disbelieved in the Day of Judgment.

### Reflection on the Verse

74:38 says every soul is trapped by its sins, then makes an exception for those on the right. From Paradise they ask the guilty what brought them to punishment. The answers are concrete: neglect of the Contact Prayers, failure to feed the poor, drifting with those who drifted, and denial of the Day of Judgment.

This aligns strikingly with Sura 90. The positive “right” category is surrounded by worship, care for the poor, moral independence, and accountability.

The symbol is therefore inseparable from a lived moral history.

## 10. Right and Light Enter the Same Scene: 57:12

### Verse

> **57:12** — The day will come when you see the believing men and women with their lights radiating ahead of them and to their right. Good news is yours that, on that day, you will have gardens with flowing streams. You will abide therein forever. This is the great triumph.

### Reflection on the Verse

57:12 gives the strongest textual basis for connecting right and light. The lights of believing men and women radiate ahead of them and to their right, and the verse immediately announces Paradise.

We should not minimize this. Right and light are directly joined in the same eschatological scene.

But the verse still does not say that right *is* light. “Their light” is one thing; “to their right” describes where it radiates. The distinction matters. The right side can be a direction in which light is manifested without becoming the source or definition of light.

## 11. 'Perfect Our Light': The Light Is Not a Mechanical Status

### Verse

> **66:8** — O you who believe, you shall repent to GOD a firm repentance. Your Lord will then remit your sins and admit you into gardens with flowing streams. On that day, GOD will not disappoint the prophet  and those who believed with him. Their light will radiate in front of them and to their right. They will say, “Our Lord, perfect our light for us, and forgive us; You are Omnipotent.”

### Reflection on the Verse

66:8 repeats the scene: light radiates in front of the believers and to their right. Yet the believers pray, “Our Lord, perfect our light for us, and forgive us.”

This makes the symbolism deeper than a fixed right-left code. Light is connected to repentance, forgiveness, and dependence on GOD. Even the believers ask for its completion.

So the important question is not merely where the right side is, but how a human being receives and grows in the light GOD grants.

## 12. Light Cannot Be Borrowed

### Verse

> **57:13** — On that day, the hypocrite men and women will say to those who believed, “Please allow us to absorb some of your light.” It will be said, “Go back behind you, and seek light.” A barrier will be set up between them, whose gate separates mercy on the inner side, from retribution on the outer side.

> **57:14** — They will call upon them, “Were we not with you?” They will answer, “Yes, but you cheated your souls, hesitated, doubted, and became misled by wishful thinking, until GOD’s judgment came. You were diverted from GOD by illusions.

> **57:15** — “Therefore, today no ransom can be taken from you, nor from those who disbelieved. Your abode is the fire; it is your lord, and miserable abode.

### Reflection on the Verse

The hypocrites ask the believers for some of their light. They cannot simply borrow it. A barrier appears, and their earlier self-deception, hesitation, doubt, and wishful thinking are exposed.

Physical nearness to believers did not generate inner light. Religious proximity is not the same as spiritual reality.

This is crucial for the people-of-the-right theme: standing near people of the right is not the same as becoming one of them. Each soul carries its own history before GOD.

## 13. From Darkness into Light: The Quran Defines the Axis through Guidance

### Verse

> **2:257** — GOD is Lord of those who believe; He leads them out of darkness into the light. As for those who disbelieve, their lords are their idols; they lead them out of the light into darkness—these  will  be  the  dwellers of Hell; they abide in it forever. 

### Reflection on the Verse

2:257 defines the light-darkness contrast directly: GOD leads the believers out of darkness into the light, while idols lead disbelievers from light into darkness.

No right or left language appears in the verse. The axis is lordship, guidance, faith, and idolatry.

This is why “right = light, left = darkness” can only be a partial association, not a complete Quranic definition. The Quran grounds light first of all in GOD’s guidance.

## 14. Light Begins Now: Walking Among People with It

### Verse

> **6:122** — Is one who was dead and we granted him life, and provided him with light that enables him to move among the people, equal to one in total darkness from which he can never exit? The works of the disbelievers are thus adorned in their eyes.

### Reflection on the Verse

6:122 shows that light is not limited to the Hereafter. GOD gives a person light by which he moves among people, while another remains in total darkness from which he cannot emerge.

This brings the future scene into the present. The light that marks believers is already relevant to how we walk, judge, speak, and live now.

The verse does not state that this worldly light is physically identical to the light of 57:12. But the ethical continuity is hard to miss: guidance illuminates life before it illuminates the final scene.

## 15. The Source of Light Is Not the Right Side; It Is GOD

### Verse

> **24:35** — GOD is the light of the heavens and the  earth.  The  allegory  of  His  light is  that  of  a  concave  mirror  behind  a lamp  that  is  placed  inside  a  glass container.  The  glass  container  is like  a  bright,  pearl-like  star.  The  fuel thereof  is  supplied  from  a  blessed oil-producing  tree,  that  is  neither eastern,  nor  western.  Its  oil  is  almost self-radiating;  needs  no  fire  to  ignite it.  Light upon light.  GOD  guides to His light whoever wills (to be guided).  GOD  thus  cites  the  parables  for the people. GOD is fully aware of all things.

### Reflection on the Verse

24:35 sets the decisive boundary: GOD is the light of the heavens and the earth, and GOD guides to His light whoever wills to be guided.

Therefore the right side cannot be treated as the source of light. In 57:12 the light radiates to the right, but 24:35 tells us where the deepest meaning of light is rooted—in GOD and His guidance.

The allegory even speaks of a blessed tree that is neither eastern nor western. We should not force that detail into a theory about all directions, but it certainly warns against reducing divine light to a simple geographic side.

## 16. Darkness upon Darkness: Where Is the Word 'Left'?

### Verse

> **24:40** — Another  allegory  is  that  of  being  in total  darkness  in  the  midst  of  a violent  ocean,  with  waves  upon waves,  in  addition  to  thick  fog. Darkness  upon  darkness—if  he looked  at  his  own  hand,  he  could barely  see  it.  Whomever  GOD  deprives of light, will have no light. 

### Reflection on the Verse

24:40 gives one of the Quran’s strongest images of darkness: a violent ocean, waves upon waves, thick fog, darkness upon darkness. A person can barely see his own hand. The conclusion is that whoever GOD deprives of light has no light.

The word “left” never appears.

That matters. The Quran can describe spiritual darkness in full intensity without using left-side language. So left and darkness may be associated within the larger picture of loss, but they are not defined as synonyms.

## 17. Satan Comes from the Right Too: Why Physical Right Cannot Be Automatically Holy

### Verse

> **7:17** — “I  will  come  to  them  from  before them,  and  from  behind  them,  and from  their  right,  and  from  their  left, and  You  will  find  that  most  of  them are unappreciative.”

### Reflection on the Verse

7:17 is the balancing verse that prevents superstition. Satan says he will approach people from before them, behind them, from their right, and from their left.

If the ordinary physical right side were intrinsically sacred or automatically luminous, this statement would be difficult to explain. The verse shows that direction itself does not protect a person.

The positive “right” of the Hereafter is therefore not a magical property of physical space. The protection is guidance from GOD, not geometry.

## 18. The Final Day Opens in Light: Judgment, Record, and Justice

### Verse

> **39:69** — Then  the  earth  will  shine  with  the light  of  its  Lord.  The  record  will  be proclaimed,  and  the  prophets  and the  witnesses  will  be  brought  forth. Everyone  will  then  be  judged  equitably, without the least injustice.

> **14:1** — A.L.R.* A scripture that we revealed  to  you,  in  order  to  lead  the people  out  of  darkness  into  the light—in  accordance  with  the  will  of their  Lord—to  the  path  of  the  Almighty, the Praiseworthy.

### Reflection on the Verse

39:69 says the earth will shine with the light of its Lord, the record will be announced, witnesses will be brought forward, and judgment will occur without injustice. 14:1 says scripture was revealed to bring people from darkness into light.

Light therefore belongs to a broad Quranic field: revelation, truth becoming visible, record, testimony, justice, and guidance.

This helps explain why light and the people of the right can appear in the same final scene. They belong to the same positive outcome. Yet the center remains GOD’s light and justice; right is one sign inside that greater reality.

## 19. Arabic Word Roots: Right, Left, Bliss, Misery, Light, and Darkness

### Verse

> **56:27** — Those of the right side, will be on the right side.

### Reflection on the Verse

The Arabic terms show that “right,” “left,” “bliss/misery,” “light,” and “darkness” are related in the Quranic picture without being the same word families.

**يَمِين — y-m-n (ي م ن):** used for the right side/right hand, as in 56:27 and 69:19. The same root has other Quranic uses as well, so it cannot simply mean “Paradise” everywhere.

**مَيْمَنَة — maymanah (ي م ن):** the form in 56:8 and 90:18. Rashad Khalifa’s translation renders the passages through deserved bliss/happiness, showing how this root becomes associated with a positive final outcome.

**شِمَال — shimal (ش م ل):** the ordinary left/left-hand field, used in 56:41 and 69:25.

**مَشْأَمَة — mash'amah (ش أ م):** the contrasting group in 56:9 and 90:19. It is not from the same root as *shimal*. The authorized translation foregrounds misery in these contexts.

**نُور — nur (ن و ر):** light. In 57:12 and 66:8, the believers’ *nuruhum* radiates before them and to their right. In 24:35 the same root is tied directly to GOD and His guidance.

**ظُلُمَات — zulumat (ظ ل م):** darknesses, used in 2:257, 6:122 and 24:40 for states of lost guidance and inability to see.

The linguistic map supports an important caution: **y-m-n is not n-w-r, and sh-m-l / sh-ʾ-m are not z-l-m.** The Quran does not make “right” and “light,” or “left” and “darkness,” one lexical concept. Their relationship emerges because they meet in certain moral and eschatological scenes.

## Other Claims

### Verse

> **57:12** — The day will come when you see the believing men and women with their lights radiating ahead of them and to their right. Good news is yours that, on that day, you will have gardens with flowing streams. You will abide therein forever. This is the great triumph.

### Reflection on the Verse

Classical Arabic lexicography associates the y-m-n root not only with the right side but also with prosperity and fortunate outcome, while the sh-ʾ-m root carries associations of misfortune or inauspiciousness. The Quranic Arabic Corpus records *maymanah* at 56:8 and 90:18 and *mash'amah* at 56:9 and 90:19. Lane’s Lexicon likewise preserves these broader classical associations.

This background can help explain why the authorized translation foregrounds bliss and misery in those passages. Yet it would still be an overreach to claim that every Quranic use of “right” means holy light and every use of “left” means spiritual darkness. Satan approaches from both right and left in 7:17; 84:10 describes the failed person receiving the record from behind the back; and 24:40 describes darkness without mentioning the left at all.

Traditional interpretations often treat the right hand as a sign of honor and salvation and the left as a sign of loss. That can fit 69:19-25, but the main conclusion of this article does not depend on those later interpretations.

The cautious conclusion is: **the Quran strongly associates the right side with Paradise and a positive final outcome, and strongly associates light with guidance and the success of believers. 57:12 and 66:8 directly join these themes. But the text does not define right as a synonym for light or left as a synonym for darkness.**

## Conclusion

### Verse

> **66:8** — O you who believe, you shall repent to GOD a firm repentance. Your Lord will then remit your sins and admit you into gardens with flowing streams. On that day, GOD will not disappoint the prophet  and those who believed with him. Their light will radiate in front of them and to their right. They will say, “Our Lord, perfect our light for us, and forgive us; You are Omnipotent.”

### Reflection on the Verse

We can now answer the original question with greater precision: **Yes, the Quran creates a real and powerful association between “right” and “light,” but saying “right = light and left = darkness” as an absolute equation would go beyond the text.**

Those on the right are associated with Paradise and bliss (56:27, 56:38). The one receiving the record in the right hand believed in accountability and reaches a happy outcome (69:19-24; 84:7-9). Sura 90 fills this identity with moral substance: freeing, feeding, believing, patience, and mercy. Sura 74 adds prayer, care for the poor, moral independence, and belief in Judgment.

Then the light scene arrives. In 57:12 and 66:8 the believers’ light really does radiate ahead of them and to their right. The joining of right and light is explicit.

But the source of light is never the right side. 24:35 identifies GOD as the light of the heavens and the earth and speaks of guidance to His light. 2:257 grounds the light-darkness contrast in GOD’s guidance versus the authority of idols. 6:122 makes light a way of walking among people in this life. 24:40 can describe darkness upon darkness without mentioning the left at all.

Finally, 7:17 sets the boundary: Satan can approach from the right as well as the left. Ordinary physical rightness therefore does not save anyone.

“Right” becomes, for me, something larger than a compass direction but more limited than a synonym for light. **It is an eschatological sign associated with acceptance, a good account, and Paradise. Light is the deeper Quranic reality of guidance granted by GOD, illuminating both the present walk and the final Day.**

So perhaps the most important question is not, “Where is the right side in Paradise?” It is: **Am I living today by the light that brings a person out of darkness? When my record is opened, will I want to hide it—or will I be able to say, like the person in 69:19, “Come read my record”?**
