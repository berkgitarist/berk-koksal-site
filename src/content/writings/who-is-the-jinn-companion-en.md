# [43:36] Who Is the Jinn Companion? Its Role, Repentance, Paradise, and Final Accountability

*A Nineteen-Part Reflection on Quran 43:36*

**Author:** Berk KÖKSAL  
**Date:** October 13, 2026  
**Place:** Mersin

### Verse

> **43:36**
>
> وَمَن يَعْشُ عَن ذِكْرِ ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ نُقَيِّضْ لَهُۥ شَيْطَٰنًا فَهُوَ لَهُۥ قَرِينٌ

## Introduction

**Greetings. Peace be upon you.**

The expression “jinn companion” can easily become the beginning of a frightening story. Is an unseen being always with me? Does it plant evil ideas? Does it trust Satan? Could it come to recognize its mistakes, repent, believe, and enter Paradise? What happens to it after the human dies? Does it have relationships with other jinn? Is there a reward for changing its course? These are compelling questions. Their urgency, however, is not itself evidence that every answer has been revealed.

Reading 43:36-39 alongside 50:23-29 and sura 72, I see two dangers. One is transferring all responsibility for my wrongdoing to an invisible being. The other is speaking as though I possess a complete biography of an invisible creature and then attributing my certainty to GOD. I want to avoid both.

This inquiry also connects with my earlier writing on [Entering Paradise or Reaching the High Heaven?](https://berkkoksal.com/writings/cennete-girmek-mi-ust-cennete-ulasmak-mi-en/) and Who Is the Teacher of the Quran?. How much can a human know about another creature's final rank? When does an act of teaching or influence become a claim to authority? At what point does textual understanding end and personal interpretation begin?

I will study the companion's misleading influence, moral agency, responsibility, possible repentance, the inclusion of jinn in promises of Paradise, relationships among jinn, and the justice of recompense. Rashad Khalifa developed a detailed account of the jinn companion, and I will examine his specific statements in their own section. I do not want to blur the difference between a verse and a commentator's reconstruction.

Above all, I do not want fascination with the fate of an unseen companion to make me forget my own responsibility. Perhaps the hardest question at the end is not whether I know everything about that being, but whether I am truly willing to trust GOD.

## 1. The First Question: When Is a Companion Assigned?

### Verse

> **43:36** — Anyone  who  disregards  the  message  of  the  Most  Gracious,  we  appoint  a  devil  to  be  his  constant  companion.*

### Reflection on the Verse

I cannot begin with 43:36 by skipping its condition. The verse speaks of someone who disregards the message of the Most Gracious. It does not simply name a companion; it connects companionship to a person's response to guidance. Can I infer directly from this sentence that every newborn is assigned the same kind of jinn at birth? No. That is a distinct interpretive claim. The explicit statement here is about neglecting the message and being assigned a devil as a constant companion.

That distinction may appear minor, yet the entire inquiry depends on it. The moment we ask who this being is, when it comes, how it operates, and what eventually happens to it, we may be tempted to fill silence with a complete narrative. But the gaps in a verse do not become revealed information simply because I can imagine a plausible answer.

There is another point: being a constant companion does not mean possessing unlimited authority. A presence may be persistent without being sovereign. Nothing in 43:36 elevates this companion to the rank of GOD. I want to investigate it without surrendering to fear, as though an unseen being had acquired independent command over my life.

My first reflection, then, concerns proximity and authority. Something can be close to me without deserving obedience. The greater danger would be allowing fascination with a companion to overshadow the very message that the verse says was disregarded.

**Verse references:** 43:36.

## 2. The Subtle Mechanism: Making Wrong Look Right

### Verse

> **43:37** — Such  companions  will  divert  them from  the  path,  yet  make  them  believe that they are guided.

> **41:25** — We assign to them companions who adorn everything they do in their eyes. Thus, they end up incurring the same fate as the previous communities of jinns and humans, who were also losers.

### Reflection on the Verse

The warning in 43:37 goes beyond being led toward a wrong action: a person may be diverted while believing that he is guided. Verse 41:25 adds another mechanism, the beautification of deeds. Read together, they suggest something more dangerous than an obvious invitation to wrongdoing: moral error can be presented as righteousness.

I recognize the possibility in ordinary life. Someone injures another person and calls it justified correction. Someone confuses pride with defending truth. Someone enjoys humiliating a rival while imagining that he stands for justice. These are my illustrations, not a list expressly provided by the verses. I cannot diagnose the hidden condition of everyone whose confidence troubles me.

In fact, the first person I ought to examine is myself. What if the explanation that feels most comfortable is precisely the one that excuses my failure? What if I need to be right more than I need to discover what is right? Neither popularity nor private certainty supplies the verification that truth demands.

The verses allow me to discuss the companion's misleading influence and its capacity to make wrongdoing attractive. They do not allow me to identify the unseen author of each sentence passing through my mind. Claiming such access would create another illusion of privileged guidance. The responsible response is to test the content of an idea rather than pretend to possess a map of its invisible origin.

**Verse references:** 43:37, 41:25.

## 3. A Regret Too Late: “What a Miserable Companion!”

### Verse

> **43:38** — When  he  comes  before  us  he  will say,  “Oh  I  wish  you  were  as  far  from me  as  the  two  easts.*  What  a  miserable companion!”

> **43:39** — It  will  not  console  you  on  that  day, as  transgressors,  that  both  of  you will share in the retribution.

### Reflection on the Verse

In 43:38, the speaker longs for an immense distance from the companion. Verse 43:39 then speaks of two transgressors sharing retribution without consolation. Companionship here is not an innocent closeness. It has consequences.

What troubles me is the timing of recognition. A person can spend a lifetime tolerating, even welcoming, a harmful direction only to recognize its cost when there is no opportunity to undo the history. The wish for distance exposes how a relationship that once felt useful may become unbearable when the truth can no longer be avoided.

Yet I should not turn this scene into a verdict on every jinn. The verse concerns the two transgressors described there. Other passages expressly speak of jinn who submit. It would be inaccurate to erase that possibility with the judgment of a particular pair.

There is a moral question here that applies to visible relationships as well. Is a friend helping me by applauding everything I do, or by refusing to encourage wrongdoing? If I make someone else's injustice feel righteous, have I been loyal to that person or contributed to the harm?

If an inquiry into an unseen companion teaches me to judge my own alliances more honestly, it has practical worth. If it merely produces frightening stories while leaving my own conduct untouched, it may have missed the ethical force of the warning.

**Verse references:** 43:38, 43:39.

## 4. The Companion at Judgment: Witness or Defendant?

### Verse

> **50:23** — The companion said, “Here is my formidable testimony.*”

> **50:27** — His companion said, “Our Lord, I did not mislead him; he was far astray.”

> **50:28** — He said, “Do not feud in front of Me; I have sufficiently warned you.

> **50:29** — “Nothing can be changed now. I am never unjust towards the people.”

### Reflection on the Verse

Sura 50 presents another scene. Every soul arrives with a herder and a witness; then a companion speaks of testimony. Later a companion denies having misled the human, and GOD ends the dispute.

I do not want to collapse every role into a single being too quickly. The text uses distinct expressions: a herder and witness in 50:21, a companion in 50:23, and a companion again in 50:27. How these roles relate is a question of interpretation. Rashad Khalifa identifies the lifelong jinn as a principal witness in his notes and Appendix 7. That is his reading of the passage, not a substitute for its wording.

The defense in 50:27 is also revealing. The companion says that he did not lead the human astray. The presence of a statement in a judgment scene does not automatically certify that statement as the final verdict. The divine response refuses the quarrel and points to the warning already given.

Can two participants save themselves through mutually shifting blame? The passage gives me no reason to trust that strategy. It explicitly anchors the outcome in GOD's justice, not in the cleverness of a defendant's explanation.

The practical question is therefore about the present. Why postpone repentance and responsibility until the scene in which the text says nothing can be changed? The opportunity to take a different course belongs to life now, not to an imagined last-minute courtroom defense.

**Verse references:** 50:21, 50:23, 50:27, 50:28, 50:29.

## 5. Satan, Jinn, and Companion: Are They Identical?

### Verse

> **18:50** — We said to the angels, “Fall prostrate before Adam.” They fell prostrate, except Satan. He became a jinn, for he disobeyed the order of His Lord.* Will you choose him and his descendants as lords instead of Me, even though they are your enemies? What a miserable substitute!

> **7:27** — O children of Adam, do not let the devil dupe you as he did when he caused the eviction of your parents from  Paradise,  and  the  removal of their garments to  expose their bodies. He and his tribe see you, while you do not see them. We appoint the devils as companions of those who do not believe.

> **15:27** — As  for  the  jinns,  we  created  them, before that, from blazing fire.

### Reflection on the Verse

Verse 18:50 says that Satan became a jinn after disobeying his Lord, and it mentions his descendants. Verse 7:27 speaks of Satan and his tribe seeing humans while remaining unseen to them. Verse 15:27 discusses the creation of jinn. These distinctions matter, but none of them justifies collapsing every jinn into the single person of Satan.

A being may represent a rebellious direction without being identical to Iblis. Nor does belonging to the category of jinn erase moral differences among its members. Sura 72 will speak directly of those differences.

In my earlier writing about the Heavenly Society, the central questions involved ego and GOD's absolute authority. They remain relevant here, but I cannot turn a reconstruction of pre-earthly history into an exact biography of every unseen creature.

The warning of 18:50 is about authority. It asks why anyone would take Satan and his descendants as lords in place of GOD when they are enemies. That warning could be lost if my fascination with hidden powers becomes stronger than my commitment to worship GOD alone.

A distinction therefore protects the inquiry: examining the origin of a companion is not the same as granting that companion spiritual jurisdiction over me. I need neither deny what the passage says nor pretend that it says everything I might wish to know.

**Verse references:** 18:50, 7:27, 15:27.

## 6. Do Jinn Influence One Another? Networks of Deception

### Verse

> **6:112** — We have permitted the enemies of every  prophet—human  and  jinn devils—to  inspire  in  each  other fancy words, in order to deceive. Had your Lord willed, they would not have done it. You shall disregard them and their fabrications. 

> **114:4** — “From the evils of sneaky whisperers. 

> **114:5** — “Who whisper into the chests of the people. 

> **114:6** — “Be they of the jinns, or the people.”

### Reflection on the Verse

Verse 6:112 broadens the picture beyond a private contest between one human and one jinn. Human and jinn devils inspire embellished words to one another in order to deceive. Verses 114:4-6 also identify whisperers from among both jinn and humans.

That means influence is not necessarily confined to a single invisible pair. There can be exchanges among deceivers. But it would be a serious error to infer that all members of the jinn community are wicked. The passages speak specifically of devils and whisperers.

In visible human life, a misleading claim often acquires strength through repetition: one person invents it, another gives it attractive language, and a third passes it along. That analogy helps me understand reciprocal suggestion, but it tells me nothing about an unseen communications system or the organization of jinn society.

Could a particular companion be influenced by other jinn? The mutual inspiration mentioned in 6:112 makes such a possibility intelligible. It does not disclose whom my personal companion has met, whether such a companion has a social circle, or how its unseen relationships operate.

An inquiry into invisible beings must not become a license to invent invisible detail. What I can assess is what happens to information within reach: Do I verify it? Do I beautify a falsehood? Do I share it because it feels compelling? In that respect, the warning about deceptive speech concerns my own responsibility today.

**Verse references:** 6:112, 114:4, 114:5, 114:6.

## 7. Are All Jinn on One Path? The Question of Agency

### Verse

> **72:11** — “ ‘Some of us are righteous, and some are less than righteous; we follow various paths.

> **72:14** — “ ‘Among us are the submitters, and among us are the compromisers.’ ” As for those who submitted, they are on the right path.

> **72:15** — As for the compromisers, they will be fuel for Gehenna.

### Reflection on the Verse

Without these verses I cannot think responsibly about the possible future of a jinn companion. Verse 72:11 says that jinn follow different paths. Verse 72:14 distinguishes submitters from compromisers; 72:15 describes the fate of the latter.

Therefore the statement “Every jinn is inevitably wicked” erases the moral diversity that the passage itself records. The opposite claim, “Every jinn will eventually be saved,” erases its warning just as surely. Both are convenient generalizations rather than careful readings.

When I ask whether a companion can change, I must first recognize that jinn are presented as moral beings with different orientations. Submission is a genuine distinction among them. Still, I cannot move from that principle to a prediction about the outcome of a particular invisible individual.

We make similar mistakes about people. A human being may hold destructive ideas at one time and abandon them later; that possibility does not guarantee the change. Responsibility remains meaningful precisely because the outcome is not mine to announce.

There is a question of justice beneath this discussion. If the Quran speaks of varying paths among jinn, why would I insist on speaking of them as a single undifferentiated moral class? Ultimate knowledge of a creature's conduct belongs to GOD. I ought not pose as an eyewitness to a life I cannot observe.

So the next question becomes sharper: Can a jinn who was once aligned with error hear guidance and respond differently? The following passages show that such a response is possible among jinn.

**Verse references:** 72:11, 72:14, 72:15.

## 8. Can Jinn Hear Truth and Carry It to Others?

### Verse

> **72:1** — Say, “I was inspired that a group of jinns listened, then said, ‘We have heard a wonderful Quran.*

> **72:2** — “ ‘It guides to righteousness, and we have believed in it; we will never set up any idols beside our Lord.

> **46:29** — Recall  that  we  directed  a  number  of jinns  to  you,  in  order  to  let  them hear  the  Quran.  When  they  got there,  they  said,  “Listen.”  As  soon as  it  was  over,  they  rushed  to  their people, warning.*

> **46:30** — They  said,  “O  our  people,  we  have heard  a  book  that  was  revealed  after Moses,  and  confirms  the  previous scriptures.  It  guides  to  the  truth;  to the right path.

> **46:31** — “O  our  people,  respond  to  the  call of GOD, and believe in Him. He will then  forgive  your  sins,  and  spare  you a painful retribution.”

### Reflection on the Verse

Verses 72:1-2 describe jinn who hear the Quran, recognize its guidance toward righteousness, and believe. Verses 46:29-31 describe jinn who listen, return to their own people, and urge them to answer GOD's call.

This scene corrects a one-sided picture of the unseen world. The Quran does not portray jinn solely as deceivers. Some listen, evaluate, believe, and pass on a warning to their community.

If I want to discuss a jinn's ability to recognize error, I can begin here. Jinn can hear guidance, respond to its content, and communicate what they have discovered. Those are meaningful features of moral agency, without requiring me to invent a complete psychology of jinn consciousness.

I must also preserve an obvious limit. These verses speak of a group of jinn; they do not identify those jinn as the personal companions of specific humans. They say nothing about sharing a human's name, living inside a particular body, or operating through a specified neurological mechanism.

The personal lesson is one of humility in influence. I cannot force another being to recognize truth, even in ordinary human relationships. I can speak truthfully, refuse to participate in deception, and attempt to live by what I claim to believe. I cannot manufacture conviction by demanding it.

Perhaps that is why this scene matters for the companion question. It gives room for transformation without making supernatural negotiation a condition for it.

**Verse references:** 72:1, 72:2, 46:29, 46:30, 46:31.

## 9. Mutual Advantage and Its Hidden Cost

### Verse

> **6:128** — The  day  will  come  when  He  summons  all  of  them  (and  says):  “O  you jinns,  you  have  claimed  multitudes of humans.” Their human companions  will  say,  “Our  Lord,  we  enjoyed  each  others’  company  until  we wasted  the  life  span  You  had  set  for us.”  He  will  say,  “Hell  is  your  destiny.”  They  abide  therein  forever,  in accordance  with  GOD’s  will.  Your Lord is Wise, Omniscient.

> **72:6** — “ ‘Human beings used to seek power through jinn beings, but they only afflicted them with a lot of adversity.

### Reflection on the Verse

In 6:128, a relationship between jinn and their human companions is remembered as an enjoyment that lasted until their allotted time had been wasted. Verse 72:6 speaks of humans seeking power through jinn and receiving adversity instead.

Together they show that a destructive relationship need not operate through terror alone. The human companions in 6:128 describe mutual enjoyment. People can remain attached to a harmful association because it promises advantage, status, excitement, or an imagined form of protection.

This makes the warning about seeking power through jinn especially important. Someone may claim special contact with unseen beings or offer privileged information from them. Yet the verse does not authorize me to treat such a claim as proof of truth. Precisely because the alleged source is unseen, its authority must not be assumed.

The point also turns back toward me. Sometimes I may cling to an error not merely because I was deceived but because it serves a desire. If a false story makes me feel powerful, perhaps I become invested in defending it.

Companionship is not automatically moral solidarity. Two beings can affirm each other's false direction until the passage of time reveals what has been lost. That is a severe thought, and one worth applying to the relationships I can actually examine.

**Verse references:** 6:128, 72:6.

## 10. Does Trusting Satan Remove Responsibility?

### Verse

> **14:22** — And  the  devil  will  say, after  the judgment had been issued, “GOD has promised you the truthful promise, and I promised you, but I broke my promise. I had no power over you; I simply invited you, and you accepted  my  invitation.  Therefore, do not blame me, and blame only yourselves. My complaining cannot help you, nor can your complaining help me. I have disbelieved in your idolizing  me.  The  transgressors have  incurred  a  painful  retribution.”

> **50:27** — His companion said, “Our Lord, I did not mislead him; he was far astray.”

### Reflection on the Verse

Verse 14:22 gives a striking post-judgment speech. Satan acknowledges that he made a promise and broke it; he says that he only invited people, who accepted his invitation. In 50:27, the companion likewise rejects the charge of causing the human's deviation.

These statements put pressure on the temptation to transfer all responsibility elsewhere. “I was made to think it,” “someone else deceived me,” or “I had no part in the decision” may express an element of someone's experience, but they cannot automatically erase personal agency.

I must also resist the opposite excess. The verses do not establish that every unwanted thought is a deliberate moral choice. Verse 14:22 addresses the acceptance of an invitation, not the involuntary appearance of mental content. Assigning guilt simply because a troubling idea entered a person's mind would go beyond what is written.

Did a companion begin by trusting Satan's case? Rashad Khalifa argues that it did. I will examine his account separately. The distinct paths described for jinn in sura 72 make the companion's own decision an important question. Influence may be real without destroying responsibility.

Neither a human blaming a companion nor a companion blaming a human can replace GOD's judgment. The passage warns me against treating explanation as acquittal.

**Verse references:** 14:22, 50:27.

## 11. How Could a Companion Recognize Its Error?

### Verse

> **7:30** — Some He guided, while others are committed to straying. They have taken the devils as their masters, instead of GOD, yet they believe that they are guided.

> **4:38** — They give money to charity only to show off, while disbelieving in GOD and the Last Day. If one’s companion is the devil, that is the worst companion.

> **17:36** — You  shall  not  accept  any  information,  unless  you  verify  it  for  yourself. I  have  given  you  the  hearing,  the eyesight,  and  the  brain,  and  you  are responsible for using them.

### Reflection on the Verse

Verse 7:30 describes people who take devils as masters yet believe themselves guided. Verse 4:38 condemns companionship with the devil. Verse 17:36 calls for the verification of information. Together, they suggest an important question about recognizing error.

If a being does not realize that it is mistaken, ceasing one act may not be enough; the belief that made the act seem justified may also need to be examined. That is a possible moral inference, not a revealed account of a jinn's cognitive mechanisms.

I know from my own work that abandoning an attractive explanation can be harder than acquiring a new fact. A belief may have accumulated years of effort, identity, and public commitment. Admitting that it was wrong can feel like losing part of oneself.

I do not want to imagine a companion as fixed forever in one of two simplified categories. The differing paths described in sura 72 argue for greater care. But they do not make me an observer of my own unseen companion's present beliefs.

Perhaps the most responsible way to approach the question is to turn it around: What evidence would persuade me that one of my cherished ideas is wrong? If I study a companion's possible blindness while refusing to examine my own, the inquiry has already gone off course.

**Verse references:** 7:30, 4:38, 17:36.

## 12. Where Does Satan’s Power End?

### Verse

> **16:99** — He  has  no  power  over  those  who believe and trust in their Lord.

> **16:100** — His  power  is  limited  to  those  who choose  him  as  their  master;  those who choose him as their god.

> **17:65** — “As  for  My  servants,  you  have  no power  over  them.”  Your  Lord  suffices as an advocate.

### Reflection on the Verse

Verses 16:99-100 do not describe Satan as an unlimited force. His power is denied over those who believe and trust in their Lord and limited to those who choose him as master or god. Verse 17:65 restates the boundary concerning GOD's servants.

Without these passages, the idea of an unseen companion might turn human life into a picture of helplessness. If some being accompanies me, am I merely its instrument? If a distressing idea appears, have I lost freedom? The verses place a limit on Satan's authority rather than elevating him into a second ruler.

Yet I must not turn this into a psychological promise. The passages do not say that a person who trusts GOD will never suffer anxiety, grief, or intrusive thoughts. The absence of sovereign power is not identical to the absence of emotional difficulty.

There is an opposite danger too. To say Satan cannot compel the faithful is not to say that deceptive invitations are harmless. A lie can mislead without physical force. The proper response is neither panic nor careless confidence.

For me trust means refusing to make a limited creature an independent author of destiny. At the same time, I cannot stop evaluating my own decisions. This balance protects both the seriousness of moral choices and the primacy of GOD.

**Verse references:** 16:99, 16:100, 17:65.

## 13. Whispers, My Own Thoughts, and Seeking Refuge

### Verse

> **50:16** — We  created  the  human,  and  we know what he whispers to himself. We are closer to him than his jugular vein.

> **41:36** — When  the  devil  whispers  an  idea  to you,  you shall  seek  refuge  in  GOD. He is the Hearer, the Omniscient.

> **7:200** — When  the  devil  whispers  to  you  any whisper,  seek  refuge  in  GOD;  He  is Hearer, Omniscient.

### Reflection on the Verse

Verse 50:16 mentions what a human whispers to himself. Verses 41:36 and 7:200 instruct seeking refuge in GOD when the devil whispers. Read together, they demand a distinction: I cannot assign the origin of every thought to one unseen companion.

People feel fear, misread events, remember painful experiences, and become anxious about the future. The verses discuss devilish whispers, but they do not provide a diagnostic chart for all mental experiences. Declaring every troubling thought to be the voice of a jinn would go beyond their wording.

Seeking refuge in GOD remains central, but I do not turn it into an obligation to converse with an unseen being. The instruction tells me where to turn in the face of an evil whisper. It does not require me to hunt for secret voices, identify an invisible author behind every idea, or await supernatural signs.

If a thought urges injustice, I can examine and refuse it. If suspicion tempts me to accuse someone without evidence, I can stop and verify. If fear grows through imagined scenarios, I need not make decisions as though those scenarios were established facts. These are practical reflections, not promises of an instant spiritual cure.

Nor do the passages warrant calling serious mental distress a failure of faith. Seeking reliable professional assistance when needed is not disqualified by the instruction to seek refuge. Reflection should never become a way to dismiss human suffering.

**Verse references:** 50:16, 41:36, 7:200.

## 14. Can a Jinn Companion Repent?

### Verse

> **46:31** — “O  our  people,  respond  to  the  call of GOD, and believe in Him. He will then  forgive  your  sins,  and  spare  you a painful retribution.”

> **39:53** — Proclaim: “O My servants who exceeded the limits, never despair of GOD’s mercy. For GOD forgives all sins. He is the Forgiver, Most Merciful.”

> **50:32** — This is what was promised to every repenter, steadfast.

### Reflection on the Verse

Verse 46:31 is decisive for this question. Jinn urge their own people to respond to GOD's call, believe, and receive forgiveness for their sins. Forgiveness is therefore not excluded from the moral horizon of jinn; it is expressly part of an appeal addressed to them.

Verse 39:53 speaks against despairing of GOD's mercy, and 50:32 connects Paradise with repentance and steadfastness. Yet I cannot use those statements to announce that any particular companion has already been forgiven. I do not possess such knowledge.

Repentance deserves more than an imagined formula. If a companion truly abandons its error, what would become of the old claim that something besides GOD deserves ultimate trust? Verse 72:2 gives an example of jinn explicitly rejecting idolatry after hearing the Quran. That provides a direction for reflection, not a detailed timetable of inner change.

I can sincerely wish that another creature turn away from harm. Compassion makes room for that hope. But I cannot believe, repent, or decide in its place. It remains a moral subject, not an extension of my will.

So I separate two questions. Can jinn receive the call to belief and forgiveness? Verse 46:31 clearly supports that possibility. Has my unseen companion actually repented? I have no basis for claiming to know. Hope in mercy is not the same as a verdict on an unknown individual.

**Verse references:** 46:31, 39:53, 50:32.

## 15. Can It Enter Paradise? Gardens for Jinn as Well

### Verse

> **55:46** — For those who reverence the majesty of their Lord, two gardens (one for  the  jinns  and  one  for  the  humans).

> **55:62** — Below them are two gardens (one for the jinns and one for the humans).

> **72:14** — “ ‘Among us are the submitters, and among us are the compromisers.’ ” As for those who submitted, they are on the right path.

### Reflection on the Verse

The Turkish rendering of 55:46 explicitly names gardens for both jinn and humans, and 55:62 describes two additional gardens below them. Verse 72:14 identifies submitting jinn as being on the right path. These texts make it difficult to claim that jinn as a category are excluded from salvation.

In my earlier essay, [Entering Paradise or Reaching the High Heaven?](https://berkkoksal.com/writings/cennete-girmek-mi-ust-cennete-ulasmak-mi-en/), I explored distinctions among the gardens. Here the question is more personal: If a particular companion, understood as a representative of Satan, abandons that position, could it too reach Paradise?

The secure textual foundation is that some jinn submit and Paradise is described in terms that include jinn. It does not identify the garden reserved for my hypothetical personal companion. Nor can I ignore 43:39, which warns about the shared retribution of the transgressing pair described there.

I find myself asking whether I am able to wish for righteousness and rescue beyond my own life. Hope for another being's reform is not the same as an authority to secure its outcome. I cannot act as a gatekeeper of Paradise or as the unseen creature's appointed intercessor.

The discipline remains unchanged: I can accept the promise given and acknowledge the warning stated. I must leave the undisclosed fate of an individual to GOD.

**Verse references:** 55:46, 55:62, 72:14.

## 16. The Reward System: Points, Deeds, or Justice?

### Verse

> **6:132** — Everyone  will  attain  a  rank  commensurate  with  their  deeds.  Your Lord  is  never  unaware  of  anything they do.

> **46:19** — They  all  attain  the  ranks  they  have deserved,  in  accordance  with  their works.  He  will  pay  them  for  their works, without the least injustice.

> **50:31** — Paradise will be offered to the righteous, readily.

> **50:32** — This is what was promised to every repenter, steadfast.

> **50:35** — They get anything they wish therein, and we have even more.

### Reflection on the Verse

It would be easy to answer “What reward does a jinn companion receive?” with a points table. How many points for guiding a human? How many lost for a misleading whisper? At what number would Paradise become guaranteed? None of these accounting rules appears in the passages under consideration.

Verse 6:132 relates rank to deeds, and 46:19 speaks of deserved ranks and compensation without injustice. Verses 50:31-35 describe Paradise in connection with repentance, steadfastness, and reverence for the Most Gracious.

I can therefore speak of accountability, conduct, degrees, and justice. I cannot invent a precise reward formula for converting another being and attach it to revelation. Nor should I assume that results visible to me exhaust what GOD knows about intention and responsibility.

A companion's own orientation matters. If a being persisted in wrong while attempting to claim another's good deeds as its own, companionship alone would not establish redemption. The mutual reckoning of 6:128 and 43:39 guards against a simplistic theory of shared merit. And my own imagined righteousness cannot certify the ultimate condition of an unseen partner.

The strength of divine justice is precisely that I am not its scorekeeper. The structure of reward is disclosed only to the extent the verses disclose it. What belongs to me is to pursue what is right without reducing goodness to a competition for secret spiritual points.

**Verse references:** 6:132, 46:19, 50:31, 50:32, 50:35.

## 17. Judgment and Accountability: Do Partners Share a Fate?

### Verse

> **6:130** — O  you  jinns  and  humans,  did  you  not receive  messengers  from  among you,  who  narrated  to  you  My  revelations,  and  warned  you  about  the meeting  of  this  day?  They  will  say, “We  bear  witness  against  ourselves.”  They  were  totally  preoccupied with  the  worldly  life,  and  they  will bear  witness  against  themselves  that they were disbelievers.

> **6:128** — The  day  will  come  when  He  summons  all  of  them  (and  says):  “O  you jinns,  you  have  claimed  multitudes of humans.” Their human companions  will  say,  “Our  Lord,  we  enjoyed  each  others’  company  until  we wasted  the  life  span  You  had  set  for us.”  He  will  say,  “Hell  is  your  destiny.”  They  abide  therein  forever,  in accordance  with  GOD’s  will.  Your Lord is Wise, Omniscient.

> **50:29** — “Nothing can be changed now. I am never unjust towards the people.”

> **43:39** — It  will  not  console  you  on  that  day, as  transgressors,  that  both  of  you will share in the retribution.

### Reflection on the Verse

Verse 6:130 addresses jinn and humans together and asks them to account for warnings they received. Verse 6:128 portrays the outcome of a destructive human-jinn association. Verse 50:29 rejects any injustice on GOD's part.

These passages make me consider the relationship between shared behavior and individual responsibility. Two beings can influence one another without becoming one moral person. Their closeness does not establish a rule by which every action of one is automatically entered on the record of the other. No such mechanical scoring scheme is given.

The pair in 43:39 share retribution as transgressors. That does not mean every friendship necessarily produces an identical afterlife outcome. The insistence on ranks corresponding to deeds in 6:132 remains an important balance.

Could a companion recognize its mistakes? The testimony of 6:130 describes a confrontation with wrongdoing. Timing matters: recognition while change is still possible is different from recognition after judgment. The call to forgiveness in 46:31 supports hope for repentance in life; 50:29, by contrast, warns of a point at which nothing can be changed.

I do not want to secure my own destiny through an imaginary theory of automatic partnership. “If I am good, my companion is automatically good” and “If my companion is bad, I am helpless” are equally convenient and equally unsafe shortcuts. Justice means taking responsibility while leaving the full judgment to GOD.

**Verse references:** 6:130, 6:128, 50:29, 43:39.

## 18. The Companion’s Relations with Other Jinn and Its Own Duty

### Verse

> **51:56** — I did not create the jinns and the humans  except  to  worship  Me alone.

> **72:11** — “ ‘Some of us are righteous, and some are less than righteous; we follow various paths.

> **46:29** — Recall  that  we  directed  a  number  of jinns  to  you,  in  order  to  let  them hear  the  Quran.  When  they  got there,  they  said,  “Listen.”  As  soon as  it  was  over,  they  rushed  to  their people, warning.*

> **46:31** — “O  our  people,  respond  to  the  call of GOD, and believe in Him. He will then  forgive  your  sins,  and  spare  you a painful retribution.”

> **6:112** — We have permitted the enemies of every  prophet—human  and  jinn devils—to  inspire  in  each  other fancy words, in order to deceive. Had your Lord willed, they would not have done it. You shall disregard them and their fabrications.

### Reflection on the Verse

Verse 51:56 places jinn and humans within the same purpose of worship. Verse 72:11 describes differing paths among jinn. Verses 46:29-31 show jinn returning to their people with a warning, while 6:112 describes deceptive inspiration exchanged among jinn devils. Together, these passages prevent me from reducing an entire moral being to a single function in one human life.

Even if a particular jinn accompanies me, its own accountability cannot simply disappear. The Quran shows jinn receiving, sharing, and responding to messages. To say their only possible act is to mislead a human would narrow the picture beyond what the other verses allow.

Could a transformed companion influence other jinn for good? The jinn who warn their people in 46:29-31 show that positive communication among jinn is conceivable and, in that scene, actual. But that does not disclose where my hypothetical companion would go, whom it would persuade, or what specific reward it might receive.

Not knowing these details need not create an emergency. My own obligation before GOD is already substantial. Nor am I required to treat anyone's claim of privileged access to jinn as religious authority, particularly in light of the warning in 72:6.

Two modest conclusions are more useful than a fantastic unseen biography: jinn are not described as morally uniform, and I cannot claim to know the hidden social life of a particular companion. Between those statements lies the boundary of honest reflection.

**Verse references:** 51:56, 72:11, 46:29, 46:31, 6:112.

## 19. Arabic Roots: Companionship, Whispering, Return, and Recompense

### Verse

> **43:36** — Anyone  who  disregards  the  message  of  the  Most  Gracious,  we  appoint  a  devil  to  be  his  constant  companion.*

> **50:27** — His companion said, “Our Lord, I did not mislead him; he was far astray.”

> **114:4** — “From the evils of sneaky whisperers. 

> **46:31** — “O  our  people,  respond  to  the  call of GOD, and believe in Him. He will then  forgive  your  sins,  and  spare  you a painful retribution.”

> **6:132** — Everyone  will  attain  a  rank  commensurate  with  their  deeds.  Your Lord  is  never  unaware  of  anything they do.

### Reflection on the Verse

At this point I want to examine several Arabic roots. The word translated as “companion” does not always specify a jinn simply by appearing in a verse.

**قَرِين** (*qarīn*) in 43:36 and 50:27 relates to **ق ر ن** (*q-r-n*), an area of meaning involving association, pairing, and close accompaniment. The word describes a relationship, not automatically the species of the one who accompanies. Verse 43:36 adds the identification as a devil; the nature of the companion in the 50:27 judgment scene must be read with its context.

**جِنّ** (*jinn*) is associated with the root **ج ن ن** (*j-n-n*), related to concealment. This connection can illuminate the language of invisibility, but it does not disclose the anatomy of jinn or a physical mechanism by which one enters a person's thoughts.

The expression **ٱلْوَسْوَاسِ** (*al-waswās*) in 114:4 uses the repeated consonantal pattern **و س و س** (*w-s-w-s*) and belongs to the language of whispers and insistent suggestion. The imagery of making deeds attractive in 41:25 involves **ز ي ن** (*z-y-n*). Suggesting an idea and adorning an action may be related in effect without being the same word.

The forgiveness offered in 46:31 is associated with **غ ف ر** (*gh-f-r*). The expression **أَوَّاب** (*awwāb*) in 50:32 is linked to **أ و ب** (*ʾ-w-b*), the idea of return. Meanwhile the ranks in 6:132 restrain any temptation to invent an exact point-based reward formula.

Roots are aids to careful reading, not independent authorities for creating new doctrine. The moment an etymology becomes a complete unseen biography, I have moved beyond the verse. The task is to return from the word's possibilities to the sentence's actual claims.

**Verse references:** 43:36, 50:27, 114:4, 46:31, 6:132.

## Rashad Khalifa’s Personal Views on the Jinn Companion

### Verse

> **43:36** — Anyone  who  disregards  the  message  of  the  Most  Gracious,  we  appoint  a  devil  to  be  his  constant  companion.*

> **50:23** — The companion said, “Here is my formidable testimony.*”

> **50:27** — His companion said, “Our Lord, I did not mislead him; he was far astray.”

> **72:14** — “ ‘Among us are the submitters, and among us are the compromisers.’ ” As for those who submitted, they are on the right path.

> **55:46** — For those who reverence the majesty of their Lord, two gardens (one for  the  jinns  and  one  for  the  humans).

> **17:36** — You  shall  not  accept  any  information,  unless  you  verify  it  for  yourself. I  have  given  you  the  hearing,  the eyesight,  and  the  brain,  and  you  are responsible for using them.

### Reflection on the Verse

Rashad Khalifa's account cannot be omitted from a serious inquiry because the introduction and Appendix 7 of his *Quran: The Final Testament* present a detailed model of the jinn companion. That detail makes a distinction even more necessary: **a verse, a translator's note, an appendix, and a personal statement in a recorded discussion are not the same kind of evidence.** I present his views here under his own name.

#### 1. Companionship from birth and the representation of Satan's position

In his [Introduction](https://www.masjidtucson.org/quran/appendices/introduction.html), Rashad Khalifa argues that a jinn is assigned whenever a human is born. Its initial role is to represent Satan's point of view: the claim that GOD alone is not enough. [Appendix 7](https://www.masjidtucson.org/quran/appendices/appendix7.html) develops this account, saying that the companion remains with the human from birth until death and serves as a witness at judgment. The introduction also claims that after the human dies, the jinn is released and lives on for several centuries.

These are not details expressly stated in 43:36. That verse connects a devil companion to disregard of the Most Gracious' message. Nor does 50:23-28 specify the birth of a second being at the same moment as every human, or disclose the remaining centuries of a companion's life. Khalifa's model is a **composite interpretation**, not a replacement for the verses' individual wording.

#### 2. The assertion that the companion shares its human's name

In a 1989 recording identified as [Quran Study 49](https://submission.ws/en/media/watch/zhCEM0fy2fo), around **38:19-39:10**, Khalifa states that the jinn companion is born at the same time as the human and bears the same name. He illustrates this with his own name. This claim can be directly attributed to him because it appears in the recording. Yet no explicit same-name rule appears in the Arabic and English text of 43:36 or 50:27.

This matters because the assertion is unusually specific. I can examine what the speaker meant and why it fits his model. I cannot promote an unexpressed biographical detail into a rule spoken explicitly by GOD.

#### 3. Can the companion change? Khalifa's most consequential argument

In [Appendix 7](https://www.masjidtucson.org/quran/appendices/appendix7.html), Khalifa argues that a human who convinces the jinn companion to uphold GOD's absolute authority can be redeemed together with it. Conversely, he portrays the companion's success in persuading the human to accept Satan's position as a disaster for both. His [Introduction](https://www.masjidtucson.org/quran/appendices/introduction.html) states that many companions are converted to GOD's point of view by their human counterparts.

This is the most interesting part of the model to me. The jinn is not treated as an unchangeable machine built for evil. It is another moral participant whose own direction may be altered. But the possibility that a companion can change is very different from knowledge that a particular companion has changed. Verses 72:11, 72:14, and 46:31 leave room for the former; they do not certify the latter.

#### 4. The claim that the companion hears the Quran and takes part in an inner debate

In a recorded [Quran study](https://submission.ws/en/media/watch/OUw7qBTWy8c), around **30:24-32:56** and **1:20:15-1:22:20**, Khalifa describes the jinn companion as attending to what its human hears or reads from the Quran. He portrays the companion as initially objecting to worship and later becoming convinced, perhaps even cooperating with its human in pursuing what is right.

The account offers a dramatic picture of mutual moral influence. It does not establish an observable physical mechanism by which a companion enters thoughts, follows a person's reading, or supplies specific sentences at specific moments. That distinction is especially important when the topic touches mental experience.

I do not take the recordings as instructions to hold compulsory conversations with invisible beings or to label every thought as a jinn's voice. The explicit direction of 41:36 and 7:200 is to seek refuge in GOD; 17:36 reinforces responsibility for verifying information.

#### 5. Direct contact with Satan or representation of his ideology?

In [another recorded discussion](https://submission.ws/en/media/watch/pXJ7Cku7LEk), around **23:12-24:06**, Khalifa describes the companion as a representative of Satan whose initial instinct favors evasion of worship. His language of representing Satan should not automatically be converted into a detailed claim of ongoing communication between Satan and every jinn.

Within his wider account, jinn were beings more firmly attracted to Satan's claim during the dispute in the Heavenly Society. But 38:69 mentions the dispute without recording every creature's private conversation, the exact proportions of the groups, or the method by which later companions are paired. Those further steps belong to his reconstruction.

#### 6. Pairing, foreknowledge, and freedom

In the same [study transcript](https://submission.ws/en/media/watch/OUw7qBTWy8c), approximately **32:42-33:01**, Khalifa discusses a believing human being matched with a jinn destined to believe, and a disbelieving human matched with a corresponding jinn. His Appendix 7, however, emphasizes the possibility of mutual persuasion. That produces a genuine question: how should an outcome described as already known or matched be understood alongside the freedom to change?

One possible reading is that Khalifa is attempting to hold together GOD's foreknowledge and creatures' freedom of choice. But I should not pretend that this resolves every tension in his recorded explanations. An honest inquiry may have to leave the problem open instead of presenting a perfect system never stated as such in the verses.

#### 7. Redemption, Paradise, and reward

For Khalifa, a companion's departure from Satan's position concerns its own salvation, not only its ability to influence the human. That is why the possibility of joint redemption is central to [Appendix 7](https://www.masjidtucson.org/quran/appendices/appendix7.html). The model can be considered alongside 55:46 and 55:62, which refer to gardens including jinn. Yet neither this model nor the cited verses provides a fixed points scheme in which a companion receives a specified rank for converting a certain number of others.

I have explored the limits of claims about heavenly degrees in [Entering Paradise or Reaching the High Heaven?](https://berkkoksal.com/writings/cennete-girmek-mi-ust-cennete-ulasmak-mi-en/). The boundary returns here. Taking another being's hope of redemption seriously is not the same as knowing that being's rank in Paradise. Verses 6:132 and 46:19 affirm justice and recompense without opening every individual's account to us.

Khalifa's views deserve careful consideration precisely as his views. His emphasis on the absolute authority of GOD raises morally serious questions. It does not require me to treat every detail of his reconstruction as though it were an independent verse. I can listen attentively, test the claims, and leave final religious authority where his central principle itself says it belongs: with GOD alone.

**Verse references:** 43:36, 50:23, 50:27, 72:14, 55:46, 17:36.

## Other Claims

### Verse

> **50:21** — Every soul comes with a herder and a witness.

> **50:23** — The companion said, “Here is my formidable testimony.*”

> **16:99** — He  has  no  power  over  those  who believe and trust in their Lord.

### Reflection on the Verse

Traditional accounts also discuss the *qarin* in varying ways. Some hadith-based explanations say that every human has a companion from among the jinn; some describe an exception involving the Prophet's companion and its submission. Examples can be found in [summaries of the traditional position](https://archive-1446.islamqa.info/amp/en/answers/26226). I can acknowledge that these reports exist without making their details identical to the Quran's explicit wording.

Another reading of 50:23 identifies the companion in that verse more closely with a witness in the judgment scene than with the jinn accused of misleading the person. The herder and witness in 50:21 make that question understandable. Whether the companion of 50:23 and the companion of 50:27 have precisely the same role remains a matter for contextual analysis, not a detail settled by the bare word alone.

There is also a tension between the conditional phrasing of 43:36 and the account of universal companionship from birth. Some may distinguish an earlier attachment from a later active devilish influence. That is a possible harmonization, not something explicitly stated in the verse itself.

Popular accounts sometimes attribute knowledge of every human secret, independent access to the future, or sweeping control over human destiny to a companion. Each such claim needs evidence of its own. The limits described in 16:99-100 and 17:65 give no warrant for granting Satan unlimited sovereignty.

I neither accept a tradition simply because it is old nor reject a person because an interpretation differs from mine. The questions are more precise: Which passage supports the claim? What does that passage actually say? Where does commentary begin? An honest inquiry need not fill every hidden detail.

**Verse references:** 43:36, 50:21, 50:23, 50:27, 16:99.

## Conclusion

### Verse

> **43:36** — Anyone  who  disregards  the  message  of  the  Most  Gracious,  we  appoint  a  devil  to  be  his  constant  companion.*

> **72:11** — “ ‘Some of us are righteous, and some are less than righteous; we follow various paths.

> **72:14** — “ ‘Among us are the submitters, and among us are the compromisers.’ ” As for those who submitted, they are on the right path.

> **46:31** — “O  our  people,  respond  to  the  call of GOD, and believe in Him. He will then  forgive  your  sins,  and  spare  you a painful retribution.”

> **55:46** — For those who reverence the majesty of their Lord, two gardens (one for  the  jinns  and  one  for  the  humans).

> **6:132** — Everyone  will  attain  a  rank  commensurate  with  their  deeds.  Your Lord  is  never  unaware  of  anything they do.

> **17:65** — “As  for  My  servants,  you  have  no power  over  them.”  Your  Lord  suffices as an advocate.

### Reflection on the Verse

When I began this article, the question seemed almost simple: Who is the jinn companion? As the verses accumulated, the question became larger. It was not enough to read of its misleading influence. I wanted to understand its possible agency, recognition of error, repentance, relations with other jinn, hope of Paradise, and recompense.

The warning of 43:36 cannot be ignored. A person's relationship with the Most Gracious' message belongs at the center of the discussion. Verses 43:37 and 41:25 describe the danger of feeling guided while being drawn away from the path. Verses 50:27-29 make blaming another being an inadequate final defense.

But those are not all the passages about jinn. Verses 72:11 and 72:14-15 describe morally different paths among them. Verses 72:1-2 and 46:29-31 describe jinn who hear truth, believe, and warn their people. Verse 46:31 holds out forgiveness. Verses 55:46 and 55:62 speak of gardens in terms that include jinn. Verses 6:132 and 46:19 connect recompense with deeds and justice.

Four distinctions now seem essential.

First, **being a jinn is not identical to persisting in devilish conduct.** The moral picture of jinn in the Quran is not monochrome.

Second, **the possibility that a jinn can turn toward righteousness is not knowledge that my particular companion has already turned.** Hope for salvation is not a final verdict on an unknown being.

Third, **influence and responsibility do not cancel one another.** Satan may invite, but that invitation does not make him sovereign. Human choices remain morally significant, yet we must also examine our own assumptions and susceptibility to deception.

Fourth, **Rashad Khalifa's detailed reconstruction must remain distinct from the verses themselves.** I have considered his account of companionship from birth, a shared name, a long jinn lifespan, mutual persuasion, and joint redemption. His willingness to consider the reform of the companion makes the diversity of jinn in sura 72 especially interesting. Yet every detail still requires a clear account of its source.

I return, finally, to what belongs to me. I cannot inspect an invisible creature's future. I cannot always know the origin of every idea entering my mind. I can, however, verify claims, refuse injustice, speak truthfully, seek refuge in GOD, and avoid disregarding His message.

The most valuable result of reflecting on an unseen companion may therefore be a clearer sense of my own responsibility, not an imaginary ability to observe that being's private life. If I mistake falsehood for guidance, I ought to learn how to notice it. If a thought invites injustice, I need not accept it. If I trust GOD, I should not grant independent divine authority to something unseen.

Thinking about a companion's repentance must not become an excuse to postpone my own. Wondering whether it will enter Paradise cannot justify wasting my life. Trying to calculate its reward cannot replace GOD's just judgment.

After all these questions, one statement remains simple, though not always easy to live:

**I do not know the final fate of an unseen companion. I do know that I must not abandon my responsibility to trust GOD, seek truth, and answer for my own deeds.**

Perhaps the question I most need to ask is this: Am I making excuses for Satan's argument in my own life, or deepening my testimony to the oneness of GOD? No unseen companion can make that decision in my place.

**Verse references:** 43:36, 72:11, 72:14, 46:31, 55:46, 6:132, 17:65.
