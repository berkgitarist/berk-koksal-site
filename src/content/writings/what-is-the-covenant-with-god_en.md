# What Is the Covenant Made with GOD? What Was the Covenant, and When Was It Made?

**Author:** Berk KÖKSAL  
**Date:** July 31, 2026  
**Place:** Mersin

The main purpose of this article is to contemplate the verses together with other relevant verses and to explore, sincerely and deeply, what bearing witness to the oneness of GOD means for human life in terms of knowledge, justice, truthfulness, and submission.

When we speak about a “covenant with GOD,” the first question is natural: **What exactly did we promise?** The second is more difficult: **When was that promise made?**

The Quran does not use covenant language for only one event. It speaks of a covenant addressed to the Children of Adam, specific covenants with the Children of Israel, the believers’ statement “We hear and we obey,” obligations attached to scripture, and a distinct covenant with the prophets. If all of these are collapsed into one event, the distinctions in the text disappear.

I will therefore begin with the most universal scene: the testimony of the descendants of Adam in 7:172. Then I will examine the unusually explicit wording of 36:60-61, which tells the Children of Adam what the covenant requires. Finally, when asking “when?”, I will keep what the verse itself states separate from later interpretation.

## 1. “Am I Not Your Lord?”: Humanity’s Testimony

### Verse

**7:172**

**Arabic:** وَإِذْ أَخَذَ رَبُّكَ مِنۢ بَنِىٓ ءَادَمَ مِن ظُهُورِهِمْ ذُرِّيَّتَهُمْ وَأَشْهَدَهُمْ عَلَىٰٓ أَنفُسِهِمْ أَلَسْتُ بِرَبِّكُمْ قَالُوا۟ بَلَىٰ شَهِدْنَآ أَن تَقُولُوا۟ يَوْمَ ٱلْقِيَٰمَةِ إِنَّا كُنَّا عَنْ هَٰذَا غَٰفِلِينَ

**English:** Recall that your Lord summoned all the descendants of Adam, and had them bear witness for themselves: “Am I not your Lord?” They all said, “Yes. We bear witness.” Thus, you cannot say on the Day of Resurrection, “We were not aware of this.”

**Turkish:** Hani Rabbin Âdem’in soyundan gelenlerin tümünü toplamış ve onlara kendileri için şahitlik ettirmişti: “Ben sizin Rabbinizim değil mi?” Hepsi de “Evet. Biz şahitlik ediyoruz” dediler. Böylece, Diriliş Günü’nde “Biz bunun farkında değildik” diyemezsiniz.

### Reflection on the Verse

7:172 opens a remarkable scene. The descendants of Adam are summoned and made to bear witness concerning themselves. The question is not merely whether GOD exists. The question is: **“Am I not your Lord?”** The response is collective and explicit: **“Yes. We bear witness.”**

That wording matters. The human being is not merely given information about an external reality; the verse speaks of people bearing witness for themselves. The testimony reaches into identity and responsibility.

The focus on Lordship is also important. To recognize GOD as Lord is not simply to acknowledge a proposition. It raises the question of ultimate belonging, authority, worship, and return.

The verse itself then connects this testimony to accountability on the Day of Resurrection. The testimony therefore functions not only as knowledge but as the ground on which excuses are removed.

**Verse reference: 7:172.**

## 2. What Is the Covenant? Do Not Worship the Devil; Worship GOD Alone

### Verse

> **36:60** — Did  I  not  covenant  with  you,  O Children of Adam, that you shall not worship the devil? That he is your most ardent enemy?

> **36:61** — And  that  you  shall  worship  Me alone? This is the right path.

### Reflection on the Verse

If the question is, “What was the covenant actually about?”, 36:60-61 gives one of the clearest answers in the Quran. GOD addresses the **Children of Adam** and explicitly speaks of a covenant.

Its moral direction has two sides.

First: **do not worship the devil.**

Second: **worship GOD alone.**

Then comes the conclusion: **“This is the right path.”**

This removes the covenant from the realm of vague religious mystery. In the wording of the Quran, the covenant is centered on Tawhid. Humanity is not to hand its worship to the enemy that leads it away from GOD; worship belongs to GOD alone.

Read beside 7:172, a powerful coherence appears. One passage presents the testimony to GOD’s Lordship; the other states the covenantal direction of life under that Lordship. Yet because the verses do not explicitly say, “These are exactly the same event,” it is more careful to describe the relationship as a strong textual connection rather than an unqualified identity.

**Verse references: 36:60-61; 7:172.**

## 3. When Was the Covenant Made? What the Verse Says—and Does Not Say

### Verse

> **7:172** — Recall that your Lord summoned all the descendants of Adam, and had them bear witness for themselves: “Am I not your Lord?” They all said, “Yes. We bear witness.” Thus, you cannot say on the Day of Resurrection, “We were not aware of this.”

### Reflection on the Verse

The most important discipline in answering “when?” is refusing to become more precise than the text.

7:172 recalls an event in which the descendants of Adam were summoned and made to testify. The verse also tells us why the testimony matters: so that on the Day of Resurrection people cannot say, “We were not aware of this.”

But the verse does not give a calendar date. It does not tell us, in its own wording, how many stages before earthly birth this happened, the physical or spiritual mechanism involved, or a detailed chronology of pre-earthly existence.

What can be said directly is this: **the Quran describes a testimony involving all the descendants of Adam and places it prior to the final accountability in which ignorance can no longer be claimed.**

Questions about the precise metaphysical mechanics belong to interpretation. I will return to those in the “Other Claims” section.

The safest Quran-centered answer to “when?” is therefore not an invented date. It is that the testimony is presented as already established before the Day of Resurrection and as relevant to human accountability, while the verse leaves its detailed cosmological timing unstated.

**Verse reference: 7:172.**

## 4. Why Was the Testimony Taken? Closing the Excuse “We Did Not Know”

### Verse

> **7:172** — Recall that your Lord summoned all the descendants of Adam, and had them bear witness for themselves: “Am I not your Lord?” They all said, “Yes. We bear witness.” Thus, you cannot say on the Day of Resurrection, “We were not aware of this.”

> **7:173** — Nor can you say, “It was our parents who  practiced  idolatry,  and  we simply followed in their footsteps. Will You punish us because of what others have innovated?” 

### Reflection on the Verse

7:172-173 closes two major excuses.

The first is: **“We were not aware.”**

The second is: **“Our parents practiced idolatry; we merely followed them.”**

This moves the covenant away from being a curiosity about an unseen past and into the heart of moral responsibility.

A person may inherit religious language, customs, fears, and loyalties from family or society. But inherited practice cannot become an absolute defense before GOD.

The human being remains responsible for his or her own response to truth.

The covenant therefore means more than a forgotten event. It means that “I only followed the environment I inherited” cannot erase personal responsibility.

**Verse references: 7:172-173.**

## 5. Tawhid Is at the Center of the Covenant

### Verse

> **36:60** — Did  I  not  covenant  with  you,  O Children of Adam, that you shall not worship the devil? That he is your most ardent enemy?

> **36:61** — And  that  you  shall  worship  Me alone? This is the right path.

### Reflection on the Verse

36:60-61 makes the center of the covenant strikingly simple: **worship GOD alone.**

This simplicity matters because covenant language can easily become buried under many secondary details. The Quran later speaks about justice, scripture, prayer, charity, promises, family duties, and social responsibility. But the center remains the question of ultimate worship.

Tawhid is therefore not merely the statement that one GOD exists. It asks where ultimate obedience, fear, hope, dependence, and devotion are directed.

The covenant asks a basic question beneath all religious identity: **Whom do I serve?**

Rejecting servitude to the devil and worshiping GOD alone is called the right path. The covenant is therefore not only a proposition about GOD; it is a direction of life.

**Verse references: 36:60-61.**

## 6. “He Has Taken a Pledge from You”: Faith and Covenant

### Verse

> **57:8** — Why should you not believe in GOD when the messenger is inviting you to believe in your Lord? He has taken a pledge from you, if you are believers.

### Reflection on the Verse

57:8 places the remembered pledge directly inside the call to faith. The messenger invites people to believe in their Lord, while the verse reminds them that a pledge has already been taken from them.

This makes the covenant more than a distant memory. It is brought into the present moment of belief.

Revelation does not introduce humanity to an alien Lord. It calls people back to **their Lord**.

The verse therefore gives faith the quality of remembrance and response. Belief is not simply acquiring new information; it is answering a claim already connected to human responsibility.

**Verse reference: 57:8.**

## 7. “We Hear and We Obey”: The Lived Response to Covenant

### Verse

> **5:7** — Remember  GOD’s  blessing  upon you,  and  His  covenant  that  He covenanted  with  you:  you  said,  “We hear  and  we  obey.”  You  shall  observe GOD; GOD  is  fully aware of the innermost thoughts.

### Reflection on the Verse

5:7 summarizes the response to covenant in two movements: **hearing and obeying.**

That matters because a covenant cannot be reduced to having once said “yes.” Testimony must become responsiveness when guidance arrives.

Hearing without living turns revelation into information.

Obedience without knowing whom one ultimately obeys can also become dangerous when human authority is confused with divine authority.

The verse keeps the covenant anchored in GOD and immediately reminds us that GOD knows the innermost thoughts. The covenant therefore reaches speech, intention, and action together.

**Verse reference: 5:7.**

## 8. Is the Covenant Only a Sentence? Commandments, Justice, and the Straight Path

### Verse

> **6:151** — Say,  “Come  let  me  tell  you  what your  Lord  has  really  prohibited  for you: You shall not set up idols besides  Him.  You  shall  honor  your parents.  You  shall  not  kill  your children  from  fear  of  poverty—we provide  for  you  and  for  them.  You shall  not  commit  gross  sins,  obvious or hidden. You shall not kill—GOD has  made  life  sacred—except  in  the course  of  justice.  These  are  His commandments  to  you,  that  you may understand.”

> **6:152** — You  shall  not  touch  the  orphans’ money  except  in  the  most  righteous manner,  until  they  reach  maturity. You shall give full weight and full measure  when  you  trade,  equitably. We  do  not  burden  any  soul  beyond its  means.  You  shall  be  absolutely just  when  you  bear  witness,  even against  your  relatives.  You  shall  fulfill your covenant with GOD. These are  His  commandments  to  you,  that you may take heed.

> **6:153** — This  is  My  path—a  straight  one. You shall follow it, and do not follow  any  other  paths,  lest  they  divert you  from  His  path.  These  are  His commandments  to  you,  that  you may be saved.

### Reflection on the Verse

6:151-153 forms a powerful unit. It begins with the prohibition of idols and continues with honoring parents, protecting children, avoiding gross sins, respecting the sanctity of life, guarding orphan wealth, using equitable measure, and bearing just testimony.

Then 6:152 says: **“You shall fulfill your covenant with GOD.”**

The next verse gathers these commandments under the language of the straight path.

The covenant is therefore not merely a metaphysical confession. Recognition of GOD’s Lordship enters ethics.

If a person claims to worship GOD alone while cheating in measure, consuming the rights of the vulnerable, or abandoning justice when relatives are involved, the spoken covenant and the lived covenant have separated.

Tawhid does not remove ethics. It gives ethics its center.

**Verse references: 6:151-153.**

## 9. The Specific Covenant with the Children of Israel

### Verse

> **2:83** — We made a covenant with the Children  of  Israel:  “You  shall  not  worship except GOD. You shall honor your  parents  and  regard  the  relatives,  the  orphans,  and  the  poor. You shall treat the people amicably. You shall observe the Contact Prayers (Salat) and give the obligatory charity (Zakat).” But you turned away, except a few of you, and  you became averse.

> **2:84** — We  made  a  covenant  with  you,  that you  shall  not  shed  your  blood,  nor shall  you  evict  each  other  from  your homes.  You  agreed  and  bore  witness.

### Reflection on the Verse

Alongside the universal address to the Children of Adam, the Quran also records covenants with particular communities. 2:83-84 is an explicit example involving the Children of Israel.

The covenant again begins with monotheism: **worship none except GOD.** It then includes honoring parents, relatives, orphans, and the poor, treating people amicably, observing prayer, giving charity, not shedding one another’s blood, and not expelling one another from their homes.

This teaches an essential distinction.

The word “covenant” in the Quran does not refer to only one event.

And yet the specific communal covenant still reflects the same moral center: devotion to GOD and responsibility toward people.

It would therefore be inaccurate to collapse the covenant of the Children of Israel into the same historical event as the testimony in 7:172.

**Verse references: 2:83-84.**

## 10. Mount Sinai: Holding Firmly to the Covenant

### Verse

> **2:63** — We made a covenant with you, as we raised Mount Sinai above you: “You shall uphold what we have given you strongly,  and  remember  its  contents, that you may be saved.”

> **2:93** — We made a covenant with you, as we raised  Mount  Sinai  above  you, saying, “You shall uphold the commandments  we  have  given  you, strongly, and listen.” They said, “We hear, but we disobey.” Their hearts became filled with adoration for the calf,  due  to  their  disbelief.  Say, “Miserable indeed is what your faith dictates upon you, if you do have any faith.”

### Reflection on the Verse

2:63 and 2:93 speak about covenant in the setting of Mount Sinai. The repeated demand is to **uphold strongly** what was given and to listen.

This covenant belongs to a particular historical and communal context. That is another reason why the question “When did GOD make the covenant?” cannot be answered with a single date for every covenant mentioned in the Quran.

The covenant addressed to the Children of Adam is one layer; the Sinai covenant with the Children of Israel is another.

2:93 also shows the opposite of covenant faithfulness: “We hear, but we disobey.” Knowledge of the command is not the same as fidelity to it.

A covenant requires more than hearing. It requires holding firmly to what was given.

**Verse references: 2:63, 2:93.**

## 11. What Happens When the Covenant Is Violated?

### Verse

> **5:12** — GOD  had  taken  a  covenant  from  the Children  of  Israel,  and  we  raised among  them  twelve  patriarchs.  And GOD said, “I am with you, so long as you  observe  the  Contact  Prayers (Salat),  give  the  obligatory  charity (Zakat),  and  believe  in  My  messengers  and  respect  them,  and  continue  to  lend  GOD  a    loan    of righteousness.  I  will  then  remit  your sins,  and  admit  you  into  gardens with  flowing  streams.  Anyone  who disbelieves  after  this,  has  indeed strayed off the right path.”

> **5:13** — It  was  a  consequence  of  their  violating  the  covenant  that  we  condemned  them,  and  we  caused  their hearts  to  become  hardened.  Consequently,  they  took  the  words  out  of context,  and  disregarded  some  of the  commandments  given  to  them. You  will  continue  to  witness  betrayal  from  them, excepting  a  few of them.  You  shall  pardon  them,  and disregard  them.  GOD  loves  those who are benevolent.

### Reflection on the Verse

5:12 lists concrete elements of covenant faithfulness: prayer, charity, belief in and respect for the messengers, and righteous giving. It then speaks of forgiveness and Paradise.

5:13 describes the consequences of violating the covenant: hardened hearts, words taken out of context, and commandments disregarded.

That sequence is worth contemplating.

A covenant can first become inconvenient.

Then words can be rearranged around desire.

Then inconvenient commandments can quietly disappear from practice.

Eventually a person may still speak the language of scripture while living far from the responsibility the covenant required.

Covenant faithfulness therefore includes allowing revelation to correct us rather than reshaping revelation around ourselves.

**Verse references: 5:12-13.**

## 12. Upholding Scripture and Speaking Only the Truth About GOD

### Verse

> **7:169** — Subsequent to them, He substituted new generations who inherited the scripture. But they opted for the worldly life instead, saying, “We will be forgiven.” But then they continued to opt for the materials of this world. Did they not make a covenant to uphold the scripture, and not to say about GOD except the truth? Did they not study the scripture?  Certainly,  the  abode  of  the Hereafter is far better for those who maintain righteousness. Do you not understand?

> **7:170** — Those  who  uphold  the  scripture, and  observe  the  Contact  Prayers (Salat), we never fail to recompense the pious.

> **7:171** — We raised the mountain above them like an umbrella, and they thought it was going to fall on them: “You shall uphold what we have given you, strongly,  and  remember  the  contents thereof, that you may be saved.”

### Reflection on the Verse

7:169 places two responsibilities together: upholding scripture and not saying about GOD except the truth.

This makes theological speech a covenantal responsibility.

To remain faithful is not merely to perform acts of worship. It is also to refuse to present tradition, desire, speculation, or personal opinion as though it were revelation from GOD.

7:170 gives the positive picture of those who uphold scripture and prayer. 7:171 repeats the command to hold firmly to what has been given.

The covenant therefore asks us not merely to possess scripture, but to be possessed by its moral demand—to allow it to correct us rather than using it only to confirm us.

**Verse references: 7:169-171.**

## 13. What Does a Covenant-Keeping Life Look Like?

### Verse

> **13:20** — They  are  the  ones  who  fulfill  their pledge  to  GOD,  and  do  not  violate the covenant.

> **13:21** — They  join  what  GOD  has  commanded  to  be  joined,  reverence their  Lord,  and  fear  the  dreadful reckoning.

> **13:22** — They  steadfastly  persevere  in  seeking  their  Lord,  observe  the  Contact Prayers  (Salat),  spend  from  our provisions  to  them  secretly  and publicly,  and  counter  evil  with  good. These  have  deserved  the  best  abode. 

### Reflection on the Verse

13:20 speaks of those who fulfill their pledge to GOD and do not violate the covenant. The following verses show what that faithfulness looks like.

They preserve what GOD commands to be joined.

They reverence their Lord.

They fear the reckoning.

They persevere.

They observe prayer.

They spend secretly and publicly.

They counter evil with good.

The covenant becomes visible in relationships, worship, money, patience, and response to wrongdoing.

Perhaps the healthiest way to remember the covenant is therefore not to remain trapped only in the question “What happened long ago?” but to ask: **What is the promise becoming in my life today?**

**Verse references: 13:20-22.**

## 14. What Is Broken When the Covenant Is Broken?

### Verse

> **13:25** — As  for  those  who  violate  GOD’s covenant  after  pledging  to  keep it,  and  sever  what  GOD  has  commanded  to  be  joined,  and  commit evil,  they  have  incurred  condemnation;  they  have  incurred  the  worst destiny.

> **2:27** — who violate GOD’s covenant after pledging to uphold it, sever what GOD has commanded to be joined, and  commit  evil.  These  are  the losers.

### Reflection on the Verse

13:25 and 2:27 present almost the same moral structure: violating GOD’s covenant, severing what GOD commanded to be joined, and committing evil.

This means covenant is not a private spirituality sealed off between the individual and GOD.

When the bond with GOD is corrupted, the damage can appear in human bonds as well.

Family responsibility, justice, trust, promises, and social order are not detached from Tawhid.

A claim of faithfulness to GOD that produces the destruction of human responsibilities contradicts the integrated picture in these verses.

The covenant returns the human being to the world with greater responsibility, not less.

**Verse references: 13:25; 2:27.**

## 15. Why Is a Covenant a Great Responsibility?

### Verse

> **16:91** — You shall fulfill your covenant with GOD when you make such a covenant. You shall not violate the oaths after swearing (by God) to carry them out, for you have made GOD a guarantor  for  you.  GOD  knows everything you do.

> **17:34** — You  shall  not  touch  the  orphans’ money  except  for  their  own  good, until  they  reach  maturity.  You  shall fulfill  your  covenants,  for  a  covenant is a great responsibility.

### Reflection on the Verse

16:91 commands people to fulfill a covenant made with GOD and not to violate oaths after making GOD a guarantor. 17:34 gives the principle in a simple sentence: **a covenant is a great responsibility.**

This brings covenant language into ordinary moral life as well.

To invoke GOD, give one’s word, and then abandon that word when interests change is not merely a social failure. It enters the field of accountability before GOD.

Promises should therefore not be religious decoration for emotional moments.

A covenant binds future conduct.

It asks not only, “What do you believe?” but also, “What kind of person are you after you give your word?”

**Verse references: 16:91; 17:34.**

## 16. Trading the Covenant Away for a Cheap Price

### Verse

> **3:77** — As for those who trade away GOD’s covenant, and their obligations, for a cheap price, they receive no share in the Hereafter. GOD will not speak to them, nor look at them, on the Day of Resurrection, nor will He purify them. They have incurred a painful retribution.

### Reflection on the Verse

3:77 uses a severe commercial image: people can trade away GOD’s covenant and their obligations for a **cheap price**.

The covenant does not always have to be rejected openly. It can be exchanged.

For money.

For prestige.

For social position.

For belonging.

For short-term safety.

For the satisfaction of winning.

The phrase “cheap price” exposes the scale of the exchange. Whatever appears large in this world remains small when compared with the responsibility before GOD.

The real test of covenant may therefore come when faithfulness becomes costly.

**Verse reference: 3:77.**

## 17. A Distinct Covenant with the Prophets

### Verse

> **3:81** — GOD took a covenant from the prophets, saying, “I will give you the scripture and wisdom. Afterwards, a messenger will come to confirm all existing scriptures. You shall believe in him and support him.” He said, “Do you agree with this, and pledge to fulfill this covenant?” They said, “We agree.” He said, “You have thus borne witness, and I bear witness along with you.”

### Reflection on the Verse

3:81 explicitly identifies another covenant: **a covenant with the prophets.**

The parties are not all the Children of Adam. The content is also distinct from 36:60-61. The prophets are given scripture and wisdom, and they are told to believe in and support a messenger who comes confirming the existing scriptures.

This distinction is essential.

The Quran contains humanity’s testimony to Lordship.

It contains a covenantal command to the Children of Adam to worship GOD alone.

It contains specific covenants with the Children of Israel.

And it contains a separate covenant with the prophets.

The text itself gives us layered covenant language.

**Verse reference: 3:81.**

## 18. A “Solemn Pledge”: Is the Prophets’ Covenant the Same as Humanity’s Covenant?

### Verse

> **33:7** — Recall  that  we  took  from  the  prophets  their  covenant,  including  you (O  Muhammad),  Noah,  Abraham, Moses,  and  Jesus  the  son  of  Mary. We  took  from  them  a  solemn pledge.*

> **33:8** — Subsequently,  He  will  surely  question  the  truthful  about  their  truthfulness,  and  has  prepared  for  the disbelievers  (in  this  Quranic  fact)  a painful retribution.

### Reflection on the Verse

33:7 recalls the covenant with the prophets and calls it a **solemn pledge**, naming Muhammad, Noah, Abraham, Moses, and Jesus son of Mary.

Read beside 7:172 and 36:60-61, the most important lesson is the need to preserve distinctions.

The subject of 7:172 is the descendants of Adam.

The addressees of 36:60 are the Children of Adam.

The parties in 3:81 and 33:7 are the prophets.

Therefore, the question “When was the covenant with GOD made?” should not be answered by forcing every covenant verse into one moment.

The universal human question concerns Lordship and worship. The prophetic covenant has its own content.

Keeping these layers distinct prevents later interpretations from replacing what the verses themselves actually say.

**Verse references: 33:7-8; 3:81; 7:172; 36:60-61.**

## 19. Arabic Word Roots: Covenant, Testimony, Lordship, and Worship

### Verse

> **36:60** — Did  I  not  covenant  with  you,  O Children of Adam, that you shall not worship the devil? That he is your most ardent enemy?

> **36:61** — And  that  you  shall  worship  Me alone? This is the right path.

### Reflection on the Verse

Several Arabic root fields help clarify the covenant language.

**ع ه د — ʿ-h-d:** The word **أَعْهَدْ — aʿhad** in 36:60 comes from this root. Its field includes covenanting, charging, entrusting, and binding someone by a commitment. In the verse, GOD’s charge to the Children of Adam concerns not worshiping the devil.

**و ث ق — w-th-q:** This is the root behind **مِيثَاق — mīthāq**, a binding or firmly established covenant. This word family appears in passages such as 5:7, 5:12, and 3:81.

**ش ه د — sh-h-d:** In 7:172, **شَهِدْنَا — shahidnā** means “we bear witness.” Humanity is not merely informed; it is made witness concerning itself.

**ر ب ب — r-b-b:** In 7:172, **رَبِّكُمْ — rabbikum** means “your Lord.” The center of the testimony is GOD’s Lordship.

**ع ب د — ʿ-b-d:** In 36:61, **اعْبُدُونِي — uʿbudūnī** carries the command to worship/serve GOD. The covenant moves from acknowledged Lordship into exclusive worship.

Placed together, these roots suggest a coherent movement:

**recognizing Lordship → bearing witness → carrying a binding commitment → worshiping GOD alone.**

The covenant is therefore not merely a “yes” spoken in an unseen past. It is testimony to Lordship becoming worship in the present.

For linguistic verification: <a href="https://corpus.quran.com/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Quranic Arabic Corpus</a>.

**Verse references: 7:172; 36:60-61; 5:7; 3:81.**

## Other Claims

### Verse

> **7:172** — Recall that your Lord summoned all the descendants of Adam, and had them bear witness for themselves: “Am I not your Lord?” They all said, “Yes. We bear witness.” Thus, you cannot say on the Day of Resurrection, “We were not aware of this.”

> **7:173** — Nor can you say, “It was our parents who  practiced  idolatry,  and  we simply followed in their footsteps. Will You punish us because of what others have innovated?” 

### Reflection on the Verse

Across Islamic intellectual history, 7:172-173 has often been discussed under names such as the **Covenant of Alast**, the primordial covenant, or *mīthāq alast*. But there has not been only one interpretation of how the event should be understood.

A major stream of classical commentary treats it as a real act of testimony involving Adam’s descendants. Ibn Kathir’s commentary on 7:172 presents the descendants as testifying to GOD’s Lordship and relates the account to the human *fitrah*. See: <a href="https://quran.com/7:172/tafsirs/en-tafisr-ibn-kathir" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Quran.com — Ibn Kathir, 7:172</a>.

An academic survey of the tafsir literature identifies two broad approaches: one reads the event as a real pre-earthly occurrence, while another reads it figuratively as an expression of the human disposition to recognize GOD’s existence and oneness. This disagreement itself is a reason to distinguish the Quran’s wording from later models explaining how the event occurred. See: <a href="https://dergipark.org.tr/en/pub/auifd/article/487592" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">The Primordial Covenant between God and Man (Bazm-i Alast) in the Tafsīr Literature</a>.

The note attached to 7:172 in Rashad Khalifa’s **Authorized English Translation** also states that every human being is born with instinctive knowledge about GOD. The Turkish text reproduces this as a Turkish translation of the Authorized English Translation; it is not itself an authorized translation. The note is explanatory material attached to the translation, not the wording of the verse itself.

The note attached to 33:7 requires additional caution. The **Turkish translation of the Authorized English Translation** says the prophets’ covenant was made before the creation of the world and was fulfilled in Mecca on **January 20, 1972**. Rashad Khalifa’s **Authorized English Translation**, however, gives the fulfillment date in its source note as **Dec. 21, 1971**. The Turkish translation and the Authorized English Translation therefore disagree on that date.

For that reason, this article does not treat either date as though it were stated in 33:7. The verse itself says that a solemn covenant was taken from the prophets; it does not provide a date for that covenant in the verse text.

The safest common ground remains what the verses directly establish: 7:172 describes humanity’s testimony to GOD’s Lordship; 36:60-61 states the covenantal command to the Children of Adam to reject worship of the devil and worship GOD alone; 3:81 and 33:7 describe a distinct covenant with the prophets. Detailed metaphysical timelines belong to interpretation and should remain distinguishable from the verse itself.

## Conclusion

### Verse

> **36:60** — Did  I  not  covenant  with  you,  O Children of Adam, that you shall not worship the devil? That he is your most ardent enemy?

> **36:61** — And  that  you  shall  worship  Me alone? This is the right path.

### Reflection on the Verse

We can now return directly to the three questions at the beginning.

**What is the covenant made with GOD?**

At the universal human level, the clearest covenant wording addressed to the Children of Adam is in 36:60-61: **do not worship the devil; worship GOD alone.** This is called the right path.

7:172 takes the matter deeper. All the descendants of Adam are described as bearing witness to GOD’s Lordship: “Am I not your Lord?”—“Yes. We bear witness.”

So, staying within what the verses support, the heart of the covenant can be expressed this way:

**Recognize GOD as Lord, worship Him alone, and do not surrender to the direction that leads away from Him.**

Then comes the second question: **When was it made?**

7:172 describes the descendants of Adam being summoned and made to testify. The purpose is tied to future accountability on the Day of Resurrection, so that ignorance cannot be claimed. But the verse itself does not provide a calendar date or a detailed cosmological chronology.

It is therefore more careful to say that the Quran presents the testimony as already established before final judgment, without claiming a more precise timeline than the verse gives.

The Quran also speaks of multiple other covenants. There are covenants with the Children of Israel in the Sinai setting. There is the remembered covenant associated with “We hear and we obey.” There is covenantal responsibility to uphold scripture and speak truth about GOD. There is a distinct covenant with the prophets.

Once these layers are separated, the larger picture becomes clearer.

The universal covenant is centered on Tawhid.

But Tawhid is not merely a sentence.

6:151-153 links covenant to justice, the sanctity of life, protection of the vulnerable, fair measure, and truthful testimony.

13:20-22 shows covenant faithfulness in perseverance, prayer, generosity, preserved relationships, and answering evil with good.

16:91 and 17:34 make promise-keeping a serious responsibility.

3:77 warns against selling GOD’s covenant for a cheap price.

So the question “When did I make a covenant with GOD?” should not remain only a search for the distant past.

The harder question is:

**Am I faithful to that covenant now?**

Do I truly recognize GOD as Lord?

Do I worship Him alone?

What happens when fear, status, tradition, ego, a person, or an institution competes with that allegiance?

When revelation reaches me, can I say, “We hear and we obey”?

When justice works against my own interest, do I still hold to the covenant?

When I give my word, do I remember that GOD sees it?

Do I excuse inherited error by saying, “I only followed those before me”?

7:173 closes that escape.

Perhaps this is the deepest meaning of covenant: throughout life, the human being continually reveals who is truly Lord at the center of his or her existence.

The testimony may belong to the past; faithfulness is lived in the present.

And the Quran gives the right path in the simplest possible form:

**Worship GOD alone.**
