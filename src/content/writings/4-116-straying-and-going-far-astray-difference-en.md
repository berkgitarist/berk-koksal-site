# [4:116] What Is the Difference Between Straying and Going Far Astray? Guidance, Monotheism, and the Limits of Distance

**19 Reflections on Guidance, Monotheism, Truth, Justice, and the Direction of the Self**

**Author:** Berk KÖKSAL

## Introduction

Peace. Peace be upon you.

Calling someone 'astray' is already a grave statement. Calling someone 'far astray' sounds still more severe. Does the Quran use those expressions as two formally defined spiritual categories? Or does it describe departure from guidance with different qualifications in different settings? I do not want to settle a question about people's hearts before examining the language.

Two verses first caught my attention. In 2:16, straying is chosen at the expense of guidance. In 4:116, a warning about idolizing something beside GOD ends with being far astray. When I compare the Arabic, the root **ض ل ل** appears in both, while 4:116 adds a qualification associated with distance: **بَعِيدًا**. Yet this is not the whole story. Verse 22:12 uses an Arabic construction involving distance where the authorized English text says 'real straying.' In 67:29, the English and Turkish convey being far astray while the Arabic uses a different adjective. Ignoring those details would let my search vocabulary create a certainty the wording has not earned.

I am not writing to expel people, assign fixed ranks of condemnation, or claim the most severe religious labels for my opponents. The urgent issue is the course of my own life. Do I replace guidance with my preferences? Can I remain just while disagreeing? Do I treat a familiar opinion as untouchable? Am I willing to turn around when I recognize a mistake?

In <a href="https://berkkoksal.com/writings/we_found_our_parents_doing_this_en/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">We Found Our Parents Doing This</a> I explored the difference between inheriting a belief and establishing it as true. In <a href="https://berkkoksal.com/writings/the-fine-line-between-proclaiming-and-proclaiming-yourself/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">The Fine Line Between Proclaiming and Proclaiming Yourself</a> I asked how someone who carries a message might begin to elevate himself. Both concerns meet here: finding a word is easy; using it without pretending to possess GOD's final knowledge requires much greater care.

My intention is to reflect on the verses together and ask what witnessing to the oneness of GOD demands of knowledge, justice, truthful speech, and submission. Before these verses allow me to question another person's direction, I want to allow them to question mine.

## 1. Far Astray: Where Does the Weight of the Expression Come From?

### Verse

> **4:116**
>
> Arabic: إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ لَا يَغْفِرُ أَن يُشْرَكَ بِهِۦ وَيَغْفِرُ مَا دُونَ ذَٰلِكَ لِمَن يَشَآءُ وَمَن يُشْرِكْ بِٱللَّهِ فَقَدْ ضَلَّ ضَلَٰلًۢا بَعِيدًا
>
> English: GOD  does  not  forgive  idol  worship (if  maintained  until  death),*  and  He forgives  lesser  offenses  for  whomever  He  wills.  Anyone  who  idolizes any  idol  beside  GOD  has  strayed  far astray.
>
> Turkish: TANRI puta tapmayı (eğer ölene kadar devam ettirilirse) bağışlamaz* ve O, daha küçük suçları irade ettiği kimse için bağışlar. Her kim TANRI’nın yanında herhangi bir puta taparsa, uzak bir sapkınlıkla sapmıştır.

Source: <a href="https://kuranteyit.com/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">kuranteyit.com</a>

Translation: Rashad Khalifa’s Authorized English Translation; the Turkish text is the Turkish translation of the Authorized English Translation.

### Reflection on the Verse

What arrests my attention in 4:116 is the language of distance added to straying. The statement follows a warning about idolizing something beside GOD. It concerns more than an isolated error; it raises the question of losing one's direction of allegiance. But I cannot convert that intensity into a measured distance. The verse supplies no number of steps, no fixed scale, and no sentence saying that returning is inherently impossible.

The opening also speaks about forgiveness. I need to remember that the parenthetical clarification is part of Rashad Khalifa’s Authorized English Translation. A warning about persisting in idol worship is not permission for me to declare another living person's final destiny. The verse first asks something demanding of me: where have I placed my ultimate reliance?

Could I profess the oneness of GOD while treating a person, an inherited habit, or my own preferences as the final court of appeal? I do not conclude that every such dependence automatically has the same status as idolatry. I do, however, see a serious reason to inspect the direction of my loyalty.

Verse references: 4:116.

## 2. Straying Itself: A Choice Set Against Guidance

### Verse

> **2:16** — It is they who bought the straying, at the expense of guidance. Such trade never prospers,  nor  do  they  receive any guidance.

### Reflection on the Verse

In 2:16 there is no adjective meaning 'far.' Instead, straying is set against guidance in the language of a transaction. The people described are not pictured merely as travelers who misplaced a map. The verse speaks of exchanging one direction for another. This does not establish that every mistake is made with identical awareness, but it gives human choice moral weight.

I recognize a smaller temptation in myself. When acting on what I know is right becomes inconvenient, I can start bargaining with my conscience. The wish to win an argument, to remain admired, or to preserve my group's approval can become a substitute for examining the truth. I cannot claim that each example meets the verse's precise description; I can ask whether I am becoming careless about what I trade away.

So the first distinction is linguistic and contextual. Straying can name departure from guidance; 'far' further characterizes that departure. Its absence does not prove innocence, and its presence alone does not establish a universal, irreversible stage.

Verse references: 2:16.

## 3. What Does the Word “Far” Add?

### Verse

> **4:136** — O you who believe, you shall believe in  GOD  and  His  messenger,  and  the scripture  He  has  revealed  through His  messenger,  and  the  scripture He  has  revealed  before  that.  Anyone  who  refuses  to believe in GOD, and His angels, and His scriptures, and  His  messengers,  and  the  Last Day, has indeed strayed far astray.

### Reflection on the Verse

The opening address of 4:136 matters: it speaks to believers. The verse then names GOD, revelation, messengers, angels, and the Last Day, relating refusal to believe to going far astray. 'Far' is not merely a dramatic flourish; it is attached to a serious refusal concerning the sources of guidance.

But I cannot see the entire inward life of someone else. A doubt, a question, and the refusal described in the verse are not automatically interchangeable. The passage does not appoint me to inspect people's hearts or pronounce their ultimate fate. A term of moral gravity should not become a convenient label for anyone who disagrees with me.

I therefore want to read 'far' with the whole sentence, not as a ruler detached from its context. What is being refused? What is the verse addressing? Its adjective does not cancel those questions.

Verse references: 4:136.

## 4. Arabic Distance, but “Real Straying” in Translation

### Verse

> **22:12** — He idolizes beside GOD what possesses no power to harm him or benefit him; such is the real straying.

### Reflection on the Verse

Verse 22:12 made me reconsider a search based only on translated words. The authorized English text calls the situation 'real straying,' and the Turkish translation follows that sense. Yet the Arabic closes with **ٱلضَّلَٰلُ ٱلْبَعِيدُ**, a phrase in which a word for straying is modified by an adjective associated with distance. Recognizing that form does not authorize me to replace Rashad Khalifa’s Authorized English Translation with my own translation. It shows that a translation is not necessarily a word-for-word gloss.

The context also matters: something unable to benefit or harm is being idolized beside GOD. That brings the verse close to the concern of 4:116, without requiring their wording to be identical in Turkish or English.

Had I searched only for the literal Turkish expression 'uzak bir sapkınlık,' I could have missed this verse entirely. Finding a phrase in a translation and tracing a concept through Arabic are related but different tasks.

Verse references: 22:12.

## 5. The Same Turkish Distance, a Different Arabic Adjective

### Verse

> **67:29** — Say,  “He  is  the  Most  Gracious;  we believe  in  Him,  and  we  trust  in  Him. You  will surely  find out who is really far astray.” 

> **7:60** — The leaders among his people said, “We see that you are far astray.”

> **7:61** — He said, “O my people, I am not astray; I am a messenger from the Lord of the universe.

### Reflection on the Verse

These verses expose a subtle translation problem. The Turkish text of 67:29 uses 'uzak bir sapkınlık,' and the leaders in 7:60 accuse a messenger of being very far astray. But the Arabic in both passages uses **ضَلَٰلٍ مُّبِينٍ**. **مُّبِينٍ** belongs to the semantic field of clarity or manifestness; it is not the same adjective as **بَعِيد**. The authorized English wording conveys the force of the accusation, not a uniform Arabic phrase in every occurrence.

Then comes 7:61: the messenger rejects the accusation. This is more than a linguistic footnote. Leaders can pronounce a sweeping religious judgment with great confidence and still be wrong. The loudness of an accusation does not strengthen its evidence.

I take two cautions from this comparison. A translated phrase cannot be treated as a complete map of Arabic vocabulary. And a grave spiritual label does not become true merely because I or a respected group uses it. In 67:29 the truth of the dispute is also left to be known; I should not rush to occupy that final seat.

Verse references: 67:29, 7:60, 7:61.

## 6. Claiming Belief While Following Another Authority

### Verse

> **4:60** — Have  you  noted  those  who  claim that  they  believe  in  what  was  revealed  to  you,  and  in  what  was revealed  before  you,  then  uphold the  unjust  laws  of  their  idols?  They were  commanded  to  reject  such laws.  Indeed, it  is the devil’s  wish  to lead them far astray.

### Reflection on the Verse

Verse 4:60 turns from the claim of belief to the question of authority. Those who claim to accept revelation are described as upholding the unjust laws of their idols. The devil's wish to lead them far astray makes the contradiction more consequential than an ordinary verbal inconsistency.

I must be careful here. Learning from a scholar, taking expert advice, or joining a community does not automatically amount to idol worship. The verse does not grant a limitless labeling power. It does, however, challenge what I make unquestionable when it collides with revelation.

The reflection becomes personal. Do I seek a more comfortable ruling whenever a difficult truth confronts me? Do I justify my conduct by a profession of faith while submitting my decisions to something else? Distance may begin in that separation between declared conviction and actual allegiance.

Verse references: 4:60.

## 7. Monotheism: Must an Idol Always Be a Statue?

### Verse

> **9:31** — They  have  set  up  their  religious leaders  and  scholars  as  lords,*  instead of GOD. Others deified the Messiah, son of Mary. They were all commanded  to  worship  only  one god. There is no god except He. Be He glorified, high above having any partners.

> **4:116** — GOD  does  not  forgive  idol  worship (if  maintained  until  death),*  and  He forgives  lesser  offenses  for  whomever  He  wills.  Anyone  who  idolizes any  idol  beside  GOD  has  strayed  far astray.

### Reflection on the Verse

The warning in 9:31 troubles me precisely because learning normally involves teachers. I benefit from the efforts of people who have studied a subject before me. Receiving help from another person's knowledge and raising that person to the place of GOD are not the same act. The verse puts that boundary before me.

Read beside 4:116, this keeps me from reducing idolatry to physical statues alone. It also prevents the opposite excess: declaring every respected teacher, writer, or researcher an idol. Neither move is licensed simply by placing the two verses together.

The same standard must apply to my writing. A reflection is not revelation. Agreement with an author is not proof of submission to GOD, and disagreement is not proof of straying. I want to leave references that can be checked, not build a loyalty that cannot be questioned.

Verse references: 9:31, 4:116.

## 8. Straying Oneself and Diverting Others

### Verse

> **4:167** — Surely,  those  who  disbelieve  and repel  from  the  way  of  GOD  have strayed far astray.

### Reflection on the Verse

Verse 4:167 does not speak only about disbelief. It also names turning people away from GOD's path. That second action matters. A person can bear responsibility for an individual choice; words, influence, and authority can also affect other people's access to guidance. The verse places both actions together.

If I publish articles, teach, or build a research tool, the question becomes concrete. What happens when I omit the context of a verse, give a convenient but incomplete citation, or make a reader afraid to investigate? I cannot automatically assign the exact judgment of 4:167 to every editorial error, but the possibility of misleading others is too serious to treat lightly.

In this passage, distance is connected to something social as well as inward. When my need to defend an interpretation begins to obstruct another person's opportunity to examine the message, my direction deserves immediate scrutiny.

Verse references: 4:167.

## 9. Making the Path Crooked: The Cost of Misplaced Priorities

### Verse

> **14:3** — They  are  the  ones  who  give  priority to  this  life  over  the  Hereafter,  repel from the way of GOD,  and seek to make  it  crooked;  they  have  gone  far astray.

### Reflection on the Verse

Verse 14:3 places several things side by side: preferring this life to the Hereafter, repelling people from GOD's way, and seeking to make that way crooked. Being far astray is tied not only to choosing wrongly but to altering how the path is presented to others. Priorities may reveal more than declarations.

I cannot turn every wish for comfort, recognition, or income into a prohibition; the verse provides no such exhaustive list. But I need to recognize the danger when those desires become reasons to distort evidence or silence an inconvenient passage.

Perhaps distance sometimes grows through reordered priorities rather than through a single spectacular decision. That is my reflection, not a psychological law established by the verse. It is enough to ask which concern sits above truth when I must choose.

Verse references: 14:3.

## 10. Inherited Error and the Temptation to Treat Opinion as Proof

### Verse

> **5:77** — Say, “O people of the scripture, do not  transgress  the  limits  of  your religion beyond the truth, and do not  follow  the  opinions  of  people who  have  gone  astray,  and  have misled  multitudes  of  people;  they are far astray from the right path.”

> **2:170** — When  they  are  told,  “Follow  what GOD  has  revealed  herein,”  they  say, “We  follow  only  what  we  found  our parents  doing.”  What  if  their  parents  did  not  understand,  and  were not guided?

### Reflection on the Verse

In 5:77 I notice a warning about following opinions of people who have themselves strayed and misled many others. Verse 2:170 raises a related concern: inheriting a practice is not the same as establishing its truth. One passage questions the spread of human opinion; the other questions unquestioned continuity.

I do not want to mock family, ancestors, or the teachers from whom I learned. An old claim is not false because it is old, and a new one is not true because it is new. What matters is the basis on which I accept it. Am I protecting a view merely because it came from someone I trust?

If I turn this warning into a competition over who can insult tradition most forcefully, I may reproduce another kind of blindness. Testing a belief and humiliating the people who hold it are different actions. Searching for guidance still requires truthful and just conduct.

Verse references: 5:77, 2:170.

## 11. Arrogance: Refusing to Change Direction Even When Confronted

### Verse

> **7:146** — I  will  divert  from  My  revelations those  who  are  arrogant  on  earth, without justification.  Consequently, when  they  see  every  kind  of  proof they  will  not  believe.  And  when  they see  the  path  of  guidance  they  will not  adopt  it  as  their  path,  but  when they  see  the  path  of  straying  they  will adopt  it  as  their  path.  This  is  the consequence  of  their  rejecting  our proofs,  and  being  totally  heedless thereof.

### Reflection on the Verse

Verse 7:146 is severe: encountering signs and accepting them are not the same thing. Arrogance is linked to seeing the path of guidance yet refusing to adopt it. That makes me question a comforting assumption about research. Collecting information may not be enough if my need to protect myself determines what I am willing to see.

A view defended for years can become part of my identity. Revising it may feel like losing something greater than an argument. Evidence can stand before me while pride prevents a fair examination. I do not treat every hesitation as the specific arrogance judged in the verse; I treat arrogance as a possibility worth examining in myself.

When a verse challenges my conclusion, perhaps it is my conclusion that needs correction. I want this passage to examine my resistance before I use it to diagnose anyone else's.

Verse references: 7:146.

## 12. Can Feeling Right Hide Straying?

### Verse

> **18:104** — “They  are  the  ones  whose  works  in this  life  are  totally  astray,  but  they think that they are doing good.”

### Reflection on the Verse

Verse 18:104 unsettles me differently. Work can be earnest, extensive, and personally satisfying while its direction remains mistaken. The amount of visible effort does not automatically demonstrate that the work is rightly guided.

An article may take months. An application may reach thousands. A religious presentation may be moving. None of that by itself proves the soundness of a reflection. I am not claiming that every well-meant effort is wasted. The narrower warning is enough: thinking I am doing good and actually doing good are not identical.

The more certain I feel about a conclusion, the more valuable another verification may become. If I reserve the possibility of error exclusively for other people, I can write about distance without noticing my own direction.

Verse references: 18:104.

## 13. The Companion Who Makes Me Feel Guided

### Verse

> **43:36** — Anyone  who  disregards  the  message  of  the  Most  Gracious,  we  appoint  a  devil  to  be  his  constant  companion.*

> **43:37** — Such  companions  will  divert  them from  the  path,  yet  make  them  believe that they are guided.

> **43:38** — When  he  comes  before  us  he  will say,  “Oh  I  wish  you  were  as  far  from me  as  the  two  easts.*  What  a  miserable companion!”

### Reflection on the Verse

The companionship described in 43:36–38 makes me see that going astray need not feel unpleasant from the inside. A person can be diverted while remaining convinced of being guided. Feeling certain is not the same as having examined the truth.

My surroundings can become full of voices that echo my own views. I do not assume that every objection is correct, but neither is agreement an independent proof. These verses are not direct descriptions of modern technology. My connection is a personal reflection on how companionship may shape one's perception of direction.

A voice that flatters my certainty is not necessarily useful. A correction that sends me back to the verse, verification, justice, and personal responsibility may matter more, even when it is uncomfortable.

Verse references: 43:36, 43:37, 43:38.

## 14. Responsibility for the Direction I Choose

### Verse

> **4:115** — As  for  him  who  opposes  the  messenger,  after  the  guidance  has  been pointed  out  to  him,  and  follows other  than  the  believers’  way,  we  will direct  him  in  the  direction  he  has chosen,  and  commit  him  to  Hell; what a miserable destiny! 

> **6:153** — This  is  My  path—a  straight  one. You shall follow it, and do not follow  any  other  paths,  lest  they  divert you  from  His  path.  These  are  His commandments  to  you,  that  you may be saved.

### Reflection on the Verse

The direction one chooses in 4:115 brings responsibility to the center of the reflection. Guidance is described as becoming clear, yet another path is followed. Verse 6:153 then contrasts the straight path with paths that lead people away. Direction is not merely a theory held in the mind.

What I listen to, what I verify, what I excuse, and what injustice I overlook can influence my course. These verses do not claim that everyone has identical knowledge or circumstances. Still, I cannot endlessly transfer the responsibility for my choices to another person.

Saying 'I grew up around this' or 'I was taught this' can explain how I acquired a belief. It does not prove that I should leave it forever unexamined. Reflecting on distance makes the next step, rather than the last label, more important to me.

Verse references: 4:115, 6:153.

## 15. Knowing My Limits: Verification Before Labeling

### Verse

> **17:36** — You  shall  not  accept  any  information,  unless  you  verify  it  for  yourself. I  have  given  you  the  hearing,  the eyesight,  and  the  brain,  and  you  are responsible for using them.

> **39:18** — They  are  the  ones  who  examine  all words,  then  follow  the  best.  These are  the  ones  whom  GOD  has  guided;  these  are  the  ones  who  possess intelligence.

### Reflection on the Verse

Verse 17:36 brings verification into the moral sphere of knowledge. Verse 39:18 adds the responsibility to examine what is heard and follow the best. Seen beside them, the sentence 'that person is far astray' becomes an enormous claim. What do I actually know, and what am I merely inferring?

I should start with the exact verse, read its context, compare related expressions, and distinguish the assessment of an idea from the judgment of a person. A word sounding severe does not establish that I may apply it freely.

In <a href="https://berkkoksal.com/writings/we_found_our_parents_doing_this_en/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">We Found Our Parents Doing This</a>, I asked whether inherited confidence is evidence. Here the question sharpens: even if I can locate a verse, have I established that it describes the individual I am tempted to condemn?

Verse references: 17:36, 39:18.

## 16. Justice Even Toward a Person Who Disagrees

### Verse

> **5:8** — O  you  who  believe,  you  shall  be  absolutely  equitable,  and  observe GOD,  when  you  serve  as  witnesses. Do  not  be  provoked  by  your  conflicts  with  some  people  into  committing  injustice.  You  shall  be  absolutely  equitable,  for  it  is  more  righteous. You  shall  observe  GOD.  GOD  is fully  Cognizant  of  everything  you do.

> **4:135** — O  you who  believe,  you  shall  be absolutely  equitable,  and  observe GOD,  when  you  serve  as  witnesses, even  against  yourselves,  or  your parents,  or  your  relatives.  Whether the  accused  is  rich  or  poor,  GOD takes  care  of  both.  Therefore,  do not  be  biased  by  your  personal wishes.  If  you  deviate  or  disregard (this  commandment),  then  GOD  is fully  Cognizant  of  everything  you do.

### Reflection on the Verse

Words as grave as 'far astray' require me to read the verses about justice. Verse 5:8 warns against allowing conflicts to provoke injustice. Verse 4:135 calls for equitable testimony even when it goes against oneself or loved ones. A religious dispute does not suspend ethical accountability.

I may consider another person's argument mistaken, yet I must not attribute to them words they never said or deliberately strip their statement of context. Saying that I defend the truth cannot exempt me from fairness. If I injure people while claiming to guide them, I need to examine my own witness.

Justice does not mean treating all opinions as equally true. It does mean refusing to do wrong to a person while trying to assess what is true. That is a boundary I do not want to lose in a debate about distance.

Verse references: 5:8, 4:135.

## 17. Who Knows Best Who Has Gone Astray?

### Verse

> **16:125** — You shall invite to the path of your Lord  with  wisdom  and  kind  enlightenment, and debate with them in the best possible manner. Your Lord knows best who has strayed from His path, and He knows best who are the guided ones.

> **6:164** — Say,  “Shall  I  seek  other  than  GOD as a lord, when He is the Lord of all things?  No  soul  benefits  except from  its  own  works,  and  none  bears the  burden  of  another.  Ultimately, you  return  to  your  Lord,  then  He informs  you  regarding  all  your  disputes.”

### Reflection on the Verse

The ending of 16:125 places a powerful limit on my knowledge. The verse calls for wisdom and kind engagement, then states who knows best those who have strayed and those who are guided. I may argue carefully, but I cannot know the full inner life of another person.

Verse 6:164 brings me back to responsibility for my own deeds. Condemning others can offer a temporary feeling of superiority, yet it does not settle my own account. A more useful question is what I do with the knowledge available to me today.

Thus I want to distinguish studying the warning of far straying from declaring people's final standing. I take the warning seriously without claiming a knowledge the verses have not given me.

Verse references: 16:125, 6:164.

## 18. Recognizing Straying Without Closing the Door of Return

### Verse

> **7:149** — Finally,  when  they  regretted  their action,  and  realized  that  they  had gone  astray,  they  said,  “Unless  our Lord  redeems  us  with  His  mercy, and forgives us, we will be losers.”

> **39:53** — Proclaim: “O My servants who exceeded the limits, never despair of GOD’s mercy. For GOD forgives all sins. He is the Forgiver, Most Merciful.”

> **39:54** — You shall obey your Lord, and submit to Him totally, before the retribution  overtakes  you,  then  you cannot be helped.

### Reflection on the Verse

In 7:149, people recognize that they have gone astray, regret what they have done, and turn toward GOD's mercy. The passage does not diminish the seriousness of error. It does challenge the idea that the word 'straying' must always be read as an irreversible verdict on every living person.

I read the hope of 39:53 beside the urgency of 39:54. Hope need not be an excuse to postpone change; it can support turning back today. Still, I do not possess the authority to guarantee the forgiveness or condemnation of a particular person.

Perhaps one difficult part of returning is recognizing that I may have been mistaken while feeling righteous. I am not inventing a formally named type of repentance from the verse. I am trying to face honestly what recognizing an error may demand from me.

Verse references: 7:149, 39:53, 39:54.

## 19. Instead of Measuring Distance, Correcting My Direction

### Verse

> **6:153** — This  is  My  path—a  straight  one. You shall follow it, and do not follow  any  other  paths,  lest  they  divert you  from  His  path.  These  are  His commandments  to  you,  that  you may be saved.

> **2:256** — There shall be no compulsion in religion: the right way is now distinct from the wrong way. Anyone who denounces the devil and believes in GOD  has  grasped  the  strongest bond; one that never breaks. GOD is Hearer, Omniscient.

> **4:116** — GOD  does  not  forgive  idol  worship (if  maintained  until  death),*  and  He forgives  lesser  offenses  for  whomever  He  wills.  Anyone  who  idolizes any  idol  beside  GOD  has  strayed  far astray.

### Reflection on the Verse

I do not want to finish this inquiry by inventing a numerical scale for spiritual distance. Verse 6:153 directs attention to the straight path and warns against paths that lead away from it. Verse 2:256 distinguishes the right way from the wrong and rejects compulsion in religion. Verse 4:116 gives a grave warning concerning idolatry. Read together, these passages give direction and limits.

I do not conclude that I can force another person into guidance. Nor do I conclude that every path is interchangeable or that 'far astray' is merely empty rhetoric. Taking guidance seriously and refusing religious coercion are both responsibilities I must hold in view.

At first I wanted to know just how far 'far' meant. Now a more personal question remains: What do I do when guidance confronts me? Can I admit what I do not know, remain fair in conflict, and change direction when I recognize an error? Those answers matter more to my life than a label placed on someone else.

Verse references: 6:153, 2:256, 4:116.

## On Arabic Word Roots

### Verse

> **2:16** — It is they who bought the straying, at the expense of guidance. Such trade never prospers,  nor  do  they  receive any guidance.

> **4:116** — GOD  does  not  forgive  idol  worship (if  maintained  until  death),*  and  He forgives  lesser  offenses  for  whomever  He  wills.  Anyone  who  idolizes any  idol  beside  GOD  has  strayed  far astray.

> **22:12** — He idolizes beside GOD what possesses no power to harm him or benefit him; such is the real straying.

> **67:29** — Say,  “He  is  the  Most  Gracious;  we believe  in  Him,  and  we  trust  in  Him. You  will surely  find out who is really far astray.” 

> **7:60** — The leaders among his people said, “We see that you are far astray.”

### Reflection on the Verse

**ض ل ل — ḍ-l-l:** The word **ٱلضَّلَٰلَةَ** in 2:16, the construction **ضَلَّ ضَلَٰلًا** in 4:116, and **ضَلُّوا۟** in 7:149 belong to a common root family associated with straying or losing the way. A shared root does not make every context equivalent; subject, action, and purpose within each sentence still matter.

**ب ع د — b-ʿ-d:** In 4:116, **بَعِيدًا** is an adjective associated with distance, qualifying **ضَلَٰلًۢا**. The construction **ضَلَّ ضَلَٰلًۢا بَعِيدًا** joins a verb, a related verbal noun, and an adjective. Similar constructions occur in 4:60, 4:136, and 4:167. Verse 14:3 uses **فِى ضَلَٰلٍۭ بَعِيدٍ**. The language strongly qualifies the straying, but it does not provide a numerical taxonomy of spiritual distance.

**The translation choice in 22:12:** The Arabic has **ٱلضَّلَٰلُ ٱلْبَعِيدُ**, while Rashad Khalifa’s Authorized English Translation ends with **"such is the real straying."** I do not replace that authorized rendering with a new translation. The contrast shows why searching an English phrase alone cannot exhaust the Arabic patterns.

**ب ي ن — b-y-n:** The word **مُّبِينٍ**, appearing in 67:29 and 7:60, belongs to the semantic field of being clear or manifest. It is not **بَعِيد**. The authorized translation conveys 'far astray' in these contexts, but the Arabic expressions are not identical to the one in 4:116. The messenger's response in 7:61 reminds me that the confidence of an accuser cannot establish the accusation's truth.

**ه د ي — h-d-y:** The reference to guidance in 2:16 forms the counterpoint to straying. **ش ر ك — sh-r-k**, associated with joining partners in 4:116, explains why the warning is directly tied to monotheism. I should read these roots in their sentences rather than treat an isolated root as a complete definition of a person's spiritual state.

For grammatical cross-checks, see <a href="https://corpus.quran.com/wordbyword.jsp?chapter=4&verse=116" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">4:116 word-by-word analysis</a>, <a href="https://corpus.quran.com/wordbyword.jsp?chapter=4&verse=60" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">4:60 word-by-word analysis</a>, <a href="https://corpus.quran.com/wordbyword.jsp?chapter=22&verse=12" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">22:12 word-by-word analysis</a>, and <a href="https://corpus.quran.com/wordbyword.jsp?chapter=67&verse=29" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">67:29 word-by-word analysis</a>. These resources help inspect morphology; they are not substitutes for the authorized verse texts.

Verse references: 2:16; 4:60; 4:116; 4:136; 4:167; 7:60–61; 7:149; 14:3; 22:12; 67:29.

## Other Claims

### Verse

> **4:116** — GOD  does  not  forgive  idol  worship (if  maintained  until  death),*  and  He forgives  lesser  offenses  for  whomever  He  wills.  Anyone  who  idolizes any  idol  beside  GOD  has  strayed  far astray.

> **22:12** — He idolizes beside GOD what possesses no power to harm him or benefit him; such is the real straying.

> **67:29** — Say,  “He  is  the  Most  Gracious;  we believe  in  Him,  and  we  trust  in  Him. You  will surely  find out who is really far astray.” 

> **7:60** — The leaders among his people said, “We see that you are far astray.”

> **7:61** — He said, “O my people, I am not astray; I am a messenger from the Lord of the universe.

> **16:125** — You shall invite to the path of your Lord  with  wisdom  and  kind  enlightenment, and debate with them in the best possible manner. Your Lord knows best who has strayed from His path, and He knows best who are the guided ones.

### Reflection on the Verse

Several interpretive approaches are possible. Some may treat 'far' as a sign of especially grave or entrenched straying; others may emphasize its rhetorical force. But the phrase does not by itself provide a numbered sequence through which every person passes until return is impossible. A rigid taxonomy would need explicit textual support beyond the adjective.

Another approach expects each Arabic word to have the same English equivalent wherever it occurs. Concordance can aid research, but translation does not always operate by mechanical substitution. The Arabic **ٱلضَّلَٰلُ ٱلْبَعِيدُ** in 22:12 and **ضَلَٰلٍ مُّبِينٍ** in 67:29 are different constructions, even though related language of straying appears in the authorized English renderings. A grammatical aid such as the <a href="https://corpus.quran.com/wordbyword.jsp?chapter=4&verse=116" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Quranic Arabic Corpus</a> can help examine these differences without becoming an alternative authority over the verse text.

Traditional or community-centered readings can sometimes make it easy to apply 'far astray' to everyone outside a favored religious position. Other readers may react by weakening the warning until it carries little force. I find neither shortcut convincing. The gravity of 4:116 and 4:136 deserves attention, while the false confidence of the accusation in 7:60–61 and the limits placed on human knowledge and manner in 16:125 remain just as relevant.

Modern psychology offers a separate comparison. Ziva Kunda’s <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2270237/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">1990 review of motivated reasoning</a> examined how desired conclusions may influence reasoning; the <a href="https://dictionary.apa.org/confirmation-bias" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">APA Dictionary of Psychology</a> describes the tendency to seek confirming evidence. These accounts should not be turned into a universal diagnosis, much less proof that an individual has entered the Quranic state described as “far astray.” A psychological explanation is not a divine verdict. My principal purpose is to keep the possibility of self-examination open.

Verse references: 2:16; 4:60; 4:116; 4:136; 4:167; 7:60–61; 16:125; 22:12; 67:29.

## Conclusion

### Verse

> **2:16** — It is they who bought the straying, at the expense of guidance. Such trade never prospers,  nor  do  they  receive any guidance.

> **4:116** — GOD  does  not  forgive  idol  worship (if  maintained  until  death),*  and  He forgives  lesser  offenses  for  whomever  He  wills.  Anyone  who  idolizes any  idol  beside  GOD  has  strayed  far astray.

> **4:167** — Surely,  those  who  disbelieve  and repel  from  the  way  of  GOD  have strayed far astray.

> **7:149** — Finally,  when  they  regretted  their action,  and  realized  that  they  had gone  astray,  they  said,  “Unless  our Lord  redeems  us  with  His  mercy, and forgives us, we will be losers.”

> **16:125** — You shall invite to the path of your Lord  with  wisdom  and  kind  enlightenment, and debate with them in the best possible manner. Your Lord knows best who has strayed from His path, and He knows best who are the guided ones.

> **39:53** — Proclaim: “O My servants who exceeded the limits, never despair of GOD’s mercy. For GOD forgives all sins. He is the Forgiver, Most Merciful.”

### Reflection on the Verse

At the beginning I was looking for two separate labels: straying and being far astray. Reading the passages together brings me to a more careful conclusion. **The Arabic root associated with straying is, in certain verses, further qualified by language of distance.** This appears in statements about monotheism, refusal of revelation, and hindering people from GOD's path. But I do not find a basis for treating every usage as a slot on one fixed numerical scale.

In 2:16, a direction is chosen at the expense of guidance. In 4:116, monotheism is central. Verse 4:167 introduces the harm of turning others away. Verse 7:149 shows people recognizing that they have gone astray and seeking mercy. And 16:125 places the fullest knowledge of who is astray with GOD. These passages are not interchangeable. Their differences help me understand the seriousness of the question.

I keep returning to 7:60–61. Community leaders can utter a forceful accusation against a messenger while believing themselves correct. Pronouncing a word confidently does not make the testimony accurate. What I say, why I say it, and what I know matter. If I become unjust while claiming to defend truth, the claim alone does not justify me.

I therefore do not want to study straying only as a description of other people. My deepest concern is failing to recognize when my own direction has changed—perhaps even feeling guided while moving away. I also do not want to erase the possibility of return when the verses themselves speak of regret and mercy. The final judgment is not mine.

**The difference between straying and going far astray lies in the qualification and context of the expression. I do not see enough textual support to make them two universally fixed, irreversible spiritual ranks.** I want these words to examine my direction rather than become weapons against other people.

The following nineteen points are not additional rules inserted into the Quran. They are a practical checklist I am leaving for my own reflection.

1. **Read the whole verse before debating its vocabulary.** (4:116; 22:12)
2. **Do not assume that straying and far straying form automatic grades.** (2:16; 4:116)
3. **Do not assume that identical English phrasing means identical Arabic adjectives.** (7:60; 67:29)
4. **Examine what you choose in place of guidance.** (2:16)
5. **Question what you have raised to final authority beside GOD.** (4:116; 9:31)
6. **Compare professed belief with the authority you actually follow.** (4:60)
7. **Be accountable for words that may obstruct another person’s search.** (4:167)
8. **Do not bend truth to serve worldly priorities.** (14:3)
9. **Do not confuse inherited opinion with proof.** (5:77; 2:170)
10. **Consider whether pride is resisting evidence.** (7:146)
11. **Remember that feeling productive does not guarantee correctness.** (18:104)
12. **Examine companions who always make you feel right.** (43:36–38)
13. **Do not transfer responsibility for your chosen direction to others.** (4:115; 6:153)
14. **Verify claims for yourself.** (17:36)
15. **Investigate an accusation before attaching it to someone.** (7:60–61)
16. **Stay equitable even toward someone with whom you are in conflict.** (5:8; 4:135)
17. **Invite with wisdom and the best manner you can.** (16:125)
18. **Do not postpone turning back when you recognize an error.** (7:149; 39:53–54)
19. **Attend to your direction today rather than claiming another person’s final judgment.** (16:125; 6:153)

Verse references: 2:16; 4:60; 4:116; 4:136; 4:167; 5:8; 6:153; 7:60–61; 7:149; 14:3; 16:125; 17:36; 18:104; 22:12; 39:53–54; 67:29.
