# The Impatient Human Being: Is What We Want Really Good for Us?

**Author:** Berk KÖKSAL  
**Date:** 03.10.2026  
**Place:** Mersin

The main purpose of this article is to reflect on the verses together with other related verses and to explore, in a sincere and profound language, what witnessing the oneness of GOD means in human life in terms of knowledge, justice, truthfulness, and submission.

Peace. Peace be upon you.

Haste is not merely moving quickly. It can be demanding an outcome before its time, speaking before knowledge is complete, demanding punishment immediately, or assuming that what I want now must also be what is good for me.

17:11 says that the human being can pray for what may hurt him while thinking he is praying for something good. 2:216 adds that we may dislike what is good for us and love what is bad for us. GOD knows while we do not.

Patience, then, is not a technique for passive waiting. It is an admission of the limits of human knowledge, a refusal to make our timetable sovereign, and a commitment to remain truthful even before the outcome appears.

## 1. What We Think Is Good May Harm Us

### Verse

> **17:11**
>
> وَيَدْعُ ٱلْإِنسَٰنُ بِٱلشَّرِّ دُعَآءَهُۥ بِٱلْخَيْرِ وَكَانَ ٱلْإِنسَٰنُ عَجُولًا
>
> The human being often prays for something that may hurt him, thinking that he is praying for something good. The human being is impatient.
>
> İnsan iyi bir şey için dua ettiğini sanıp sıklıkla kendisine zarar verebilecek şeyler için dua eder. İnsan sabırsızdır.

Source: <a href="https://kuranteyit.com/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">kuranteyit.com</a>

### Reflection on the Verse

17:11 exposes a difficult human possibility: a person may pray for something harmful while thinking he is asking for something good. Desire and knowledge are not identical.

I can want something intensely and interpret delay as evil. Yet intensity does not give me comprehensive knowledge of consequences.

Prayer therefore also requires humility. To ask sincerely is not the same as claiming to know the whole good of what is asked.

**Verse references:** 17:11.

## 2. Impatience as a Human Weakness

### Verse

> **21:37** — The  human  being  is  impatient  by nature.  I  will  inevitably  show  you  My signs; do not be in such a hurry.

### Reflection on the Verse

21:37 describes the human being as impatient and immediately says that the signs will be shown, so there is no need to demand them prematurely.

Impatience here becomes more than temperament. It can become an attempt to force divine timing into human timing.

The verse does not deny that the signs will come. It denies the authority of our haste over when they must come.

**Verse references:** 21:37.

## 3. Haste in Knowledge: Speaking Before Revelation Is Complete

### Verse

> **20:114** — Most  Exalted  is  GOD,  the  only true  King.  Do  not  rush  into  uttering the  Quran  before  it  is  revealed  to you,  and  say,  “My  Lord,  increase  my knowledge.”

### Reflection on the Verse

20:114 tells the messenger not to rush the Quran before its revelation is completed and teaches the prayer, “My Lord, increase my knowledge.”

Human beings are prone to treating partial knowledge as complete knowledge. We see one piece and rush to announce the whole.

The prayer for increased knowledge is therefore a discipline of humility: understanding more accurately matters more than answering more quickly.

**Verse references:** 20:114.

## 4. The Tongue Must Not Outrun the Truth

### Verse

> **75:16** — Do  not  move  your  tongue  to  hasten it.
>
> **75:17** — It  is  we  who  will  collect  it  into Quran.
>
> **75:18** — Once  we  recite  it,  you  shall  follow such a Quran.
>
> **75:19** — Then it is we who will explain it.

### Reflection on the Verse

75:16-19 warns against moving the tongue in haste and places the gathering, recitation, and explanation of the Quran under GOD's authority.

Even listening can be hurried. A person can prepare a conclusion before the statement is complete or build a doctrine before the verses have been considered together.

Following is not the same as running ahead. Receiving, listening, and then following belong to the ethics of knowledge.

**Verse references:** 75:16; 75:17; 75:18; 75:19.

## 5. Do Not Rush Even for Punishment

### Verse

> **46:35** — Therefore, be patient like the messengers before you who possessed strength  and resorted  to  patience. Do not be in a hurry to see the retribution that will inevitably come to them. The day they see it, it will seem as if they lasted one hour of the day. This is a proclamation: Is it not the wicked who are consistently annihilated?

### Reflection on the Verse

46:35 commands patience like the resolute messengers and says not to rush the punishment promised to the rejecters.

When injustice is experienced, the desire to see immediate consequences can feel identical with the desire for justice. The verse separates them.

Justice may be true while its timing remains outside my possession. Wanting justice is one thing; claiming the timetable of justice is another.

**Verse references:** 46:35.

## 6. What If Every Request Were Accelerated?

### Verse

> **10:11** — If  GOD  hastened  the  retribution  incurred  by  the  people,  the  way  they demand  provisions,  they  would  have been  annihilated  long  ago.  However,  we  leave  those  who  do  not believe  in  meeting  us  in  their  transgressions, blundering.

### Reflection on the Verse

10:11 says that if retribution were accelerated for people as quickly as they seek provisions, they would already have been finished.

The verse breaks the automatic equation between speed and good. Delay can leave space for return, correction, and continued responsibility.

It would be too much to claim that every delay has one specific hidden reason, but the verse is enough to show that “immediate” is not a synonym for “merciful” or “right.”

**Verse references:** 10:11.

## 7. Rushing Punishment as a Form of Defiance

### Verse

> **10:50** — Say,  “Whether  His  retribution comes  to  you  by  night  or  by  day, why  are  the  transgressors  in  such  a hurry?

### Reflection on the Verse

10:50 asks why transgressors are in such a hurry for the punishment. Here haste is not innocent curiosity; it becomes defiance.

A person can demand, “Let it happen now,” as if truth must submit to a human test schedule.

But divine truth does not depend on satisfying a challenge at the time chosen by the challenger.

**Verse references:** 10:50.

## 8. “Bring It Now” Is Not Knowledge

### Verse

> **29:53** — They  challenge  you  to  bring  the retribution!  If  it  were  not  for  a predetermined  appointment, the retribution  would  have  come  to them  immediately.*  Certainly,  it  will come  to  them  suddenly,  when  they least expect it.

### Reflection on the Verse

29:53 describes people challenging the messenger to bring the punishment and says that there is a predetermined appointment.

The delay of an outcome does not erase the reality of the outcome. Nor does it suspend responsibility during the interval.

Time given is still moral time. The person continues to choose, act, and become.

**Verse references:** 29:53.

## 9. Rushing the Hour Versus Fearing Its Truth

### Verse

> **42:18** — Challenging  it  are  those  who  do  not believe  in  it.  As  for  those  who  believe,  they  are  concerned  about  it, and  they  know  that  it  is  the  truth. Absolutely,  those  who  deny  the Hour have gone far astray.

### Reflection on the Verse

42:18 contrasts those who challenge the Hour with those who believe in it and are concerned about it.

The same future event can generate two radically different postures: mocking haste or moral attentiveness.

For the believer, the essential question is not “why has it not come yet?” but “what condition will I be in when it comes?”

**Verse references:** 42:18.

## 10. Not Knowing Tomorrow

### Verse

> **31:34** — With GOD is the knowledge regarding  the  Hour  (end  of  the  world).*  He is the One who sends down the rain, and  He  knows  the  contents  of  the womb. No soul knows what will happen  to  it  tomorrow,  and  no  one knows in which land he or she will die. GOD is Omniscient, Cognizant.

### Reflection on the Verse

31:34 says no soul knows what will happen to it tomorrow or where it will die. Planning is possible; possession of the future is not.

Impatience often grows from discomfort with uncertainty. Exact dates and guarantees can create an illusion of control.

The verse does not make uncertainty an excuse for irresponsibility. It makes the present more serious.

**Verse references:** 31:34.

## 11. Steadfastness Is Not the Same as Slowness

### Verse

> **3:200** — O you who believe, you shall be steadfast, you shall persevere, you shall be united, you shall observe GOD, that you may succeed.

### Reflection on the Verse

3:200 commands firmness, perseverance, unity, and reverence for GOD. Patience is therefore not inactivity.

Haste tries to possess the result. Steadfastness continues the right action even before the result is visible.

Speed can be useful, but if direction is wrong it only carries the person farther away. Steadfastness protects direction.

**Verse references:** 3:200.

## 12. Time, Truth, and Patience

### Verse

> **103:1** — By the afternoon. 
>
> **103:2** — The human being is utterly lost. 
>
> **103:3** — Except  those  who  believe  and  lead a  righteous  life,  and  exhort  one another  to  uphold  the  truth,  and  exhort one another to be steadfast.

### Reflection on the Verse

Sura 103 places time beside human loss and then exempts those who believe, live righteously, and encourage one another toward truth and steadfastness.

The passing of time does not justify panic. It makes faithfulness within limited time more important.

A short life does not require premature conclusions, but it does require that truth and righteous action not be endlessly postponed.

**Verse references:** 103:1; 103:2; 103:3.

## 13. The Anxious Human Being

### Verse

> **70:19** — Indeed, the human being is anxious.
>
> **70:20** — If  touched  by  adversity,  despondent.
>
> **70:21** — If blessed by wealth, stingy.

### Reflection on the Verse

70:19-21 depicts the human being as anxious, despairing under adversity, and withholding when blessed with wealth.

Impatience can therefore be emotional volatility: today's pain becomes the whole truth, tomorrow's abundance becomes self-sufficiency.

Reflection does not deny emotion. It refuses to enthrone a temporary feeling as the final measure of reality.

**Verse references:** 70:19; 70:20; 70:21.

## 14. Haste Inside Prayer

### Verse

> **17:11** — The human being often prays for something that may hurt him, thinking that he is praying for something good. The human being is impatient.

### Reflection on the Verse

Returning to 17:11, the verse speaks not merely of action but of prayer itself. A person can approach GOD impatiently.

Prayer can quietly move from “I desire this” to “I know this must be good.” The verse unsettles that second claim.

Submission in prayer may therefore mean speaking the desire honestly while leaving the complete knowledge of its consequences to GOD.

**Verse references:** 17:11.

## 15. Seeking Help Through Steadfastness and Prayer

### Verse

> **2:153** — O  you  who  believe,  seek  help through  steadfastness  and  the  Contact  Prayers  (Salat).  GOD  is  with those who steadfastly persevere.

### Reflection on the Verse

2:153 commands believers to seek help through steadfastness and the Contact Prayers and says that GOD is with those who steadfastly persevere.

Haste can make the human being feel that everything must be solved by immediate personal control. The verse instead joins perseverance with worship and help-seeking.

When the outcome is not ours to control, we are not left with nothing to do: we can pray, remain truthful, and fulfill our responsibility.

**Verse references:** 2:153.

## 16. Even Patience Is by GOD’s Help

### Verse

> **16:127** — You  shall  resort  to  patience—and your patience is attainable only with GOD’s help. Do not grieve over them, and do not be annoyed by their schemes.

### Reflection on the Verse

16:127 commands patience and then says that patience can only be attained with GOD's help.

This prevents patience from becoming a monument to the ego. Even steadfastness is not an independent human power to boast about.

The person perseveres, but does not worship his own capacity to persevere.

**Verse references:** 16:127.

## 17. The Reward of Steadfastness Is Not Measured by Immediate Visibility

### Verse

> **39:10** — Say,  “O  My  servants  who  believed, you  shall  reverence  your  Lord.”  For those  who  worked  righteousness  in this  world,  a  good  reward.  GOD’s earth  is  spacious,  and  those  who steadfastly  persevere  will  receive their  recompense  generously,  without limits.

### Reflection on the Verse

39:10 states that those who steadfastly persevere receive their reward generously and without limit.

The human mind often wants immediate visible returns. The verse widens the measure of value beyond short-term results.

A righteous act does not become worthless merely because its outcome is not yet visible.

**Verse references:** 39:10.

## 18. What We Love Can Be Bad; What We Dislike Can Be Good

### Verse

> **2:216** — Fighting may be imposed on you, even though you dislike it. But you may dislike something which is good for you, and you may like something which is bad for you. GOD knows while you do not know.

### Reflection on the Verse

2:216 says that we may dislike something that is good for us and like something that is bad for us. GOD knows while we do not.

This deepens 17:11. Desire is real, but desire is not omniscience. Aversion is real, but aversion is not a final moral verdict.

Patience begins when I stop making my immediate attraction or repulsion the measure of the whole good.

**Verse references:** 2:216.

## 19. Arabic Root Reflections: Haste, Calling, Patience, Good and Harm

### Verse

> **17:11** — The human being often prays for something that may hurt him, thinking that he is praying for something good. The human being is impatient.
>
> **21:37** — The  human  being  is  impatient  by nature.  I  will  inevitably  show  you  My signs; do not be in such a hurry.
>
> **2:153** — O  you  who  believe,  seek  help through  steadfastness  and  the  Contact  Prayers  (Salat).  GOD  is  with those who steadfastly persevere.

### Reflection on the Verse

The Quranic field of haste in 17:11 and 21:37 is connected with **ع ج ل (ʿ-j-l)**, carrying the sense of speeding and seeking something prematurely. The verses make haste a moral and epistemic issue, not merely a matter of physical speed.

The language of calling or prayer is connected with **د ع و (d-ʿ-w)**. 17:11 shows that the human act of calling does not guarantee that what is requested is truly beneficial.

Steadfastness is expressed through **ص ب ر (s-b-r)**, a root associated with endurance and holding firm. 2:153 joins this endurance to seeking help and to GOD's nearness to those who persevere.

The fields of good and harm in 17:11 remind us that the human evaluation of what is beneficial can fail. Desire asks; GOD knows.

**Verse references:** 17:11; 21:37; 2:153.

## Other Claims

Historical and devotional literature contains many classifications of patience, discussions of unanswered or delayed prayer, and theories about the relationship between prayer and destiny. These may be meaningful within their own traditions, but they do not determine the main reflection here.

The direct verses are sufficient for the core argument: 17:11 says a human being can ask for harm while thinking it good; 21:37 describes human impatience; 20:114 warns against haste in knowledge; 46:35 commands patience rather than rushing the outcome; and 2:216 states that human preference does not perfectly identify good and bad.

It would go beyond these verses to claim that every delay has one specific hidden divine explanation. The safer conclusion is that speed is not always good and human knowledge does not encompass the outcome.

## Conclusion

Haste often arises not from the speed of life but from the desire to own the result. When I say “now,” I may be expressing more than desire; I may be assuming I know the right timing and the complete good.

17:11 breaks that assumption: a person can ask for what harms him while thinking it good. 2:216 exposes the same limit from another direction: what I love can be bad for me and what I dislike can be good. GOD knows; I do not.

Patience is therefore not paralysis. It is steadfast truthfulness without possession of the outcome. 3:200, 2:153, 16:127, and Sura 103 all connect perseverance with active righteousness, worship, and truth.

**A right life is not the acceleration of everything. It is continuing what is right, in the right measure, while leaving the final outcome to GOD.**
