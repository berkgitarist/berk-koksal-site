# [49:10] Who Is Our True Family? Is Our Family in This World Fake?

**Author:** Berk KÖKSAL  
**Date:** November 06, 2026  
**Location:** Mersin

The central purpose of this article is to reflect on 49:10 together with related verses and to ask what witnessing to the oneness of GOD means for family, love, mercy, knowledge, justice, truthful speech, and submission, while taking seriously both the value and the limits of kinship.

Greetings. Peace be upon you.

**Who is my real family?** Before I can answer that question, I think of ordinary things: the sound of a parent's voice, a child running into a room, two people learning to share a home, a sibling who remembers a childhood that no one else can remember in quite the same way. Family is often the first world we know before we are old enough to choose anything. It can be a place of tenderness. It can also be where some of our deepest hurts begin. Can I call such bonds “fake” simply because they are not eternal?

The Quran confronts me with an extraordinary description: **believers are presented as members of one family.** If belief creates a family, what happens to the family into which I was born? Does Noah's son show that blood means nothing? And if so, why does the Quran also speak about righteous parents, spouses, and children entering Paradise together? I cannot answer one verse by making the other disappear.

I want to avoid replacing one sentimental certainty with another. In my reflection on <a href="https://berkkoksal.com/writings/responding_to_evil_with_what_is_best_en/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Responding to Evil with What Is Best</a>, I considered the difficult relationship between justice and forgiveness. The same tension enters the home. Can love be used to excuse wrongdoing? Can loyalty to truth become a justification for cruelty toward someone I love? I want to think through these questions without calling an entire human relationship false and without turning it into an unquestionable source of authority.

## 1. The Family of Believers: What Makes It a Family?

### Verse

> **49:10**
>
> إِنَّمَا ٱلْمُؤْمِنُونَ إِخْوَةٌ فَأَصْلِحُوا۟ بَيْنَ أَخَوَيْكُمْ وَٱتَّقُوا۟ ٱللَّهَ لَعَلَّكُمْ تُرْحَمُونَ
>
> The believers are members of one family; you shall keep the peace within your family and reverence GOD, that you may attain mercy.
>
> İnananlar tek bir ailenin fertleridir; ailenizde barışı muhafaza edin ve TANRI’ya derin saygı duyun ki merhamete erişesiniz.

Source: <a href="https://kuranteyit.com/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">kuranteyit.com</a>

### Reflection on the Verse

I cannot hurry past the wording of 49:10. It does not merely say that believers should be friendly with one another. It calls them members of one family. This relationship is not established by a shared surname, a genetic line, or having grown up under one roof. Faith establishes a bond that carries responsibility toward other people.

Then comes a demand that prevents this beautiful word from becoming decoration: keep the peace within your family. So members of this family can disagree. They may hurt one another, misunderstand motives, or find themselves on opposite sides of an argument. Being called a family does not make them incapable of conflict. It makes their response to conflict an ethical matter.

Does the verse therefore cancel my relationship with my mother, father, spouse, or child? I find no such statement here. It identifies another real bond; it does not erase the obligations attached to the first. Nor can I use the word “family” to glorify my own circle while mistreating those beyond it. If I call someone my brother or sister in faith and refuse to behave justly toward that person, what have I preserved other than a title?

**Verse reference:** 49:10.

## 2. Is Our Earthly Family Real, or Just an Appearance?

### Verse

> **4:1** — O people, observe your Lord; the One who created you from one being, and created from it its mate, then spread from the two many men and women. You shall regard GOD, by whom you swear, and regard the parents. GOD is watching over you.*

> **30:21** — Among  His  proofs  is  that  He  created  for  you  spouses  from  among yourselves,  in  order  to  have  tranquility  and  contentment  with  each other,  and  He  placed  in  your  hearts love  and  care  towards  your  spouses. In  this,  there  are  sufficient  proofs for people who think.

### Reflection on the Verse

Verse 4:1 places the human family within the story of creation and immediately recalls a duty of regard for parents. Verse 30:21 describes tranquility, love, and care between spouses as signs of GOD. These are not presented as meaningless illusions. They are part of the moral and emotional life for which human beings are responsible.

Still, I must not confuse the word **real** with **everlasting**. A moment can be real without lasting forever. The love expressed in caring for a newborn is not made false by the fact that the child will grow. A marriage may bring real care even though the people in it remain mortal and capable of failure. To say that the world is temporary is not to say that every relationship within it is a deception.

The question becomes more precise when I put it this way: What does an earthly bond rightly give me, and what can it never guarantee? Love and responsibility appear clearly in these verses. Final judgment and eternal destiny must be considered through other verses. Calling family “fake” before making those distinctions would be a conclusion in search of evidence.

**Verse references:** 4:1, 30:21.

## 3. Noah's Son: When Blood Is Not Enough

### Verse

> **11:42** — As it sailed with them in waves like hills, Noah called his son, who was isolated: “O my son, come ride with us; do not be with the disbelievers.”

> **11:45** — Noah implored his Lord: “My Lord, my son is a member of my family, and Your promise is the truth. You are the wisest of the wise.”

> **11:46** — He said, “O Noah, he is not of your family. It is unrighteous to ask Me for something you do not know.* I enlighten you, lest you be like the ignorant.”

### Reflection on the Verse

The scene of Noah calling to his son is not an abstract theological diagram. A father calls out; the son remains apart. The father wants him to come aboard. I hear both the warning and the closeness of that appeal. When Noah later speaks to his Lord, he refers to his son as a member of his family. The human relationship is visible throughout the passage.

Then comes the difficult reply: he is not of Noah's family. I cannot responsibly turn that into a claim that the son was not biologically his. Verse 11:42 has already called him Noah's son. In context, the issue appears to be the limit of kinship as a claim upon GOD's judgment, not a denial that an ordinary father-son relationship existed.

That distinction matters. The story does not say, “All families in this world are false.” It shows a father and a son whose genuine earthly relationship cannot become a guarantee of salvation. I can love a child and call that child toward what I understand to be right. I cannot believe on the child's behalf. I cannot transform my love into a verdict from GOD. The wound of the story lies precisely in the fact that the family bond is real while its power is limited.

**Verse references:** 11:42, 11:45-46.

## 4. The Limits of Love and Knowledge: Noah's Prayer

### Verse

> **11:43** — He said, “I will take refuge on top of a hill, to protect me from the water.” He said, “Nothing can protect anyone today from GOD’s judgment; only those worthy of His mercy (will be saved).” The waves separated them, and he was among those who drowned.

> **11:47** — He said, “My Lord, I seek refuge in You, lest I implore You again for something I do not know. Unless You forgive me, and have mercy on me, I will be with the losers.”

### Reflection on the Verse

Verse 11:43 is painful because it leaves no room for a convenient ending. The son seeks safety on a mountain; Noah points to the limits of protection before GOD's judgment. The waves separate them. I find it impossible to read this as a reason to feel superior to someone else's struggling family.

Noah's response in 11:47 is the part that stays with me: he asks forgiveness and acknowledges the danger of asking about something he does not know. There are things love allows me to know intimately. I may recognize a loved one's expression, fears, gifts, and history. But personal closeness does not give me complete knowledge of that person's standing before GOD.

I do not want to use this passage as a weapon by telling someone, “You are not my real family,” merely because we have an argument or differ in belief. That would take a statement made in a specific revealed narrative and convert it into my own authority to judge hearts. The first lesson I need from Noah's prayer may be much less comfortable: Can I admit where my knowledge ends, even when someone I love is involved? Can I admit that limit when I am tempted to condemn someone I dislike?

**Verse references:** 11:43, 11:45-47.

## 5. Sharing a Home Does Not Mean Sharing a Destiny

### Verse

> **66:10** — GOD cites as examples of those who disbelieved the wife of Noah and the wife of Lot. They were married to two of our righteous servants, but they betrayed them and, consequently, they could not help them at all against GOD. The two of them were told, “Enter the Hellfire with those who deserved it.” 

> **66:11** — And GOD cites as an example of those who believed the wife of Pharaoh. She said, “My Lord, build a home for me at You in Paradise, and save me from Pharaoh and his works; save me from the transgressing people.”

### Reflection on the Verse

Verse 66:10 speaks of the wives of Noah and Lot. Their husbands were righteous servants, yet the wives' own choices were not erased by marriage. Closeness to a righteous person did not substitute for their own response to GOD. The value of the marriage as a human relationship and the moral outcome of each individual are different questions.

The next example moves in the opposite direction. In 66:11, Pharaoh's wife turns toward GOD and asks to be saved from Pharaoh and his works. A person can live in the household of someone whose conduct is deeply wrong and still seek another way. Read together, the passages do not tell me that marriage is a sham. They tell me that marriage cannot automatically determine a soul's direction.

I cannot say, “My spouse is right, so I must be right,” any more than I can say, “My spouse has done wrong, so I have no possibility of righteousness.” But I must not swing to the other extreme and declare every difference within a household proof that the bond was never genuine. These verses are about responsibility before GOD. The daily duties of mercy, fairness, and care still need to be read alongside them.

**Verse references:** 66:10-11.

## 6. Reunion in Paradise: A Hope That Changes the Question

### Verse

> **52:21** — For those who believed, and their children  also  followed  them  in belief, we will have their children join them. We never fail to reward them for any work. Every person is paid for what he did.

> **13:23** — They  enter  the  gardens  of  Eden, together  with  the  righteous  among their  parents,  their  spouses,  and their  children.  The  angels  will  enter to them from every door.

### Reflection on the Verse

Whenever I hear that earthly family will be meaningless after death, I return to 52:21. It speaks about believers whose children follow them in faith and about those children being joined with them. Then it emphasizes that each person receives the consequence of what they have done. Reunion and individual accountability are not competing ideas in this verse. They stand together.

Verse 13:23 speaks of the righteous among parents, spouses, and children entering the gardens of Eden together. I cannot treat that promise as if it were absent. It means I should not declare that death necessarily reduces every family bond to nothing. At the same time, the expression **the righteous among them** matters. I cannot remove that qualification and announce a guaranteed future for every person I love.

I see neither an automatic separation between the family of faith and the family of birth nor an unconditional merger of the two. A family that shares belief and righteousness is given a meaningful hope of reunion. That hope is not mine to distribute as a certificate to named individuals. It is a reason to live faithfully and to leave final knowledge with GOD.

**Verse references:** 52:21, 13:23.

## 7. Becoming Family: Reconciliation and Shared Direction

### Verse

> **49:10** — The believers are members of one family; you shall keep the peace within your family and reverence GOD, that you may attain mercy.

> **3:103** — You  shall  hold  fast  to  the  rope  of GOD,  all  of  you,  and  do  not  be divided.  Recall  GOD’s  blessings upon  you—you  used  to  be  enemies and  He  reconciled  your  hearts.  By His  grace,  you  became  brethren. You were at the brink of a pit of fire, and  He  saved  you  therefrom.  GOD thus  explains  His  revelations  for you, that you may be guided.

### Reflection on the Verse

Verse 3:103 remembers people who had once been enemies, whose hearts were reconciled until they became brethren. Verse 49:10 asks the family of believers to preserve peace. The pairing is powerful: some forms of family are received at birth; others emerge through a shared direction and are strengthened through acts that protect the relationship.

I do not want to romanticize this. Reconciliation is not a word that makes injury disappear. Sometimes it requires someone to admit wrongdoing. Sometimes it requires the person who feels most certain to listen more carefully. Those are reflections on the command to preserve peace, not a claim that these verses give a detailed procedure for every dispute.

Calling other believers family widens the horizon of care beyond blood. It should not become a reason to despise the people to whom I am already related. If I expand the word “family” but narrow my capacity for mercy, something has gone wrong in my reading. A bond of faith should make me more accountable, not less.

**Verse references:** 49:10, 3:103.

## 8. Brotherhood Cannot Be Reduced to a Label

### Verse

> **9:71** — The believing men and women are allies of one another. They advocate righteousness and forbid evil, they observe  the Contact  Prayers  (Salat) and give the obligatory charity (Zakat), and they obey GOD and His messenger. These will be showered by GOD’s mercy. GOD is Almighty, Most Wise.

> **59:9** — As  for  those  who  provided  them with  a  home  and  a  refuge,  and  were believers  before  them,  they  love those  who  immigrated  to  them,  and find  no  hesitation  in  their  hearts  in helping  them.  In  fact,  they  readily give  them  priority  over  themselves, even  when  they  themselves  need what  they  give  away.  Indeed,  those who  overcome  their  natural  stinginess are the successful ones.

### Reflection on the Verse

What does it cost to say, “We are family”? Verse 9:71 describes believing men and women as allies of one another. Verse 59:9 brings that solidarity into ordinary need, describing people who welcomed others and preferred them even while they themselves were in need. One passage speaks about the relationship; the other makes me feel its weight.

I can share someone's religious vocabulary without sharing their burdens. I can call a person a brother and then disappear when that person needs help. The example of 59:9 makes such contradictions difficult to ignore. The people it describes did not wait until they had no needs of their own before showing care.

That does not let me demand an identical level of sacrifice from every person in every circumstance. It does ask me to examine what I mean by brotherhood. Is the bond visible in fairness, support, and generosity, or only in words we use when we already agree? The name “family” is precious precisely because it should mean more than a label.

**Verse references:** 9:71, 59:9.

## 9. Parents Do Not Disappear When We Discover a Spiritual Family

### Verse

> **17:23** — Your  Lord  has  decreed  that  you shall not worship except Him, and your parents shall be honored. As long as one or both of them live, you shall never say to them, “Uff” (the slightest gesture of annoyance), nor shall you shout at them; you shall treat them amicably.

> **17:24** — And  lower  for  them  the  wings  of humility,  and  kindness,  and  say, “My  Lord,  have  mercy  on  them,  for they have raised me from infancy.”

### Reflection on the Verse

Discovering the family of believers does not remove my parents from the moral picture. Verses 17:23-24 speak with remarkable directness about how parents should be treated, right down to a small expression of irritation and the manner of speech. Respect is not only an opinion that I hold about them. It enters my voice, gestures, patience, and humility.

I am struck by how worshiping GOD alone appears beside honoring parents. Submission to GOD does not elevate me into a little ruler over my household. It asks something of my character in the closest relationships I have. Even when I think I am right, I can speak cruelly. Being correct and behaving kindly are not the same achievement.

This does not mean that every action by a parent is automatically just. Honor is not the same as declaring a human being incapable of error. In my earlier reflection on <a href="https://berkkoksal.com/writings/responding_to_evil_with_what_is_best_en/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Responding to Evil with What Is Best</a>, I considered how justice and forgiveness can be held in view together. Within the family the question is even more personal: Can I preserve truth without turning a disagreement into a license for humiliation?

**Verse references:** 17:23-24.

## 10. When Parents Are Wrong: Respect Without Surrendering Conscience

### Verse

> **31:14** — We  enjoined  the  human  being  to honor  his  parents.  His  mother  bore him,  and  the  load  got  heavier  and heavier.  It  takes  two  years  (of  intensive  care)  until  weaning.  You  shall be  appreciative  of  Me,  and  of  your parents.  To  Me  is  the  ultimate  destiny.

> **31:15** — If  they  try  to  force  you  to  set  up any idols beside Me, do not obey them. But  continue  to  treat  them  amicably in this world. You shall follow only the  path  of  those  who  have  submitted  to  Me.  Ultimately,  you  all return to Me, then I will inform you of  everything you have done.

### Reflection on the Verse

Verse 31:14 recalls the effort of a mother carrying and caring for her child and calls for appreciation toward GOD and parents. The next verse establishes a limit: if parents try to force a person to set up idols beside GOD, they are not to be obeyed, but they are still to be treated amicably in this world. I find that combination deeply demanding.

Love does not turn every parental demand into truth. Yet refusing an improper demand does not give me permission to abandon civility. There are two disciplines here: not allowing a relationship to replace GOD, and not allowing a conflict to erase the human being in front of me.

I should also be careful not to exaggerate what is written. Verse 31:15 specifically addresses pressure to commit idolatry. I cannot label every ordinary disagreement with a parent as the exact situation described in that verse. Still, it reveals an important distinction between honoring someone and assigning that person unlimited authority over belief. It also turns the question back on me: Can I disagree while taking responsibility for my own words?

**Verse references:** 31:14-15.

## 11. Kinship Has Rights: Giving, Care, and Justice

### Verse

> **16:90** — GOD advocates justice, charity, and regarding the relatives. And He forbids evil, vice, and transgression. He enlightens you, that you may take heed.

> **2:215** — They ask you about giving: say, “The charity you give shall go to the parents, the relatives, the orphans, the poor, and the traveling alien.” Any good  you  do,  GOD  is  fully  aware thereof.

> **24:22** — Those among you who are blessed with resources and wealth shall be charitable  towards  their  relatives, the poor, and those who have immigrated for the sake of GOD. They shall treat them with kindness and tolerance; do you not love to attain GOD’s forgiveness? GOD is Forgiver, Most Merciful.

### Reflection on the Verse

We sometimes search for the meaning of real family in enormous metaphysical questions, while neglecting what happens when a relative is in need. Verse 16:90 advocates justice, charity, and regard for relatives. Verse 2:215 includes parents and kin among those to whom charity should go. Verse 24:22 speaks of kindness and tolerance as well as generosity toward relatives. These passages do not describe kinship as an empty fiction.

Their challenge is practical. Can I announce that I belong to a spiritual family while ignoring the ordinary needs of someone who depends on me? Can I give assistance without turning it into an instrument of control? Can I defend one relative without violating the rights of another? The connection between justice and care in 16:90 makes these questions unavoidable.

Yet regard for relatives should not be stretched into the claim that any relative may demand limitless money or power over another person's life. The verses authorize concern and generosity, not unchecked domination. When generosity becomes leverage, the outward act and the inward moral direction may no longer match.

**Verse references:** 16:90, 2:215, 24:22.

## 12. When Love Takes the Place of GOD

### Verse

> **9:24** — Proclaim:  “If  your  parents,  your children, your siblings, your spouses, your family, the money you have earned, a business you worry about, and the homes you cherish are more beloved to you than GOD and His messenger,** and the striving in His cause, then just wait until GOD brings His judgment.” GOD does not guide the wicked people.

> **63:9** — O  you  who  believe,  do  not  be  distracted  by  your  money  and  your children  from  remembering  GOD. Those who do this are the losers. 

### Reflection on the Verse

Verse 9:24 makes me ask an uncomfortable question: Is loving my family the same as placing my family above every other obligation? It names parents, children, spouses, and family while examining what occupies the highest place in a human life. Verse 63:9 warns against letting money and children distract believers from remembering GOD.

The warning does not make parental love a defect. It asks me to see what happens when love becomes a justification for abandoning truth. A person can become so frightened of losing a loved one that they begin to excuse harm or deny what they know. A parent may confuse personal fear with a child's good. These are reflections prompted by the question of ultimate allegiance, not detailed legal rulings contained in either verse.

I want to love deeply without asking love to become my god. The people closest to me do not become less valuable when I refuse to make them absolute. Perhaps they become safer from the expectations and fears I would otherwise place upon them. Loyalty to GOD and tenderness toward family need not be enemies, provided I do not confuse their roles.

**Verse references:** 9:24, 63:9.

## 13. How Can a Blessing Also Be a Test?

### Verse

> **18:46** — Money and children are the joys of this life, but the righteous works provide  an  eternal  recompense from your Lord, and a far better hope.

> **64:14** — O  you  who  believe,  your  spouses and  your  children  can  be  your enemies;  beware.  If  you  pardon,  forget,  and  forgive,  then  GOD  is  Forgiver, Most Merciful.

> **64:15** — Your  money  and  children  are  a  test, and  GOD  possesses  a  great  recompense.

### Reflection on the Verse

Verse 18:46 describes money and children as joys of this life. Verse 64:15 calls money and children a test. Both can be true. Being tested through something does not mean that thing is fake or evil. Often the things I cherish most reveal my fears, my priorities, and the quality of my choices.

Verse 64:14 warns that spouses and children *can* be enemies, but the verse also speaks of pardoning, overlooking, and forgiving. The distinction between **can be** and **are** is essential. I have no right to turn a warning about possible opposition into a general accusation against every family member. Nor does forgiveness mean that harm must be denied or every damaging situation endlessly endured.

Instead of rushing to ask whether my family is a test for me, perhaps I should ask how I become a test for them. Does my love help another person act with conscience, or does it tighten around that person like possession? Calling someone a blessing does not give me unlimited power over their life. In fact, it may remind me to act more carefully.

**Verse references:** 18:46, 64:14-15.

## 14. Different Beliefs and Difficult Boundaries: Must Every Bond Be Cut?

### Verse

> **58:22** — You will not find people who believe in GOD and the Last Day befriending those who oppose GOD and His messenger, even if they were their parents, or their children, or their siblings, or their tribe. For these, He decrees faith into their hearts, and supports them with inspiration from Him, and admits them into gardens with flowing streams wherein they abide forever. GOD is pleased with them,  and  they  are  pleased  with Him. These are the party of GOD. Most assuredly, GOD’s party are the winners.

> **60:8** — GOD  does  not  enjoin  you  from befriending  those  who  do  not  fight you  because  of  religion,  and  do  not evict  you  from  your  homes.  You  may befriend  them  and  be  equitable towards  them.  GOD  loves  the  equitable.

> **60:9** — GOD  enjoins  you  only  from  befriending  those  who  fight  you  because  of  religion,  evict  you  from your  homes,  and  band  together  with others  to  banish  you.  You  shall  not befriend  them.  Those  who  befriend them are the transgressors.

### Reflection on the Verse

Verse 58:22 is firm about allegiance to people who oppose GOD and His messenger, even when they are close relatives. I should not soften its wording just because it is uncomfortable. But if I read it without 60:8-9 and conclude that every family relationship involving differing beliefs must be severed, I lose distinctions that the Quran itself makes. Verse 60:8 permits friendship and equitable conduct toward people who do not fight over religion or drive others from their homes. Verse 60:9 separately addresses those who do.

These are not interchangeable categories. Opposition, aggression, friendship, equitable conduct, and kinship must be considered in their stated contexts. I want to avoid turning my own impatience into an absolute command supposedly issued by GOD. I also cannot claim comprehensive knowledge of another person's hidden faith or final destiny.

The challenge is to hold two things together: loyalty to what I understand from revelation, and justice toward people. It may be especially difficult when the person concerned is someone I love, because emotion tempts me to erase distinctions. But careful distinctions are exactly what justice requires.

**Verse references:** 58:22, 60:8-9; compare 31:15.

## 15. Divorce and Separation: Does a Changed Household Become a False One?

### Verse

> **2:233** — Divorced mothers shall nurse their infants two full years, if the father so wishes. The father shall provide the mother’s food and clothing equitably. No one shall be burdened beyond his ability. No mother shall beharmed on account of her infant, nor shall the father be harmed because of his infant. (If the father dies), his inheritor shall assume these responsibilities. If the infant’s parents mutually agree to part, after due consultation, they commit no error by doing so. You commit no error by hiring nursing mothers, so long as you pay them equitably. You shall observe GOD, and know that GOD is Seer of everything you do.

> **4:135** — O  you who  believe,  you  shall  be absolutely  equitable,  and  observe GOD,  when  you  serve  as  witnesses, even  against  yourselves,  or  your parents,  or  your  relatives.  Whether the  accused  is  rich  or  poor,  GOD takes  care  of  both.  Therefore,  do not  be  biased  by  your  personal wishes.  If  you  deviate  or  disregard (this  commandment),  then  GOD  is fully  Cognizant  of  everything  you do.

### Reflection on the Verse

When a marriage ends, people sometimes describe everything that preceded it as fake. The hurt may be genuine, but the conclusion does not necessarily follow. Verse 2:233 continues to speak about the responsibilities of mother and father after separation. It addresses the infant's care, fair provision, burdens that must not exceed capacity, avoiding harm to either parent through the child, and mutual consultation. A change in the couple's relationship does not erase all obligations toward the child.

I cannot use this verse to invent a complete custody system or a universal visitation timetable. Its direct subject is nursing and the associated responsibilities. What it does offer is an important ethical boundary: a parent's grievance should not be turned into a pretext for harming the other parent through the child. Verse 4:135 calls for justice even when testimony concerns one's own parents or relatives.

A child may love both mother and father. That love should not become a weapon in the adults' conflict. If the form of a household changes while human rights and duties remain, why should the earlier bond be called wholly unreal? These passages do not promise that every marriage must continue. They challenge the assumption that a painful separation excuses injustice.

**Verse references:** 2:233, 4:135.

## 16. The Day of Judgment: Why Kinship Cannot Answer for Me

### Verse

> **23:101** — When  the  horn  is  blown,  no  relations  among  them  will  exist  on  that day,  nor  will  they  care  about  one another.

> **80:34** — That  is  the  day  when  one  flees  from his brother.

> **80:35** — From his mother and father.

> **80:36** — From his spouse and children.

> **80:37** — Each  one  of  them,  on  that  day,  worries about his own destiny.

> **19:95** — All of them will come before Him on the Day of Resurrection as individuals.

### Reflection on the Verse

Verses 23:101 and 80:34-37 describe a moment in which the bonds that seemed most protective during earthly life cannot serve as automatic refuge. People flee from close relatives, and each is absorbed in their own destiny. Verse 19:95 says that all come before GOD as individuals. Even the person who knows me best cannot take responsibility for my final account.

But I must distinguish the scenes and conditions. The description in 23:101 cannot simply erase the hope of reunion in 52:21. One passage shows that lineage is no guarantee at the reckoning; the other speaks of believers being joined with children who followed them in faith. Treating those as contradictory may come from ignoring the difference between the passages rather than reading both carefully.

So I want to change my question. Instead of asking, “Will my family save me?” I might ask, “What kind of person was I when I was with them?” A surname, the affection of a parent, or the reputation of a spouse cannot become my righteousness. If I will answer as an individual, my justice and mercy within the family are also my own responsibility today.

**Verse references:** 23:101, 80:34-37, 19:95; compare 52:21.

## 17. Arabic Roots: Family, Brotherhood, Lineage, and Righteousness

### Verse

> **49:10** — The believers are members of one family; you shall keep the peace within your family and reverence GOD, that you may attain mercy.

> **11:46** — He said, “O Noah, he is not of your family. It is unrighteous to ask Me for something you do not know.* I enlighten you, lest you be like the ignorant.”

> **23:101** — When  the  horn  is  blown,  no  relations  among  them  will  exist  on  that day,  nor  will  they  care  about  one another.

> **13:23** — They  enter  the  gardens  of  Eden, together  with  the  righteous  among their  parents,  their  spouses,  and their  children.  The  angels  will  enter to them from every door.

### Reflection on the Verse

In the Arabic of 49:10, **إِخْوَةٌ** (*ikhwatun*) belongs to the semantic field of brotherhood associated with the root **أ-خ-و** (*ʾ-kh-w*). In Rashad Khalifa’s Authorized English Translation the wording is **“members of one family”**; the Turkish translation of the Authorized English Translation says **“tek bir ailenin fertleridir.”** Looking at the word's family of meanings helps me recognize the closeness being described, but the verse's command to keep peace is what places that closeness into lived responsibility.

The word **أَهْلِكَ** (*ahlika*) in 11:46 concerns family or household and is associated with **أ-ه-ل** (*ʾ-h-l*). In 23:101, **أَنسَابَ** (*ansāba*) draws from **ن-س-ب** (*n-s-b*), the field of lineage and descent. These are related but not identical questions: Who is related to me in this life, and what authority or protection does that relationship provide when I stand before GOD?

Verse 13:23 uses **صَلَحَ** (*ṣalaḥa*), associated with the root **ص-ل-ح** (*ṣ-l-ḥ*), in its reference to the righteous among parents, spouses, and children. The condition matters. Roots can illuminate the distinctions between family, descent, and righteousness, but no isolated dictionary entry gives me permission to overwrite a verse's context or announce a new doctrine. I use the language to read more carefully, not to claim certainty that the text does not grant.

**Verse references:** 49:10, 11:46, 23:101, 13:23.

## 18. Other Claims

### Verse

> **49:10** — The believers are members of one family; you shall keep the peace within your family and reverence GOD, that you may attain mercy.

> **52:21** — For those who believed, and their children  also  followed  them  in belief, we will have their children join them. We never fail to reward them for any work. Every person is paid for what he did.

> **17:23** — Your  Lord  has  decreed  that  you shall not worship except Him, and your parents shall be honored. As long as one or both of them live, you shall never say to them, “Uff” (the slightest gesture of annoyance), nor shall you shout at them; you shall treat them amicably.

### Reflection on the Verse

Several historical, religious, and social readings of family can be considered. One approach treats blood ties as the only real family and reduces spiritual kinship to a metaphor. Another takes the opposite position and calls earthly family false because the world is temporary. I find it difficult to reconcile either absolute claim with 49:10 on the family of believers, 17:23 on parents, and 52:21 on reunion.

There is also a view that anyone regarded as a member of the faithful family is automatically guaranteed reunion in the Hereafter. Yet 52:21 describes children following in faith, and 13:23 identifies the righteous among relatives. I have no warrant to declare that a named person's unseen condition fulfills those requirements. The opposite claim—that every family member is secretly an enemy—would likewise flatten the careful warning in 64:14, where opposition is presented as a possibility rather than a universal identity.

Different historical and sectarian discussions have also treated Noah's son in 11:46 in different ways. Such readings ought to be approached cautiously. Whatever explanation is proposed, the description of him as Noah's son in 11:42 and Noah's own words in 11:45 remain part of the same account. I do not use external interpretations as the foundation of the reflection above. They remain possibilities to be assessed rather than verdicts imposed upon the verses.

**Verse references:** 49:10, 17:23, 11:42, 11:45-46, 13:23, 52:21, 64:14.

## 19. Conclusion: Family Is Not False, but It Cannot Replace the Truth

### Verse

> **49:10** — The believers are members of one family; you shall keep the peace within your family and reverence GOD, that you may attain mercy.

> **11:46** — He said, “O Noah, he is not of your family. It is unrighteous to ask Me for something you do not know.* I enlighten you, lest you be like the ignorant.”

> **52:21** — For those who believed, and their children  also  followed  them  in belief, we will have their children join them. We never fail to reward them for any work. Every person is paid for what he did.

> **16:90** — GOD advocates justice, charity, and regarding the relatives. And He forbids evil, vice, and transgression. He enlightens you, that you may take heed.

### Reflection on the Verse

I return to the first question with greater care: **Is our family in this world fake?** I cannot derive such a general conclusion from these verses. Parents are to be honored, love and care between spouses are counted among GOD's signs, and the rights of relatives are upheld. I do not want to call something false merely because it is not guaranteed to last forever.

Nor can I say, “Because these people are my family, we will certainly share the same final destiny.” Noah's son makes the limit of kinship unmistakable. The examples of Noah's and Lot's wives show that living beside a righteous person does not remove the need to make one's own choices. The description of each person arriving individually for judgment makes that limit more difficult to evade. Kinship is a gift; it is not a power that overrides GOD's judgment.

The family described in 49:10 matters for precisely this reason. It shows that true responsibilities can exist beyond ancestry, in a community shaped by belief and the duty to keep peace. Yet 52:21 and 13:23 keep me from imagining that all earthly bonds simply disappear: there is hope of reunion for family members joined by belief and righteousness. I must honor that hope without claiming to know the hidden outcome of particular people.

Finally, the question turns toward my own life. Do I use the word “family” to control someone or to become more fair and compassionate toward them? When a person close to me acts differently from what I want, can I respond without turning my love into ownership? What kind of family member am I to my parents, spouse, children, relatives, and brothers and sisters in faith? **The search for true family should begin by examining how truthful I am in the relationships already entrusted to me.**

This is where my reflection now stands: an earthly family can be real, and the family of believers can also be real. Neither bond can occupy the place of GOD. I want to love without making an idol of love, acknowledge limits without denying mercy, and work righteousness without pretending to know the final judgment. The rest belongs to GOD, whose knowledge exceeds both my affection and my fear.

**Verse references:** 49:10, 11:42, 11:45-47, 17:23-24, 30:21, 16:90, 52:21, 13:23, 66:10-11, 19:95.

GOD knows best.
