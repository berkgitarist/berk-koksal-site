# Responding to Evil with What Is Best: Where Does Justice End and Forgiveness Begin?

**Author:** Berk KÖKSAL  
**Date:** 12.10.2026  
**Place:** Mersin

The main purpose of this article is to reflect on the verses together with other related verses and to explore, in a sincere and profound language, what witnessing the oneness of GOD means in human life in terms of knowledge, justice, truthfulness, and submission.

Peace. Peace be upon you.

“Respond to evil with good” can become a sentimental slogan if it is separated from the rest of the Quranic balance. 41:34 calls for the nicest possible response. 42:40 recognizes a just, equivalent response to wrongdoing while praising forgiveness. 42:41 explicitly states that those who defend themselves after injustice commit no error.

The verses therefore do not erase justice in the name of forgiveness, nor do they allow justice to become unlimited retaliation. 5:8 forbids hostility from producing injustice. 16:126 limits response to equivalence and then points to patience as better. 3:134 praises restraint of anger and forgiveness.

The central question of this article is: **How can a person resist evil without allowing evil to reproduce itself through him?** The answer is neither passive surrender nor the sanctification of revenge. The verses build a demanding moral discipline between them.

## 1. The Good Response and the Bad Response Are Not Equal

### Verse

> **41:34**
>
> وَلَا تَسْتَوِى ٱلْحَسَنَةُ وَلَا ٱلسَّيِّئَةُ ٱدْفَعْ بِٱلَّتِى هِىَ أَحْسَنُ فَإِذَا ٱلَّذِى بَيْنَكَ وَبَيْنَهُۥ عَدَٰوَةٌ كَأَنَّهُۥ وَلِىٌّ حَمِيمٌ
>
> Not  equal  is  the  good  response  and the  bad  response.  You  shall  resort to  the  nicest  possible  response. Thus,  the  one  who  used  to  be  your enemy,  may  become  your  best friend.
>
> İyi yanıt ile kötü yanıt eşit değildir. Sen mümkün olan en güzel yanıtı vermeye başvur. Böylece, vaktiyle düşmanın olan o kişi senin en iyi dostun olabilir.

### Reflection on the Verse

41:34 begins with a moral distinction: the good response and the bad response are not equal. Being harmed does not automatically make every form of retaliation morally equivalent to goodness.

The verse then calls for the nicest possible response. That is difficult because pain often creates the desire to make the other person feel pain in return.

The verse mentions the possibility that an enemy may become a close friend. It does not promise this outcome in every case. The response is not a manipulation technique; it is a way of protecting one’s own moral direction.

**Verse references:** 41:34.
## 2. The Best Response Requires Strength of Character

### Verse

> **41:35** — None  can  attain  this  except  those who  steadfastly  persevere.  None can  attain  this  except  those  who  are extremely fortunate.

### Reflection on the Verse

41:35 openly acknowledges that this ethic is difficult. It belongs to those who steadfastly persevere.

Anger can be easier than restraint. Immediate retaliation may create emotional relief, while patience requires a person to remain governed by principle under pressure.

The verse therefore prevents us from confusing gentleness with weakness. The best response may require more inner strength than the harshest one.

**Verse references:** 41:35.
## 3. Repelling Evil with Good

### Verse

> **23:96** — Therefore,  counter  their  evil  works with  goodness;  we  are  fully  aware  of their claims.

### Reflection on the Verse

23:96 commands that bad deeds be answered with good while reminding us that GOD fully knows the claims being made.

This does not make evil good. It refuses to allow evil to dictate the moral form of the response.

GOD’s knowledge also means I do not have to act as though the entire moral universe depends upon my ability to punish every wrong personally.

**Verse references:** 23:96.
## 4. Restraining Anger and Forgiving

### Verse

> **3:134** — who  give  to  charity  during  the  good times, as well as the bad times. They are  suppressors  of  anger,  and  pardoners of the people. GOD loves the charitable.

### Reflection on the Verse

3:134 describes those who restrain anger and forgive people. Anger is not denied; it is governed.

Forgiveness may therefore be an ethical decision before it becomes an emotion. A person may still feel hurt while refusing to let hurt dictate conduct.

The verse places this discipline among the qualities GOD loves, making emotional self-government part of righteousness.

**Verse references:** 3:134.
## 5. Forgive, Advocate Tolerance, Ignore the Ignorant

### Verse

> **7:199** — You  shall  resort  to  pardon,  advocate  tolerance,  and  disregard  the  ignorant.

### Reflection on the Verse

7:199 gives three concise directions: take the path of forgiveness, advocate tolerance, and ignore the ignorant. This means not every provocation deserves an answer.

Sometimes a debate continues not because truth requires it, but because the ego wants the final word. This connects with *The Most Argumentative Creature*.

Ignoring ignorance is not abandoning truth. It can be the refusal to let another person’s disorder determine one’s own rhythm.

**Verse references:** 7:199.
## 6. Just Recompense Exists, and So Does Forgiveness

### Verse

> **42:40** — Although  the  just  requital  for  an injustice  is  an  equivalent  retribution,  those  who  pardon  and  maintain  righteousness  are  rewarded  by GOD. He does not love the unjust.

### Reflection on the Verse

42:40 is one of the clearest balancing verses on the subject. It recognizes an equivalent response to injustice and then declares a reward with GOD for those who forgive and maintain righteousness.

Forgiveness is therefore not converted into compulsory passivity. A just response remains possible, but equivalence imposes a boundary: anger cannot enlarge the punishment.

Forgiveness opens another moral possibility beyond the strict exercise of one’s right.

**Verse references:** 42:40.
## 7. Defending One’s Rights Is Not Wrongdoing

### Verse

> **42:41** — Certainly, those who stand up for their rights, when injustice befalls them, are not committing any error.
>
> **42:42** — The wrong ones are those who treat the people unjustly, and resort to aggression without provocation. These have incurred a painful retribution.

### Reflection on the Verse

42:41 states that those who defend themselves after injustice do not commit an error. 42:42 then locates wrongdoing in unjust treatment and unprovoked aggression.

This distinction is crucial. Defense and aggression are not the same.

Any ethic of forgiveness that is used to silence the victim loses this Quranic balance. A person may speak, seek justice, and set limits without becoming an aggressor.

**Verse references:** 42:41; 42:42.
## 8. Patience and Forgiveness as Character Strength

### Verse

> **42:43** — Resorting to patience and forgiveness reflects a true strength of character.

### Reflection on the Verse

42:43 describes patience and forgiveness as reflecting true strength of character. Forgiveness is therefore not simply surrender.

To see the wrong, possess the right to respond, and yet choose restraint can require considerable inner power.

The harm done to me does not have to become the architect of the person I become.

**Verse references:** 42:43.
## 9. The Response Must Be Equivalent

### Verse

> **16:126** — And if you punish, you shall inflict an equivalent punishment. But if you resort to patience (instead of revenge), it would be better for the patient ones.

### Reflection on the Verse

16:126 states that if punishment is chosen it must be equivalent, then adds that patience is better for those who can be patient.

Retaliation naturally tends to expand. Each side experiences its own suffering more intensely and can therefore justify escalation. The word “equivalent” interrupts that cycle.

Justice asks for measure; revenge asks for satisfaction. The verse teaches me not to confuse them.

**Verse references:** 16:126.
## 10. Patience Comes with GOD’s Help

### Verse

> **16:127** — You  shall  resort  to  patience—and your patience is attainable only with GOD’s help. Do not grieve over them, and do not be annoyed by their schemes.
>
> **16:128** — GOD is with those who lead a righteous life, and those who are charitable.

### Reflection on the Verse

16:127 connects patience with GOD’s help. This is important because severe injustice can exhaust ordinary human strength.

16:128 then states that GOD is with those who lead a righteous life and are charitable. The best response is therefore not a performance of personal nobility; it belongs to a life oriented toward GOD.

The center is not “Look how generous I am,” but “How do I remain righteous before GOD in this situation?”

**Verse references:** 16:127; 16:128.
## 11. Hostility Must Not Corrupt Justice

### Verse

> **5:8** — O  you  who  believe,  you  shall  be  absolutely  equitable,  and  observe GOD,  when  you  serve  as  witnesses. Do  not  be  provoked  by  your  conflicts  with  some  people  into  committing  injustice.  You  shall  be  absolutely  equitable,  for  it  is  more  righteous. You  shall  observe  GOD.  GOD  is fully  Cognizant  of  everything  you do.

### Reflection on the Verse

5:8 commands complete justice and forbids conflict with other people from provoking injustice. This may be one of the hardest forms of justice: justice toward the person I dislike.

The ego easily creates exceptions for enemies. The verse removes those exceptions.

The best response may take different forms—mercy, silence, defense, legal action—but none of them can legitimize injustice.

**Verse references:** 5:8.
## 12. The Wronged Person Has a Voice

### Verse

> **4:148** — GOD does not like the utterance of bad language, unless one is treated with gross injustice. GOD is Hearer, Knower.

### Reflection on the Verse

4:148 states that GOD does not like bad language except in the case of one who has suffered grave injustice. This creates space for the wronged person to speak about what happened.

Forgiveness must not be weaponized to demand silence from the victim. The Quranic balance allows the wrong to be named.

The goal is not to conceal injustice, but to prevent injustice from multiplying through the response.

**Verse references:** 4:148.
## 13. When the Ignorant Speak, Say Peace

### Verse

> **25:63** — The worshipers of the Most Gracious are those who tread the earth gently, and when the ignorant speak to them, they only utter peace.

### Reflection on the Verse

25:63 describes the servants of the Most Gracious as walking humbly and responding to the ignorant with peace.

Silence or peace can feel like defeat to the ego. Yet the verse presents them as marks of worship.

Sometimes the most beautiful response is not the sentence that defeats the other person, but the one that refuses to let anger rule the heart.

**Verse references:** 25:63.
## 14. Leaving Empty Talk Behind

### Verse

> **28:55** — When they come across vain talk, they disregard it and say, “We are responsible for our deeds, and you are  responsible  for  your  deeds. Peace be upon you. We do not wish to behave like the ignorant ones.” 

### Reflection on the Verse

28:55 describes people who ignore vain talk and state that each side is responsible for its own deeds, ending with peace.

This gives a boundary to debate. Not every false claim must be pursued indefinitely.

Turning back to one’s own responsibility can be more faithful than being drawn into endless conflict.

**Verse references:** 28:55.
## 15. A Kind Word Is Better than Charity Followed by Insult

### Verse

> **2:263** — Kind  words  and  compassion  are better than a charity that is followed by insult. GOD is Rich, Clement.

### Reflection on the Verse

2:263 prefers kind words and compassion to charity followed by insult. Goodness is therefore not only about the external act, but also about the manner in which it is performed.

A person can help materially and then injure with speech. A person can also speak a true sentence in a humiliating way.

The best response includes tone, not merely outcome.

**Verse references:** 2:263.
## 16. Justice and Kindness Toward Those Who Are Not Hostile

### Verse

> **60:8** — GOD  does  not  enjoin  you  from befriending  those  who  do  not  fight you  because  of  religion,  and  do  not evict  you  from  your  homes.  You  may befriend  them  and  be  equitable towards  them.  GOD  loves  the  equitable.

### Reflection on the Verse

60:8 allows friendship and justice toward those who do not fight people over religion or expel them from their homes, and states that GOD loves the just.

Difference is not automatically converted into hostility.

This expands the theme from personal conflict into social ethics: conduct and justice matter more than the instinct to divide everyone into permanent enemies and allies.

**Verse references:** 60:8.
## 17. Defending Truth Without Losing the Manner

### Verse

> **16:125** — You shall invite to the path of your Lord  with  wisdom  and  kind  enlightenment, and debate with them in the best possible manner. Your Lord knows best who has strayed from His path, and He knows best who are the guided ones.

### Reflection on the Verse

16:125 commands invitation with wisdom, gentle enlightenment, and the nicest possible manner of debate. Being right does not remove responsibility for the way truth is communicated.

This connects directly with *The Most Argumentative Creature*. A person can defend truth in a way that contradicts the morality of truth.

The best response belongs in intellectual and religious disagreement as much as in personal conflict.

**Verse references:** 16:125.
## 18. The Measure Between Justice and Forgiveness

### Verse

> **41:34** — Not  equal  is  the  good  response  and the  bad  response.  You  shall  resort to  the  nicest  possible  response. Thus,  the  one  who  used  to  be  your enemy,  may  become  your  best friend.
>
> **42:40** — Although  the  just  requital  for  an injustice  is  an  equivalent  retribution,  those  who  pardon  and  maintain  righteousness  are  rewarded  by GOD. He does not love the unjust.
>
> **5:8** — O  you  who  believe,  you  shall  be  absolutely  equitable,  and  observe GOD,  when  you  serve  as  witnesses. Do  not  be  provoked  by  your  conflicts  with  some  people  into  committing  injustice.  You  shall  be  absolutely  equitable,  for  it  is  more  righteous. You  shall  observe  GOD.  GOD  is fully  Cognizant  of  everything  you do.

### Reflection on the Verse

41:34 calls for the nicest response. 42:40 balances equivalent recompense with forgiveness. 5:8 prohibits hostility from corrupting justice. Together they provide a measure rather than a single mechanical rule.

Forgiveness is not required in every situation, and punishment is not required in every situation. What is required is that evil not drive me into injustice.

The boundary I see is this: forgiveness must not erase justice, and justice must not become revenge that makes mercy impossible.

**Verse references:** 41:34; 42:40; 5:8.
## 19. Arabic Roots: Goodness, Evil, Repelling, Patience, and Forgiveness

### Verse

> **41:34** — Not  equal  is  the  good  response  and the  bad  response.  You  shall  resort to  the  nicest  possible  response. Thus,  the  one  who  used  to  be  your enemy,  may  become  your  best friend.
>
> **42:40** — Although  the  just  requital  for  an injustice  is  an  equivalent  retribution,  those  who  pardon  and  maintain  righteousness  are  rewarded  by GOD. He does not love the unjust.
>
> **16:126** — And if you punish, you shall inflict an equivalent punishment. But if you resort to patience (instead of revenge), it would be better for the patient ones.

### Reflection on the Verse

In 41:34, the field of goodness is expressed through **ح س ن (ḥ-s-n)** and the field of evil through **س و ء (s-w-ʾ)**. The verse explicitly denies their moral equality.

The response is expressed through **د ف ع (d-f-ʿ)**, a root of pushing back, repelling, or answering. The command is not passive disappearance but a transformed response: repel with what is best.

Patience is expressed through **ص ب ر (ṣ-b-r)** in 41:35, 42:43, and 16:126-127. Here patience is not lifeless endurance; it is the capacity to preserve moral direction under pressure.

Forgiveness is connected with **ع ف و (ʿ-f-w)**, visible in 42:40 and 3:134. It is the possibility of relinquishing a right of retaliation without calling the original wrong good.

Justice is connected with **ع د ل (ʿ-d-l)**, central to 5:8. Together these roots show the balance: evil may be resisted, but resistance must remain beautiful, measured, patient, and just.

**Verse references:** 41:34; 42:40; 16:126.
## Other Claims

The relationship between forgiveness and justice has been discussed across religious, legal, philosophical, and political traditions. Some approaches praise forgiveness as the highest virtue, while others sharply distinguish private forgiveness from public justice.

Those histories do not determine the main conclusion here. The verses already provide a sufficient balance: 42:40 allows equivalent recompense and praises forgiveness; 42:41 allows self-defense; 5:8 forbids injustice toward enemies; 41:34 calls for the nicest response.

It would therefore be too broad to claim either that the Quran requires forgiveness in every case or that seeking justice is contrary to forgiveness.

## Conclusion

Responding to evil with what is best does not mean pretending that evil never happened. It does not mean refusing to defend one’s rights. The Quranic path is more demanding: recognize the injustice, respond if necessary, but do not exceed the limit; do not let anger corrupt justice; do not despise forgiveness when forgiveness is possible.

The thought that remains with me is simple: **another person’s evil cannot become the excuse for my own evil.**

Being wronged does not authorize me to lie, humiliate, over-punish, or sanctify hatred. I may forgive when forgiveness is right. I may defend my rights when defense is necessary. In both cases I remain responsible before GOD for justice.

GOD knows best.
