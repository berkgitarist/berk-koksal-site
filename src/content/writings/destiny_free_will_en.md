## 1. Why Do I Choose Within a Life Already Written?

### Verse

**57:22**

**Arabic**

<blockquote data-verse="57:22" data-language="ar">
<p lang="ar" dir="rtl">مَآ أَصَابَ مِن مُّصِيبَةٍ فِى ٱلْأَرْضِ وَلَا فِىٓ أَنفُسِكُمْ إِلَّا فِى كِتَٰبٍ مِّن قَبْلِ أَن نَّبْرَأَهَآ إِنَّ ذَٰلِكَ عَلَى ٱللَّهِ يَسِيرٌ</p>
</blockquote>

**English — Rashad Khalifa’s Authorized English Translation**

<blockquote data-verse="57:22" data-language="en">
<p lang="en">Anything  that  happens  on  earth,  or to  you,  has  already  been  recorded, even  before  the  creation.  This  is easy for GOD to do.</p>
</blockquote>

**Turkish — the Turkish translation of the Authorized English Translation**

<blockquote data-verse="57:22" data-language="tr">
<p lang="tr">Yeryüzünde ya da size olan her şey, yaratılıştan bile önce, zaten kaydedilmiştir. Bunu yapmak TANRI için kolaydır.</p>
</blockquote>

Source: <a href="https://kuranteyit.com/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">kuranteyit.com</a>

### Reflection on the Verse

When I do something wrong, should the fact that it was known beforehand comfort me? When I have not yet made a decision, does GOD’s knowledge of its outcome make my decision insignificant? Destiny can sound like a distant subject of debate. Yet it enters our lives through an apology, a postponement, an act of courage, or a decision to give up.

My first responsibility before 57:22 is to let its breadth stand. I cannot treat events on earth and in my life as surprises outside GOD’s knowledge, discovered only afterward. But moving immediately from the existence of a record to my own lack of responsibility is a separate claim. I must distinguish what the verse states from the conclusion I might want to draw.

The two possibilities in the title may therefore have been set against each other too quickly. An event being recorded, a human being willing, GOD’s will, and accountability for human conduct are different sides of the question. If I mention one while quietly removing the others, I have not explained the problem. I have made it smaller.

I do not want to approach this subject to declare my freedom unlimited. Nor do I want to approach it to escape my actions. I am seeking an understanding in which I can bear my responsibility without diminishing GOD’s power. 57:22 provides a strong beginning, but I should not make it speak in place of every other relevant passage.

**Verse references:** 57:22.

## 2. The Call After the Record: Relinquish Pride, Not Justice

### Verse

<blockquote data-verse="57:23" data-language="en">
<p lang="en"><strong>57:23</strong> — Thus,  you  should  not  grieve  over anything  you  miss,  nor  be  proud  of anything  He  has  bestowed  upon you. GOD does not love those who are boastful, proud.</p>
</blockquote>

<blockquote data-verse="57:25" data-language="en">
<p lang="en"><strong>57:25</strong> — We  sent  our  messengers  supported by  clear  proofs,  and  we  sent  down  to them  the  scripture  and  the  law,  that the  people  may  uphold  justice.  And we  sent  down  the  iron,  wherein there  is  strength,  and  many  benefits for  the  people.  All  this  in  order for  GOD  to  distinguish  those  who would  support  Him  and  His  messengers,  on  faith.  GOD  is  Powerful, Almighty.</p>
</blockquote>

<blockquote data-verse="12:86" data-language="en">
<p lang="en"><strong>12:86</strong> — He said, “I simply complain to GOD about my dilemma and grief, for I know from GOD what you do not know.</p>
</blockquote>

### Reflection on the Verse

The placement of 57:23 immediately after 57:22 makes me pause. The concern is not only where the future is written, but who I become when I gain or lose. I can turn an achievement into proof of my personal superiority, or a loss into evidence that nothing meaningful remains. The verse challenges these extremes.

Yet I do not want to turn this call into harshness toward someone who is suffering. Jacob’s grief in 12:86 belongs to the same QURAN. His turning to GOD is not a conversation in which he hides his sorrow. Reflecting on destiny therefore does not give me an easy authority to judge another person’s mourning. Seeking trust amid loss is one thing; telling the person who has suffered to be silent is another.

Moreover, 57:25 explicitly brings upholding justice into view. Predetermined recording and human responsibility appear in the same passage. I do not see a portrait of an inactive, indecisive, indifferent person here. I am led to consider someone who does not boast of personal power, yet does not abandon what can be done for justice.

This is my first self-examination: Do I use the word destiny to endure my own loss, or to remain indifferent to someone else’s? The first may express a search for trust. I cannot comfortably place the second beside these verses.

**Verse references:** 57:23; 57:25; 12:86; 57:22.

## 3. Are Measure, Record, and Compulsion the Same Thing?

### Verse

<blockquote data-verse="54:49" data-language="en">
<p lang="en"><strong>54:49</strong> — Everything  we  created  is  precisely measured.</p>
</blockquote>

<blockquote data-verse="54:52" data-language="en">
<p lang="en"><strong>54:52</strong> — Everything  they  did  is  recorded  in the scriptures.</p>
</blockquote>

<blockquote data-verse="54:53" data-language="en">
<p lang="en"><strong>54:53</strong> — Everything,  small  or  large,  is  written down.</p>
</blockquote>

### Reflection on the Verse

54:49 is brief; the explanations I could build around it are long. I must not mistake the length of my explanation for detail supplied by the verse. A statement about the measured character of creation does not, in that sentence, describe every stage by which human willing takes place.

54:52–53 also speak of the record of what people have done. This is not a world in which the actions I consider small simply cease to matter. A decision remaining out of public sight does not mean it remains outside accountability. The idea of a record can make me more attentive to my life.

But I need to separate two questions. What does it tell us that something is measured or written? What else is needed to explain how the person’s choice comes about? I cannot present every answer I give to the second question as though it were already contained in the first statement.

This distinction does not reduce GOD’s governance to watching from a distance. Verses about His will remain before us. My concern is not to turn one word into a key that silences every other word. I want to consider measure, knowledge, will, and accountability together, rather than name a connection I have not yet explained and declare the inquiry finished.

**Verse references:** 54:49; 54:52; 54:53; 81:29; 57:22.

## 4. Being Able to Choose Is Not Being Master of the Outcome

### Verse

<blockquote data-verse="76:2" data-language="en">
<p lang="en"><strong>76:2</strong> — We created the human from a liquid mixture,  from  two  parents,  in  order to  test  him.  Thus,  we  made  him  a hearer and a seer.</p>
</blockquote>

<blockquote data-verse="76:3" data-language="en">
<p lang="en"><strong>76:3</strong> — We  showed  him  the  two  paths,  then, he  is  either  appreciative,  or  unappreciative.</p>
</blockquote>

<blockquote data-verse="18:29" data-language="en">
<p lang="en"><strong>18:29</strong> — Proclaim:  “This  is  the  truth  from your  Lord,”  then  whoever  wills  let him  believe,  and  whoever  wills  let him  disbelieve.  We  have  prepared for  the  transgressors  a  fire  that  will completely  surround  them.  When they  scream  for  help,  they  will  be given  a  liquid  like  concentrated  acid that  scalds  the  faces.  What  a  miserable  drink!  What  a  miserable  destiny!</p>
</blockquote>

### Reflection on the Verse

76:2–3 invite me to consider the human being as one who hears, sees, and is shown paths. This is not an account in which my own response has been entirely removed. How I stand before what is shown to me has weight.

Nor should I detach the language of believing and disbelieving in 18:29 from the rest of the verse. Speaking of willing does not declare every choice equally valuable or without consequences. Choice and accountability stand together.

If I imagine freedom as the power to make whatever I choose right, I miss that relationship. Being able to make a wrong decision does not make wrongness disappear. Being able to violate someone’s rights does not mean those rights have become mine. This is the question that the distinction between choice and consequence directs toward everyday life, not a claim to unlimited power.

There is a considerable distance between being responsible for my choices and being the master of every condition. Not having chosen where I was born does not make every word I speak unrelated to me. Wanting something does not ensure that it happens. I therefore want neither to call unlimited control freedom nor to treat limited control as the absence of all responsibility.

**Verse references:** 76:2; 76:3; 18:29.

## 5. Does Human Will Stand Outside GOD’s Will?

### Verse

<blockquote data-verse="81:28" data-language="en">
<p lang="en"><strong>81:28</strong> — For those who wish to go straight.</p>
</blockquote>

<blockquote data-verse="81:29" data-language="en">
<p lang="en"><strong>81:29</strong> — Whatever you will is in accordance with the will of GOD, Lord of the universe.</p>
</blockquote>

<blockquote data-verse="76:29" data-language="en">
<p lang="en"><strong>76:29</strong> — This  is  a  reminder:  whoever  wills shall choose the path to his Lord.</p>
</blockquote>

<blockquote data-verse="76:30" data-language="en">
<p lang="en"><strong>76:30</strong> — Whatever you will is in accordance with GOD’s will. GOD is Omniscient, Wise. </p>
</blockquote>

### Reflection on the Verse

Once these verses are separated, an excessively easy answer can be produced in either direction. Reading only of human willing, I might claim a realm independent of GOD. Speaking only of GOD’s will while silencing human response, I might empty the same passage of its call to the person.

The two emphases follow one another in 81:28–29. The same proximity appears in 76:29–30. Their relationship calls for explanation, but explaining it need not begin by ignoring one side. Human willing is mentioned, and it is not detached from GOD’s will.

The difficult question for me is this: Does a will’s not being absolute mean that it does not exist at all? Before these verses, I cannot present my willing as an independent sovereignty. Yet declaring the call to human choice merely apparent would also require further grounds.

In my reflection, monotheism does not make the human being a second absolute will. Nor is responsibility preserved by removing the person altogether from what the person does. I am trying to hold both boundaries. Without claiming to know every detail of their relationship, I do not want to make them enemies. Submission here is not the abandonment of thought. It is refusing to let my explanation take GOD’s place.

**Verse references:** 81:28; 81:29; 76:29; 76:30.

## 6. “Had GOD Willed, I Would Not Have Done It”: Destiny as an Excuse

### Verse

<blockquote data-verse="6:148" data-language="en">
<p lang="en"><strong>6:148</strong> — The idol worshipers say, “Had GOD willed, we would not practice idolatry, nor would our parents, nor would we prohibit anything.” Thus did those before them disbelieve, until they incurred our retribution. Say,  “Do  you  have  any  proven knowledge that you can show us? You follow nothing but conjecture; you only guess.” </p>
</blockquote>

<blockquote data-verse="6:149" data-language="en">
<p lang="en"><strong>6:149</strong> — Say,  “GOD  possesses  the  most powerful argument; if He wills He can guide all of you.”</p>
</blockquote>

<blockquote data-verse="39:7" data-language="en">
<p lang="en"><strong>39:7</strong> — If  you  disbelieve,  GOD  does  not need  anyone.  But  He  dislikes  to  see His  servants  make  the  wrong  decision.  If  you  decide  to  be  appreciative, He is pleased for you. No soul bears  the  sins  of  any  other  soul.  Ultimately,  to  your  Lord  is  your  return, then  He  will  inform  you  of  everything  you  had  done.  He  is  fully aware of the innermost thoughts.</p>
</blockquote>

### Reflection on the Verse

The defense presented in 6:148 brings the discussion of destiny directly into moral life. Instead of examining an action, a person can turn the fact that it happened into an excuse. An action’s having occurred and its having been approved are not the same thing.

The following verse, 6:149, does not remove GOD’s power to guide. I therefore cannot read the passage as though holding people responsible required limiting GOD’s power. The excuse is rejected while that power is affirmed. My task is not to insert my preferred convenience between the two statements.

39:7 separately places decision and approval before us. Speaking about GOD’s will is not identical to saying which conduct pleases Him. Declaring betrayal, falsehood, or injustice approved merely because it occurred loses that distinction.

In <a href="https://berkkoksal.com/writings/the_most_argumentative_creature_en/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">my reflection on seeking truth and needing to be right</a>, I asked what argument serves. The question is more personal here: Am I trying to understand destiny, or looking for a larger word that will save me from apologizing?

**Having done something wrong does not show that I had the right to do it. Speaking about destiny does not automatically sever my relationship to that wrong.**

**Verse references:** 6:148; 6:149; 39:7.

## 7. Guidance and Straying: Do Not Leave One Sentence Alone

### Verse

<blockquote data-verse="10:99" data-language="en">
<p lang="en"><strong>10:99</strong> — Had  your  Lord  willed,  all  the  people  on  earth  would  have  believed.* Do you  want to  force  the people  to become believers?</p>
</blockquote>

<blockquote data-verse="10:100" data-language="en">
<p lang="en"><strong>10:100</strong> — No  soul  can  believe  except  in  accordance  with  GOD’s  will.  For  He places  a  curse  upon  those  who refuse to understand.</p>
</blockquote>

<blockquote data-verse="16:93" data-language="en">
<p lang="en"><strong>16:93</strong> — Had  GOD  willed,  He  could  have made you one congregation. But He sends astray whoever chooses to go astray, and He guides whoever wishes to be guided.* You will surely be asked  about  everything  you  have done.</p>
</blockquote>

<blockquote data-verse="61:5" data-language="en">
<p lang="en"><strong>61:5</strong> — Recall  that  Moses  said  to  his  people,  “O  my  people,  why  do  you  hurt me,  even  though  you  know  that  I  am GOD’s  messenger  to  you?”  When they  deviated,  GOD  diverted  their hearts.  For  GOD  does  not  guide  the wicked people.</p>
</blockquote>

### Reflection on the Verse

Reading 10:99–100 together prevents a comfortable one-sidedness. The desire to force people to believe is questioned; the relationship between belief and GOD’s will follows. Refusal to understand is also mentioned. Human response, will, and guidance are not disconnected here.

16:93 explicitly speaks of choosing to go astray and wishing to be guided. Its final sentence speaks of being questioned about what one has done. I should read these expressions without replacing them with a more familiar formulation. Neither the person’s choice nor GOD’s guidance is something to discard.

61:5 describes a sequence between a particular people’s deviation and the diversion of their hearts. That sequence challenges the idea that human response is insignificant. Nevertheless, I cannot derive from one event a complete scheme explaining everyone’s inner life. I cannot claim to know the hidden cause of every rejection or pronounce someone’s possibility of return exhausted.

My principal question is not what is written in other people’s hearts. Am I ignoring something I could understand because I do not want it? Am I shutting myself against a warning to protect a mistake? Reflection on guidance should make me a researcher responsible for my own direction, not a judge distributing other people’s final destinies.

**Verse references:** 10:99; 10:100; 16:93; 61:5.

## 8. Does Being Created Remove Me from What I Do?

### Verse

<blockquote data-verse="37:95" data-language="en">
<p lang="en"><strong>37:95</strong> — He  said,  “How  can  you  worship what you carve?</p>
</blockquote>

<blockquote data-verse="37:96" data-language="en">
<p lang="en"><strong>37:96</strong> — “When  GOD  has  created  you,  and everything you make!”</p>
</blockquote>

<blockquote data-verse="14:22" data-language="en">
<p lang="en"><strong>14:22</strong> — And  the  devil  will  say, after  the judgment had been issued, “GOD has promised you the truthful promise, and I promised you, but I broke my promise. I had no power over you; I simply invited you, and you accepted  my  invitation.  Therefore, do not blame me, and blame only yourselves. My complaining cannot help you, nor can your complaining help me. I have disbelieved in your idolizing  me.  The  transgressors have  incurred  a  painful  retribution.”</p>
</blockquote>

### Reflection on the Verse

The question of worshiping carved objects stands immediately before 37:96. If I leave that context behind and turn the sentence solely into material for an abstract argument, I may miss its challenge to a created being who exalts a created object.

Being able to carve something does not make it worthy of worship. My ability to make something does not make me the source of my own existence. Bringing these verses close to my creative work, I consider the boundary between acknowledging my effort and assigning it absolute power. Having made decisions about a work does not mean that everything began with me.

14:22, meanwhile, distinguishes an invitation from the response to it. The person is not portrayed as unrelated to that invitation. Entirely assigning one’s acceptance to another being does not provide an escape from accountability.

This account should not be expanded into a claim that everyone under every form of pressure is equally free; another verse specifically addresses coercion. Yet a distinction is clear here: Being influenced is not the same as treating the entire action as someone else’s. While examining who invited me, I also need to examine my acceptance. The tool I use, the person I follow, or the voice that influences me does not bear my whole decision in my place.

**Verse references:** 37:95; 37:96; 14:22; 16:106.

## 9. Is the Record an Excuse, or the Basis of Accountability?

### Verse

<blockquote data-verse="17:13" data-language="en">
<p lang="en"><strong>17:13</strong> — We have recorded the fate of every human being; it is tied to his neck. On the Day of Resurrection we will hand him a record that is accessible.</p>
</blockquote>

<blockquote data-verse="17:14" data-language="en">
<p lang="en"><strong>17:14</strong> — Read your own record. Today, you suffice as your own reckoner.</p>
</blockquote>

<blockquote data-verse="17:15" data-language="en">
<p lang="en"><strong>17:15</strong> — Whoever is guided, is guided for his own good, and whoever goes astray does so to his own detriment. No sinner will bear the sins of anyone else. We never punish without first sending a messenger.</p>
</blockquote>

<blockquote data-verse="53:38" data-language="en">
<p lang="en"><strong>53:38</strong> — No soul bears the sins of another soul.</p>
</blockquote>

<blockquote data-verse="53:39" data-language="en">
<p lang="en"><strong>53:39</strong> — Every human being is responsible for his own works.</p>
</blockquote>

<blockquote data-verse="53:40" data-language="en">
<p lang="en"><strong>53:40</strong> — And  everyone’s  works  will  be shown.</p>
</blockquote>

<blockquote data-verse="53:41" data-language="en">
<p lang="en"><strong>53:41</strong> — Then they will be paid fully for such works.</p>
</blockquote>

### Reflection on the Verse

The record in 17:13 opens, in the next verse, onto a person’s confrontation with the self. It is not a document distancing me from my actions, but a record placing what belongs to me before me. Thinking of it only as something canceling my choices neglects the passage’s concern with accountability.

53:38–41 likewise bring together responsibility, the showing of works, and recompense. Not taking on a sin that does not belong to me and not transferring my own conduct to someone else appear as two sides of the same honesty. Making myself guilty of everything can distort that honesty, just as making myself responsible for nothing can.

I consider the language I use after a mistake. I can narrate an event entirely in passive constructions: It happened this way; circumstances brought it about; no other path remained. There may have been genuine coercion. Yet sometimes this language quietly removes the small but consequential decisions I made.

Reflecting on accountability does not mean claiming that I created every condition of my life. It means honestly identifying what belongs to me. What did I say? What did I knowingly conceal? What did I do when correction was possible? The moral effect of the question of destiny should not be to remove these questions, but to make me take them more seriously.

**In these verses, a recorded life is not described as an unaccountable life.**

**Verse references:** 17:13; 17:14; 17:15; 53:38; 53:39; 53:40; 53:41.

## 10. Freedom Does Not Mean Everyone Has the Same Means

### Verse

<blockquote data-verse="2:286" data-language="en">
<p lang="en"><strong>2:286</strong> — GOD never burdens a soul beyond its means: to its credit is what it earns, and against it is what it commits. “Our Lord, do not condemn us if we forget or make mistakes. Our Lord, and protect us from blaspheming against You, like those before us have done. Our Lord, protect us from sinning until it becomes too late for us to repent. Pardon us and forgive us. You are our Lord and Master. Grant us victory over the disbelieving people.”</p>
</blockquote>

<blockquote data-verse="16:106" data-language="en">
<p lang="en"><strong>16:106</strong> — Those  who  disbelieve  in  GOD,  after having  acquired  faith,  and  become fully  content  with  disbelief,  have  incurred  wrath  from  GOD.  The  only ones  to  be  excused  are  those  who are  forced  to  profess  disbelief,  while their hearts are full of faith.*</p>
</blockquote>

### Reflection on the Verse

It is easy, while declaring people responsible, to imagine everyone with the same strength, knowledge, and circumstances. 2:286 calls for greater care. It places a boundary between what a soul is burdened with and its means. The same verse includes prayer concerning forgetting, mistakes, forgiveness, and help.

16:106 makes an explicit distinction in a particular situation: Disbelief embraced inwardly and disbelief professed under compulsion are not evaluated in the same way. I cannot expand this specific context into an automatic ruling for every situation. But I can see that coercion behind words that look identical from the outside cannot simply be ignored.

This does not mean that I can read another person’s entire inner life. On the contrary, it requires awareness of what I do not know when making judgments. If I assume everyone possesses the comfortable conditions available to me, I may commit injustice in the name of responsibility.

The same honesty is necessary toward myself. Acknowledging a real limit is not abandoning every effort. I can recognize what is not in my hands without overlooking a small possibility that is. After a mistake, I can seek forgiveness and correction rather than close the matter with the word destiny. Between absolute power and total ineffectiveness, these verses place the concrete life that human beings actually live.

**Verse references:** 2:286; 16:106.

## 11. Why Does Deciding to Change Matter?

### Verse

<blockquote data-verse="13:11" data-language="en">
<p lang="en"><strong>13:11</strong> — Shifts  (of  angels)  take  turns,  staying with each one of you—they are in front  of  you  and  behind  you.  They stay with you, and guard you in accordance  with  GOD’s  commands. Thus,  GOD  does  not  change  the condition of  any  people  unless  they themselves  make  the  decision  to change.  If  GOD  wills  any  hardship for any people, no force can stop it. For  they  have  none  beside  Him  as Lord and Master.</p>
</blockquote>

<blockquote data-verse="8:53" data-language="en">
<p lang="en"><strong>8:53</strong> — GOD does not change a blessing He has  bestowed  upon  any people unless  they  themselves  decide  to change.  GOD  is  Hearer,  Omniscient.</p>
</blockquote>

### Reflection on the Verse

The reference in 13:11 to a people’s decision to change prevents me from speaking about destiny only in the language of waiting. A community’s response matters in the text. Alongside it stands the statement that no power can stop a hardship GOD wills. Human change and absolute control are not confused.

I must not take the middle of the verse and forget its ending. To say that changing yourself guarantees every result you want would add an assurance the text does not supply. A change being necessary does not show that it is, by itself, sufficient for every desired outcome.

The connection in 8:53 between a blessing and a people’s change also deserves attention. It suggests that my response is not insignificant. It does not, however, give me authority to attribute every loss suffered by another community or individual to a moral failing from the outside. Taking the stated connection seriously is different from claiming to know every cause of a particular calamity.

Its relevance to my life begins closer to home. Might I sustain the conditions feeding a bad habit and then explain my failure to change through destiny? Might I postpone correcting a false statement when correction is possible, then wonder why its consequences continue? I can be honest before these questions without first explaining every detail of destiny. Deciding to change does not mean taking GOD’s place; it means responding from within the responsibility addressed to me.

**Verse references:** 13:11; 8:53.

## 12. Does Prayer Change Destiny?

### Verse

<blockquote data-verse="2:186" data-language="en">
<p lang="en"><strong>2:186</strong> — When  My  servants  ask  you  about Me, I am always near. I answer their prayers  when they pray  to Me. The people shall respond to Me and believe in Me, in order to be guided.</p>
</blockquote>

<blockquote data-verse="13:39" data-language="en">
<p lang="en"><strong>13:39</strong> — GOD  erases  whatever  He  wills,  and fixes.  With  Him  is  the  original  Master Record.</p>
</blockquote>

### Reflection on the Verse

If something is written, why do I pray? Before this question becomes a reason to stop praying, it should open an inquiry into what I expect of prayer.

In 2:186, prayer is not presented as speaking into a void. Nearness, response, and the person’s own response to GOD appear together. If I measure prayer solely by whether a desired event occurs on my chosen date, I may miss much of that relationship. The verse does not offer a contract under which I determine the form and timetable of every wish.

13:39 speaks of erasing, fixing, and the original Master Record. Every detail of the relationship between this record and the record in 57:22 is not explained. I cannot draw layers of my own and confidently describe which changes and when. Nor should I turn the verse into a formula saying that every prayer necessarily changes a particular record.

Equally, moving from the existence of a record to the meaninglessness of prayer would leave 2:186 out. As someone praying, I am not only requesting an external outcome. I am also expressing my need, my limit, and the One toward whom I turn. If I abandon what I ought to do after praying, I may have separated my request from my responsibility.

What I can trust here is not that I have solved how every record operates. It is that the verse takes prayer and response seriously. A detail I do not know does not invalidate the call explicitly addressed to me.

**Verse references:** 2:186; 13:39; 57:22.

## 13. Is Planning Contrary to Submission?

### Verse

<blockquote data-verse="3:159" data-language="en">
<p lang="en"><strong>3:159</strong> — It was mercy from GOD that you became  compassionate  towards them.  Had  you  been  harsh  and mean-hearted, they would have abandoned you. Therefore, you shall pardon them and ask forgiveness for them, and consult them. Once you make a decision, carry out your plan, and trust in GOD. GOD loves those who trust in Him.*</p>
</blockquote>

<blockquote data-verse="12:67" data-language="en">
<p lang="en"><strong>12:67</strong> — And  he  said,  “O  my  sons,  do  not enter  from  one  door;  enter  through separate  doors.  However,  I  cannot save  you  from  anything  that  is  predetermined  by  GOD.  To  GOD  belongs  all  judgments.  I  trust  in  Him, and  in  Him  shall  all  the  trusters  put their trust.”</p>
</blockquote>

### Reflection on the Verse

3:159 brings the discussion of destiny to my own desk. Consultation, decision, carrying out a plan, and trust can meet within the same life. I do not have to abandon one in order to choose another.

12:67 makes this conjunction more concrete. Jacob recommends a precaution; in the same speech, he says he cannot save his sons from anything predetermined by GOD. Taking a precaution does not make him consider himself an absolute protector. Not being an absolute protector does not make him declare precaution meaningless.

In <a href="https://berkkoksal.com/writings/do-you-really-trust-god-the-word-of-trust-the-obsession-with-control-and-the-hard-test-of-submission/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">my reflection on trusting GOD and trying to control the outcome</a>, I stood between these extremes. The question of destiny brings the distinction back: Am I fulfilling my task, or demanding that every outcome obey me?

When I work on a piece of music, preparation, experimentation, revision, and decision are part of my effort. I cannot control its entire reception. The possibility of failure does not automatically make avoiding work the more faithful option; success does not make me flawless. This is what I learn when I bring 3:159 close to my creative work.

To plan without idolizing my plan. To work without treating the result as something owed to me. Not to conclude that all meaning has ended because one door closes. For me, these describe the side of submission that knows its limits within action rather than escaping from action.

**Verse references:** 3:159; 12:67.

## 14. Do Not Make Destiny an Excuse for Oppression

### Verse

<blockquote data-verse="4:78" data-language="en">
<p lang="en"><strong>4:78</strong> — Wherever  you  are,  death  will  catch up with you, even if you live in formidable  castles.  When  something good  happens  to  them,  they  say, “This  is  from  GOD,”  and  when something  bad  afflicts  them,  they blame  you.  Say,  “Everything  comes from  GOD.”  Why  do  these  people misunderstand almost everything?</p>
</blockquote>

<blockquote data-verse="4:79" data-language="en">
<p lang="en"><strong>4:79</strong> — Anything  good  that  happens  to  you is  from  GOD,  and  anything  bad  that happens to you is from  you. We have sent  you  as  a  messenger  to  the people,*  and  GOD  suffices  as  witness.</p>
</blockquote>

<blockquote data-verse="2:155" data-language="en">
<p lang="en"><strong>2:155</strong> — We  will  surely  test  you  through some  fear,  hunger,  and  loss  of  money, lives, and crops. Give good news to the steadfast.*</p>
</blockquote>

<blockquote data-verse="42:41" data-language="en">
<p lang="en"><strong>42:41</strong> — Certainly, those who stand up for their rights, when injustice befalls them, are not committing any error.</p>
</blockquote>

<blockquote data-verse="42:42" data-language="en">
<p lang="en"><strong>42:42</strong> — The wrong ones are those who treat the people unjustly, and resort to aggression without provocation. These have incurred a painful retribution.</p>
</blockquote>

<blockquote data-verse="4:40" data-language="en">
<p lang="en"><strong>4:40</strong> — GOD does not inflict an atom’s weight of injustice. On the contrary, He multiplies the reward manifold for the righteous work, and grants from Him a great recompense.</p>
</blockquote>

### Reflection on the Verse

4:78–79 are not verses I can pass over easily. One speaks of everything coming from GOD; the other relates the bad that befalls a person to that person. I cannot remove either statement to preserve the other. But encountering both does not mean I have learned the hidden cause of every pain suffered by someone before me.

2:155 places fear, hunger, and loss in the context of a test. 42:41–42 distinguish the person subjected to injustice from the person committing it. Before these verses, blaming every victim with a single sentence may be a new injustice rather than an explanation. If I do not know why a particular suffering occurred, I should not use that ignorance against the one experiencing it.

Similarly, placing an oppressor’s act within destiny does not make the conduct right. While 42:42 condemns the person who acts unjustly, 42:41 does not treat defending one’s rights as an error. I cannot let the idea of prior recording become an excuse that erases this distinction.

I do not possess a map explaining every pain here. But I have the measure of 4:40, which does not attribute injustice to GOD, and the language of 42:41–42, which distinguishes human conduct. Not knowing the whole wisdom does not require me to justify an injustice I can recognize.

It is easy to see a wound and deliver a lesson on destiny in place of the person who bears it. It is harder to consider the help, honesty, or correction that falls to me. Reading these verses, I want to take on that second difficulty first.

**Verse references:** 4:78; 4:79; 2:155; 42:41; 42:42; 4:40.

## 15. If Tomorrow Is Unknown, Why Turn Back Today?

### Verse

<blockquote data-verse="39:53" data-language="en">
<p lang="en"><strong>39:53</strong> — Proclaim: “O My servants who exceeded the limits, never despair of GOD’s mercy. For GOD forgives all sins. He is the Forgiver, Most Merciful.”</p>
</blockquote>

<blockquote data-verse="39:54" data-language="en">
<p lang="en"><strong>39:54</strong> — You shall obey your Lord, and submit to Him totally, before the retribution  overtakes  you,  then  you cannot be helped.</p>
</blockquote>

<blockquote data-verse="31:34" data-language="en">
<p lang="en"><strong>31:34</strong> — With GOD is the knowledge regarding  the  Hour  (end  of  the  world).*  He is the One who sends down the rain, and  He  knows  the  contents  of  the womb. No soul knows what will happen  to  it  tomorrow,  and  no  one knows in which land he or she will die. GOD is Omniscient, Cognizant.</p>
</blockquote>

### Reflection on the Verse

One of the heaviest forms of the question of destiny is the dark verdict a person issues against the self: My end is already bad; nothing I do can matter. But I need to ask what knowledge I possess when I say this.

31:34 reminds me of my limit concerning what will happen to me tomorrow. GOD’s knowing and my knowing are not identical. Accepting that a record exists does not mean I have opened it and read my own end. I cannot turn my fear into certain knowledge of the future.

In 39:53–54, the call not to despair of mercy accompanies the call to obey and submit. Hope is not a numbness that lets me wait without changing anything. It is an opening that keeps me from declaring return meaningless when I recognize my wrong.

Looking at my past, I therefore want to relinquish two conveniences: Making what I did insignificant, and making what I did the only possible definition of my entire future. Admitting a wrong is different from enclosing myself within it forever.

If there is a truthful word I can speak today, a mistake I can correct, or an injustice I can abandon, I should not use an unknown end to postpone it. It is true that I do not know the hidden details of destiny. It is also true that a call to return is addressed to me. The first truth does not have to silence the second.

**Verse references:** 39:53; 39:54; 31:34.

## 16. If GOD Knows, Why Am I Tested?

### Verse

<blockquote data-verse="67:2" data-language="en">
<p lang="en"><strong>67:2</strong> — The One who created death and life for the purpose of distinguishing those among you who would do better.* He is the Almighty, the Forgiving.</p>
</blockquote>

<blockquote data-verse="17:36" data-language="en">
<p lang="en"><strong>17:36</strong> — You  shall  not  accept  any  information,  unless  you  verify  it  for  yourself. I  have  given  you  the  hearing,  the eyesight,  and  the  brain,  and  you  are responsible for using them.</p>
</blockquote>

### Reflection on the Verse

This question carries the deepest tension of the subject. If the outcome is known, why is there a test? Considering 57:22 alongside 67:2, I cannot claim that the test fills a gap in GOD’s knowledge. But neither can I conclude that lived life is therefore an insignificant spectacle. 67:2 speaks of distinguishing through doing better.

An action being known and an action being lived and performed are not the same statement. 53:39–41 speak of people’s own works and their recompense. What opens to reflection is not a person who can move outside knowledge, but a person whose life and action are taken seriously. The verses do not provide a mechanism explaining every metaphysical detail of this conjunction. Nevertheless, the emphasis on knowledge does not remove the emphasis on works and recompense.

17:36 includes inquiry itself within my responsibility. I cannot be satisfied with finding the answer that makes me comfortable. If I select a verse about destiny while ignoring a verse about choice, or do the reverse, I may find what I seek without attending to the whole.

The question I asked in <a href="https://berkkoksal.com/writings/finding-is-one-thing-understanding-is-another-verses-in-the-age-of-search-engines/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">my reflection on the difference between finding and understanding</a> applies here as well. Locating an expression in KuranTeyit does not guarantee that I have placed it rightly in my life.

Researching destiny itself confronts me with a choice: Will I honestly carry a point that remains unclear, or add a statement the text does not make in order to appear certain?

**Verse references:** 67:2; 17:36; 57:22; 53:39; 53:40; 53:41.

## 17. Arabic Word Roots: Do Not Compress Destiny into One Word

### Verse

<blockquote data-verse="54:49" data-language="en">
<p lang="en"><strong>54:49</strong> — Everything  we  created  is  precisely measured.</p>
</blockquote>

### Reflection on the Verse

**Measure — ق د ر.** The word بِقَدَرٍ in 54:49 belongs to this root. Rashad Khalifa’s Authorized English Translation uses “precisely measured”; the Turkish translation of the Authorized English Translation has “tam ölçüsündedir.” This usage supports reflection on measure. Yet the root’s presence alone does not establish that a human being makes no choices. The meaning indicated by a word and a complete account of willing do not have the same scope. <a href="https://corpus.quran.com/wordmorphology.jsp?location=(54:49:5)" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">54:49, the word for measure</a>.

**Record and writing — ك ت ب.** The word كِتَٰبٍ in 57:22 comes from this root. The verse also places the record before creation; that temporal relationship is carried by the sentence as a whole, not by the root alone. Finding the root does not mean I have learned how the record is kept, what form it takes, or every detail of its relationship with human will. Accepting that something is written is different from accepting every image I imagine about it. <a href="https://corpus.quran.com/wordmorphology.jsp?location=(57:22:12)" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">57:22, the word for record</a>.

**Willing — ش ي أ.** The words تَشَآءُونَ and يَشَآءَ in 81:29 share this root. One has a plural human subject; the other concerns GOD’s will. A common root does not make the subjects equal in power. Nor does the presence of both verbs allow me to erase one without explaining their relationship. The connection made by the sentence is not exhausted by a dictionary meaning. <a href="https://corpus.quran.com/wordmorphology.jsp?location=(81:29:2)" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">81:29, human willing</a>; <a href="https://corpus.quran.com/wordmorphology.jsp?location=(81:29:5)" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">81:29, GOD’s willing</a>.

**Knowledge and wisdom — ع ل م; ح ك م.** The words عَلِيمًا and حَكِيمًا at the end of 76:30 belong to these roots. Knowledge and wisdom are mentioned in the sentence about will. The direction I take from this is not to portray GOD’s will as ignorance or purposeless arbitrariness, while also refusing to identify my limited explanation with His knowledge. This is a reflection, not an elaborate theory of destiny contained within the roots. <a href="https://corpus.quran.com/wordmorphology.jsp?location=(76:30:10)" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">76:30, the word concerning knowledge</a>; <a href="https://corpus.quran.com/wordmorphology.jsp?location=(76:30:11)" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">76:30, the word concerning wisdom</a>.

**Earning — ك س ب.** The words كَسَبَتْ and ٱكْتَسَبَتْ in 2:286 are different verb forms of the same root. The verse speaks of what counts to a person’s credit and against that person. Recognizing the forms matters, but their difference alone cannot yield the entire mechanism of responsibility. Preserving the relationship shown in the sentence is already a serious task: The soul’s means and what it earns are mentioned together. <a href="https://corpus.quran.com/wordmorphology.jsp?location=(2:286:9)" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">2:286, the verb for earning</a>; <a href="https://corpus.quran.com/wordmorphology.jsp?location=(2:286:12)" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">2:286, the other form of earning</a>.

These roots are not five ornaments attached to one ready-made answer. They help preserve distinctions among measure, record, willing, knowledge, and earning. Rather than force them into one meaning, I need to attend to what each does within its verse.

**Verse references:** 54:49; 57:22; 81:29; 76:30; 2:286.

## 18. Other Claims

### Verse

<blockquote data-verse="57:22" data-language="en">
<p lang="en"><strong>57:22</strong> — Anything  that  happens  on  earth,  or to  you,  has  already  been  recorded, even  before  the  creation.  This  is easy for GOD to do.</p>
</blockquote>

### Reflection on the Verse

The explanatory note accompanying 57:22 in Rashad Khalifa’s Authorized English Translation says that people are completely free to side with GOD or Satan, while GOD knows each person’s decision precisely. The note also says that the videotape of a person’s life, from birth to death, is already recorded. These are explicit statements in the note; “videotape” does not occur in the verse itself. Distinguishing the note from the verse does not hide its approach. It preserves the fact that they are not the same kind of text. <a href="https://kuranteyit.com/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">57:22 and its explanatory note</a>.

Rashad Khalifa’s **Appendix 14: Predestination** likewise considers freedom to believe or disbelieve alongside GOD’s knowledge of all decisions. It relates earthly life to an earlier choice to undergo a test, and explains guidance in terms of GOD’s knowledge of souls. This is the author’s supplementary explanatory framework, not an account whose every detail appears in the single sentence of 57:22. <a href="https://submission.org/App14.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Rashad Khalifa, Appendix 14: Predestination</a>.

Historical theological discussions do not offer only one account. Approaches associated with the Jabriyya greatly restrict or deny human influence in action, whereas the Mu‘tazila emphasize human agency in morally responsible acts. Ash‘ari and Maturidi accounts approach the relationship among creation, willing, and acquisition differently. These are internally varied and detailed approaches; a few labels cannot settle the whole discussion. <a href="https://islamansiklopedisi.org.tr/kader" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Yusuf Şevki Yavuz, Kader</a>.

The distinction “knowing is not compelling” is also worth considering. Yet stating it does not answer every question about infallible knowledge and willing in a single move. Watching a familiar recording and explaining GOD’s knowledge before creation are not on the same level. The familiarity of an analogy does not guarantee the completeness of an explanation.

Detailed models in which prayer changes some records but not others can also be discussed. But 13:39 alone does not name every kind of record or specify which change every prayer will produce. Such a model must be considered with its additional grounds.

These different explanations can be examined. I do not want to treat any as certain knowledge that quietly supplies details absent from the principal verses. When the boundaries among a verse, an explanatory note, a historical theory, and my own reflection are lost, the effort to understand destiny can turn someone else’s explanation into an unquestionable authority.

**Verse references:** 57:22; 13:39; 81:29; 53:39.

## 19. Conclusion: If Everything Is Known, What Will I Do?

### Verse

<blockquote data-verse="53:39" data-language="en">
<p lang="en"><strong>53:39</strong> — Every human being is responsible for his own works.</p>
</blockquote>

### Reflection on the Verse

I can no longer answer the title’s question by silencing either group of verses. 57:22 states that events were recorded before creation. 81:28–29 and 76:29–30 do not detach human willing from GOD’s will. 53:39 explicitly states a person’s responsibility for personal works. The framework placed before us holds the existence of a record together with human accountability.

This does not mean that people possess a will independent of GOD and unlimited in power. I do not determine every condition and outcome of life. But that limit is not sufficient grounds for treating my choices as nonexistent. For me, speaking of freedom first means acknowledging the seriousness of my choices and conduct before the call addressed to me.

I am not claiming to have derived from these verses a complete mechanism explaining how the whole relationship operates. Yet I need not cover an explicitly stated responsibility with a point I have not explained. Inquiry and action do not have to wait for each other. While trying to understand more, I can abandon an injustice I already recognize today.

I can pray, because prayer and response are taken seriously. I can consult and decide, because trust does not replace carrying out a plan. I can correct a wrong, because I am called not to despair of mercy and to turn toward GOD. I can resist injustice, because an event’s being recorded does not make its perpetrator right.

I want to examine what the word destiny changes most in my life. Does it make me more honest, or help me find better excuses? Does it reduce my arrogance about outcomes while increasing my responsibility in conduct? Does it make me more attentive to another person’s suffering, or make silencing that person easier?

**I trust that GOD knows; I do not turn the future I do not know into a certificate of my own innocence.**

I cannot encompass my whole life. But if I am able to investigate whether a word before me is true or false, or whether a decision infringes another person’s rights, that responsibility belongs to me. The greatness of destiny does not diminish this apparently small responsibility.

The life I seek is not one in which everything is in my hands. It is one in which, knowing that everything is not in my hands, I do not escape accountability for what I can do.

**Verse references:** 53:39; 57:22; 81:28; 81:29; 76:29; 76:30; 2:186; 3:159; 39:53; 39:54; 42:41; 42:42; 17:36.
