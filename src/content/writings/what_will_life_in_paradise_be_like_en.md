# What Will Life in Paradise Be Like? What Will We Do in Eternal Life, and What Will We Need?

**Author:** Berk KÖKSAL  
**Date:** October 01, 2026  
**Place:** Mersin

The main purpose of this article is to contemplate the verses together with other relevant verses and to explore, sincerely and deeply, what bearing witness to the oneness of GOD means for human life in terms of knowledge, justice, truthfulness, and submission.

## 1. “Happily Busy”: Is Paradise Passive Stillness?

### Verse

**36:55**

**Arabic:** إِنَّ أَصْحَٰبَ ٱلْجَنَّةِ ٱلْيَوْمَ فِى شُغُلٍ فَٰكِهُونَ

**English:** The dwellers of Paradise will be, on that day, happily busy.

**Turkish:** Cennet sakinleri, o gün, mutlu bir biçimde meşgul olacaklar.

Source: <a href="https://kuranteyit.com/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">kuranteyit.com</a>

### Reflection on the Verse

36:55 opens the question directly: the dwellers of Paradise will be “happily busy.” The verse does not portray eternal life as frozen scenery, endless sleep, or an empty state in which nothing happens. There is occupation, but it is joined to happiness rather than to anxiety, survival, deadlines, or exhaustion.

That distinction matters. In this world, being busy is often driven by lack: we must earn, repair, protect, hurry, and recover. In Paradise, the same field of activity is described together with joy. It is therefore reasonable to see a life of living activity rather than forced labor, while refusing to invent a list of specific occupations that the verses never provide.

Eternity is not presented as emptiness. It is presented as peaceful, joyful life.

## 2. Companionship, Shade, and Rest

### Verse

> **36:56** — They abide with their  spouses in beautiful  shade,  enjoying  comfortable furnishings.

> **36:57** — They will have fruits therein; they will have anything they wish.

> **36:58** — Greetings  of  peace  from  a  Most Merciful Lord.

### Reflection on the Verse

36:56-58 does not portray Paradise as isolated individual existence. Spouses are together, reclining in beautiful shade, enjoying fruits and whatever they wish, while receiving greetings of peace from a Most Merciful Lord.

The language includes companionship, rest, and provision. Yet the verses do not say these things exist because Paradise contains loneliness, exhaustion, hunger, or deprivation. We should therefore distinguish between an offered delight and a biological necessity.

What is explicit is a life of companionship, comfort, fulfilled desire, and peace from GOD.

## 3. “Whatever the Hearts Desire and the Eyes Wish For”

### Verse

> **43:68** — O My servants, you will have no fear on that day, nor will you grieve.

> **43:69** — They are the ones who believed in our revelations, and were submitters.

> **43:70** — Enter Paradise, together with your spouses, and rejoice.

> **43:71** — Offered to them will be golden trays and cups, and they will find everything the hearts desire and the eyes wish for. You live therein forever.

> **43:72** — Such is the Paradise that you inherit, in return for your works.

> **43:73** — You will have in it all kinds of fruits, from which you eat.

### Reflection on the Verse

43:71 uses remarkably broad language: whatever the hearts desire and the eyes wish for. Desire is not described as erased; it is described as answered.

In worldly life desire frequently produces pain because we cannot obtain what we want, we lose what we obtain, or what we want harms us. In Paradise, desire exists within a setting where fear and grief have been removed.

The verses speak of joy, companionship, serving vessels, and fruit. They present a life that is experienced and enjoyed, not merely an abstract state. They do not, however, explain the biological mechanics of that life. The safest conclusion is the one the verse itself gives: beautiful desire will not be left unanswered.

## 4. “Anything They Wish—and Even More”

### Verse

> **50:34** — Enter it in peace; this is the Day of Eternity.

> **50:35** — They get anything they wish therein, and we have even more.

### Reflection on the Verse

50:35 addresses one of the most natural questions about eternity: would an endless life eventually run out of novelty? The verse says they receive anything they wish, and that GOD has even more.

Paradise is therefore not presented as a finite warehouse of pleasures that can eventually be exhausted. The phrase “even more” keeps the horizon open beyond present imagination.

We should not define what that “more” must be. The verse leaves it open. That openness itself teaches humility: an eternal gift cannot be measured only by the limited categories of present experience.

## 5. A Consciousness Without Fear or Grief

### Verse

> **41:30** — Those who proclaim: “Our Lord is GOD,” then lead a righteous life, the angels  descend  upon  them:  “You shall have no fear, nor shall you grieve. Rejoice in the good news that Paradise has been reserved for you.

> **41:31** — “We  are  your  allies  in  this  life,  and in  the  Hereafter.  You  will  have  in  it anything  you  wish  for;  you  will  have anything you want.

> **41:32** — “(Such  is  your)  ultimate  abode, from a Forgiver, Most Merciful.”

### Reflection on the Verse

41:30-32 does not describe Paradise only by external abundance. It first removes fear and grief, then speaks of whatever the souls desire.

Many worldly needs are responses to fear: security, preservation, preparation for loss. Grief is our relation to what has already been lost. If both are removed, the very meaning of “need” changes.

Paradise therefore appears not simply as a place with unlimited resources, but as a transformed condition of existence in which abundance is not poisoned by anxiety.

## 6. Envy Removed from the Heart

### Verse

> **15:45** — As  for  the  righteous,  they  will  enjoy gardens and springs.

> **15:46** — Enter therein, peaceful and secure. 

> **15:47** — We  remove  all  jealousy  from  their hearts.  Like  one  family,  they  will  be on adjacent furnishings.

> **15:48** — Never  will  they  suffer  any  fatigue therein;  never  will  they  be  evicted therefrom.

### Reflection on the Verse

15:47 says that envy is removed from their hearts. That detail is essential for understanding social life in Paradise.

In this world, relationships are often damaged by comparison: who received more, who is more honored, who possesses more beauty or status. Paradise includes togetherness without the corrosive comparison that turns another person’s gift into my sense of loss.

The same passage removes fatigue and the possibility of expulsion. Competition, exhaustion, and fear of losing the home disappear together.

## 7. Food, Drink, Fruit, and Water: Necessity or Gift?

### Verse

> **56:25** — They  never  hear  any  nonsense therein, nor sinful utterances.

> **56:26** — Only the utterance: “Peace, peace.”

> **56:27** — Those of the right side, will be on the right side.

> **56:28** — In lush orchards.

> **56:29** — Fragrant fruits.

> **56:30** — Extended shade.

> **56:31** — Abundant water.

> **56:32** — Many fruits.

> **56:33** — Never ending; never forbidden.

> **56:34** — Luxurious furnishings.

### Reflection on the Verse

56:28-34 speaks of lush orchards, fragrant fruit, shade, abundant water, fruit that never ends and is never forbidden, and luxurious furnishings.

The Quran clearly speaks of eating and drinking in Paradise. It does not say that people must eat in order to avoid bodily collapse. In this world food is tied to mortality; elsewhere the Paradise passages remove fatigue and death.

The careful conclusion is therefore simple: food and drink are real parts of the promised experience, but the verses do not authorize us to define them as the same survival necessities we know here.

## 8. Rivers of Water, Milk, Drink, and Honey

### Verse

> **47:15** — The  allegory  of  Paradise  that  is promised for the righteous is this: it has rivers of unpolluted water, and rivers of fresh milk, and rivers of wine—delicious  for  the  drinkers— and rivers of strained honey. They have all kinds of fruits therein, and forgiveness from their Lord. (Are they better) or those who abide forever in the hellfire, and drink hellish water that tears up their intestines?

### Reflection on the Verse

47:15 explicitly introduces an allegory of Paradise: rivers of unpolluted water, fresh milk, delightful drink, purified honey, every kind of fruit, and forgiveness from the Lord.

That wording protects us from two extremes. We should not empty the verse by saying the promised delights mean nothing real. But we should also not force the allegorical description into the exact physics and biology of this world.

The central picture is abundance, purity, delight, and forgiveness. The last element may be the deepest: Paradise is not only provision; it is peace in one’s relationship with GOD.

## 9. Being Served: Labor and Necessity in Paradise

### Verse

> **76:12** — He  rewards  them  for  their  steadfastness with Paradise, and silk.

> **76:13** — They  relax  therein  on  luxurious  furnishings.  They  suffer  neither  the heat of the sun, nor any cold.

> **76:14** — The  shade  covers  them  therein,  and the fruits are brought within reach.

> **76:15** — They  are  served  drinks  in  silver  containers  and  cups  that  are  translucent.

> **76:16** — Translucent  cups,  though  made  of silver; they rightly deserved all this.

> **76:17** — They  enjoy  drinks  of  delicious  flavors.

> **76:18** — From  a  spring  therein  known  as “Salsabeel.”

> **76:19** — Serving  them  will  be  immortal  servants.  When  you  see  them,  they  will look like scattered pearls.

> **76:20** — Wherever  you  look,  you  will  see bliss, and a wonderful dominion.

> **76:21** — On  them  will  be  clothes  of  green velvet,  satin,  and  silver  ornaments. Their  Lord  will  provide  them  with pure drinks.

### Reflection on the Verse

76:12-21 describes reclining, shade, reachable fruit, drinks, servants, beauty, and bliss. The presence of service does not support the idea that the dwellers are trapped in endless survival labor.

Yet we also cannot conclude that there is no activity of any kind, because 36:55 already speaks of happy occupation. The distinction may be between activity and hardship: occupation without exhaustion, life without forced maintenance.

Paradise remains alive, but the struggle to keep oneself alive is no longer the center of life.

## 10. Meeting One Another and Remembering the Past

### Verse

> **52:17** — The righteous  have deserved gardens and bliss.

> **52:18** — They  enjoy  what  their  Lord  has reserved for them; their Lord has spared them the retribution of Hell.

> **52:19** — Eat and drink happily, in return for your works.

> **52:20** — They relax on luxurious furnishings, and we match them with beautiful spouses.

> **52:21** — For those who believed, and their children  also  followed  them  in belief, we will have their children join them. We never fail to reward them for any work. Every person is paid for what he did.

> **52:22** — We will supply them with fruits and meats that they love.

> **52:23** — They will enjoy drinks that are never polluted, and never sinful to drink.

> **52:24** — Serving them will be servants like protected pearls.

> **52:25** — They will meet each other and reminisce among themselves.

> **52:26** — They will say, “We used to be kind and humble among our people.

> **52:27** — “GOD has blessed us, and has spared us the agony of ill winds.

### Reflection on the Verse

52:25 says the people of Paradise meet one another and reminisce. This suggests that eternal life is not a state without memory.

They speak about their former life and recognize GOD’s favor in saving them. The past is remembered, but it is no longer an open wound; it becomes part of understanding the mercy they have received.

The passage also speaks of believing descendants joining them. The verses preserve recognition, companionship, and conversation without explaining every biological or legal form that family life may take there.

## 11. Social Life Without Sinful or Empty Speech

### Verse

> **56:25** — They  never  hear  any  nonsense therein, nor sinful utterances.

> **56:26** — Only the utterance: “Peace, peace.”

### Reflection on the Verse

56:25-26 gives a small but profound detail: they hear neither nonsense nor sinful utterances, only “Peace, peace.”

Human exhaustion is not only physical. Lies, humiliation, gossip, manipulation, conflict, and noise exhaust the soul. Paradise is social life purified at the level of speech.

A person no longer needs to guard himself against the tongue of another. Language itself becomes part of peace.

## 12. Praise of GOD Continues in Paradise

### Verse

> **10:9** — As for those who believe and lead a righteous life, their Lord guides them, by virtue of their belief. Rivers will flow beneath them in the gardens of bliss.

> **10:10** — Their  prayer  therein  is:  “Be  You glorified,  our  god,”  their  greeting therein  is,  “Peace,”  and  their  ultimate  prayer  is:  “Praise  be  to  GOD, Lord of the universe.”

### Reflection on the Verse

10:10 describes the prayers and greetings of the people of Paradise: glorification of GOD, peace, and praise of the Lord of the universe.

Remembering GOD therefore does not end when hardship ends. Worship is not shown merely as a tool for obtaining rescue from difficulty.

The one who has entered safety still praises GOD. This suggests a purer form of worship: praise not driven by fear, but by recognition, gratitude, and nearness to truth.

## 13. Enjoying Paradise “As We Please”

### Verse

> **39:73** — Those  who  reverenced  their  Lord will  be  led  to  Paradise  in  throngs. When  they  get  to  it,  and  its  gates  are opened,  its  guards  will  say,  “Peace be  upon  you;  you  have  won.  Therefore, you abide herein forever.”

> **39:74** — They  will  say,  “Praise  be  to  GOD, who  fulfilled  His  promise  to  us,  and made  us  inherit  the  earth,  enjoying Paradise  as  we  please.”  What  a beautiful  recompense  for  the  workers!

> **39:75** — You  will  see  the  angels  floating around  the  throne,  glorifying  and praising  their  Lord.  After  the  equitable  judgment  is  issued  to  all,  it  will be  proclaimed:  “Praise  be  to  GOD, Lord of the universe.”

### Reflection on the Verse

39:74 records the people of Paradise praising GOD for enabling them to enjoy Paradise as they please. The verse carries a striking sense of freedom.

Worldly life is structured by limitations: time, money, sickness, geography, danger, and mortality. Paradise is described as an arena in which enjoyment is not continually blocked by such constraints.

That freedom follows just judgment. It is not chaos; it is secure freedom after justice has been completed.

## 14. A Life Without Another Death

### Verse

> **44:51** — The righteous will be in a secure position.

> **44:52** — Enjoying gardens and springs.

> **44:53** — Wearing velvet and satin; close to each other.

> **44:54** — We grant them wonderful spouses.

> **44:55** — They enjoy  in it all  kinds  of  fruits,  in perfect peace.

> **44:56** — They  do  not  taste  death  therein— beyond  the  first  death*—and  He has  spared  them  the  retribution  of Hell.

> **44:57** — Such  is  the  blessing  from  your  Lord. Such is the great triumph.

### Reflection on the Verse

44:56 is explicit: beyond the first death, the people of Paradise do not taste death there.

Once death is removed, many of the needs that structure worldly life change meaning. We save, hurry, reproduce, preserve, and plan under the shadow of mortality.

The verses do not explain the physics of eternal time. They make a more fundamental promise: death will not return. That may be why using mortal experience to imagine the boredom of immortality is already starting from the wrong condition.

## 15. Whatever They Wish: Fulfillment Rather Than Lack

### Verse

> **25:15** — Say,  “Is  this  better  or  the  eternal Paradise  that  is  promised  for  the righteous?  It  is  their  well  deserved reward; a well deserved destiny.”

> **25:16** — They  get  anything  they  wish  therein, forever.  This  is  your  Lord’s  irrevocable promise.

### Reflection on the Verse

25:16 says they receive whatever they wish forever, and calls this an irrevocable promise from the Lord.

In this world, a need is often defined by absence. In Paradise, the verses do not describe a permanent gap between desire and provision.

This does not make the creature independent of GOD. Every fulfilled desire remains a gift under GOD’s promise. Freedom from deprivation is not freedom from the Giver.

## 16. Entering with Peace: Security as a Gift

### Verse

> **16:30** — As for the righteous, when they are asked, “What do you think of these revelations from your Lord,” they say, “Good.” For those who lead a righteous life, happiness, and the abode of the Hereafter is even better. What a blissful abode for the righteous.

> **16:31** — The gardens of Eden are reserved for them, wherein rivers flow. They have  anything  they  wish  therein. GOD thus rewards the righteous. 

> **16:32** — The angels terminate their lives in a  state  of  righteousness,  saying, “Peace be upon you. Enter Paradise (now) as a reward for your works.”*

### Reflection on the Verse

16:31 says they have whatever they wish, while 16:32 welcomes them with peace. Abundance and peace repeatedly appear together.

Other passages remove envy, fear, grief, harmful speech, fatigue, and death. Paradise therefore appears as multilayered security: security from loss, from harm, from inner corruption, and from an ending.

Perhaps one of the deepest human needs in this world is exactly what Paradise makes permanent: to exist without expecting the good to be taken away.

## 17. Inexhaustible Provision and Shade

### Verse

> **13:35** — The  allegory  of  Heaven,  which  is promised  for  the  righteous,  is  flowing  streams,  inexhaustible  provisions,  and  cool  shade.  Such  is  the destiny  for  those  who  observe  righteousness,  while  the  destiny  for  the disbelievers is Hell.

### Reflection on the Verse

13:35 describes inexhaustible provisions and cool shade. Scarcity is one of the basic structures of worldly economics; limited resources produce competition, saving, fear, and exclusion.

Paradise is described differently. Provision does not run out.

When inexhaustible provision is read together with the removal of envy, a remarkable moral picture appears: another person’s abundance no longer threatens my own. External abundance and internal purification belong together.

## 18. Not Making Paradise Say What We Do Not Know

### Verse

> **17:36** — You  shall  not  accept  any  information,  unless  you  verify  it  for  yourself. I  have  given  you  the  hearing,  the eyesight,  and  the  brain,  and  you  are responsible for using them.

### Reflection on the Verse

Curiosity about Paradise is natural. Will people sleep? Create new things? Have professions? Have children? How will bodies function? How will time be experienced?

17:36 gives an important boundary: do not accept information unless you verify it. We may speak confidently where the scripture speaks clearly, but we should not fill every silence with imagination and then call the result religious knowledge.

This restraint does not weaken hope. GOD has already said: happy occupation, whatever they wish, even more, peace, no fatigue, and no death. The unknown mechanism does not need invention.

Sometimes faith means trusting what has been revealed and remaining quiet where revelation remains quiet.

## 19. Arabic Word Roots: The Language of Joyful Occupation

### Verse

> **36:55** — The dwellers of Paradise will be, on that day, happily busy.

### Reflection on the Verse

The vocabulary of 36:55 opens a concentrated field of meaning.

**أَصْحَاب — ṣ-ḥ-b (ص ح ب):** companionship, accompanying, people belonging with a place or condition. “The dwellers/companions of Paradise” suggests a community rather than isolated existence.

**الْجَنَّة — j-n-n (ج ن ن):** a root field associated with covering or concealment; in Quranic usage, *al-jannah* is established as the garden/Paradise.

**الْيَوْم — y-w-m (ي و م):** day. The verse places the scene in the decisive “that Day.”

**شُغُل — sh-gh-l (ش غ ل):** occupation, being engaged or busy. The Quranic Arabic Corpus identifies the word in 36:55 within the field of being occupied. The striking feature is that this occupation is immediately joined with a word of delight.

**فَاكِهُون — f-k-h (ف ك ه):** a field of delight, enjoyment, cheer; the same root family is also connected with fruit. In 36:55 it describes a joyful state, while fruit is mentioned explicitly in 36:57. The closeness reinforces the atmosphere of pleasurable occupation, but it does not authorize us to invent specific activities.

Other important root fields in the article are **س ل م (s-l-m)** for peace and safety, **خ ل د (kh-l-d)** for abiding permanence, **ش ه و (sh-h-w)** for desire, **ن ع م (n-ʿ-m)** for blessing, and **ر ز ق (r-z-q)** for provision. Together they portray Paradise not merely as endless duration, but as enduring life surrounded by peace, fulfilled desire, community, and provision.

## Other Claims

### Verse

> **47:15** — The  allegory  of  Paradise  that  is promised for the righteous is this: it has rivers of unpolluted water, and rivers of fresh milk, and rivers of wine—delicious  for  the  drinkers— and rivers of strained honey. They have all kinds of fruits therein, and forgiveness from their Lord. (Are they better) or those who abide forever in the hellfire, and drink hellish water that tears up their intestines?

### Reflection on the Verse

Across history, many detailed pictures of Paradise have been proposed. Some readings take the descriptions of food, drink, spouses, furniture, and gardens as physically continuous with the present bodily order; others read much of the language as symbolic or spiritual. The authorized translation of 47:15 itself uses the phrase “the allegory of Paradise,” which calls for caution about claiming that every descriptive detail must operate through the exact physics and biology of this world.

Likewise, people may speculate about professions, technology, artistic production, children, sleep, digestion, exploration, or social institutions in Paradise. The verses used in this article do not explicitly establish those details. 36:55 tells us that the people of Paradise are happily busy; it does not provide a complete catalog of their activities.

It is therefore useful to distinguish possibility from revealed certainty. The common ground directly supported by the verses is a joyful life of companionship, safety, fulfilled desire, inexhaustible provision, good speech, gratitude, absence of fatigue, absence of death, and enduring peace.

## Conclusion

### Verse

> **50:35** — They get anything they wish therein, and we have even more.

### Reflection on the Verse

The question at the beginning was simple: What will we do in eternal life, and what will we need?

The verses do not say that nothing happens. 36:55 says the people of Paradise are happily busy. They are with spouses, meet others, remember the past, eat and drink, recline, praise GOD, exchange peace, and enjoy Paradise as they please.

But this is not merely the present world stretched infinitely. Fatigue is gone. Death is gone. Fear and grief are gone. Envy is removed. Provision does not run out. Desire is not described as continually separated from fulfillment.

So what will be “needed”? The Quran speaks of food, drink, shade, companionship, service, clothing, dwelling, and beauty, but it never says these function as the same biological necessities that govern mortal survival. Paradise is described less as a place where shortages are endlessly repaired and more as a realm where GOD continuously gives.

What moves me most is not merely the quantity of the gifts, but their security. Everything we love here can be lost. In Paradise the promise says that they remain forever, are never expelled, and do not die again. Perhaps the hardest gift for us to imagine is not abundance itself, but beauty that is no longer temporary.

Then 50:35 leaves the horizon open: they receive whatever they wish, “and we have even more.” Paradise is therefore not the sum of what we can currently imagine. GOD’s “more” exceeds the limits of human imagination.

Contemplating Paradise should not drive us to manufacture details. It should deepen trust. What has been revealed is already enough to transform hope: eternity is not empty, happiness is not passive, companionship is not poisoned, provision does not fail, death does not return, speech becomes peace, and all of it is rooted in the mercy of GOD.

Perhaps the greatest change is that eternal life is no longer lived in the language of lack. It is lived in the language of gift.
