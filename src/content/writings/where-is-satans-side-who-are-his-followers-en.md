# [58:19] Where Is Satan's Side? Who Are the Followers of Satan?

**Author:** Berk KÖKSAL  
**Date:** November 02, 2026  
**Place:** Mersin

The central purpose of this article is to reflect on Quran 58:19 together with related verses and to ask what bearing witness to the oneness of GOD means for our loyalties, knowledge, justice, truthfulness, and submission. Rather than using “the party of the devil” as a convenient accusation against others, I want to examine the choices by which a human being may begin to follow an invitation that leads away from GOD.

Peace. Peace be upon you.

How does a person know which side they are on? Is it determined by the name they use, the people around them, or the group they defend? Or do the choices made when nobody is watching reveal more? When I began to think seriously about the expression “the party of the devil,” I did not imagine a location on a map. I thought of a direction that can change quietly within a human life. A person may profess belief in GOD and still need to ask which voice they obey when truth conflicts with personal advantage.

In my earlier reflection <a href="https://berkkoksal.com/writings/the_steps_of_satan_en/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">The Steps of Satan</a>, I asked whether profound deviation always begins with something dramatic. In <a href="https://berkkoksal.com/writings/loving-god-alone-en/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Loving GOD Alone</a>, I questioned the point at which love or loyalty becomes an authority beside GOD. Both questions return when I read 58:19. There is Satan's influence, there is the disregard of GOD's message, and there is a result described with remarkable seriousness. Perhaps the frightening question is not simply whether I can recognize the enemy, but whether I can recognize when I have lost my own direction.

I do not want to write this as a catalogue of names belonging to a condemned side. I want to begin with my own accountability. Judgment belongs to GOD. My task is to take His message seriously, to acknowledge the limits of what I know, and not to escape responsibility for the choices I make.

## 1. The Party of the Devil: The Message Is Disregarded First

### Verse

> **58:19**
>
> ٱسْتَحْوَذَ عَلَيْهِمُ ٱلشَّيْطَٰنُ فَأَنسَىٰهُمْ ذِكْرَ ٱللَّهِ أُو۟لَٰٓئِكَ حِزْبُ ٱلشَّيْطَٰنِ أَلَآ إِنَّ حِزْبَ ٱلشَّيْطَٰنِ هُمُ ٱلْخَٰسِرُونَ
>
> The devil has possessed them, and has  caused  them  to  disregard GOD’s message. These are the party of the devil. Absolutely, the party of the devil are the losers.
>
> Şeytan onları ele geçirdi ve TANRI’nın mesajını göz ardı etmelerine yol açtı. Bunlar şeytanın tarafıdır. Kesinlikle şeytanın tarafı kaybedenlerdir.

Source: <a href="https://kuranteyit.com/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">kuranteyit.com</a>

> **58:14** — Have  you  noted  those  who  befriended  people  with  whom  GOD  is angry?  They  neither  belong  with you,  nor  with  them.  They  deliberately swear lies!

> **58:16** — They  used  their  oaths  as  a  means  of repelling  from  the  path  of  GOD. Consequently,  they  have  incurred  a shameful retribution.

> **58:18** — The  Day  will  come  when  GOD  resurrects  them  all.  They  will  swear  to Him  then,  just  as  they  swear  to  you now,  thinking  that  they  really  are right! Indeed, they are the real liars.

### Reflection on the Verse

The verses immediately before 58:19 must not be overlooked. Verse 58:14 speaks of allegiance to people with whom GOD is angry and of knowingly false oaths. Verse 58:16 describes oaths used to turn others away from GOD's path. Verse 58:18 portrays such false claims recurring even on the day of resurrection. The expression “the party of the devil,” then, does not appear as an isolated accusation. It follows a description of deceptive speech and actions that obstruct the truth.

In 58:19 I see three connected elements: Satan's influence, the disregard of GOD's message, and the resulting description of the party of the devil. The verse does not specify a uniform, a nation, a city, or a social title. It describes a spiritual loss of direction. That is why my first response to the question “Where is Satan's side?” is not to name a territory. It is to ask **which message actually governs my life**.

I cannot reduce this disregard to a brief lapse of memory. There is also the unsettling possibility of knowing a message and refusing to let it matter when decisions are made. Someone may hear the truth but choose a different path when it becomes inconvenient. At the same time, I should not turn the verse's grave judgment into an automatic label for every mistake or moment of distraction. The warning is serious; my knowledge of another person's final standing is limited.

Perhaps the most troubling possibility is that someone who follows the devil's invitation does not see themselves as doing so. They may point to certain good actions and feel completely secure. Before diagnosing anyone else, I therefore need to examine my own explanations and excuses. **When GOD's message conflicts with something I want, which one do I set aside?**

**Verse references:** 58:14, 58:16, 58:18-19.

## 2. Who Is on the Other Side? The Mirror of 58:22

### Verse

> **58:22** — You will not find people who believe in GOD and the Last Day befriending those who oppose GOD and His messenger, even if they were their parents, or their children, or their siblings, or their tribe. For these, He decrees faith into their hearts, and supports them with inspiration from Him, and admits them into gardens with flowing streams wherein they abide forever. GOD is pleased with them,  and  they  are  pleased  with Him. These are the party of GOD. Most assuredly, GOD’s party are the winners.

### Reflection on the Verse

Verse 58:22 does more than place another name opposite the party of the devil. It speaks of belief in GOD and the Last Day, of the limits of allegiance to those who oppose GOD and His messenger, of faith established in hearts, and of GOD's approval. Between the loss in 58:19 and the victory in 58:22, I see not merely two competing labels, but two radically different loyalties.

The verse's reference to parents, children, siblings, and kin can be difficult to read. Yet I cannot use it as permission to humiliate parents, harm children, or invent hostility toward relatives. The question is whether loyalty to a person requires us to support opposition to GOD. Loving a person and treating them justly does not mean endorsing every decision they make. Family duties and fidelity to truth need not be confused with hostility.

There is another danger here. A person may use the words “I am on GOD's side” as though they had just given themselves immunity from correction. But 58:22 is not a certificate that I can issue to myself. It is a description in GOD's message. It calls for humility and examination, not the satisfaction of looking down on others.

**Verse references:** 58:19, 58:22.

## 3. Is Satan's Side a Place, or Is It an Invitation?

### Verse

> **35:6** — The  devil  is  your  enemy,  so  treat him as an enemy. He only invites his party to be the dwellers of Hell.

> **4:76** — Those who believe are fighting for the cause of GOD, while those who disbelieve are fighting for the cause of tyranny. Therefore, you shall fight the devil’s allies; the devil’s power is nil.

### Reflection on the Verse

Verse 35:6 brings me closer to the meaning of the question. Satan is called an enemy, and his party is described as the people he invites toward a dreadful end. An invitation implies a relationship between the one who calls and those who respond. Looking for Satan's side on a map therefore misses much of the issue. What is he inviting people to choose, to support, and eventually to become?

Verse 4:76 speaks of the devil's allies within the context of conflict and tyranny. It would be a serious distortion to make this a general license to attack people whose beliefs I dislike. The struggle described by the verse cannot simply be transferred to everyday disagreements. The ethical question I can responsibly carry into my own life is whether I stand with oppression or resist it, whether I excuse injustice when it benefits me or reject it when doing so costs me something.

Someone can proclaim GOD's name before a crowd while defending cruelty for personal gain. Someone else may quietly refuse to participate in wrongdoing. I am not authorized to pronounce the final status of either person's soul. I can, however, recognize that the allegiance described in these verses concerns what people follow and support, not merely the label they wear. Opposing Satan's call is not equivalent to treating other human beings as enemies without cause.

**Verse references:** 35:6, 4:76; compare 58:19.

## 4. Choosing an Enemy as a Lord

### Verse

> **18:50** — We said to the angels, “Fall prostrate before Adam.” They fell prostrate, except Satan. He became a jinn, for he disobeyed the order of His Lord.* Will you choose him and his descendants as lords instead of Me, even though they are your enemies? What a miserable substitute!

> **7:27** — O children of Adam, do not let the devil dupe you as he did when he caused the eviction of your parents from  Paradise,  and  the  removal of their garments to  expose their bodies. He and his tribe see you, while you do not see them. We appoint the devils as companions of those who do not believe.

### Reflection on the Verse

The question in 18:50 reveals a contradiction: choosing an identified enemy and his descendants as lords instead of GOD. I hesitate to assume that this must always take the form of a person openly saying they worship Satan. People can surrender authority to an influence without describing their choices in those terms. What the verse explicitly gives us is a warning about Satan and his descendants; it does not grant me the ability to identify every hidden motive in another person's heart.

Verse 7:27 warns the children of Adam against being deceived as their parents were. That warning matters, but fear of an unseen influence can itself become an excuse for neglecting responsibility. If I attribute every failure or conflict to Satan, I may stop examining my own mistakes. I would rather take the possibility of deception seriously while keeping my feet on the ground: investigate what can be investigated, act justly, and acknowledge what I have chosen.

There is a final question I cannot ignore. In trying to resist an enemy, might I adopt the very methods that make him an enemy? If I answer deception with deception or injustice with injustice, what am I defending? The warning about taking Satan as a lord is also a reason to examine whose methods I am allowing to shape my own behavior.

**Verse references:** 18:50, 7:27.

## 5. Satan's Admission: I Invited You, and You Responded

### Verse

> **14:22** — And  the  devil  will  say, after  the judgment had been issued, “GOD has promised you the truthful promise, and I promised you, but I broke my promise. I had no power over you; I simply invited you, and you accepted  my  invitation.  Therefore, do not blame me, and blame only yourselves. My complaining cannot help you, nor can your complaining help me. I have disbelieved in your idolizing  me.  The  transgressors have  incurred  a  painful  retribution.”

> **34:21** — He  never  had  any  power  over  them. But  we  thus  distinguish  those  who believe  in  the  Hereafter  from  those who  are  doubtful  about  it.*  Your Lord is in full control of all things.

### Reflection on the Verse

When I read 14:22, I do not find a loyal leader promising to protect those who followed him. I find a voice acknowledging a broken promise and pointing back to the response of those who accepted the invitation. He also declares that he cannot help his followers. The betrayal is profound: a person may devote a life to a promise only to discover, at the moment of judgment, that the one who made it offers no rescue.

Verse 34:21 also places a limit on Satan's power. I should neither trivialize his influence nor use it to erase the human being's accountability. Someone may be tempted, frightened, persuaded by flattering words, or drawn toward the promise of advantage. But the explanation “He made me do it” does not settle the question of what the person chose.

Calling people Satan's “puppets” may sometimes evoke the way influence works, but it can also mislead. If every string belongs entirely to Satan, the human response emphasized in 14:22 disappears. What I find more disturbing is the possibility that someone who considers themselves independent can slowly hand their decisions over to misleading promises. **Following Satan does not remove a person's responsibility for following.**

**Verse references:** 14:22, 34:21.

## 6. Followers Are Not Free from Their Own Choices

### Verse

> **34:20** — Satan  found  them  readily  fulfilling his  expectations.  They  followed him, except a few believers.

> **34:21** — He  never  had  any  power  over  them. But  we  thus  distinguish  those  who believe  in  the  Hereafter  from  those who  are  doubtful  about  it.*  Your Lord is in full control of all things.

> **7:30** — Some He guided, while others are committed to straying. They have taken the devils as their masters, instead of GOD, yet they believe that they are guided.

### Reflection on the Verse

Verses 34:20-21 distinguish following from being overpowered. People are described as fulfilling Satan's expectations and following him, yet the text also denies that he had independent power over them. This prevents me from explaining the whole human story as one of helpless victims. Guidance can be offered, and misleading suggestions can be offered, but the manner in which a person responds still matters.

Verse 7:30 makes the matter harder. People who take devils as their masters are also described as believing that they are guided. Being mistaken and knowing that one is mistaken are not the same. A person may sincerely feel knowledgeable, sincere, and justified while being wrong. My own feeling of certainty, therefore, is not sufficient evidence.

I think here of influential leaders, celebrated figures, and crowds that move together. A person may lead others while following a different influence themselves. Yet I must not turn this possibility into a supernatural verdict about identifiable individuals. The sounder question is how their words, actions, and claims to authority relate to GOD's message. Leadership in public does not automatically prove independence in conscience.

**Verse references:** 34:20-21, 7:30.

## 7. Forgetting: When a Person Loses Their Own Direction

### Verse

> **58:19** — The devil has possessed them, and has  caused  them  to  disregard GOD’s message. These are the party of the devil. Absolutely, the party of the devil are the losers.

> **59:19** — Do  not  be  like  those  who  forgot GOD,  so  He  made  them  forget themselves. These are the wicked.

### Reflection on the Verse

Read together, 58:19 and 59:19 make forgetting feel much larger than a failure to remember information. The first concerns disregarding GOD's message. The second links forgetting GOD with forgetting oneself. I imagine a person who has not necessarily lost their ambition, skills, or daily routine, but has lost sight of the kind of person they are becoming.

When a person repeatedly excuses injustice, it can begin to feel ordinary. First they remain silent; later they invent an explanation; eventually they resent whoever points out the wrong. This is a possible pattern I want to examine, not a sequence explicitly enumerated in these verses. Still, the relationship between forgetting the message and forgetting oneself deserves serious reflection.

Holding strong opinions is not proof that I remember GOD. I might give excellent advice and yet fail to recognize my own unfairness. Remembrance, for me, must matter at the point of decision. If I refuse to measure my choices against the truth I claim to know, then asking “Who belongs to Satan's party?” risks becoming a weapon aimed only at other people.

**Verse references:** 58:19, 59:19.

## 8. Does Deviation Happen All at Once? The Steps of Satan

### Verse

> **2:208** — O  you  who  believe,  you  shall  embrace  total  submission;  do  not  follow the steps of Satan, for he is your most ardent enemy.

> **24:21** — O you who believe, do not follow the steps of Satan. Anyone who follows the steps of Satan, should know that he advocates evil and vice. If it were not for GOD’s grace towards you, and His mercy, none of you would have been purified. But GOD purifies whomever He wills. GOD is Hearer, Knower.

> **2:169** — He  only  commands  you  to  commit evil and vice,  and  to say about GOD what you do not know.

### Reflection on the Verse

The expression “steps” deserves patience. People do not necessarily begin by choosing the most visible form of wrongdoing. One compromise becomes convenient; another begins to seem normal. A lie may be excused as loyalty to a friend, an injustice as defense of a community, or speaking without knowledge as a way of sounding confident. These are contemporary possibilities, not a fixed sequence laid out by the verses.

Verse 2:208 places the warning against Satan's steps beside the call to total submission. Verse 24:21 associates those steps with evil and vice. Verse 2:169 adds the danger of making claims about GOD without knowledge. I find that last warning particularly searching. When a person speaks in GOD's name, the harm of an unsupported statement can extend far beyond an ordinary mistaken opinion.

One defense against this movement is to take even small acts of dishonesty seriously. Another is to remain open to correction. A wrong step does not give me authority to announce someone's entire future; neither does its apparent smallness make it harmless. The point is to recognize the direction of a choice before that direction becomes familiar and comfortable.

**Verse references:** 2:208, 24:21, 2:169.

## 9. When Wrongdoing Begins to Look Good

### Verse

> **16:63** — By GOD, we have sent (messengers) to communities before you, but the devil adorned their works in their eyes. Consequently, he is now their lord, and they have incurred a painful retribution.

> **35:8** — Note  the  one  whose  evil  work  is adorned in his eyes, until he thinks that it is righteous. GOD thus sends astray whoever wills (to go astray), and He guides whoever wills (to be guided). Therefore,  do  not  grieve over them. GOD is fully aware of everything they do.

### Reflection on the Verse

I find the beautification of wrongdoing more unsettling than open hostility. An obviously harmful proposal can be rejected. An injustice made to look admirable may gain defenders. Read together, 16:63 and 35:8 warn of a state in which people's deeds appear attractive to them and wrong begins to look right.

A person may use success, applause, numbers, or influence as proof that they are on the correct path. But being admired is not identical to being truthful. A project can be effective and still unjust. This matters especially when religious authority is claimed. Having many followers does not establish that someone has been granted authority by GOD. I mention this not to condemn a particular individual but to examine the measures by which I am tempted to judge truth.

If my wrongdoing looks beautiful in my own eyes, who can correct me? The answer cannot be to search for another unquestionable leader. I need to keep reading, asking, examining claims, and listening when an injustice is pointed out. Acknowledging that I may be mistaken is not weakness here. It may be the beginning of protection from a deeper mistake.

**Verse references:** 16:63, 35:8.

## 10. Promises, Temptation, and Imaginary Gains

### Verse

> **4:118** — GOD  has  condemned  him,  and  he said,  “I  will  surely  recruit  a  definite share of Your worshipers.*

> **4:119** — “I  will  mislead  them,  I  will  entice them,  I  will  command  them  to  (forbid the eating of certain meats by) marking  the  ears  of  livestock,  and  I will  command  them  to  distort  the creation  of  GOD.”  Anyone  who  accepts  the  devil  as  a  lord,  instead  of GOD, has incurred a profound loss.

> **4:120** — He  promises  them  and  entices them;  what  the  devil  promises  is  no more than an illusion.

> **17:64** — “You  may  entice  them  with  your voice,  and  mobilize  all  your  forces and  all  your  men  against  them,  and share  in  their  money  and  children, and  promise  them.  Anything  the devil  promises  is  no  more  than  an illusion.

### Reflection on the Verse

When I read the verses about Satan's promises, one word keeps returning to me: expectation. A person may abandon a known truth because of a future reward they imagine receiving. Power, comfort, belonging, success, and escape from fear can all become persuasive. Verses 4:118-120 speak of recruiting, misleading, and promising; they challenge me to ask why a false promise sometimes feels more compelling than a truth that requires patience.

Verse 17:64 also speaks of wealth, children, and promises. I cannot conclude from this that seeking an income, loving one's children, or having earthly hopes is itself Satanic. What matters is the possibility that these real parts of life can be exploited to draw someone into a false allegiance. A blessing is not the same as wrongdoing committed in its name.

I turn these questions toward myself. Am I defending an action because it is right, or because I expect to profit from it? Do I trust a speaker because their claims stand up to examination, or because they promise me exactly the future I want? These questions tell me more about the danger of following Satan than speculation about a secret organization ever could.

**Verse references:** 4:118-120, 17:64.

## 11. The Words That Divide People

### Verse

> **17:53** — Tell  My  servants  to  treat  each  other in  the  best  possible  manner,  for  the devil will always try to drive a  wedge among  them.  Surely,  the  devil  is man’s most ardent enemy.

> **6:112** — We have permitted the enemies of every  prophet—human  and  jinn devils—to  inspire  in  each  other fancy words, in order to deceive. Had your Lord willed, they would not have done it. You shall disregard them and their fabrications. 

### Reflection on the Verse

Verse 17:53 brings the call to treat one another in the best possible manner into the same sentence as Satan's attempt to drive people apart. Speech is not morally neutral simply because words are intangible. A deliberate insult, a false accusation, or a suspicion presented as fact can tear apart relationships and communities.

Verse 6:112 describes deceptive, embellished words circulating among human and jinn devils. I am therefore not looking only at unseen influence. Human speech itself can become a vehicle for deception. Yet the verse is not permission to call anyone whose speech I dislike a “human devil.” I can examine whether a statement is deceptive without pretending to know the speaker's ultimate standing before GOD.

The question becomes personal when I examine my own arguments. Do I try to understand someone, or merely lower them in the eyes of an audience? Do I report something I have not verified? Am I defending an action because it is just, or because I dislike the person opposing it? Resisting Satan's invitation cannot mean adopting the methods of division and deception that the verses warn against.

**Verse references:** 17:53, 6:112.

## 12. Are Satan's Followers Only Unseen Beings?

### Verse

> **6:112** — We have permitted the enemies of every  prophet—human  and  jinn devils—to  inspire  in  each  other fancy words, in order to deceive. Had your Lord willed, they would not have done it. You shall disregard them and their fabrications. 

> **6:121** — Do not eat from that upon which the name of GOD has not been mentioned, for it is an abomination. The devils inspire their allies to argue with you; if you obey them, you will be idol worshipers.*

> **2:14** — When they meet the believers, they say, “We believe,” but when alone with their devils, they say, “We are with you; we were only mocking.”

### Reflection on the Verse

The expression “human and jinn devils” in 6:112 prevents me from treating this subject as something confined to an unseen world. Deception can also move through the words people exchange. Verse 6:121 describes devils inspiring their allies in a particular dispute. I cannot simply remove that context and assume that every disagreement is the same kind of alliance.

Verse 2:14 shows people speaking differently in different company. Their words to believers are not the same as the words they speak when alone with their devils. A change of tone between social settings is not automatically hypocrisy; this verse describes a deeper contradiction involving mockery and deceit.

What concerns me is the possibility of someone presenting themselves as a guide while encouraging others to follow a misleading invitation. But my knowledge remains limited when I consider a real person. The responsible approach is not to distribute supernatural labels. It is to examine deceptive claims, intentional harm, and loyalties that make people disregard the truth. This preserves both the seriousness of the warning and the duty not to accuse unjustly.

**Verse references:** 6:112, 6:121, 2:14.

## 13. Making Human Beings Lords: A Leader Can Be a Follower

### Verse

> **9:31** — They  have  set  up  their  religious leaders  and  scholars  as  lords,*  instead of GOD. Others deified the Messiah, son of Mary. They were all commanded  to  worship  only  one god. There is no god except He. Be He glorified, high above having any partners.

> **3:64** — Say, “O followers of the scripture, let us come to a logical agreement between us and you: that we shall not worship except GOD; that we never set up any idols besides Him, nor set up any human beings as lords beside GOD.” If they turn away, say, “Bear witness that we are submitters.”

> **42:21** — They  follow  idols  who  decree  for them  religious  laws  never  authorized  by  GOD.  If  it  were  not  for the  predetermined  decision,  they would  have  been  judged  immediately.  Indeed,  the  transgressors have  incurred  a  painful  retribution.*

### Reflection on the Verse

Verses 9:31 and 3:64 challenge the elevation of human beings to the status of lords beside GOD. Verse 42:21 also addresses the following of religious laws that GOD did not authorize. These warnings mean that the question of Satan's followers cannot be directed only toward ordinary people who listen to others. Anyone who claims to speak for GOD, determines obligations, or asserts religious authority must also face the same demand for truthfulness.

A leader can appear powerful from the outside. Others wait for their words, depend on their judgment, and become afraid to disagree. But influence does not prove correctness. If someone places their own declarations ahead of GOD's message, the number of their followers cannot make that practice right. A person may walk ahead of a crowd and still be following a false authority. That is a possibility suggested by the verses, not a claim that I know the hidden spiritual status of a particular person.

My responsibility begins with my own loyalty. When I defend a leader's mistake merely because I admire them, I can strengthen the mistake. I must also distinguish learning from a teacher from making that teacher unquestionable before GOD. Monotheism becomes more than an assertion when that distinction remains intact in actual decisions.

**Verse references:** 9:31, 3:64, 42:21.

## 14. A Companion Who Makes Deviation Feel Like Guidance

### Verse

> **43:36** — Anyone  who  disregards  the  message  of  the  Most  Gracious,  we  appoint  a  devil  to  be  his  constant  companion.*

> **43:37** — Such  companions  will  divert  them from  the  path,  yet  make  them  believe that they are guided.

> **43:38** — When  he  comes  before  us  he  will say,  “Oh  I  wish  you  were  as  far  from me  as  the  two  easts.*  What  a  miserable companion!”

> **43:39** — It  will  not  console  you  on  that  day, as  transgressors,  that  both  of  you will share in the retribution.

### Reflection on the Verse

Verses 43:36-39 move from disregarding the message to companionship, deception, and regret. I notice not only that people are led astray, but that they believe they are guided. A strong feeling of certainty can accompany a profoundly mistaken direction. The confidence a companion gives me is not itself proof that the companion is right.

I also think about the fear of being alone. Sometimes a person prefers company even when the shared direction is wrong. Agreement feels like loyalty; shared error can feel like safety. But the regret portrayed in 43:38 suggests that the closeness of such a relationship is not a reliable protection when accountability arrives.

Still, these verses do not authorize me to diagnose an unseen companion behind every difficult friendship or argument. The spiritual description in the passage and my limited knowledge of other people remain distinct. The practical question is whether a relationship encourages me to examine GOD's message or discourages me from examining it. Who walks beside me matters, but the direction in which we walk matters just as much.

**Verse references:** 43:36-39.

## 15. Can Someone Commit Injustice While Claiming GOD's Side?

### Verse

> **58:22** — You will not find people who believe in GOD and the Last Day befriending those who oppose GOD and His messenger, even if they were their parents, or their children, or their siblings, or their tribe. For these, He decrees faith into their hearts, and supports them with inspiration from Him, and admits them into gardens with flowing streams wherein they abide forever. GOD is pleased with them,  and  they  are  pleased  with Him. These are the party of GOD. Most assuredly, GOD’s party are the winners.

> **60:8** — GOD  does  not  enjoin  you  from befriending  those  who  do  not  fight you  because  of  religion,  and  do  not evict  you  from  your  homes.  You  may befriend  them  and  be  equitable towards  them.  GOD  loves  the  equitable.

> **5:8** — O  you  who  believe,  you  shall  be  absolutely  equitable,  and  observe GOD,  when  you  serve  as  witnesses. Do  not  be  provoked  by  your  conflicts  with  some  people  into  committing  injustice.  You  shall  be  absolutely  equitable,  for  it  is  more  righteous. You  shall  observe  GOD.  GOD  is fully  Cognizant  of  everything  you do.

### Reflection on the Verse

Verse 58:22 sets a serious limit on allegiance to those who oppose GOD and His messenger. Yet I must read it without discarding 60:8, which allows friendship and equitable treatment toward peaceful people of different beliefs, or 5:8, which requires justice even in conflict. It would go beyond these verses to assign every person outside my community to the same spiritual category.

When someone believes they are defending a righteous cause, they may stop examining the methods used in its name. They can begin to think that the other side's error grants permission for falsehood or cruelty. I find no such permission in these verses. A profession of loyalty to GOD that accompanies injustice should make me question my own conduct more closely, not excuse it.

An article about Satan's followers should not become a machine for producing enemies among human beings. Setting a boundary and violating someone's rights are different actions. Loyalty to GOD also requires that I place justice above my anger. One revealing test of my direction may be whether I can act equitably toward someone whose position I strongly reject.

**Verse references:** 58:22, 60:8, 5:8.

## 16. Before Calling Anyone a Follower of Satan

### Verse

> **17:36** — You  shall  not  accept  any  information,  unless  you  verify  it  for  yourself. I  have  given  you  the  hearing,  the eyesight,  and  the  brain,  and  you  are responsible for using them.

> **49:6** — O you who believe, if a wicked person brings any news to you, you shall first investigate, lest you commit injustice towards some people, out of ignorance, then become sorry and remorseful for what you have done.

> **49:12** — O you who believe, you shall avoid any suspicion, for even a little bit of suspicion is sinful. You shall not spy on one another, nor shall you backbite one another; this is as abominable as eating the flesh of your dead brother.  You  certainly  abhor  this. You  shall  observe  GOD.  GOD  is Redeemer, Most Merciful.

### Reflection on the Verse

Verse 17:36 reminds me of responsibility for what I claim to know. Verse 49:6 tells us to investigate reports, while 49:12 warns against suspicion and intrusion into other people's lives. After reading these passages, I cannot treat the sentence “This person belongs to Satan's party” as a harmless remark. I usually see only fragments of another person's words and actions. I do not know all their intentions, their future, or the judgment awaiting them.

That does not require indifference to wrongdoing. I can describe a lie as a lie, recognize injustice, or establish distance from someone whose actions cause harm. But criticism of conduct is not the same as pronouncing GOD's final judgment on a soul. The moment I cross that line, I risk claiming knowledge beyond my reach.

I want to examine my speech by an equally strict measure. Do I quote people accurately? Have I checked the report I am spreading? Am I quicker to believe an accusation against someone I dislike? When discussing Satan's deception, I must not allow my own language to become deceptive. Otherwise I could claim to defend the truth while harming it through my method.

**Verse references:** 17:36, 49:6, 49:12.

## 17. The Day of Reckoning: Can Leaders Save Their Followers?

### Verse

> **14:21** — When they all stand before GOD,* the followers will say to the leaders, “We used to follow you. Can you spare us even a little bit of GOD’s retribution?” They will say, “Had GOD guided us, we would have guided you.  Now  it  is  too  late, whether we grieve or resort to patience, there is no exit for us.”

> **37:30** — “We  never  had  any  power  over  you; it is you who were wicked. 

> **37:32** — “We  misled  you,  only  because  we were astray.”

> **37:33** — Thus,  together  they  will  all  partake of the retribution on that day.

### Reflection on the Verse

Verse 14:21 portrays followers asking their leaders whether they can shield them from GOD's retribution. The scene reveals the limit of dependence on human power. Someone influential today cannot necessarily carry the burden of those who trusted them when judgment arrives. Verses 37:30-33 likewise portray accusation, misguidance, and a consequence shared by those involved.

It would be easy to turn these verses into a story that blames only the leaders. But when I recall the difference between invitation and acceptance in 14:22, I see that followers also have choices to examine. One person may lead others into error. Another may stop asking why they are following. Their responsibilities are not identical, yet neither is erased by the other's fault.

Statements such as “Everyone was doing it,” “That was how I was taught,” or “I only followed the leader” can be comforting in this world. They are not automatically adequate moral answers. Where a person has the opportunity to examine GOD's message, what they do with that opportunity matters. The lesson I take from these scenes is not to place my ultimate accountability in another human being's hands.

**Verse references:** 14:21-22, 37:30, 37:32-33.

## 18. How Can We Resist Satan's Influence?

### Verse

> **16:98** — When  you  read  the  Quran,  you  shall seek  refuge  in  GOD  from  Satan  the rejected.

> **16:99** — He  has  no  power  over  those  who believe and trust in their Lord.

> **16:100** — His  power  is  limited  to  those  who choose  him  as  their  master;  those who choose him as their god.

> **7:201** — Those  who  are  righteous,  whenever the  devil  approaches  them  with  an idea,  they  remember,  whereupon they become seers.

> **23:97** — Say,  “My  Lord,  I  seek  refuge  in  You from the whispers of the devils.

> **23:98** — “And  I  seek  refuge  in  You,  my Lord, lest they come near me.”

### Reflection on the Verse

So far I have considered how Satan's party is described and the choices associated with following him. Yet recognizing a danger is not enough. Verses 16:98-100 bring forward refuge in GOD, belief, and trust. Verse 7:201 speaks of remembering when an idea approaches, and 23:97-98 presents prayers seeking protection from devils and their nearness.

I read these verses as an invitation not to let fear govern my life. I do not need to make the unseen the center of every explanation. I need to return the direction of my trust to GOD. Protection from Satan is not endless suspicion of other people or the belief that I am incapable of error. It means keeping remembrance and trust alive, especially when I am tempted to excuse something wrong.

There is a practical responsibility here. When an idea pushes me toward anger, dishonesty, or injustice, can I examine it before defending it? Can I return to the verse rather than silencing it with my own advantage? If I have made a mistake, can I choose what is right without claiming that the future is already closed? The verses do not present protection as some secret power I possess. They place refuge in GOD and trust in Him at the center.

**Verse references:** 16:98-100, 7:201, 23:97-98.

## 19. What Reveals Which Side I Am Choosing?

### Verse

> **36:60** — Did  I  not  covenant  with  you,  O Children of Adam, that you shall not worship the devil? That he is your most ardent enemy?

> **36:61** — And  that  you  shall  worship  Me alone? This is the right path.

> **5:55** — Your  real  allies  are  GOD  and  His messenger,  and  the  believers  who observe  the  Contact  Prayers  (Salat), and  give  the  obligatory  charity (Zakat), and they bow down.

> **5:56** — Those  who  ally  themselves  with GOD  and  His  messenger,  and  those who  believed,  belong  in  the  party  of GOD;  absolutely,  they  are  the  victors.

> **9:71** — The believing men and women are allies of one another. They advocate righteousness and forbid evil, they observe  the Contact  Prayers  (Salat) and give the obligatory charity (Zakat), and they obey GOD and His messenger. These will be showered by GOD’s mercy. GOD is Almighty, Most Wise.

### Reflection on the Verse

Verses 36:60-61 place two directions in clear contrast: not worshiping the devil and worshiping GOD alone. I hear an echo of the distinction between the two parties in 58:19 and 58:22. Verses 5:55-56 and 9:71 describe genuine allegiance through belief, support for righteousness, and resistance to evil. On one side are deceptive promises; on the other is a loyalty that requires conduct and accountability.

I cannot finish by simply declaring my own side aloud and ending every question. What troubles me is the distance that can open between profession and choice. If I claim loyalty to GOD, what do I do when another person is treated unjustly? How do I respond when a leader places personal claims above a verse? Do I hide a friend's wrongdoing because I do not want to lose their approval? Which promise do I trust when it conflicts with GOD's message?

Perhaps I will not answer every such question perfectly in a single day. But refusing to ask them would be a deeper loss. **Understanding the party of the devil begins with recognizing which invitations I myself am answering.** Instead of issuing a final verdict on myself or someone else, I want to keep examining whether GOD's message truly directs my life.

**Verse references:** 36:60-61, 5:55-56, 9:71; compare 58:19, 58:22.

## Arabic Words and Their Roots: A Reflection

The word **ḥizb (حِزْب)** appears in 58:19 in relation to the devil and in 58:22 in relation to GOD. It conveys a side or a group united by allegiance. That common wording matters: the word itself is not the ultimate distinction; the direction of allegiance is. Verse 35:6 also speaks of the devil inviting his party. Reducing the word to a modern political party or registered organization would miss the spiritual and ethical contrast expressed in these verses.

In 58:19, **dhikr (ذِكْر)** belongs to the root family *dh-k-r (ذ-ك-ر)*, associated with remembering or mentioning. The language of forgetting in the same verse, together with 59:19, may be considered alongside *n-s-y (ن-س-ي)*. Reading these ideas together leads me to examine not just memory but the place that remembered truth holds in action. That last connection is my reflection on the verses, not a new definition imposed on the Arabic word.

The term **sulṭān (سُلْطَٰن)** in 14:22 and 34:21 concerns power or authority, a significant word because the influence of Satan is discussed alongside its limits. In 18:50, **awliyāʾ (أَوْلِيَآءَ)** belongs to the *w-l-y (و-ل-ي)* family, inviting reflection on guardianship and allegiance. In 2:208 and 24:21, **khuṭuwāt (خُطُوَٰت)**, related to *kh-ṭ-w (خ-ط-و)*, speaks of steps. These connections help us notice the relationship between taking steps, granting authority, remembering, and following. They should not be used to invent a command that the verses do not actually contain.

Together these words leave me with a question: Before announcing my side, whose steps am I following, what am I forgetting, and to whom have I given authority? **Verse references:** 58:19, 58:22, 35:6, 59:19, 14:22, 34:21, 18:50, 2:208, 24:21.

## Other Claims

Some traditional interpretations associate “the party of the devil” with identifiable historical groups or communities that openly opposed the message. Other approaches read the expression as a broader description of the ways human beings can abandon truth in any generation. Verse 58:19 has a context, but it does not follow that I can extend its verdict with certainty to a list of people I select today. These approaches can be considered with caution without being turned into conclusions that outrun the text.

The description of Satan possessing people in 58:19 is sometimes taken as direct influence and sometimes as a way of depicting overwhelming moral control or allegiance. These approaches raise questions that the verse alone does not settle in every unseen detail. What matters for this reflection is that 14:22 and 34:21 do not erase the question of human responsibility. Influence is described; human response is also placed under scrutiny.

Nor should “Satan's party” be treated as the automatic name of a religious sect, political movement, ethnic group, or everyone whose beliefs differ from mine. The limits of knowledge and the danger of unjust judgment remain relevant in 17:36 and 49:12. I leave these external interpretations in their proper place and allow the relationships among the verses to carry the main reflection.

## Conclusion

I began with the question “Where is Satan's side?” Verse 58:19 first showed me not an address but an alarming loss of direction: disregarding GOD's message and belonging to the party of the devil. Verse 58:22 opened the opposite view: faith, loyalty to GOD, and His approval. The contrast is more profound than a classification designed to glorify my community and condemn everyone outside it.

Who are Satan's followers? Staying within what the verses actually say, I find warnings about those who answer his invitation, take him as a master, disregard the message, trust deceptive promises, and persist in a misleading direction (58:19, 16:100, 34:20, 43:36-37). Yet 14:22 also tells us that Satan will not save his followers and that their acceptance of his invitation must be faced. No one can simply say “I was only following” and abandon every responsibility.

There is a second half to this reflection. I can accuse someone of belonging to Satan's party while lying about them, repeating an unverified report, or treating them unjustly. Then my claim and my method contradict one another. Verses 17:36, 49:6, 49:12, and 5:8 set a limit I cannot afford to forget. **Truth cannot be defended by a method that violates truth.**

So I do not want merely to announce where I belong. I want to examine whom I treat as an authority, how I speak when injustice appears, why I believe the promises offered to me, and whether GOD's message is present when I make difficult choices. When I am wrong, I want to seek correction. Before pronouncing judgment on anyone else, I want to examine my own testimony.

Satan's side may not be a place to locate on a map, but the direction in which a human being responds to an invitation is a serious matter. I want to look for the answer first in my choices, not in the names I assign to other people. And I want to remember that **the final word does not belong to me or to any leader people follow. It belongs to GOD.**
