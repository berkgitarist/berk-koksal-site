# The Teaching of Names: Does Naming a Thing Mean Knowing It?

**Author:** Berk KÖKSAL  
**Date:** September 17, 2026  
**Place:** Mersin

The central purpose of this article is to contemplate the verses together with other related verses, and to consider sincerely and deeply what bearing witness to the oneness of GOD means in human life in terms of knowledge, justice, truthfulness, and submission.

Peace. Peace be upon you.

Human beings understand the world through names. We distinguish, classify, remember, describe, and communicate through words. Names can make knowledge possible. Yet the same ability can produce an illusion: once we have named something, we may begin to believe that we have fully understood it.

2:31 says that GOD taught Adam all the names. Immediately afterward, 2:32 records the angels acknowledging that they possess no knowledge except what GOD taught them. That sequence is important. Naming belongs inside knowledge, but knowledge remains received, limited, and accountable.

53:23 presents the opposite possibility: human beings and their ancestors can make up names that GOD never authorized. A name can therefore carry knowledge, or it can become a human construction that is mistaken for truth.

The central question of this article is: **Does giving something a name reveal its reality, or can a name sometimes become only a label we have placed over what we do not truly know?**

## 1. Adam Is Taught All the Names

### Verse

> **2:31**
>
> وَعَلَّمَ ءَادَمَ ٱلْأَسْمَآءَ كُلَّهَا ثُمَّ عَرَضَهُمْ عَلَى ٱلْمَلَٰٓئِكَةِ فَقَالَ أَنۢبِـُٔونِى بِأَسْمَآءِ هَٰٓؤُلَآءِ إِن كُنتُمْ صَٰدِقِينَ
>
> He  taught  Adam  all  the  names* then presented  them to the angels, saying, “Give me the names of these, if you are right.”
>
> O, Âdem’e bütün isimleri* öğretti, sonra  “Eğer haklıysanız, bunların isimlerini bana bildirin” diyerek onları meleklere de sundu.

Source: <a href="https://kuranteyit.com/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">kuranteyit.com</a>

### Reflection on the Verse

The first thing I notice in 2:31 is that Adam does not independently create the knowledge described here. GOD teaches him. The verse therefore presents the human being as capable of learning, distinguishing, and naming, but still as a learner.

A name is more than a sound. It allows one thing to be distinguished from another, retained in memory, and communicated. Human language can carry objects, relationships, abstractions, and moral concepts.

Yet the verb “taught” prevents knowledge from becoming a reason for self-worship. Human intelligence is real, but it is not absolute or self-originating.

**Verse references:** 2:31.

## 2. The First Ethics of Knowledge: Admitting Its Limit

### Verse

> **2:32** — They  said,  “Be  You  glorified,  we have  no  knowledge,  except  that which  You  have  taught  us.  You  are the Omniscient, Most Wise.”

### Reflection on the Verse

The sequence from 2:31 to 2:32 is striking. Names are taught, and then the limit of knowledge is confessed. Knowledge and humility are not separated.

The angels do not glorify ignorance. They simply refuse to claim knowledge they do not possess. “We have no knowledge except what You have taught us” becomes a model of intellectual honesty.

Human beings often do the opposite. We place a label on a psychological state, a religious group, a person, or a social problem, and the familiarity of the label creates the feeling that we now understand the reality completely.

2:32 reminds me that naming is not omniscience.

**Verse references:** 2:32.

## 3. Knowing a Name Is Not Knowing Every Secret

### Verse

> **2:33** — He  said,  “O  Adam,  tell  them  their names.”  When  he  told  them  their names,  He  said,  “Did  I  not  tell  you that  I  know  the  secrets  of  the  heavens  and  the  earth?  I  know  what  you declare, and what you conceal.”

### Reflection on the Verse

2:33 moves from Adam naming what was presented to GOD’s knowledge of the secrets of the heavens and the earth, including what is declared and concealed.

That contrast establishes a scale. Human beings can name, observe, and explain. But naming what is visible is not the same as knowing everything hidden behind it.

I may see a person’s action, but not the whole intention. I may classify a phenomenon without knowing every cause and consequence. Language organizes reality; it does not necessarily exhaust it.

The difference between human knowledge and GOD’s knowledge protects names from becoming idols.

**Verse references:** 2:33.

## 4. Can Invented Names Become Truth Through Repetition?

### Verse

> **53:23** — These  are  but  names  that  you  made up,  you  and  your  forefathers.  GOD never  authorized  such  a  blasphemy. They  follow  conjecture,  and  personal  desire,  when  the  true  guidance  has  come  to  them  herein  from their Lord.

### Reflection on the Verse

53:23 creates a powerful contrast with 2:31. There are names taught by GOD, and there are names made up by people and their forefathers. The decisive question is authorization.

A word can be repeated for generations and still lack divine authority. Age does not transform conjecture into revelation.

This becomes especially serious in religion. When a person gives a practice, authority, institution, or human being a sacred title, the title can silently create obligations in people’s minds. But calling something sacred does not make GOD authorize it.

Naming therefore belongs to the ethics of truthfulness.

**Verse references:** 53:23.

## 5. Giving a Name Without Knowledge

### Verse

> **53:27** — Those who disbelieve in the Hereafter have given the angels feminine names.
> 
> **53:28** — They had no knowledge about this; they only conjectured. Conjecture is no substitute for the truth.

### Reflection on the Verse

53:27-28 directly separates naming from knowledge. People gave the angels feminine names, yet the next verse says they had no knowledge about the matter and followed conjecture.

This is one of the clearest lessons in the topic: **a named thing is not necessarily a known thing.** Language can cover ignorance with familiarity.

The same pattern can appear in ordinary life. A sophisticated label can make a complex problem feel solved. Calling a person by one category can make us believe we know his entire story.

The verse gives the final measure: conjecture is no substitute for truth.

**Verse references:** 53:27-28.

## 6. Names Can Be Used to Humiliate

### Verse

> **49:11** — O you who believe, no people shall ridicule other people, for they may be better than they. Nor shall any women ridicule other women, for they may be better than they. Nor shall you mock one another, or make fun of your names. Evil indeed is the reversion to wickedness after attaining faith. Anyone who does not repent after this, these are the transgressors.

### Reflection on the Verse

49:11 moves naming from epistemology into ethics. Names can be used not only to identify but to ridicule, diminish, and socially imprison another person.

A mocking label can reduce a human being to one feature, one mistake, or one stereotype. The verse interrupts this superiority by saying those being mocked may be better.

Criticism and humiliation are not the same thing. Truthful moral judgment may sometimes be necessary, but turning a person’s name into entertainment violates another boundary.

Language must serve both truth and dignity.

**Verse references:** 49:11.

## 7. An Utterance Does Not Automatically Change Reality

### Verse

> **33:4** — GOD  did  not  give  any  man  two hearts  in  his  chest.  Nor  did  He  turn your  wives  whom  you  estrange  (according  to  your  custom)  into  your mothers.*  Nor  did  He  turn  your adopted  children  into  genetic  offspring.  All  these  are  mere  utterances  that  you  have  invented.  GOD speaks  the  truth,  and  He  guides  in the (right) path.

### Reflection on the Verse

33:4 exposes the limit of social language. Human beings may invent an utterance or convention, but saying something does not automatically change the underlying reality.

That principle reaches beyond the specific examples in the verse. Calling an institution “just” does not make it just. Calling a human authority “infallible” does not create infallibility. Calling a custom “GOD’s command” does not make it revelation.

Words make claims. Truth requires more than the claim.

**Verse references:** 33:4.

## 8. Correct Naming Can Be a Matter of Justice

### Verse

> **33:5** — You  shall  give  your  adopted  children  names  that  preserve  their  relationship  to  their  genetic  parents. This  is  more  equitable  in  the  sight  of GOD.  If  you  do  not  know  their parents,  then,  as  your  brethren  in religion,  you  shall  treat  them  as members  of  your  family.  You  do  not commit  a  sin  if  you  make  a  mistake in  this  respect;  you  are  responsible for  your  purposeful  intentions. GOD is Forgiver, Most Merciful.

### Reflection on the Verse

33:5 explicitly connects naming with justice. Preserving the real relationship of adopted children to their genetic parents is described as more equitable in the sight of GOD.

This makes naming more than a technical matter. A name can preserve or obscure relationships, history, and rights.

The verse also distinguishes error from purposeful intention. The goal is not obsessive verbal perfection but truthful, just intention.

Correct naming can therefore be part of justice; false naming can become part of concealment.

**Verse references:** 33:5.

## 9. Verification Before Labeling

### Verse

> **17:36** — You  shall  not  accept  any  information,  unless  you  verify  it  for  yourself. I  have  given  you  the  hearing,  the eyesight,  and  the  brain,  and  you  are responsible for using them.

### Reflection on the Verse

17:36 asks whether I have verified what I accept as information. That question should come before I label a person, idea, or event.

Words such as “liar,” “ignorant,” “hypocrite,” or “deviant” are not neutral descriptions. They can function as verdicts. If the underlying information has not been verified, the label may become injustice.

Naming is easy. Verification is slower. The Quranic discipline moves me from the easy label toward responsible knowledge.

**Verse references:** 17:36.

## 10. Examining Words Instead of Being Captured by Them

### Verse

> **39:18** — They  are  the  ones  who  examine  all words,  then  follow  the  best.  These are  the  ones  whom  GOD  has  guided;  these  are  the  ones  who  possess intelligence.

### Reflection on the Verse

39:18 praises those who examine words and follow the best. This is an important defense against the psychological power of labels.

A concept may be old, prestigious, popular, religious, modern, or emotionally attractive. None of those qualities guarantee that the content carried by the word is true.

Examining words does not mean distrusting language itself. It means refusing to let a beautiful title replace investigation.

**Verse references:** 39:18.

## 11. Inherited Names and Concepts Do Not Carry Automatic Authority

### Verse

> **7:71** — He  said,  “You  have  incurred  condemnation  and  wrath  from  your Lord.  Do  you  argue  with  me  in defense  of  innovations  you  have  fabricated—you  and  your  parents— which  were  never  authorized  by GOD?  Therefore,  wait  and  I  will wait along with you.”

### Reflection on the Verse

7:71 confronts inherited religious constructions. Something defended by parents and generations can still lack authorization from GOD.

In *We Found Our Parents Doing This*, I reflected on the difference between inheritance and evidence. The same distinction appears here in the language of concepts and labels. A term may feel sacred because it has been used since childhood, but familiarity is not revelation.

Respect for previous generations does not require treating every inherited category as unquestionable. Parents are human; traditions are human; the standard of divine authority must remain higher than both.

**Verse references:** 7:71; 53:23.

## 12. The Name I Give Myself

### Verse

> **53:32** — They avoid gross sins and transgressions,  except  for  minor  offenses. Your Lord’s forgiveness is immense. He has been fully aware of you since He initiated you from the earth, and while you were embryos in your mothers’ bellies. Therefore, do not exalt yourselves; He is fully aware of the righteous.

### Reflection on the Verse

Human beings do not only label others. We also label ourselves: righteous, enlightened, true believers, people of knowledge, defenders of the Quran.

53:32 warns against self-exaltation and returns knowledge of righteousness to GOD. A religious identity does not function as a certificate of moral condition.

A correct name can be carried by an incorrect life. A person can belong to an admired category and still act unjustly. Self-description must never end self-examination.

**Verse references:** 53:32.

## 13. Peoples and Tribes: Identity for Recognition, Not Moral Superiority

### Verse

> **49:13** — O people, we created you from the same  male  and  female,  and  rendered you distinct peoples and tribes, that you may recognize one another. The best among you in the sight of GOD is the most righteous. GOD is Omniscient, Cognizant. 

### Reflection on the Verse

49:13 recognizes different peoples and tribes, but gives their diversity a purpose: recognition. Moral superiority is placed elsewhere, in righteousness before GOD.

This prevents identity labels from becoming total judgments. A national, tribal, ethnic, cultural, or social name can tell us something about a person’s place in human diversity. It does not tell us his complete value before GOD.

Names can help us recognize one another. They become dangerous when recognition turns into hierarchy.

**Verse references:** 49:13.

## 14. The Diversity of Languages Is Itself a Sign

### Verse

> **30:22** — Among  His  proofs  are  the  creation of  the  heavens  and  the  earth,  and  the variations  in  your  languages  and your  colors.  In  these,  there  are  signs for the knowledgeable.

### Reflection on the Verse

30:22 places the diversity of human languages among GOD’s proofs. That means language difference is not merely a problem to overcome; it can itself become a field of contemplation.

The same physical reality can carry different names in different languages. The word changes while the thing referred to may remain the same. This distinction is useful in religious thought: vocabulary and reality are not identical.

Translations, inherited terminology, and cultural associations may illuminate meaning, but they can also obscure it. Returning to context is therefore essential.

**Verse references:** 30:22.

## 15. Language Is Given for Understanding

### Verse

> **43:3** — We  have  rendered  it  an  Arabic  Quran, that you may understand.*

### Reflection on the Verse

43:3 links the Arabic Quran with understanding. Language is therefore not merely sound to be preserved; it carries meaning meant to be understood.

Pronunciation, roots, and linguistic form can all matter, but they are tools. The goal is not the accumulation of impressive vocabulary. The goal is to hear what the revelation is saying.

A sacred word can become empty if its sound is repeated while its meaning never reaches thought or conduct.

**Verse references:** 43:3.

## 16. Do Not Mix Truth With Falsehood

### Verse

> **2:42** — Do  not  confound  the  truth  with falsehood,  nor  shall  you  conceal  the truth, knowingly.

### Reflection on the Verse

A label can become a place where truth and falsehood are mixed. A noble name can be placed over a corrupt practice. A human interpretation can be presented as though it were the revealed command itself.

2:42 therefore matters deeply to naming. Misleading speech does not always require a completely invented sentence; it can be produced by blending a truthful word with a false implication.

If I call something “GOD’s law,” I should be able to show why. If it is my interpretation, honesty requires that I identify it as interpretation.

**Verse references:** 2:42.

## 17. Invented Religious Terms and the Authority to Rule

### Verse

> **12:40** — “You  do  not  worship  beside  Him except  innovations  that  you  have made  up,  you  and  your  parents. GOD  has  never  authorized  such idols.  All  ruling  belongs  to  GOD, and  He  has  ruled  that  you  shall  not worship  except  Him.  This  is  the  perfect  religion,  but  most  people  do  not know.

### Reflection on the Verse

12:40 joins human invention with the question of authority. People and their parents can create innovations, but the verse says GOD never authorized such idols and that all ruling belongs to GOD.

Human language will naturally generate explanatory terms. The danger is not every new word. The danger is moving a human term into the place of divine legislation.

Tawhid therefore requires humility in language. My terminology can help explain; it cannot become equal to GOD’s authority simply because I speak confidently.

**Verse references:** 12:40.

## 18. Majority Usage Is Not Proof of Truth

### Verse

> **6:116** — If you obey the majority of people on earth, they will divert you from the path of GOD. They follow only conjecture; they only guess.
> 
> **10:36** — Most  of  them  follow  nothing  but conjecture,  and  conjecture  is  no substitute  for  the  truth.  GOD  is  fully aware of everything they do.

### Reflection on the Verse

A term used by millions of people can still rest on conjecture. 6:116 and 10:36 break the automatic link between majority and truth.

Social language is powerful because repetition makes categories feel natural and inevitable. Yet widespread usage is not divine authorization.

“Haven’t people always called it this?” cannot end the investigation. The Quran repeatedly directs attention back to knowledge, evidence, and truth.

**Verse references:** 6:116; 10:36.

## 19. Arabic Word Roots: Name, Knowledge, Information, and Truth

### Verse

> **2:31** — He  taught  Adam  all  the  names* then presented  them to the angels, saying, “Give me the names of these, if you are right.”
> 
> **2:32** — They  said,  “Be  You  glorified,  we have  no  knowledge,  except  that which  You  have  taught  us.  You  are the Omniscient, Most Wise.”
> 
> **53:23** — These  are  but  names  that  you  made up,  you  and  your  forefathers.  GOD never  authorized  such  a  blasphemy. They  follow  conjecture,  and  personal  desire,  when  the  true  guidance  has  come  to  them  herein  from their Lord.

### Reflection on the Verse

The Arabic word for “name” in 2:31 belongs to the **اسم / أسماء (ism / asmāʾ)** field. A name identifies and makes communication possible. Yet 53:23 shows that names can also be human inventions without divine authorization. The existence of a name is therefore not proof of truth.

The root **ع ل م (ʿ-l-m)** concerns knowing and teaching. GOD teaches in 2:31, while the angels in 2:32 explicitly limit their knowledge to what He taught. Knowledge is real, but it remains dependent and bounded.

The language of informing and declaring in 2:31-33 reminds us that naming is a claim about reality. To say what a thing is requires responsibility.

The root **ح ق (ḥ-q-q)** belongs to the semantic field of truth and reality. 53:28 states that conjecture cannot replace truth. Names are instruments; truth is not created by our choice of vocabulary.

**Verse references:** 2:31-33; 53:23; 53:28; 17:36.

## Other Claims

The phrase “all the names” in 2:31 has received many interpretations. Some understand it as the names of concrete things, others as a broader human capacity for language and conceptual knowledge. The translation notes also contain a particular explanation concerning names of things people would encounter.

Those interpretations may be studied, but the main article does not depend on choosing one speculative theory. The explicit point is that GOD taught Adam the names.

Questions about the scientific origin of language, cognitive categorization, and the historical development of human speech belong to legitimate research, but they should not be silently presented as though the verse itself settled those questions.

## Conclusion

Names are indispensable to human life. Without them, memory, teaching, distinction, and shared thought become difficult. 2:31 places naming in a remarkable context of teaching and knowledge.

Yet the Quran also places strong boundaries around names. 53:23 warns about names invented without divine authorization. 53:28 separates naming from knowledge. 49:11 protects people from humiliating labels. 33:5 connects correct naming with justice. 53:32 prevents us from turning our own religious self-description into a certificate of righteousness.

The conclusion I reach is simple: **naming a thing may be the beginning of knowing it, but it is not the completion of knowledge.**

When I use a concept, I should ask where it came from. When I label a person, I should protect justice. When I use a religious title, I should ask whether GOD authorized the authority implied by that title. When I name myself “right,” I should not allow the label to end self-examination.

And above all, 2:32 should remain close: our knowledge is limited; GOD is the Omniscient, Most Wise.

GOD knows best.
