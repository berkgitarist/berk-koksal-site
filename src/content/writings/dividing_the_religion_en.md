# Dividing the Religion: How Does One Community Turn Into Sects?

**Author:** Berk KÖKSAL  
**Date:** September 13, 2026  
**Place:** Mersin

The central purpose of this article is to contemplate the verses together with other related verses, and to consider sincerely and deeply what bearing witness to the oneness of GOD means in human life in terms of knowledge, justice, truthfulness, and submission.

Peace. Peace be upon you.

A person can be born inside a religious community, learn its language, and eventually begin to treat that inherited identity as truth itself. This is not merely a problem belonging to others. I can also defend a view because it is mine rather than because it is true.

6:159 is therefore a severe mirror. It separates the messenger from those who divide their religion into sects, while leaving their judgment to GOD. The deeper question is not merely "Which group is right?" It is: to whom does the religion belong, and where does final judgment belong?

This article follows one question: **When can a community trying to preserve truth begin to make its own identity the measure of truth?**

## 1. Dividing the Religion and Leaving Judgment to GOD

### Verse

> **6:159**
>
> إِنَّ ٱلَّذِينَ فَرَّقُوا۟ دِينَهُمْ وَكَانُوا۟ شِيَعًا لَّسْتَ مِنْهُمْ فِى شَىْءٍ إِنَّمَآ أَمْرُهُمْ إِلَى ٱللَّهِ ثُمَّ يُنَبِّئُهُم بِمَا كَانُوا۟ يَفْعَلُونَ
>
> Those  who  divide  themselves  into sects do not belong with you. Their judgment  rests  with  GOD,  then  He will  inform  them  of  everything  they had done.
>
> Kendilerini mezheplere bölenler senden değildir. Onların yargısı TANRI’ya kalmıştır, sonra O onlara yapmış oldukları her şeyi bildirecektir.

Source: <a href="https://kuranteyit.com/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">kuranteyit.com</a>

### Reflection on the Verse

6:159 does two things at once: it treats division of the religion as serious, and it removes final judgment from human possession. By saying that their judgment rests with GOD, the verse confronts my own desire to judge.

This is an important boundary. Criticizing sectarianism does not authorize me to speak in GOD's name about the final destiny of others. Otherwise I can oppose one faction only to create another faction of superiority.

Tawhid here means more than saying that GOD is one. It also means acknowledging that final judgment belongs to Him.

**Verse references:** 6:159.

## 2. Do Submission and Sectarianism Point in the Same Direction?

### Verse

> **30:31** — You  shall  submit  to  Him,  reverence Him,  observe  the  Contact  Prayers (Salat),  and—whatever  you  do—do not ever fall into idol worship.
> 
> **30:32** — (Do  not  fall  in  idol  worship,)  like those  who  divide  their  religion  into sects;  each  party  rejoicing  with  what they have.

### Reflection on the Verse

30:31-32 first calls for submission to GOD, reverence, Contact Prayer, and avoidance of idolatry; it then immediately mentions those who divide their religion into sects. This suggests that fragmentation is not merely an organizational issue.

The verse also says that every faction rejoices in what it has. Satisfaction with one's own group is not itself evidence that the group is correct.

I therefore need to ask: do I defend a view because it agrees with the Quran, or because it is "our view"?

**Verse references:** 30:31; 30:32.

## 3. Holding Together to GOD's Rope

### Verse

> **3:103** — You  shall  hold  fast  to  the  rope  of GOD,  all  of  you,  and  do  not  be divided.  Recall  GOD’s  blessings upon  you—you  used  to  be  enemies and  He  reconciled  your  hearts.  By His  grace,  you  became  brethren. You were at the brink of a pit of fire, and  He  saved  you  therefrom.  GOD thus  explains  His  revelations  for you, that you may be guided.

### Reflection on the Verse

3:103 does not define unity in a vacuum. It commands people to hold together to GOD's rope and not divide. The center is not a human name, institution, or sect, but a measure tied to GOD.

The verse also connects former enemies becoming brothers with GOD's grace. Real unity is therefore more than sharing a label; it includes reconciliation.

Suppressing truth in the name of unity and manufacturing hostility in the name of truth both violate this balance.

**Verse references:** 3:103.

## 4. Dividing Despite Clear Proofs

### Verse

> **3:105** — Do  not  be  like  those  who  became divided  and  disputed,  despite  the clear  proofs  that  were  given  to  them. For  these  have  incurred  a  terrible retribution.

### Reflection on the Verse

3:105 shows that knowledge alone does not protect a person from division. People can divide and dispute despite clear proofs.

That is uncomfortable because the problem is not always ignorance. A person may see evidence and still cling to status, interest, or group.

I therefore need another question beside "How much do I know?": "Does what I know make me more truthful and just?"

**Verse references:** 3:105.

## 5. One Community and One Lord

### Verse

> **21:92** — Your  congregation  is  but  one  congregation,  and  I  alone  am  your Lord; you shall worship Me alone.
> 
> **21:93** — However,  they  divided  themselves into  disputing  religions.  All  of  them will come back to us (for judgment).

### Reflection on the Verse

21:92 presents one community and one Lord; 21:93 then says that people divided themselves into disputing religions. Religious identity is recentered.

A person should first understand himself as a servant of the Lord, not merely as a member of a group. Community unity is tied to the oneness of the Lord.

The final return of all parties to GOD also reminds us that human division does not transfer final authority to human beings.

**Verse references:** 21:92; 21:93.

## 6. Every Faction Rejoicing in What It Has

### Verse

> **23:52** — Such  is  your  congregation—one congregation—and  I  am  your  Lord; you shall reverence Me. 
> 
> **23:53** — But they tore themselves into disputing factions; each party happy with what they have.

### Reflection on the Verse

23:52-53 repeats the same tension: one community, one Lord, then disputing factions with each party happy in what it possesses.

Belonging can feel powerful. History, scholars, language, or numbers can create confidence. But confidence is not verification.

This meets the question explored in *We Found Our Parents Doing This*: inheritance and belonging can be strong, but they cannot replace evidence.

**Verse references:** 23:52; 23:53.

## 7. The Call Not to Divide from Noah to Jesus

### Verse

> **42:13** — He decreed for you the same religion decreed for Noah, and what we inspired to you, and what we decreed for Abraham, Moses, and Jesus: “You shall uphold this one religion, and do not divide it.”
>
> Monotheists  vs.  Idol Worshipers
> The  idol  worshipers  will  greatly resent what you invite them to do. GOD  redeems  to  Himself  whomever He wills; He guides to Himself only those who totally submit.

### Reflection on the Verse

42:13 links Noah, Abraham, Moses, and Jesus to the command to uphold the one religion and not divide it.

Rather than turning messengers into competing identity symbols, the verse directs attention to the common call of submission they carried.

The command not to divide appears beside devotion to one GOD, showing the direct connection between unity and tawhid.

**Verse references:** 42:13.

## 8. Jealousy and Resentment After Knowledge

### Verse

> **42:14** — Ironically, they broke up into sects only after the knowledge had come to them, due to jealousy and resentment among themselves. If it were not  for  a  predetermined  decision from your Lord to respite them for a definite interim, they would have been  judged  immediately.  Indeed, the later generations who inherited the scripture are full of doubts.

### Reflection on the Verse

42:14 places division in a more disturbing location than ignorance: jealousy and resentment after knowledge had already arrived.

Not every religious dispute is therefore a pure search for truth. A person may think he is defending truth while status, rivalry, or superiority operate underneath.

I should use this verse to examine myself, not merely diagnose others: am I angry because truth is harmed, or because my position is challenged?

**Verse references:** 42:14.

## 9. Possessing Scripture and Submitting to It

### Verse

> **2:213** — The people used to be one community when GOD sent the prophets as bearers of good news, as well as warners. He sent down with them the scripture, bearing the truth, to judge among the people in their disputes. Ironically, those who received the scripture were the ones who rejected any new scripture, despite clear proofs given to them. This is due to jealousy on their part. GOD guides those who believe to the truth that is disputed by all others, in accordance with His will. GOD guides whoever wills in a straight path.*

### Reflection on the Verse

2:213 says that scripture was sent to judge disputes, yet people who possessed scripture could still dispute after clear proofs came.

This reveals the difference between possessing a text and submitting to it. A community can hold, recite, and speak in the name of the Quran while still placing the ego ahead of it.

The right question is not "Do we possess the Quran?" but "Can the Quran actually correct me?"

**Verse references:** 2:213.

## 10. People Were One Community, Then They Disputed

### Verse

> **10:19** — The  people used  to  be  one congregation, then they disputed. If it were not for a predetermined word from your Lord, they would have  been judged immediately regarding their disputes.

### Reflection on the Verse

10:19 says that people were once one community and later disputed. It does not provide an elaborate historical theory, but it clearly treats contention as something that arose.

More importantly, final judgment is delayed. That does not transfer GOD's authority to human beings.

My task is not to issue final verdicts on humanity, but to correct my own belief, speech, and conduct before revelation.

**Verse references:** 10:19.

## 11. The Point of Return in Disagreement

### Verse

> **4:59** — O  you  who  believe,  you  shall  obey GOD,  and  you  shall  obey  the  messenger,  and  those  in  charge  among you.  If  you  dispute  in  any  matter, you  shall  refer  it  to  GOD  and  the messenger,  if  you do  believe  in  GOD and  the  Last  Day.  This  is  better  for you,  and  provides  you  with  the  best solution.

### Reflection on the Verse

4:59 does not deny disagreement. It accepts that believers may dispute, but it establishes a direction of return: refer the matter to GOD and the messenger.

Those in charge are also mentioned, yet when dispute arises their personal authority is not made final. This reveals a limit on religious authority.

A community's safety lies not in an infallible leader, but in everyone—including leaders—remaining correctable before revelation.

**Verse references:** 4:59.

## 12. Examining All Words

### Verse

> **39:18** — They  are  the  ones  who  examine  all words,  then  follow  the  best.  These are  the  ones  whom  GOD  has  guided;  these  are  the  ones  who  possess intelligence.

### Reflection on the Verse

39:18 describes the guided as those who examine all words and follow the best. Seeking truth is therefore more than repeating what one was taught.

This makes absolute sectarian loyalty difficult, because absolute loyalty tends to accept a statement because of its source before evaluating its content.

39:18 keeps the responsibility of evaluation alive. The criterion cannot simply be the name of the group.

**Verse references:** 39:18.

## 13. The Ethics of Disagreement

### Verse

> **16:125** — You shall invite to the path of your Lord  with  wisdom  and  kind  enlightenment, and debate with them in the best possible manner. Your Lord knows best who has strayed from His path, and He knows best who are the guided ones.

### Reflection on the Verse

16:125 places even the defense of truth under ethical discipline: wisdom, gentle enlightenment, and debate in the best manner.

Believing that I am right does not authorize bad conduct. When I turn another person into a label to defeat, I move away from the verse's method.

The Lord knows best who has strayed and who is guided. That statement should restrain the pride of the debater.

**Verse references:** 16:125.

## 14. If Believers Are One Family

### Verse

> **49:10** — The believers are members of one family; you shall keep the peace within your family and reverence GOD, that you may attain mercy.

### Reflection on the Verse

49:10 describes believers as members of one family and commands the preservation of peace.

This does not require erasing every difference. A family can contain differences, while continuous contempt and hostility are something else.

In religious disagreement I should first see the other person as a responsible human being, not merely as a sectarian label.

**Verse references:** 49:10.

## 15. Unity Is Not Sameness in Every Detail

### Verse

> **5:48** — Then  we  revealed  to  you  this  scripture,  truthfully,  confirming  previous scriptures,  and  superseding  them. You  shall  rule  among  them  in  accordance  with  GOD’s  revelations, and do not follow their wishes if they differ  from  the  truth  that  came  to you. For each of you, we have decreed  laws  and  different  rites.  Had GOD willed, He could have  made you  one  congregation.  But  He  thus puts  you  to  the  test  through  the revelations  He  has  given  each  of you.  You  shall  compete  in  righteousness. To GOD is your final destiny—all  of  you—then  He  will  inform you of everything you had disputed.

### Reflection on the Verse

5:48 brings an essential balance. It says that different laws and rituals were decreed, that GOD could have made everyone one community, and then commands people to compete in righteousness.

Quranic unity therefore cannot easily be reduced to uniformity in every historical or practical detail.

Unity means keeping the source of truth, moral direction, and final return in their proper place; it is not coercive sameness.

**Verse references:** 5:48.

## 16. Not Handing Final Judgment to My Group

### Verse

> **6:159** — Those  who  divide  themselves  into sects do not belong with you. Their judgment  rests  with  GOD,  then  He will  inform  them  of  everything  they had done.
> 
> **5:48** — Then  we  revealed  to  you  this  scripture,  truthfully,  confirming  previous scriptures,  and  superseding  them. You  shall  rule  among  them  in  accordance  with  GOD’s  revelations, and do not follow their wishes if they differ  from  the  truth  that  came  to you. For each of you, we have decreed  laws  and  different  rites.  Had GOD willed, He could have  made you  one  congregation.  But  He  thus puts  you  to  the  test  through  the revelations  He  has  given  each  of you.  You  shall  compete  in  righteousness. To GOD is your final destiny—all  of  you—then  He  will  inform you of everything you had disputed.

### Reflection on the Verse

Read together, 6:159 and 5:48 establish a shared limit: disagreement is real, division is serious, but final judgment belongs to GOD.

A scholar, leader, community, or sect may claim to guide me, but none can take GOD's final judgment into its own possession.

Neither can I. Tawhid protects the center of authority: final judgment belongs to GOD, not to the group.

**Verse references:** 6:159; 5:48.

## 17. Can a Religious Label Be Evidence of Truth?

### Verse

> **30:32** — (Do  not  fall  in  idol  worship,)  like those  who  divide  their  religion  into sects;  each  party  rejoicing  with  what they have.
> 
> **39:18** — They  are  the  ones  who  examine  all words,  then  follow  the  best.  These are  the  ones  whom  GOD  has  guided;  these  are  the  ones  who  possess intelligence.

### Reflection on the Verse

Placing 30:32 beside 39:18 reveals two different postures. One produces certainty from belonging; the other keeps certainty open to examination.

A religious label may describe the tradition from which a person comes, but the label itself is not evidence of truth.

Identity itself may not be the problem. The problem begins when identity rules over truth.

**Verse references:** 30:32; 39:18.

## 18. Recognizing the Sect Within Myself

### Verse

> **3:103** — You  shall  hold  fast  to  the  rope  of GOD,  all  of  you,  and  do  not  be divided.  Recall  GOD’s  blessings upon  you—you  used  to  be  enemies and  He  reconciled  your  hearts.  By His  grace,  you  became  brethren. You were at the brink of a pit of fire, and  He  saved  you  therefrom.  GOD thus  explains  His  revelations  for you, that you may be guided.
> 
> **42:14** — Ironically, they broke up into sects only after the knowledge had come to them, due to jealousy and resentment among themselves. If it were not  for  a  predetermined  decision from your Lord to respite them for a definite interim, they would have been  judged  immediately.  Indeed, the later generations who inherited the scripture are full of doubts.
> 
> **16:125** — You shall invite to the path of your Lord  with  wisdom  and  kind  enlightenment, and debate with them in the best possible manner. Your Lord knows best who has strayed from His path, and He knows best who are the guided ones.

### Reflection on the Verse

Sectarianism may exist even without official sect names. When I create a sharp moral world of "people like us" and "them," excuse the faults of my side, and magnify every fault of the other, the same structure can live inside me.

3:103 calls me to hold to GOD's rope with others, 42:14 makes me examine jealousy and resentment, and 16:125 disciplines even the manner of debate.

Before speaking about unity I need to examine my own language: am I inviting people to truth, or merely to my side?

**Verse references:** 3:103; 42:14; 16:125.

## 19. Arabic Word Roots: Division, Religion, and Factions

### Verse

> **6:159** — Those  who  divide  themselves  into sects do not belong with you. Their judgment  rests  with  GOD,  then  He will  inform  them  of  everything  they had done.

### Reflection on the Verse

In 6:159, **فَرَّقُوا (farraqu)** is connected with the root **ف-ر-ق (f-r-q)**, carrying the field of separating and dividing.

**دِينَهُمْ (dinahum)** contains **د-ي-ن (d-y-n)**, associated with religion, judgment, recompense, and obligation. This cautions against reducing religion to a social label.

**شِيَعًا (shiya'an)** is connected with **ش-ي-ع (sh-y-')**, the field of parties and factions. Roots do not explain the verse by themselves, but they sharpen its warning against turning group belonging into the measure of religion.

**Verse references:** 6:159.

## Other Claims

Some traditional and historical interpretations apply 6:159 and related verses to particular later sects or historical schisms. Other readings treat them more generally as a warning against every factional division around revelation.

The verse itself does not name a later sect. It is therefore more cautious not to declare one historical group its exclusive target. What the text clearly supports is criticism of dividing the religion, forming factions, and rejoicing in factional possession.

## Conclusion

When I return to 6:159, I see that the question of sects is deeper than "Which sect is correct?" The verse moves the issue away from competing identities and toward the ownership of religion and the location of final judgment.

3:103 calls people to hold to GOD's rope together. 21:92 and 23:52 connect one community with one Lord. 42:13 commands that the religion not be divided, while 42:14 warns that jealousy and resentment can create division even after knowledge arrives. 5:48 calls people to compete in righteousness without erasing every difference.

My conclusion is threefold: **Religious identity cannot replace truth. Human religious authority cannot possess final judgment. Unity is not truthless agreement; it is remaining oriented toward the same Lord while justice, truthfulness, and openness to correction are preserved.**

The hardest question may be this: **When a verse conflicts with my group, which one am I trying to protect—my group, or the truth?**

GOD knows best.
