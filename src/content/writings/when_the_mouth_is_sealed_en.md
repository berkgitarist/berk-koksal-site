# When the Mouth Is Sealed: How Will a Human Being Bear Witness Against Himself?

**Author:** Berk KÖKSAL  
**Date:** September 30, 2026  
**Location:** Mersin

The central purpose of this article is to reflect on the verses together with other related verses and to explore, sincerely and deeply, what bearing witness to the oneness of GOD means in human life in terms of knowledge, justice, truthfulness, and submission.

Peace. Peace be upon you.

In my earlier article *The Weight of Testimony*, I reflected on testimony as something heavier than a sentence spoken by the mouth: it is tied to knowledge, justice, and the direction of a life. In *Do Not Accept Without Verifying*, I considered hearing, sight, and the brain not as passive tools but as faculties for which we are responsible. In this reflection, those two lines meet in a much more unsettling scene.

The mouth is sealed.

But testimony does not end.

The hands and feet begin to testify.

That scene leaves me with a difficult question: if one day my verbal defense is silenced and what I actually did can stand independently of the story I tell about myself, how wide is the distance today between my self-description and my life?

The related verses do not leave this theme with the body alone. Tongue, hands, feet, hearing, eyes, skins, records, words, the earth, and the human self all enter the field of testimony in different passages. The question therefore becomes deeper than curiosity about how organs will speak: **How much can a human being cover reality with his own narrative?**

## 1. When the Mouth Is Sealed: Does Defense End, or Does Reality Begin?

### Verse

> **36:65**
>
> ٱلْيَوْمَ نَخْتِمُ عَلَىٰٓ أَفْوَٰهِهِمْ وَتُكَلِّمُنَآ أَيْدِيهِمْ وَتَشْهَدُ أَرْجُلُهُم بِمَا كَانُوا۟ يَكْسِبُونَ
>
> On  that  day  we  will  seal  their mouths; their hands and feet will bear witness to everything they had done.
>
> O gün ağızlarını mühürleyeceğiz; elleri ve ayakları yapmış oldukları her şeye şahitlik edecek.

Source: <a href="https://kuranteyit.com/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">kuranteyit.com</a>
### Reflection on the Verse

What strikes me first in 36:65 is the separation between speech and testimony. A human being can speak, explain, justify, frame, and defend. We can describe the same event with different words, soften responsibility, emphasize intention, or distribute blame. In the scene established by the verse, however, that verbal capacity is suddenly closed: the mouths are sealed.

Yet reality does not disappear. The hands and feet bear witness to what was done.

For me, this creates a crucial distinction: **speaking and bearing witness are not the same thing.** A person may say many things about himself today, but not everything he says is identical with the truth. 36:65 removes final testimony from the person’s power of self-presentation. On that day the decisive issue is not how well the person explains himself, but what he actually did.

The immediate context also matters. In 36:60-61, the children of Adam are reminded of the covenant not to worship Satan and to worship GOD alone. Verse 36:54 states that no soul will be wronged in the least and that people will be repaid precisely for what they did. Seen together, bodily testimony is not merely a frightening image; it appears within responsibility, tawhid, and justice.

When I turn the verse toward myself, the questions become immediate. What do my hands serve? Where do my feet take me? Does my tongue confess one direction while my life bears witness to another?

Perhaps the most unsettling thought is not that the hands will speak one day. Perhaps it is that my hands already know what I do today.

**Verse references:** 36:54, 36:60-65.

## 2. When the Tongue Also Bears Witness: Do Our Words Carry a Record Beyond Us?

### Verse

> **24:24** — The  day  will  come  when  their  own tongues,  hands,  and  feet  will  bear witness to everything they had done.
> **24:25** — On  that  day,  GOD  will  requite  them fully  for  their  works,  and  they  will find out that GOD is the Truth.

### Reflection on the Verse

In 36:65 the mouths are sealed, while 24:24 says that the tongues themselves will bear witness. I do not want to manufacture a mechanical explanation for how these scenes relate. The text does not give one. What it does give is a deeper distinction: the faculty that is now used to construct a defense can itself become part of the testimony.

That makes the ethics of speech heavier. The tongue does not merely affect other people; it leaves a trace of who I am. If I accuse without evidence, repeat what I have not verified, conceal what I know to be true, or speak about GOD without knowledge, the words are not simply sounds that disappear.

Verse 24:25 immediately connects this testimony with full repayment and with recognizing that GOD is the Truth. The point is not humiliation for its own sake. The point is truth becoming manifest and the account being just.

In my earlier reflection on proclaiming without concealing truth, I considered the responsibility of keeping words tied to their source. Here that responsibility becomes more personal. When I say that I am carrying GOD’s word, am I truly serving truth, or am I bending words toward my own interests?

Testimony is not limited to a courtroom. A person can testify to his character every time he speaks.

**Verse references:** 24:24-25.

## 3. Hearing, Eyes, and Skins: Can a Human Being Hide from His Own Body?

### Verse

> **41:20** — Once they get there, their own hearing, eyes, and skins will bear witness to everything they had done.
> **41:21** — They will say to their skins, “Why did you bear witness against us?” They  will  reply,  “GOD  made  us speak up; He is the One who causes everything to speak. He is the One who created you the first time, and now  you  have  been  returned  to Him.”
> **41:22** — There is no way you can hide from your own hearing, your eyes, or your skins. In fact, you thought that GOD was unaware of much of what you do.

### Reflection on the Verse

41:20-22 is one of the most unsettling passages connected with this subject. Hearing, eyes, and skins bear witness. The people ask their own skins why they testified against them. The answer is direct: GOD caused them to speak; He is the One who causes everything to speak.

I find myself thinking about how naturally I imagine the self as separate from the body it uses. I say, “I looked,” but I looked with eyes. I say, “I listened,” but I listened through hearing. I say, “I did it,” but I acted through the body. The body is the silent companion of our earthly life. In these verses, that silent companion becomes a witness.

Verse 41:22 goes deeper. There is no way to hide from one’s own hearing, eyes, or skins. The verse says they thought GOD was unaware of much of what they did. That suggests to me that wrongdoing is not only an action problem; it can be fed by a false idea about GOD. If I imagine that some region of life is unseen, I may construct a different morality there.

Tawhid therefore reaches beyond statements of worship. To acknowledge GOD as the One GOD is also to live without inventing a private zone outside His knowledge. Human privacy is real and deserves respect; but human privacy is not independence from GOD’s awareness.

What I do where no one else sees may be among the clearest testimonies to what I actually believe.

**Verse references:** 41:20-22.

## 4. “Why Did You Bear Witness Against Us?”: When Reality Appears, Whom Do We Blame?

### Verse

> **41:21** — They will say to their skins, “Why did you bear witness against us?” They  will  reply,  “GOD  made  us speak up; He is the One who causes everything to speak. He is the One who created you the first time, and now  you  have  been  returned  to Him.”
> **41:23** — This  kind  of  thinking  about  your Lord will cause you to fall, and then you become losers.
> **41:24** — If they continue the way they are, Hell will be their destiny, and if they make up excuses, they will not be excused.

### Reflection on the Verse

The first reaction in 41:21 is remarkable: “Why did you bear witness against us?” The question is directed at the skins, almost as though the problem were not the deeds but their disclosure.

That reaction makes me think about my own defensive reflexes. When a criticism is true, I may still become angry with the person who voiced it. When evidence reveals something uncomfortable, I may resent the evidence. When a verse exposes a habit, I may resist the verse rather than the habit.

Verse 41:23 says that a certain way of thinking about the Lord leads to downfall. Verse 41:24 adds that excuses will not secure acceptance. The testimony scene is therefore not only a collection of external evidence. It is also a collapse of false assumptions: “This is unseen,” “This does not matter,” “I can explain it away,” “My intention will erase the act.”

I do not read this as a denial of repentance or correction in this life. The passage is speaking about the final disclosure. Its lesson for me today is different: when truth exposes me, is my first instinct to defend myself, or to submit to truth?

Submission can appear in a very simple sentence: “Here, I was wrong.”

**Verse references:** 41:21, 41:23-24.

## 5. The Human Being Will Be His Own Judge: The Distance Between Excuse and Reality

### Verse

> **75:14** — The  human  being  will  be  his  own judge.
> **75:15** — No excuses will be accepted.

### Reflection on the Verse

These two short verses are heavy enough to stand near the center of the entire reflection: the human being will be his own judge; excuses will not be accepted.

Being one’s own judge is not the same as living in constant self-condemnation. Endless guilt does not necessarily produce righteousness. What I see here is a state of clarity in which the person can no longer deny what is already known about himself.

That turns the final account into a present question. If I will not be able to escape myself then, why should I train myself to escape myself now? If excuses will eventually lose their power, why make excuse-making part of my character today?

Human beings protect themselves. That instinct can become moral blindness. I can minimize my own wrong and magnify the wrong of another. I can judge others by principles and myself by circumstances. I can create two standards without noticing.

When I read 75:14-15 with 4:135, justice begins inside the self. The command to bear witness equitably even against oneself removes the privilege I naturally want to grant myself.

Perhaps real inner peace does not come from continually proving myself right. Perhaps it comes from becoming honest enough that truth no longer feels like an enemy.

**Verse references:** 75:14-15; 4:135.

## 6. “Read Your Own Record”: The Account Is Not Handed Over to Someone Else’s Story

### Verse

> **17:13** — We have recorded the fate of every human being; it is tied to his neck. On the Day of Resurrection we will hand him a record that is accessible.
> **17:14** — Read your own record. Today, you suffice as your own reckoner.

### Reflection on the Verse

The phrase that stays with me in 17:13-14 is simple: “Read your own record.” The person is not presented with an incomprehensible file belonging to someone else. His own record is made accessible, and he is told that he suffices as his own reckoner.

That makes justice deeply personal. The person reads not what others did to him but what he did. Social reputation does not appear here as the decisive measure. Titles, group identity, and the praise of others do not replace the record.

This also gives me a boundary for religious authority. A teacher can teach, a scholar can research, a messenger can deliver, and a community can support. But none of them will read my record in my place. I cannot transfer the final weight of my responsibility to a leader, a scholar, a family tradition, or a religious institution.

Verse 17:15 immediately adds that no bearer of sins bears the sins of another. Individual responsibility is therefore not selfish individualism. It is the non-transferability of moral accountability.

Today I do not possess a perfect record of myself. Memory is selective. I forget. I reshape. But the verse gives me a direction: measure myself less by the story I prefer and more by the quality of what I actually do.

**Verse references:** 17:13-15.

## 7. A Record That Leaves Nothing Small or Large Uncounted

### Verse

> **18:49** — The record will be shown, and you will see the guilty fearful of its contents. They will say, “Woe to us. How come this book leaves nothing, small or large, without counting it?” They will find everything they had done brought forth. Your Lord is never unjust towards anyone.

### Reflection on the Verse

The fear in 18:49 is directed at the completeness of the record: it leaves nothing small or large without counting it. That makes me think about how easily we divide moral life into “serious” and “insignificant.” A small lie, a small injustice, a small humiliation, a small act of vanity, a small compromise for advantage — individually they can look too minor to matter.

Yet character is often formed by repeated small acts. A human being does not usually become another person in one moment. The habits of the tongue, the direction of the eyes, the use of money, the response to anger, and the decisions made when no one is watching accumulate into a path.

The final sentence of the verse is crucial: the Lord is never unjust toward anyone. The comprehensive record is not presented as arbitrary surveillance. It belongs to justice. That also means small good is not lost. Verse 99:7 makes this explicit.

This gives me responsibility and hope at the same time. I cannot dismiss small evil as nothing, but I also cannot dismiss small good as useless.

Much of the moral weight of life may gather in choices for which no one applauds.

**Verse references:** 18:49; 99:7-8.

## 8. When the Record Utters the Truth: Is Memory the Same as Reality?

### Verse

> **45:28** — You  will  see  every  community kneeling.  Every  community  will  be called  to  view  their  record.  Today, you  get  paid  for  everything  you  have done.
> **45:29** — This  is  our  record;  it  utters  the  truth about you. We have been recording everything you did.

### Reflection on the Verse

45:29 speaks of the record almost as a witness: it “utters the truth” about people. Everything they did was being recorded.

Human memory does not work that way. We emphasize some events and blur others. We can preserve details that protect our self-image and suppress details that disturb it. After years of retelling, it may become difficult to distinguish the event itself from the version of it we have learned to tell ourselves.

That is why “it utters the truth about you” is so powerful to me. The final measure is not my memory, my biography, or the reputation I accumulated. The record is not required to agree with my self-image.

Verse 45:28 also speaks of every community being called to its record. Collective identity does not dissolve individual action. Belonging to a respected group, inheriting a long tradition, or standing with a majority does not erase what was actually done.

A thought from *The Weight of Testimony* returns here: the weight of testimony is not merely knowing the correct sentence, but living in a way that does not contradict it. If the record will speak truth, behavior deserves more attention than the identities I claim for myself.

**Verse references:** 45:28-29.

## 9. Not a Single Utterance: The Invisible Responsibility of Speech

### Verse

> **50:17** — Two recording (angels), at right and at left, are constantly recording.
> **50:18** — Not  an  utterance  does  he  utter without an alert witness.

### Reflection on the Verse

50:18 is simple and severe: not a single utterance is made without an alert witness. The verse makes it difficult to treat words as weightless.

In ordinary life we say things “casually”: a joke, an angry remark, gossip, an insinuation, a report we never verified. Once spoken, it can feel as though it vanishes. The verse says speech is not unrecorded.

This becomes even more serious in religious speech. When speaking about GOD, revelation, another person’s faith, or someone’s intention, words carry an added ethical burden. 17:36 refuses unverified information; 2:42 refuses the mixing of truth with falsehood. Good intention cannot make an untrue statement true.

Sometimes the most truthful religious statement is simply, “I do not know.” Silence can be more faithful than pretending to possess knowledge. The note from 2:32 at the end of this article matters to me for this reason: acknowledging the boundary of knowledge is part of the ethics of testimony.

If speech is recorded, its value should come from truthfulness, not volume.

**Verse references:** 50:17-18; 17:36; 2:42.

## 10. Honest Recorders: Life Is Not Unrecorded When No Human Being Sees

### Verse

> **82:10** — Oblivious to the fact that there are (invisible) keepers around you.
> **82:11** — They are honest recorders.
> **82:12** — They record everything you do.

### Reflection on the Verse

82:10-12 speaks of invisible keepers and honest recorders: they record everything people do. That changes the meaning of being alone.

Solitude does not remove responsibility. If morality disappears when the human audience disappears, then perhaps the audience — not GOD — was governing the behavior. What I do when nobody is watching may reveal more than what I display in public.

I do not want to turn this into an unhealthy fear of surveillance. In the surrounding Quranic picture, recording serves justice. 36:54 says no soul will be wronged in the least. 18:49 says the Lord is never unjust toward anyone. Recording therefore also means that hidden good is not lost.

A private act of generosity, an unrecognized sacrifice, restraining anger, refusing an opportunity that would be wrong, or correcting oneself without public recognition — none of these becomes nothing because no human being saw it.

Deep reverence for GOD becomes concrete here. It is not a public performance. It is maintaining the measure even where visibility ends.

**Verse references:** 82:10-12; 36:54; 18:49.

## 11. The Earth Will Tell Its News: Does Place Also Enter the Field of Testimony?

### Verse

> **99:4** — On that day, it will tell its news. 
> **99:5** — That your Lord has commanded it. 
> **99:6** — On  that  day,  the  people  will  issue from  every  direction,  to  be  shown their works. 
> **99:7** — Whoever does an atom’s weight of good will see it. 
> **99:8** — And whoever does an atom’s  weight of evil will see it.

### Reflection on the Verse

99:4 says that the earth will tell its news. The passage then says people will come out to be shown their works and that an atom’s weight of good or evil will be seen.

The testimony theme now moves beyond the human body. Hands and feet in 36:65, hearing, eyes, and skins in 41:20, and now the earth in 99:4. What a person calls “hidden” becomes a smaller and smaller category.

I do not know, from these verses alone, how the earth tells its news. I do not need to invent a physical mechanism. The moral point is already profound: human actions do not occur in a void. They happen somewhere, leave consequences, and belong to a reality greater than our private narration.

Verses 99:7-8 also keep the reflection from collapsing into fear. An atom’s weight of good will be seen too. If I imagine only evil being recorded, I have seen only half of justice.

That is a protection against despair. A good act that appears too small to matter is not nothing. A wrong that appears too small to matter is not nothing either.

Testimony binds life not to spectacle but to measure.

**Verse references:** 99:4-8.

## 12. Even Against Yourselves: The Ethics of Testimony Today

### Verse

> **4:135** — O  you who  believe,  you  shall  be absolutely  equitable,  and  observe GOD,  when  you  serve  as  witnesses, even  against  yourselves,  or  your parents,  or  your  relatives.  Whether the  accused  is  rich  or  poor,  GOD takes  care  of  both.  Therefore,  do not  be  biased  by  your  personal wishes.  If  you  deviate  or  disregard (this  commandment),  then  GOD  is fully  Cognizant  of  everything  you do.

### Reflection on the Verse

36:65 presents a future scene of testimony. 4:135 tells us how testimony should work now: be absolutely equitable even when the testimony is against ourselves, our parents, or our relatives.

That connection matters deeply to me. The Quran does not leave the human being as a passive subject who will one day be testified against. It teaches us today to become people capable of telling the truth even against our own interests.

Justice is not hardest when nothing is at stake. It becomes difficult when love, family, status, desire, or self-protection enters the situation. The verse even places rich and poor under the same measure; social position must not bend testimony.

“Against yourselves” suggests a discipline I need now. If I learn to admit my wrong while correction is still possible, truth becomes less threatening. To say “I was wrong” does not destroy dignity. It can protect justice.

In *The Weight of Testimony*, I asked why knowledge and justice meet in the same moral field. Here the practical answer is clear: accurate knowledge becomes corrupted testimony when desire bends it.

Observing GOD may require refusing to grant myself a special exemption from the truth.

**Verse references:** 4:135.

## 13. Hearing, Eyesight, and the Brain: Faculties of Responsibility Today, Witnesses Tomorrow

### Verse

> **17:36** — You  shall  not  accept  any  information,  unless  you  verify  it  for  yourself. I  have  given  you  the  hearing,  the eyesight,  and  the  brain,  and  you  are responsible for using them.

### Reflection on the Verse

17:36 connects the future testimony scene to present responsibility for knowledge. Do not accept information unless you verify it for yourself; hearing, eyesight, and the brain are faculties for which the person is responsible.

The parallel with 41:20 is striking: hearing and eyes, which are given today as means of perception, appear there as witnesses. I do not want to derive a biological or metaphysical theory from that parallel. But the moral connection is strong: hearing is responsibility, seeing is responsibility, thinking is responsibility.

Accepting a claim because someone I admire said it, because many people repeat it, or because it agrees with what I already want to believe weakens that responsibility. Religious information is not exempt. A scholar, leader, app, artificial intelligence system, or search engine cannot replace my duty to use the faculties GOD gave me.

This does not require suspicion toward everyone. It requires disciplined trust. I may benefit from other people’s work while refusing to hand over my own accountability.

In *Do Not Accept Without Verifying*, I reflected on 17:36 as an ethic of information. Read alongside 36:65 and 41:20, another layer appears: faculties I am responsible for using today can appear within the testimony about my life tomorrow.

**Verse references:** 17:36; 41:20-22.

## 14. “We Bear Witness Against Ourselves”: Admission at the End of Denial

### Verse

> **6:130** — O  you  jinns  and  humans,  did  you  not receive  messengers  from  among you,  who  narrated  to  you  My  revelations,  and  warned  you  about  the meeting  of  this  day?  They  will  say, “We  bear  witness  against  ourselves.”  They  were  totally  preoccupied with  the  worldly  life,  and  they  will bear  witness  against  themselves  that they were disbelievers.
> **6:131** — This  is  to  show  that  your  Lord  never annihilates  any  community  unjustly, while its people are unaware.
> **6:132** — Everyone  will  attain  a  rank  commensurate  with  their  deeds.  Your Lord  is  never  unaware  of  anything they do.

### Reflection on the Verse

In 6:130, jinns and humans say, “We bear witness against ourselves.” The verse adds that they were totally preoccupied with worldly life. That makes me think that one of the great forms of blindness may simply be attention captured in the wrong direction.

People do not always reject truth by loudly denying it. Sometimes life becomes so full of money, status, entertainment, fear, identity, argument, and urgency that truth receives no serious attention. The warning may be heard without being allowed to become weighty.

Verse 6:131 immediately states that GOD does not destroy a community unjustly while its people are unaware. Testimony and justice are again connected. The testimony is not an excuse for arbitrary punishment; it confirms awareness, warning, and responsibility.

Verse 6:132 says everyone attains a degree commensurate with their deeds. Collective identity cannot absorb the individual. Family, community, or religious environment does not erase the moral weight of what a person actually does.

The question I have to bring back to the present is this: if there is something about which I would one day bear witness against myself, can I recognize it now, while correction is still possible?

Reflection on the final account becomes useful only when it opens the door to present reform.

**Verse references:** 6:130-132.

## 15. “We Were Not Aware”: Testimony and Personal Responsibility

### Verse

> **7:172** — Recall that your Lord summoned all the descendants of Adam, and had them bear witness for themselves: “Am I not your Lord?” They all said, “Yes. We bear witness.” Thus, you cannot say on the Day of Resurrection, “We were not aware of this.”
> **7:173** — Nor can you say, “It was our parents who  practiced  idolatry,  and  we simply followed in their footsteps. Will You punish us because of what others have innovated?” 

### Reflection on the Verse

7:172-173 closes two excuses side by side: “We were not aware,” and “It was our parents who practiced idolatry; we merely followed them.”

These verses bring testimony directly into tawhid. Human beings are connected with bearing witness concerning their Lord, and inherited idolatry is not presented as a complete escape from responsibility.

In my previous reflection *We Found Our Parents Doing This: Can Inherited Belief Be Evidence of Truth?*, I considered the earthly form of this second excuse. Here the question moves further: another person’s belief cannot become my proof, and another person’s mistake cannot automatically become my innocence.

This also sets a limit on religious authority. Teachers can be valuable, but they cannot carry my testimony in my place. The age of a tradition, the size of a community, or the prestige of its scholars cannot erase personal accountability.

Tawhid therefore becomes more than the sentence “GOD is one.” It determines where final accountability belongs. If final accountability is to GOD, no human authority can occupy His place or replace revelation and personal responsibility.

Testimony does not abandon the human being, but it does not allow him to abandon his responsibility to someone else.

**Verse references:** 7:172-173.

## 16. Concealing the Truth: The Mouth Can Be Open and Yet the Human Being Can Still Be Silent

### Verse

> **2:42** — Do  not  confound  the  truth  with falsehood,  nor  shall  you  conceal  the truth, knowingly.

### Reflection on the Verse

In 36:65 the mouth is sealed. In 2:42 the mouth is still open, and the command is not to confound truth with falsehood or knowingly conceal truth.

That contrast is meaningful to me. If I reflect on a future moment when the ability to speak is removed, I should also reflect on what the present ability to speak is for. If the mouth is open now, do I use it to carry truth faithfully, or to blur truth until it serves me?

Concealing truth does not have to mean complete silence. The verse also warns against mixing truth with falsehood. A person may present part of the truth and insert something false into it, producing a statement that looks reliable while its direction has changed.

Religious reflection needs special discipline here. If I quote the verse accurately but then present my own inference as though the verse itself said it, have I blurred the distinction? If I hide a translation difference, or treat a beloved religious figure’s statement as binding like revelation, am I keeping truth and human addition clearly separated?

Truthful speech is not only avoiding lies. It is knowing how to distinguish what the verse says from what I infer from it.

Before the mouth is sealed, the responsibility of the mouth is to refuse concealment, refuse confusion, and refuse certainty where knowledge is absent.

**Verse references:** 2:42; 17:36.

## 17. No Soul Will Be Wronged in the Least: Justice at the Center of Testimony

### Verse

> **36:54** — On that day, no soul will be wronged in the least. You will be paid precisely for whatever you did.

### Reflection on the Verse

If I read 36:65 in isolation, bodily testimony can remain only a frightening image. But 36:54, in the same passage, says that no soul will be wronged in the least and that people will be paid precisely for what they did.

I therefore cannot separate testimony from justice. Evidence without justice can become an instrument of oppression. In the Quranic scene, the record, bodily testimony, and self-accounting come together within a declaration that there will be no injustice.

That gives me a measure for present judgment. If I judge people through rumor, anger, loyalty, or self-interest, I have learned little from the picture of the final account. 4:135 demands equitable testimony even against oneself. 17:36 refuses unverified information.

The connection with tawhid is strong. If final judgment belongs to GOD, my task is not to play GOD over other people. I cannot know human intentions with certainty. I cannot issue the final verdict on hearts. Remaining within what is known, evidenced, and clearly revealed is safer for both knowledge and justice.

Reflection on the day when mouths are sealed should not make me eager to condemn others. It should make my own testimony more just.

**Verse references:** 36:54-65; 4:135; 17:36.

## 18. The Human Being Himself Bears Witness to the Fact: Who Is the Nearest Witness?

### Verse

> **100:6** — The  human  being  is  unappreciative of his Lord. 
> **100:7** — He bears witness to this fact. 
> **100:8** — He loves material things excessively. 
> **100:9** — Does he not realize that the day will come when the graves are opened? 
> **100:10** — And all secrets are brought out. 
> **100:11** — They  will  find out,  on  that  day,  that their  Lord  has  been  fully  Cognizant of them.

### Reflection on the Verse

100:7 says that the human being bears witness to the fact of his own ingratitude. The passage then moves to excessive love of material things, the opening of graves, and the exposure of what is hidden inside.

That makes me think that we often know more about ourselves than we admit. We may say “I did not know,” when the more accurate statement is that we did not name what we already sensed. We feel the discomfort of an unjust word. We recognize when a choice was driven mainly by self-interest. Yet we can build a more convenient explanation around it.

The verse does not offer a detailed theory of conscience, but it does show that the human being is not completely foreign to his own condition. Verse 100:10 then speaks of secrets being brought out. The distance between inner and outer collapses.

So I return to the opening question: how will a human being bear witness against himself? The verses do not give only one answer. Hands, feet, tongues, hearing, eyes, skins, records, the earth, recording witnesses, and the human self all appear in different passages. I should not force them into a single mechanical theory. Their common direction is already enough: reality is not imprisoned inside the story I tell about myself.

The best preparation, then, is not to design a better future defense. It is to live today with less need for one.

**Verse references:** 100:6-11; 36:65; 75:14-15.

## 19. Arabic Word Roots: Seal, Testimony, Speech, and Reckoning

### Verse

> **36:65** — On  that  day  we  will  seal  their mouths; their hands and feet will bear witness to everything they had done.

### Reflection on the Verse

Several words in the Arabic of the central verse help me see its internal movement more carefully. The aim is not to replace the verse with a new interpretation, but to notice relationships already present in its language.

**نَخْتِمُ — nakhtimu** is connected with the root **خ ت م (kh-t-m)**, carrying the field of sealing and closing. In 36:65 the sealing of the mouths closes the ordinary channel of verbal defense. The verse does not explain a physical mechanism; at the level of the scene, however, control of speech and the act of testimony are separated.

**تَشْهَدُ — tashhadu** comes from **ش ه د (sh-h-d)**, associated with witnessing and being present as a witness. The same root appears in 4:135 for equitable testimony, in 6:130 for bearing witness against oneself, in 7:172 for bearing witness concerning oneself, and in 41:20 for the testimony of the senses. The recurrence makes testimony feel larger than courtroom language: it links knowledge, presence, and responsibility.

**تُكَلِّمُنَا — tukallimuna** is connected with **ك ل م (k-l-m)** and the semantic field of speaking. In 36:65 the hands “speak to us.” In 41:21, **أَنطَقَنَا — antaqana**, from **ن ط ق (n-t-q)**, appears when the skins say that GOD caused them to speak. Testimony is therefore not reduced to ordinary voluntary human speech; the power of GOD stands behind the scene.

**يَكْسِبُونَ — yaksibun** comes from **ك س ب (k-s-b)**, carrying the sense of acquiring or earning. The hands and feet testify to what people had “earned.” The expression suggests that actions do not merely pass away; they become connected with consequences acquired by the person. I would not push the word beyond what the verse supports, but its moral weight is clear.

In 41:20, **سَمْع — sam‘** belongs to hearing, while **أَبْصَار — absar** relates to **ب ص ر (b-s-r)** and sight. The same **b-s-r** root appears in 75:14 through **بَصِيرَةٌ — basirah**, in the authorized translation rendered as the human being being his own judge. The language creates an interesting proximity between seeing and seeing oneself clearly.

In 17:14, **حَسِيبًا — hasiban** is connected with **ح س ب (h-s-b)**, the field of reckoning and account. “You suffice as your own reckoner” directs attention away from an alien accusation and toward confrontation with one’s own record.

In 75:15, **مَعَاذِيرَهُ — ma‘adhirahu** is connected with **ع ذ ر (‘-dh-r)**, the field of excuse. The authorized translation gives the verse as “No excuses will be accepted.” The root observation therefore returns us to the same central movement: rhetorical escape loses its power before testimony.

Taken together, the central verse moves through a striking sequence: **sealed mouth, speaking hands, witnessing feet, and deeds that have been earned.** The scene is not built around argument. It is built around a life becoming manifest.

**Verse references:** 36:65; 41:20-22; 75:14-15; 17:14; 4:135; 6:130; 7:172.

## Other Claims

Different interpretations have been proposed concerning these verses. Some readings understand the testimony of hands, feet, skins, and other organs as a real and direct act of speech. This reading often points especially to 41:21, where the skins say that GOD caused them to speak.

Other approaches emphasize the evidentiary force of the scene: the body’s testimony communicates that nothing a person has done can remain concealed in the final account. In this reading, the central emphasis is total disclosure rather than a proposed physical mechanism.

Modern readers sometimes compare these passages with bodily memory, physical traces of behavior, or technological forms of recording. Such comparisons may be intellectually suggestive, but the verses themselves do not specify biological memory, digital storage, or any particular scientific mechanism. Those ideas therefore should not be presented as though they were the Quran’s explicit explanation.

My reflection does not depend on deciding among these claims. What the verses state openly is already sufficient: a person’s verbal defense will not define final reality; deeds are not lost; the body, the record, and the self appear within testimony; and the account takes place without injustice.

## Conclusion

I began with the question, “When the mouth is sealed, how will a human being bear witness against himself?” At first my attention went toward the strange image of the body speaking. As I considered the verses together, however, the question changed.

The central issue may not be the mechanism by which a hand or skin will speak in the future. The central issue is that the story I tell about my life is not identical with the truth of my life.

36:65 says mouths will be sealed and hands and feet will testify. 24:24 includes tongues. 41:20-22 adds hearing, eyes, and skins. 17:13-14 says the person will read his own record. 18:49 says the record leaves nothing small or large uncounted. 45:29 says the record utters the truth. 75:14 says the human being will be his own judge. 99:4 says the earth will tell its news. 100:7 says the human being himself bears witness to the fact.

All of this converges on one point: **reality does not depend on my ability to narrate myself.**

That thought calls me first not to fear but to truthfulness. If excuses will one day lose their power, I can choose correction over excuse today. If I will read my own record, I can care about what I am placing into it now. If hearing and sight are faculties for which I am responsible, I can practice verification. If equitable testimony may have to be against myself, I can learn to admit my own wrong while change remains possible.

Tawhid also becomes concrete here. GOD is not merely the One I say I worship. He is the Lord who knows what is concealed and declared, owns the final judgment, and wrongs no one. Human approval cannot save me from my own record. Religious prestige cannot erase personal accountability. The majority’s opinion about me cannot replace truth.

The strongest sentence this reflection leaves in me is therefore this:

**If one day my mouth will be sealed, then while it is open today I should speak truthfully. If one day my hands and feet will testify, I should try to use them today in ways I will not need to defend before GOD.**

The healthiest response to a future in which I may bear witness against myself is to become more honest with myself now.

GOD knows best.
