# [44:10–16] The Smoke Retribution: A Warning from the Sky, a Return to Old Ways

*A Nineteen-Section Reflection on Quran 44:10–16*

**Author:** Berk KÖKSAL

**Date:** October 16, 2026

**Place:** Mersin

### Verse

> **44:10**

> فَٱرْتَقِبْ يَوْمَ تَأْتِى ٱلسَّمَآءُ بِدُخَانٍ مُّبِينٍ

> Therefore,  watch  for  the  day  when the sky brings a profound smoke.*

> Bu nedenle, gökyüzünün esaslı bir duman getireceği günü gözle.*

> Source: <a href="https://kuranteyit.com/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">kuranteyit.com</a>

## Introduction

**Peace. Peace be upon you.**

It is not easy to picture a day when the sky darkens. And if the account is not of an ordinary cloud but of smoke that envelops people and brings painful retribution, the mind immediately starts supplying images. Which country? Which year? What kind of catastrophe? What could happen in the sky? Those questions interest me too.

Yet when I read 44:10–16 as a whole, something even more unsettling appears. There is smoke, a prayer for the retribution to be removed, an enlightening messenger who had already come and been rejected, a temporary easing of the retribution, and the warning that people will return to their old ways. So the passage is not merely about an event in the atmosphere. Why might a human being return to the old life even after a devastating warning?

I am curious about the physical cause of the smoke. But I do not want to turn a hypothetical asteroid, a date for a war, a chemical mechanism, or the certain fate of a community into a divine statement when the verse has not supplied those details. This matters especially here because Rashad Khalifa associated his understanding of the smoke with identifiable events. Reading his original writings from June 1986 and March 1990 alongside each other made the need to distinguish a person's interpretation from a verse unmistakable.

This reflection takes Rashad Khalifa’s Authorized English Translation as its textual basis, with the Turkish translation of the Authorized English Translation for the Turkish edition. His personal interpretations will have their own section, with dates and links to their sources. A question I explored in my earlier <a href="https://berkkoksal.com/writings/tanriya-derin-saygi-ego-saygisizlik-siniri-asmak/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">reflection on reverence, ego, and transgressing limits</a> remains with me: Even when I care deeply about what is right, can I turn a warning into an instrument for enlarging my own authority?

I want this study to deepen responsibility rather than intensify fear for its own sake. The calendar of the future is not in my hands. My choices today are.

## 1. The Smoke from the Sky: The Weight of the First Sentence

### Verse

> **44:10** — Therefore,  watch  for  the  day  when the sky brings a profound smoke.*

> **44:11** — It will envelope the people; this is a painful retribution.

### Reflection on the Verse

When I read a verse, sometimes the image reaches me before the command does. In 44:10, the sky is to bring smoke, and the reader is told to watch for that day. But if I isolate the image, I diminish the passage. The next verse speaks of people being enveloped and of painful retribution. This is not a spectacular landscape drawn for our entertainment. It is a warning addressed to human beings.

The word that makes me stop is “watch.” I can readily imagine an enormous dark sky. I cannot identify from the wording of the verse the physical cause, the first place to be affected, or a date on my calendar. Accepting the warning does not require inventing answers to questions the text has not answered.

Rashad Khalifa’s Authorized English Translation says “profound smoke.” The Turkish translation of the Authorized English Translation says “esaslı bir duman.” Both communicate gravity, but I should not turn an adjective into a scientific mechanism. Sometimes respect for a statement begins with recognizing what it leaves unsaid.

And so my question changes: Do I chiefly want to know how smoke will arrive, or do I want to understand how to live before the warning becomes an experience?

**Verse connections:** 44:10, 44:11.

## 2. Before the Smoke: Doubt and Heedlessness

### Verse

> **44:8** — There  is  no  other  god  beside  Him. He  controls  life  and  death;  your Lord  and  the  Lord  of  your  ancestors. 

> **44:9** — Indeed, they are doubtful, heedless.

> **44:10** — Therefore,  watch  for  the  day  when the sky brings a profound smoke.*

### Reflection on the Verse

The smoke does not appear without context. Immediately beforehand, the passage speaks of GOD's authority over life and death, followed by a description of doubt and heedlessness. I find this order revealing. First, who governs existence; then, how human beings respond to that knowledge; then, the warning.

Heedlessness need not always sound like a defiant denial. It may look like a calendar crowded with ordinary necessities. I wake, work, fix what goes wrong, and make arrangements for next week. Although I control neither life nor death, I slowly behave as though my own plans were the final structure of reality. Verse 44:8 remains in my memory, but loses weight in the way I live.

I do not want to confuse honest inquiry with heedlessness. Asking questions and testing claims are not sins merely because they are questions. Still, the combination of doubt and heedlessness in 44:9 challenges me to examine my motives: Am I genuinely open to the truth, or am I searching for an excuse to postpone responding to it?

The smoke makes me look upward. The verses before it make me look at my ordinary day.

**Verse connections:** 44:8, 44:9, 44:10.

## 3. “It Will Envelop the People”: Who Determines Its Reach?

### Verse

> **44:10** — Therefore,  watch  for  the  day  when the sky brings a profound smoke.*

> **44:11** — It will envelope the people; this is a painful retribution.

> **6:47** — Say,  “What  if  GOD’s  retribution came  to  you  suddenly,  or  after  an announcement,  is  it  not  the  wicked who incur annihilation?”

### Reflection on the Verse

The sentence in 44:11 is frightening. Yet its force does not authorize me to pronounce the final fate of a named country, ethnicity, or living individual. The verse does not provide a map with borders marked for punishment. To introduce my political anger into that sentence would be to add something of my own.

Verse 6:47 asks us to consider retribution coming suddenly or following an announcement. Alongside 44:10–11, that sharpens my awareness of warning and consequence. It still does not supply a geographical forecast. Before I try to describe what will happen to others, I need to examine my own response to the warning.

Being enveloped challenges my instinct to regard myself as fully protected. Wealth, knowledge, medicine, and technology matter, but they are not absolute guarantees. At the same time, I do not take the verse as an invitation to abandon reasonable precautions. Nor does it make every modern disaster proof that the predicted smoke has arrived.

The limits of my knowledge do not make the warning weaker. They keep my understanding of it honest.

**Verse connections:** 44:10, 44:11, 6:47.

## 4. Painful Retribution Is Not a Spectacle

### Verse

> **44:11** — It will envelope the people; this is a painful retribution.

> **6:131** — This  is  to  show  that  your  Lord  never annihilates  any  community  unjustly, while its people are unaware.

> **5:8** — O  you  who  believe,  you  shall  be  absolutely  equitable,  and  observe GOD,  when  you  serve  as  witnesses. Do  not  be  provoked  by  your  conflicts  with  some  people  into  committing  injustice.  You  shall  be  absolutely  equitable,  for  it  is  more  righteous. You  shall  observe  GOD.  GOD  is fully  Cognizant  of  everything  you do.

### Reflection on the Verse

I cannot read “painful” as a cinematic effect. It refers to an experience involving people who can be afraid, injured, bereaved, or in need of assistance. If my reflection becomes little more than the choreography of catastrophe, I may have lost sight of the moral seriousness of what I am reading.

Verse 6:131 insists that GOD does not annihilate a community unjustly while it is unaware. Verse 5:8 tells believers not to let conflicts provoke them into injustice. These verses prevent me from looking at victims of a calamity and casually announcing that they deserved it. A natural event must still be investigated honestly, and people affected by it must be treated justly.

Seeking justice is not the same as celebrating another person's suffering. GOD knows the final accountability of individuals; I do not possess a device for weighing souls. But I can ask whether I speak truthfully, refuse to spread alarming falsehoods, help when help is possible, and resist turning human pain into evidence for my preferred theory.

Perhaps the first test in reading a passage about retribution is whether I can retain my humanity while taking the warning seriously.

**Verse connections:** 44:11, 6:131, 5:8.

## 5. “Our Lord, Relieve This Retribution”: A Prayer Born in Fear

### Verse

> **44:12** — “Our  Lord,  relieve  this  retribution for us; we are believers.”

> **10:12** — When  adversity  touches  the  human being,  he  implores  us  while  lying down,  or  sitting,  or  standing  up.  But as  soon  as  we  relieve  his  adversity, he  goes  on  as  if  he  never  implored us  to  relieve  any  hardship!  The works  of  the  transgressors  are  thus adorned in their eyes.

### Reflection on the Verse

In 44:12, a human voice breaks through the overwhelming image. Those undergoing retribution declare that they believe and ask for relief. The next verse raises the question of how late this remembrance has come. Yet I find it difficult to dismiss the terror in that plea, because 10:12 describes something uncomfortably familiar.

When hardship arrives, we call on GOD lying down, sitting, and standing. When relief comes, we may return to life as though the hardship and the prayer had never existed. I previously explored this uneasy pattern in my <a href="https://berkkoksal.com/writings/had-the-retribution-come-immediately-en/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">reflection on mercy and the time granted to us</a>. A reckoning postponed while I have freedom to respond carries a different meaning from words forced out of me when escape is impossible.

I cannot confidently say, “I would never forget.” Forgetfulness rarely arrives as a dramatic choice. Sometimes it is the gradual accumulation of comfortable mornings. So I ask myself: When I pray for relief, do I also want the life that brought me here to change?

One of the most meaningful prayers inspired by the smoke may be the truthful prayer that can be offered before there is any smoke.

**Verse connections:** 44:12, 10:12.

## 6. Saying “We Believe” and Actually Changing

### Verse

> **44:12** — “Our  Lord,  relieve  this  retribution for us; we are believers.”

> **16:53** — Any  blessing  you  enjoy  is  from GOD. Yet, whenever you incur any adversity you immediately complain to Him.

> **16:54** — Yet, as soon as He relieves your affliction, some of you revert to idol worship.

### Reflection on the Verse

The real trial may begin immediately after the danger has passed. Verse 16:53 recognizes GOD as the source of every blessing, and 16:54 observes that some return to idol worship when their distress is relieved. This makes the declaration of belief in 44:12 feel incomplete without the question of what follows.

None of these verses empowers me to look inside someone else's heart. I should not pronounce that a particular person's prayer in a crisis was dishonest. I can, however, examine the distance between my own promises and decisions. Have I pledged to abandon wrongdoing, then quietly taken it up again at the first opportunity? Have I turned to GOD in need and later treated created things as the center of my life?

I do not want to require a spectacular emergency before practicing submission. The verses make ordinary steadiness look deeply significant. To remain grateful, fair, and faithful when the day is uneventful may be harder than making a solemn promise when the world is shaking.

I understand the desire for the smoke to clear. But will I preserve the moral clarity that the moment revealed?

**Verse connections:** 44:12, 16:53, 16:54.

## 7. An Enlightening Messenger: Warning Before Retribution

### Verse

> **44:13** — Now  that  it  is  too  late,  they  remember!  An  enlightening  messenger  had come to them.*

> **17:15** — Whoever is guided, is guided for his own good, and whoever goes astray does so to his own detriment. No sinner will bear the sins of anyone else. We never punish without first sending a messenger.

> **26:208** — We  never  annihilate  any  community without sending warners.

> **26:209** — Therefore,  this  is  a  reminder,  for  we are never unjust.

### Reflection on the Verse

In 44:13, the messenger enters the scene in the past tense: one had already come. Verse 17:15 states that retribution does not come before a messenger is sent. Verses 26:208–209 similarly connect warning with the absence of injustice. I find this sequence morally important.

A warning means that an interval for response has been offered. Before the final consequence, a person may hear, evaluate, correct a wrong, and change direction. In that sense, the messenger's arrival is not simply an announcement of danger. It is also an opening for accountable choice. There is a relationship here between justice and mercy.

But these sentences alone do not give me permission to assign a named identity to the messenger in 44:13 with unquestionable certainty. The verse does not identify him by name. Rashad Khalifa offers a particular interpretation of that identity, which I will examine in its own section, with the reasons he provided. I first need to preserve what the verse explicitly says: a messenger had come, and he was met with a response.

What happens when guidance reaches me before circumstances make it impossible to ignore?

**Verse connections:** 44:13, 17:15, 26:208, 26:209.

## 8. “Well Educated, but Crazy!”: Attacking the Person Instead of Examining the Message

### Verse

> **44:14** — But  they  turned  away  from  him,  saying, “Well educated, but crazy!”

> **49:6** — O you who believe, if a wicked person brings any news to you, you shall first investigate, lest you commit injustice towards some people, out of ignorance, then become sorry and remorseful for what you have done.

> **17:36** — You  shall  not  accept  any  information,  unless  you  verify  it  for  yourself. I  have  given  you  the  hearing,  the eyesight,  and  the  brain,  and  you  are responsible for using them.

### Reflection on the Verse

The reaction described in 44:14 gives me pause. Rather than engaging carefully with the message, the opponents dismiss the messenger with a personal insult. That temptation is familiar: a difficult argument can be avoided by humiliating the one who presents it.

Yet I must not make the verse a certificate proving that everyone who is mocked must therefore be right. Ridicule does not establish truth. Verses 49:6 and 17:36 point us toward investigating information and verifying what we accept. Honest inquiry should be neither a personal attack nor an unquestioning acceptance of human authority.

I write and publish reflections myself. If every disagreement makes me imagine that I occupy the exact position of the messenger in this passage, I turn revelation into a shield for my ego. Conversely, when listening to another person, credentials, reputation, and how strange a message initially sounds cannot replace examining the evidence.

The claim must be considered. The evidence must be tested. Human dignity should remain intact. It is possible to preserve all three responsibilities at once.

**Verse connections:** 44:14, 49:6, 17:36.

## 9. Temporary Relief: The Quiet Return to Old Ways

### Verse

> **44:15** — We  will  relieve  the  retribution  for awhile; you will soon revert.

> **7:95** — Then  we  substituted  peace  and prosperity  in  place  of  that  hardship. But  alas,  they  turned  heedless  and said,  “It  was  our  parents  who  experienced  that  hardship  before prosperity.”  Consequently,  we  punished  them  suddenly  when  they  least expected.

> **10:12** — When  adversity  touches  the  human being,  he  implores  us  while  lying down,  or  sitting,  or  standing  up.  But as  soon  as  we  relieve  his  adversity, he  goes  on  as  if  he  never  implored us  to  relieve  any  hardship!  The works  of  the  transgressors  are  thus adorned in their eyes.

### Reflection on the Verse

Of all the sentences in this passage, 44:15 may be the closest to my daily experience. Retribution is relieved, yet a return to former behavior is anticipated. I may never have witnessed the smoke described in this sura, but I know something about the strange human capacity to forget a promise as soon as life becomes comfortable again.

Verse 7:95 describes prosperity replacing hardship and people interpreting the change as little more than an ordinary historical cycle. Verse 10:12 finds the same pattern in an individual. Together they point to a disturbing possibility: the crisis may end without the inner life changing at all.

Reflection cannot be limited to making an emotional decision while afraid. I need to see what remains the next morning. If I ask forgiveness without repairing a wrong, promise honesty without correcting a misleading claim, or speak of gratitude while disregarding those I have harmed, the opening created by hardship may already have closed.

Relief makes me reflect on mercy. The prediction of relapse reminds me that mercy does not remove responsibility.

**Verse connections:** 44:15, 7:95, 10:12.

## 10. “The Big Stroke”: A Reckoning Beyond My Calendar

### Verse

> **44:16** — The  day  we  strike  the  big  stroke,  we will avenge.

> **7:97** — Did  the  people  of  the  present  communities  guarantee  that  our  retribution  will  not  come  to  them  in  the night as they sleep?

> **7:98** — Did  the  people  of  today’s  communities  guarantee  that  our  retribution  will  not  come  to  them  in  the daytime while they play?

> **7:99** — Have  they  taken  GOD’s  plans  for granted?  None  takes  GOD’s  plans for granted except the losers.

### Reflection on the Verse

Verse 44:16 ends the warning with severe words. I cannot read them casually. But before constructing a date or identifying a specific modern event with that stroke, I have to admit what the verse has not told me. I find a more reliable direction in 7:97–99, which questions the security we assume while asleep or occupied with leisure.

An ordinary day can change without our consent. The first thing to collapse may be our illusion of control. This does not mean that all preparation is pointless, or that practical safety measures are somehow unspiritual. It means that preparation for accountability cannot be replaced with supplies, calculations, or an impressive prediction.

Fear can also be used to control people. If I place the threat of the smoke behind every demand I make, I can turn a warning pointing toward GOD into a tool for enlarging my own authority. Verse 7:99 warns against taking GOD's plans for granted, not against thinking independently about the claims of human beings.

The responsible question is not merely “What year?” It is “What am I doing with the day I have?”

**Verse connections:** 44:16, 7:97, 7:98, 7:99.

## 11. One Arabic Word, Two Contexts: Smoke in Creation and Retribution

### Verse

> **41:11** — Then  He  turned  to  the  sky,  when  it was still gas, and said to it, and to the earth,  “Come  into  existence,  willingly  or  unwillingly.”  They  said, “We come willingly.”

> **44:10** — Therefore,  watch  for  the  day  when the sky brings a profound smoke.*

> **44:11** — It will envelope the people; this is a painful retribution.

### Reflection on the Verse

One of the most instructive connections in this study is 41:11. The Arabic **دُخَانٌ** there belongs to the same word family as **دُخَانٍ** in 44:10. Yet Rashad Khalifa’s Authorized English Translation renders the word as “gas” in 41:11 and “smoke” in 44:10. The Turkish translation of the Authorized English Translation likewise has “gaz” and “duman.” A shared Arabic term can function differently within different sentences.

Verse 41:11 concerns the sky in the context of creation. Verses 44:10–11 concern a painful retribution that will envelop people. These are distinct settings. I cannot therefore say that every occurrence of this Arabic word describes a punishment. Nor may I use the cosmological context of 41:11 as a shortcut to proving the physical cause of the event in 44:10.

The connection matters precisely because it imposes discipline. Comparing verses is valuable, but a lexical coincidence is not an all-purpose explanatory key. We must consider what each verse says before combining them into an answer.

Sometimes research does not remove the mystery. It teaches me to stop inventing certainty where the wording gives none.

**Verse connections:** 41:11, 44:10, 44:11.

## 12. The Turn to Pharaoh: A Story Following the Smoke

### Verse

> **44:16** — The  day  we  strike  the  big  stroke,  we will avenge.

> **44:17** — We  have  tested  before  them  the people  of  Pharaoh;  an  honorable messenger went to them.

> **44:18** — Proclaiming:  “Listen  to  me,  servants of GOD. I am an honest messenger to you.”

> **44:19** — And,  “Do  not  transgress  against GOD.  I  bring  to  you  powerful proofs.

### Reflection on the Verse

Immediately after the smoke and the big stroke, the passage turns to the people of Pharaoh. That movement seems worth examining. First we hear of an enlightening messenger who was rejected; then of an honorable messenger who describes himself as trustworthy and who brings powerful proofs. The neighboring narratives illuminate each other.

But the story of Pharaoh is not a numerical key that tells me the year or place of the smoke. The more cautious lesson is that holding power does not establish that a community is in the right, and that warnings are not necessarily ordinary arguments to be exhausted and forgotten.

The command in 44:19 not to transgress against GOD particularly strikes me. In times of fear it is easy to concentrate on what threatens us, and harder to notice where our own actions cross a boundary. That possibility applies in moments of prosperity, anger, confidence, and public disagreement.

Taking a messenger seriously should not mean yielding the mind to blind allegiance. It can mean accepting the responsibility to hear the explanation without hiding behind arrogance.

**Verse connections:** 44:16, 44:17, 44:18, 44:19.

## 13. Demanding Retribution: Can a Challenge Control Reality?

### Verse

> **29:53** — They  challenge  you  to  bring  the retribution!  If  it  were  not  for  a predetermined  appointment, the retribution  would  have  come  to them  immediately.*  Certainly,  it  will come  to  them  suddenly,  when  they least expect it.

> **29:54** — They  challenge  you  to  bring  retribution!  Hell  already  surrounds  the  disbelievers.

> **29:55** — The day will come when the retribution  overwhelms  them,  from  above them  and  from  beneath  their  feet; He will say, “Taste the consequences of your works.”

### Reflection on the Verse

Verse 29:53 speaks of those who challenge a messenger to bring retribution. I hear a warning here against both denial and theatrical certainty. A person may ask for evidence when evaluating a claim. But demanding that GOD's warning unfold on one's preferred timetable is a different sort of posture.

The verse speaks of an appointment determined in advance. It does not give that appointment to me to announce. I may wish to see the issue resolved today; reality is not obliged to follow the calendar of my impatience. Verse 29:55 connects consequence with what people have done, and I should not lose that moral dimension while wondering about the spectacular.

Nor should anyone use the uncertainty of the timing to frighten an audience into dependence. The statement “I think this may happen” belongs to a different category from “GOD has said it will happen on this date.” Making that difference explicit is part of telling the truth.

I do not want to challenge retribution to arrive. I also do not want to recruit every disaster as proof that my prediction was correct.

**Verse connections:** 29:53, 29:54, 29:55.

## 14. Respite: The Delicate Connection Between Mercy and Time

### Verse

> **35:45** — If  GOD  punished  the  people  for their  sins,  He  would  not  leave  a single  creature  on  earth.  But  He respites  them  for  a  predetermined interim.  Once  their  interim  is  fulfilled, then GOD is Seer of His servants.

> **18:58** — Yet,  your  Lord  is  the  Forgiver,  full of  mercy.  If  He  called  them  to  account  for  their  deeds,  He  would  annihilate  them  right  there  and  then. Instead,  He  gives  them  a  respite until  a  specific,  predetermined time; then they can never escape.

> **16:61** — If GOD punished the people for their transgressions, He would have annihilated every creature on earth. But He respites them for a specific, predetermined time. Once their interim ends, they cannot delay it by one hour, nor advance it.

### Reflection on the Verse

As I read these verses, something other than smoke emerges: time. Verse 35:45 says that were GOD to punish people immediately for their sins, no creature would remain on earth, yet a respite is granted. Verse 18:58 explicitly connects forgiveness and mercy with a period before reckoning.

Respite is easy to misread. When I do something wrong and nothing terrible immediately follows, I may imagine that the wrong was harmless. A comfortable life can begin to seem like proof that every choice within it has been approved. These verses disturb that assumption.

Yet delay is not merely an ominous countdown. It may be time in which there is still something I can do: apologize to someone I injured, correct a false claim, return what I unjustly took, or complete an act of kindness I have postponed. None of those actions depends on knowing when the smoke will appear.

I return to a question from my earlier reflection on delayed retribution: Is this interval permission to remain as I am, or an opening that has not yet closed?

**Verse connections:** 35:45, 18:58, 16:61.

## 15. The Door of Hope: Turning Before the Warning Becomes a Reality

### Verse

> **39:53** — Proclaim: “O My servants who exceeded the limits, never despair of GOD’s mercy. For GOD forgives all sins. He is the Forgiver, Most Merciful.”

> **39:54** — You shall obey your Lord, and submit to Him totally, before the retribution  overtakes  you,  then  you cannot be helped.

> **39:55** — And  follow  the  best  path  that  is pointed out for you by your Lord, before the retribution overtakes you suddenly when you least expect it.

> **6:54** — When  those  who  believe  in  our revelations  come  to  you,  you  shall say,  “Salãmun  ‘Alaykum  (Peace  be upon  you).  Your  Lord  has  decreed that  mercy  is  His  attribute.  Thus, anyone  among  you  who  commits  a transgression  out  of  ignorance,  and repents  thereafter  and  reforms, then  He  is  Forgiving,  Most  Merciful.”

### Reflection on the Verse

The imagery of the smoke is severe. If I leave it by itself, I could leave the reader without any room for hope. But 39:53 says never to despair of GOD's mercy. Verses 39:54–55 call for submission and for following the best path before retribution arrives. Verse 6:54 speaks of forgiveness and mercy for someone who repents and reforms.

The existence of a warning does not make fear the whole of faith. I need to recognize the danger; I also need to hear that a way forward remains open. Perhaps warning has value precisely because there is still time in which people can change direction.

To me, repentance cannot mean only a deeply felt sentence at the end of a troubled night. The call to reform requires something that enters ordinary behavior. If I have harmed someone, I can seek repair. If I have repeated a falsehood, I can correct it. If pride has shaped my decisions, I can reconsider the way I wield certainty.

I would rather use the open door today than wait for smoke to give my intentions a more dramatic background.

**Verse connections:** 39:53, 39:54, 39:55, 6:54.

## 16. Justice: Not Turning Another Person’s Pain into a Verdict

### Verse

> **5:8** — O  you  who  believe,  you  shall  be  absolutely  equitable,  and  observe GOD,  when  you  serve  as  witnesses. Do  not  be  provoked  by  your  conflicts  with  some  people  into  committing  injustice.  You  shall  be  absolutely  equitable,  for  it  is  more  righteous. You  shall  observe  GOD.  GOD  is fully  Cognizant  of  everything  you do.

> **4:135** — O  you who  believe,  you  shall  be absolutely  equitable,  and  observe GOD,  when  you  serve  as  witnesses, even  against  yourselves,  or  your parents,  or  your  relatives.  Whether the  accused  is  rich  or  poor,  GOD takes  care  of  both.  Therefore,  do not  be  biased  by  your  personal wishes.  If  you  deviate  or  disregard (this  commandment),  then  GOD  is fully  Cognizant  of  everything  you do.

> **6:131** — This  is  to  show  that  your  Lord  never annihilates  any  community  unjustly, while its people are unaware.

### Reflection on the Verse

When news of a disaster arrives, it can divide the world between those living through it and those discussing it from somewhere safe. Judging from a distance is deceptively easy. I place 5:8 and 4:135 alongside the smoke for that reason: justice in testimony, even when inconvenient, and fairness toward people with whom we disagree.

If I do not know which people would be affected by the smoke, in what way, or at what time, I cannot assign blame to an entire population to fill my ignorance. Rashad Khalifa's proposed geographical scenario has its place in the dedicated section of this article. It does not give me a license to declare every inhabitant of a region guilty.

Verse 6:131 reminds me that GOD does not annihilate communities unjustly while their people are unaware. I, meanwhile, am a human being with incomplete information. That difference should make my words more careful and my response to suffering more humane.

It is easy to praise divine justice in abstract language. Allowing justice to govern my own speech is a much more immediate test.

**Verse connections:** 5:8, 4:135, 6:131.

## 17. Drawing a Line Between a Prediction and a Divine Warning

### Verse

> **17:36** — You  shall  not  accept  any  information,  unless  you  verify  it  for  yourself. I  have  given  you  the  hearing,  the eyesight,  and  the  brain,  and  you  are responsible for using them.

> **27:65** — Say, “No one in the heavens and the earth knows the future except GOD. They do not even perceive how or when they will be resurrected.” 

> **31:34** — With GOD is the knowledge regarding  the  Hour  (end  of  the  world).*  He is the One who sends down the rain, and  He  knows  the  contents  of  the womb. No soul knows what will happen  to  it  tomorrow,  and  no  one knows in which land he or she will die. GOD is Omniscient, Cognizant.

> **49:6** — O you who believe, if a wicked person brings any news to you, you shall first investigate, lest you commit injustice towards some people, out of ignorance, then become sorry and remorseful for what you have done.

### Reflection on the Verse

A prediction about a catastrophe can attract attention quickly. “It will happen this year” makes a stronger headline than “We do not know.” But 17:36 calls for personal verification, and 49:6 requires investigation before accepting reports. Verse 27:65 ties knowledge of the future to GOD; 31:34 reminds me that a soul does not even know what will happen to it tomorrow.

I am not claiming that GOD never discloses future information to a messenger; the distinction in 72:26–27 must also be remembered. But a feeling, an inference, or an impressive calculation does not automatically become revelation. If I offer a precise year as my own expectation, I am responsible for admitting whether it was borne out.

Revising an interpretation is not automatically dishonorable. Research often requires correction. The danger begins when a revisable interpretation is presented as though it had always been a direct, unambiguous sentence from GOD.

This is one of the disciplines I most want to keep in a study of the smoke: a frightening claim is not more truthful merely because it is vivid.

**Verse connections:** 17:36, 27:65, 31:34, 49:6.

## 18. Oneness and Testimony: Who Holds Final Authority?

### Verse

> **44:8** — There  is  no  other  god  beside  Him. He  controls  life  and  death;  your Lord  and  the  Lord  of  your  ancestors. 

> **3:18** — GOD  bears  witness  that  there  is  no god except He, and so do the angels and  those  who  possess  knowledge. Truthfully  and  equitably,  He  is  the absolute  god;  there  is  no  god  but He, the Almighty, Most Wise.

> **2:42** — Do  not  confound  the  truth  with falsehood,  nor  shall  you  conceal  the truth, knowingly.

> **3:79** — Never would a human being whom GOD blessed with the scripture and prophethood say to the people, “Idolize me beside GOD.” Instead, (he would say), “Devote yourselves absolutely to your Lord alone,” according to the scripture you preach and the teachings you learn.

### Reflection on the Verse

Verse 44:8 names GOD as the One who controls life and death. That brings a question of oneness to the center of the smoke passage: To whom do I submit? To the word warning me, or to every interpretation offered by the person through whom I first encountered that word?

Verse 3:18 places testimony beside truth and justice. Verse 2:42 forbids mixing truth with falsehood. That can happen not only through a changed quotation but also through adding a date to a verse and later talking as though the date had been there all along. Verse 3:79 warns against turning a human being associated with scripture into an object of worship beside GOD.

I choose to study Rashad Khalifa's writings seriously, and I want my own reflections to be evaluated just as seriously. I should be able to explain why I find an interpretation persuasive. But if I accept it merely because one person said it, I surrender the very responsibility to investigate that I say I value.

Behind the smoke, I find a question about testimony: Am I bearing witness to GOD, or am I handing over the power to speak conclusively on GOD's behalf to another human being?

**Verse connections:** 44:8, 3:18, 2:42, 3:79.

## 19. Preparing Today: What Will I Do While Awaiting the Smoke?

### Verse

> **44:10** — Therefore,  watch  for  the  day  when the sky brings a profound smoke.*

> **39:54** — You shall obey your Lord, and submit to Him totally, before the retribution  overtakes  you,  then  you cannot be helped.

> **39:55** — And  follow  the  best  path  that  is pointed out for you by your Lord, before the retribution overtakes you suddenly when you least expect it.

> **10:98** — Any community that believes will surely be rewarded for believing. For example, the people of Jonah: when they believed, we relieved the humiliating retribution they had been suffering in this world, and we made them prosperous.

### Reflection on the Verse

As I return to the beginning, the form of my question has changed. I started by wondering what the smoke is, how it might form, and what the sky would look like. I end with a closer question: What can I change on a day when nothing extraordinary appears to be happening?

Verses 39:54–55 call for obedience and submission before retribution arrives. Verse 10:98 describes a community whose belief was followed by relief from humiliating retribution in this world. These verses direct me toward taking responsibility without trying to control the final outcome or its timing.

Preparation should not mean threatening others. It can mean speaking more truthfully, examining reports before sharing them, refusing to present inference as revelation, meeting suffering with fairness, and learning gratitude without first requiring a crisis. I would rather correct my own arrogance than pretend to measure the invisible condition of a stranger's soul.

Perhaps the most valuable aspect of this warning is that it can still be heard before the smoke arrives. I do not know when that day will be. But I may know which obligations I am postponing today. I want this reflection to leave me not with an apocalyptic schedule, but with greater responsibility for the life I am actually living.

**Verse connections:** 44:10, 39:54, 39:55, 10:98.

### Verse

> **17:36** — You  shall  not  accept  any  information,  unless  you  verify  it  for  yourself. I  have  given  you  the  hearing,  the eyesight,  and  the  brain,  and  you  are responsible for using them.

> **17:92** — “Or unless you cause masses from the sky, as you claimed, to fall on us. Or unless you bring GOD and the angels before our eyes.

> **5:8** — O  you  who  believe,  you  shall  be  absolutely  equitable,  and  observe GOD,  when  you  serve  as  witnesses. Do  not  be  provoked  by  your  conflicts  with  some  people  into  committing  injustice.  You  shall  be  absolutely  equitable,  for  it  is  more  righteous. You  shall  observe  GOD.  GOD  is fully  Cognizant  of  everything  you do.

> **6:131** — This  is  to  show  that  your  Lord  never annihilates  any  community  unjustly, while its people are unaware.

> **74:31** — We appointed angels to be guardians of Hell, and we assigned their number (19) (1) to disturb the disbelievers, (2)  to  convince the  Christians and Jews (that this is a divine scripture), (3) to  strengthen  the  faith  of  the faithful, (4) to  remove  all  traces  of  doubt from the hearts of Christians, Jews, as well as the believers, and (5)  to  expose  those  who  harbor doubt in their hearts, and the disbelievers; they will say, “What did GOD mean by this allegory?” GOD thus  sends  astray  whomever  He wills,  and  guides  whomever  He wills. None knows the soldiers of your Lord except He. This is a reminder for the people.

### Reflection on the Verse

Rashad Khalifa's understanding of the smoke deserves a dedicated discussion here. His comments go beyond a general meditation on the verse: they include specific proposals about historical events, a physical mechanism, geography, and the identity of the messenger in 44:13. Faithfulness to his words means representing these proposals in full without conflating them with the Quran's own wording.

#### The footnotes to 44:10 and 44:13 in the Authorized English Translation

The heading preceding 44:10 in Rashad Khalifa’s Authorized English Translation describes the smoke as a major prophecy. His footnote to 44:10 identifies the smoke and Gog and Magog as two signs yet to be fulfilled. This is an explanatory footnote, not additional wording in verse 44:10: the verse itself does not mention Gog and Magog.

#### June 1986: Connecting the smoke with Chernobyl

The final page of the <a href="https://quranalone.com/articles/17_1986_06/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">June 1986 *Muslim Perspective*, edited by Rashad Khalifa</a>, identifies the Chernobyl nuclear reactor accident with the descriptions in Quran 44:10–15. It presents this as the fulfillment of an end-times Quranic sign.

This is an important primary historical record. Chernobyl was an actual nuclear disaster; the publication associated that disaster with the smoke passage. But that association is not in the words of the verse. The text does not say Chernobyl, nuclear reactor, or 1986. Those specifics belong to an interpretation made at that time.

A researcher can take a connection seriously and later reconsider it. Indeed, understanding can develop. My concern is not to deny the possibility of revision, but to prevent a revisable explanation from occupying the place reserved for the Quran itself.

#### March 1990: A large asteroid impact and worldwide dust cloud

The second page of the <a href="https://www.masjidtucson.org/publications/books/sp/1990/mar/page2.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">March 1990 *Submitters Perspective*</a> gives a far more detailed scenario. Khalifa expects a large asteroid to strike somewhere in the Middle East, producing intense heat, devastation in the region, and a dust cloud spreading around the world and obscuring the sun for months. He relates this scenario to 44:9–15 and also invokes 17:92, which includes a demand for masses to fall from the sky.

In the same publication he says he does not know the exact date, although he has a strong feeling that the event may occur before the end of 1990. The qualification matters. His own phrasing reports an expectation, not certain knowledge of the time. No asteroid impact of the size and effects he described occurred in the Middle East before 1990 ended. Thus his stated timeframe did not come to pass as expected, and it would be inaccurate to report it as a verified fulfillment or a date supplied by the verse.

The <a href="https://quranalone.com/articles/64_1990_03" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">scanned original newsletter</a> is accessible as well. On the page, he distinguishes a section presenting his prediction from another section presenting what he calls GOD's words. Preserving that distinction seems to me one of the fairest ways to read him.

#### His identification of the messenger in 44:13

In his 1990 article, Khalifa argues that the messenger in 44:13 is GOD's Messenger of the Covenant. Among the reasons he presents are the sum 44 + 13 = 57, another numerical total based on suras and verses, a connection between the Arabic **ذِكْرَىٰ** in 44:13 and the language of reminder in 74:31, and a perceived resemblance between the insult described in 44:14 and accusations directed against him.

Understanding these arguments is necessary if I am to represent his reasoning fairly. It does not, however, change the fact that the verse does not explicitly provide the messenger's name. A numerical correspondence or similarity of insults may be meaningful within his interpretation without becoming the explicit words of 44:13.

The March 1990 article also makes severe generalizations about a region and its opponents. I cannot take those words as permission to declare an entire living population guilty. The call to fairness in 5:8 and the statement about injustice in 6:131 challenge that move. No geographical label grants me knowledge of every individual's responsibility before GOD.

#### What the changing explanations teach me

In 1986, the smoke was associated with Chernobyl as a fulfilled sign. In 1990, Khalifa described a massive asteroid impact as a future event. These cannot simply be treated as identical explanations of the same moment. Hiding the difference would not be an act of respect toward his work. I would rather describe what he wrote, when he wrote it, and which parts were interpretations.

I take the warning about smoke seriously. But seriousness should not lead me to announce an asteroid date, label every catastrophe a divine sentence, or elevate a person's forecast above the Quranic text. Verse 17:36 asks me to verify information; 39:54 calls me to submission while the opportunity remains.

I do not know the exact physical cause of the smoke. I can know what Rashad Khalifa proposed about it, and I can tell the difference between what the verse says and what he inferred. That distinction does not obstruct inquiry. It is one of the conditions of truthful inquiry.

## The Arabic Roots of Key Words

### Verse

> **41:11** — Then  He  turned  to  the  sky,  when  it was still gas, and said to it, and to the earth,  “Come  into  existence,  willingly  or  unwillingly.”  They  said, “We come willingly.”

> **44:11** — It will envelope the people; this is a painful retribution.

> **44:16** — The  day  we  strike  the  big  stroke,  we will avenge.

### Reflection on the Verse

To examine the smoke, I need to examine the words. But investigating an Arabic root should clarify relationships within the verses, not authorize us to replace their wording. The noun **دُخَانٍ (dukhān)** comes from the root **د خ ن**, associated with smoke. The **دُخَانٌ (dukhān)** in 41:11 is the same Arabic noun in a different grammatical setting. Yet Rashad Khalifa’s Authorized English Translation renders it “gas” in 41:11 and “smoke” in 44:10. Context matters.

The command **فَارْتَقِبْ (fa-rtaqib)** comes from **ر ق ب**, connected with watching and attentive expectation. The adjective **مُّبِينٍ (mubīn)** derives from **ب ي ن**, the semantic field of being evident or clear. Describing a smoke as distinct or manifest does not give me privileged knowledge of its mechanism. A sign can be identified without the full physical explanation being supplied.

The verb **يَغْشَى (yaghshā)** comes from **غ ش ي**, a root associated with covering or enveloping. **عَذَابٌ (ʿadhāb)** belongs to **ع ذ ب**, used here in the sense of retribution, while **أَلِيمٌ (alīm)** is connected with **أ ل م**, pain. Thus 44:10–11 places a visible event, its enveloping reach, and its painful effect into a closely connected sequence.

The imperative **ٱكْشِفْ (ikshif)** comes from **ك ش ف**, associated with unveiling or removing something. It is the verb in the plea for relief. **ٱلذِّكْرَىٰ (adh-dhikrā)** belongs to **ذ ك ر**, the field of remembering and being reminded. **عَآئِدُونَ (ʿāʾidūn)** comes from **ع و د**, returning. Read together, the verbs in 44:12–15 offer a deeply troubling pattern: pleading, remembering, relief, and relapse.

Finally, **ٱلْبَطْشَةَ (al-baṭshah)** comes from **ب ط ش**, associated with a forceful seizure or strike. I do not build a physics model of the future from these roots. Their value is to remind me that the passage is more than a cloud: it is a story about warning, the response to mercy, and the danger of returning to what should have been left behind.

### Reflection on the Verse

Different explanations of the smoke have appeared throughout the history of interpretation. In some traditional accounts, it is associated with a past famine and the impression of smoke produced by severe hunger. Other interpretations understand it as an extraordinary sign yet to appear near the end of the world. A <a href="https://kuran.diyanet.gov.tr/tefsir/Duh%C3%A2n-suresi/4424/10-16-ayet-tefsiri" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">traditional commentary overview</a> discusses these contrasting approaches.

Modern proposals include nuclear explosions, volcanic ash, industrial pollution, and asteroid impacts. Such events may have physical mechanisms that produce atmospheric haze or dust, but this does not establish that any one of them is what Quran 44:10–16 necessarily describes. Physical possibility is different from textual certainty.

A past-event reading seeks to make sense of the temporary removal and subsequent return in 44:15 within historical circumstances. A future-event reading takes those words as part of a sequence still to unfold. Both approaches must address questions raised by the passage, and neither grants me permission to add a confirmed date where the verse does not give one.

Some alarming interpretations also assign collective guilt to particular nations, sects, or contemporary political groups. I cannot present such judgments as the verse's plain instruction. The requirement of justice in 5:8 is a serious limit upon my own conclusions.

### Reflection on the Verse

I began by trying to understand the smoke. I end by thinking more intensely about the human being before it. Quran 44:10–16 warns of smoke that will be seen and of painful retribution. Yet it also portrays a plea in fear, a messenger previously rejected, a temporary reprieve, and a return to old ways. The sequence leaves me with a question more demanding than the physical description: What have I learned from a warning?

Reading Rashad Khalifa's writings together was important. His association of the passage with Chernobyl in June 1986 and the asteroid scenario in March 1990 tell me how he reflected on these verses at different times. But changing explanations do not mean the Quranic text has changed. Studying his reflections is one thing. Reading a date, region, or mechanism into 44:10 as though it explicitly appeared there is another.

I am not taking an apocalypse schedule away from this article. The absence of such a schedule does not make the warning insignificant. Verse 39:54 calls for submission before retribution arrives. Verse 39:53 tells me not to despair of GOD's mercy. Verse 17:36 reminds me that accepting information brings a duty of verification. And verse 5:8 asks me to remain just even toward people I oppose or fear.

If I cannot bring those commands into everyday life, how much would knowing the precise physics of the smoke really accomplish? Can I admit a mistake today? Do I investigate a report before circulating it? Do I exploit another person's pain to prove myself right? Does the life I lead support the words I speak?

If the smoke comes, it will come according to what GOD has decreed. I do not appoint its date, physical cause, or scope. My responsibility is to acknowledge the boundaries of my knowledge without dismissing the warning, to refuse to turn fear into a weapon against others, and not to squander the interval still available to me.

Perhaps the most meaningful preparation is not to wait for the color of the sky to change. It is to change my direction while the sky is still clear.

That is what I most desire: not to be someone who returns to the old ways as soon as retribution is relieved, and not to become so fascinated with the smoke that I postpone mercy, truthfulness, and submission.
