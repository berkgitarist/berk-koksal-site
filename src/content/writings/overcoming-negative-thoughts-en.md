# [1:1] Overcoming Negative Thoughts: Seeking Refuge in GOD and Trusting Him

*A Nineteen-Part Reflection on Quran 1:1*

**Author:** Berk KÖKSAL  
**Date:** October 17, 2026  
**Location:** Mersin

## Introduction

**Greetings. Peace be upon you.**

A negative thought can remain in the mind longer than the event that gave rise to it. Sometimes we exhaust today by fearing possibilities we have not yet encountered. But is experiencing a thought the same as submitting to it? Where do seeking refuge in GOD, trusting Him, and verifying knowledge belong in this question?

The purpose of this article is to reflect on 1:1 alongside related verses and to consider sincerely what bearing witness to GOD’s oneness means for knowledge, justice, truthfulness, and submission in daily life. I do not want to reduce negative thoughts to a technique for feeling better. I want to ask where I turn when fear rises within me, what I accept as knowledge, and which responsibilities I must not abandon.

Can a person acknowledge fear without giving it the authority to pronounce a final judgment? This reflection is my attempt to approach that question without making the verses say more than they do.

## 1. Where Everything Begins: Seeking Refuge in GOD’s Mercy

### Verse

> **1:1**
>
> بِسْمِ ٱللَّهِ ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ ٱلرَّحِيمِ
>
> In the name of GOD, Most Gracious, Most Merciful.*
>
> En Lütufkâr, En Merhametli TANRI’nın adıyla.*

Source: <a href="https://kuranteyit.com/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">kuranteyit.com</a>

### Reflection on the Verse

At times, a person becomes exhausted inside his own mind. Fear of things that have not happened, regret over past decisions, uncertainty about the future, and words spoken by others can become an invisible burden. Life continues on the outside, while another struggle unfolds within.

I begin by asking myself: **Does the appearance of a thought in my mind oblige me to believe it?** Will every possibility that frightens me come true? Does every surge of anxiety carry reliable knowledge about tomorrow? Or am I sometimes carrying the weight of an event that has not yet occurred?

The Quran’s first verse directs me somewhere other than the details of my fears. It begins in the name of GOD, and its opening speaks of grace and mercy. This is not a promise that every negative thought will disappear immediately. Verse 1:1 makes no such promise. It does, however, give me reason to remember GOD’s mercy even while I am afraid.

My first task may not be to defeat every possibility my mind produces. It may be to remember whom I turn to while those possibilities are present. If fear becomes my unquestioned ruler, events that have not happened begin governing my conduct today. The direction established by 1:1 reminds me that I am not reducible to my fear. I can turn toward GOD before my thoughts become quiet.

**Verse references:** 1:1.

## 2. From Whom Am I Really Seeking Help?

### Verse

> **1:5** — You alone we worship. You alone we ask for help.

### Reflection on the Verse

This verse asks me to distinguish two things. Receiving help from another person is one matter; placing my entire trust in that person’s power is another. I can visit a doctor, speak with a friend, or seek advice from someone experienced. None of these actions requires me to treat another human being as the ultimate source of security.

When my mind is crowded with fear, the questions often come quickly. Who will understand me? Who will support me? Who can resolve this problem? Seeking support is not necessarily wrong. But tying all my peace to another person’s answer can create a new vulnerability, because human knowledge, time, and resources are limited.

Verse 1:5 leads me to examine the direction of my appeal for help. When I say that I ask GOD for help, do I nevertheless trust only the causes I can see? Does my confidence collapse whenever people delay, disappoint me, or respond differently from what I expected?

I cannot derive from this verse a command to cut off human relationships. Rather, I see a distinction between accepting help and treating the helper as absolute. **Asking GOD for help does not release me from responsibility. It tells me where my trust belongs while I carry out that responsibility.**

**Verse references:** 1:5.

## 3. Must I Accept Every Thought That Enters My Mind?

### Verse

> **41:36** — When  the  devil  whispers  an  idea  to you,  you shall  seek  refuge  in  GOD. He is the Hearer, the Omniscient.

### Reflection on the Verse

Verse 41:36 is one of the most direct passages for this subject. It speaks of the devil whispering an idea and tells the listener to seek refuge in GOD. A distinction matters here: having a thought is not necessarily the same as accepting it and making it a rule for one’s decisions.

A person may suddenly think that he is inadequate, that something terrible is about to happen, or that every path ahead is closed. But this verse does not state that every unpleasant thought must come from the devil. I need to respect that limit. Treating every moment of anxiety as a spiritual offense could make a person even more frightened of his own mind.

Instead, I can examine what a thought rests upon and where it urges me to go. Does it push me toward an unverified accusation, injustice, or despair? When the whisper is of the kind described in the verse, its instruction is unmistakable: seek refuge in GOD.

The closing description of GOD as Hearer and Omniscient matters to me. Even when I cannot explain my inner struggle to anyone else, I need not imagine it is outside His knowledge. I understand refuge here not merely as repeating a phrase, but as redirecting the struggle within me toward GOD.

**Verse references:** 41:36.

## 4. Where Do I Turn When a Whisper Arrives?

### Verse

> **7:200** — When  the  devil  whispers  to  you  any whisper,  seek  refuge  in  GOD;  He  is Hearer, Omniscient.

### Reflection on the Verse

When I encounter a troubling thought, I sometimes enter an argument with the thought itself. I try to convince myself that one feared event will not happen, only to imagine another. Answering one question generates two more. It is possible to become mentally exhausted before the feared event has even occurred.

Verse 7:200 makes me ask: Must I debate every thought until it is completely silent? In the situation of a devilish whisper, the verse tells me to turn toward GOD. I therefore see another option besides following every detail of an alarming idea.

This is not an invitation to ignore actual dangers. If I have made a mistake, I should correct it. If I owe someone something, I should make it right. If there is a concrete risk, I should take reasonable precautions. But considering an unverified disaster scenario is not the same as treating it as established fact.

The whisper may not disappear immediately; the verse does not give a timetable. Yet I can place some distance between a thought’s presence and my surrender to it. This is what makes refuge meaningful to me: I do not have to give fear the final word.

**Verse references:** 7:200.

## 5. The Influence of Hidden Whispers on Inner Life

### Verse

> **114:1** — Say, “I seek refuge in the Lord of the people. 
>
> **114:2** — “The King of the people. 
>
> **114:3** — “The god of the people. 
>
> **114:4** — “From the evils of sneaky whisperers. 
>
> **114:5** — “Who whisper into the chests of the people. 
>
> **114:6** — “Be they of the jinns, or the people.”

### Reflection on the Verse

What catches my attention in these verses from Sura 114 is that refuge is sought in the Lord, King, and god of the people. A sentence lodged in the mind may have been heard long ago. A belittling remark, a threat, or repeated pessimism can continue to echo after the speaker has left.

Verse 114:6 mentions whisperers from among both the jinn and the people. I therefore cannot assume that every harmful influence comes only from an unseen source. But I must avoid the opposite mistake as well. Not every criticism is an evil whisper. Someone may point out an error I truly need to correct.

I ask myself whether I examine the origin and content of the words that keep returning to me. Am I remembering something I have actually investigated, or have I adopted a judgment someone once pronounced over me? It is easy to carry another person’s sentence for years without ever testing it.

These verses do not identify another human being as my final refuge. They turn me toward the Lord of the people. For me, that means I may listen seriously to others without making their frightening judgments my absolute measure. Not every voice I hear must become the ruler within me.

**Verse references:** 114:1; 114:2; 114:3; 114:4; 114:5; 114:6.

## 6. The Joy of the Heart: Remembering GOD

### Verse

> **13:28** — They  are  the  ones  whose  hearts rejoice  in  remembering  GOD.  Absolutely,  by  remembering  GOD,  the hearts rejoice.

### Reflection on the Verse

As I think about negative thoughts, I do not want to focus only on what should leave my mind. What takes its place matters too. One fear may diminish while another uncertainty begins. The events beyond my control will not simply come to an end.

Verse 13:28 directs my attention from the fears themselves to remembering GOD. In my earlier Turkish reflection, <a href="https://berkkoksal.com/writings/sessiz-zikir-ses-bittiginde-hatirlayis-devam-eder-mi/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Silent Remembrance: Does It Continue After the Sound Ends?</a>, I explored a related question: when sound disappears, does remembrance continue?

As a musician, I know that sound can affect emotion. A melody may soothe someone, and a piece of music can change the atmosphere of a difficult day. But I do not want to reduce the rejoicing described in 13:28 to music or any other external stimulus. The verse connects that joy to remembering GOD. Music ends, people disperse, and a person may be alone. Does remembrance remain?

The verse does not mean that anyone who remembers GOD can never grieve or feel anxious. The joy of the heart is not identical to a life without difficulties. What remains for me is the possibility of turning the direction of my trust toward GOD again and again, even as my emotions change.

**Verse references:** 13:28.

## 7. Is GOD Not Sufficient for His Servant?

### Verse

> **39:36** — Is GOD not sufficient for His servant? They frighten you with the idols  they  set  up  beside  Him. Whomever GOD sends astray, nothing can guide him.

### Reflection on the Verse

The question in 39:36 unsettles me. I say I trust GOD, yet I can see how large certain possibilities become in my imagination: financial hardship, a failed project, being misunderstood, a plan falling apart. Such possibilities may require attention, but their existence is not proof that the future is bound to be disastrous.

The verse speaks specifically of being frightened with idols set up beside GOD. I should not use it to claim that every fear is imaginary. The deeper issue is what I regard as ultimate. If losing a person, position, resource, or carefully constructed plan appears to mean that my entire world must collapse, I need to examine the authority I have given that thing.

In an earlier Turkish reflection, <a href="https://berkkoksal.com/writings/tanriya-derin-saygi-ego-saygisizlik-siniri-asmak/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Deep Reverence for GOD: Ego, Disrespect, and Crossing the Boundary</a>, I considered the danger of making the self an absolute measure. I find a related trial here. At times, it is not an external authority I magnify beyond its place; it is my own fear.

**Do I recognize GOD’s sufficiency, or do I grant an independent sovereignty to whatever frightens me?** I ask that question to examine myself, not to pronounce judgment upon anyone else.

**Verse references:** 39:36.

## 8. Keeping the Direction of Faith When Fear Grows

### Verse

> **3:173** — When  the  people  say  to  them, “People  have  mobilized  against  you; you  should  fear  them,”  this  only strengthens  their  faith,  and  they  say, “GOD  suffices  us;  He  is  the  best Protector.” 

### Reflection on the Verse

Verse 3:173 describes frightening news. People are told that others have mobilized against them. What draws my attention is that this warning does not move the believers’ trust away from GOD. Sometimes trust is tested precisely where fear is present.

It can be easy to speak about trusting GOD when everything appears stable. But can I keep the same direction when uncertainty rises, unsettling reports arrive, or my plans fail to develop as expected? That is the question in my own life.

Someone may insist that I will fail. Someone else may magnify a danger or speak of a possible outcome as though it had already happened. I should neither dismiss such statements automatically nor accept them without examination. I cannot turn 3:173 into a command to abandon precautions. The verse describes a posture of faith under intimidation; it does not praise carelessness.

For me, trusting GOD is not claiming to know every result. It is refusing to forget His protection while results remain unknown. I want to continue seeing reality and doing what is right without allowing fear to take complete possession of my decisions.

**Verse references:** 3:173.

## 9. The Desire to Control the Future and Submission

### Verse

> **65:3** — And  will  provide  for  him  whence  he never  expected.  Anyone  who  trusts in GOD, He suffices him. GOD’s commands  are  done.  GOD  has decreed for everything its fate.

### Reflection on the Verse

One of the exhausting impulses of the mind is the desire to control the future completely. We make a decision and want to know all its consequences beforehand. We begin a project and demand a guarantee of success. We choose a path and try to exclude the possibility of error. Yet our knowledge and resources remain limited.

Verse 65:3 places trust in GOD beside His sufficiency and His decree concerning the fate of things. It reminds me of a balance I try to maintain: **make a plan, work seriously, and leave the outcome to GOD.** Saying this is easier than living it when the result matters deeply to me.

After working hard on something, I cannot claim that GOD must fulfill my plan exactly as I imagined it. The statement about provision from unexpected places does not mean every worldly wish will be granted without condition. Trust is not an attempt to impose my preferred outcome upon GOD.

Sometimes a way opens; sometimes it closes; sometimes an unexpected development changes everything. I cannot claim to know the final significance of each event. I therefore want to understand submission not as comfort born from knowing tomorrow, but as fidelity while admitting that tomorrow is unknown. Being unable to control everything does not mean I am required to control everything.

**Verse references:** 65:3.

## 10. Trusting GOD Does Not Mean Remaining Inactive

### Verse

> **3:159** — It was mercy from GOD that you became  compassionate  towards them.  Had  you  been  harsh  and mean-hearted, they would have abandoned you. Therefore, you shall pardon them and ask forgiveness for them, and consult them. Once you make a decision, carry out your plan, and trust in GOD. GOD loves those who trust in Him.*

### Reflection on the Verse

Consultation, decision, action, and trust in GOD appear together in 3:159. That sequence matters. Submission is not waiting without investigation or withholding the effort a situation requires.

If I decide without adequate research, I cannot conceal my responsibility by attributing every resulting problem to fate. But if I work carefully and still fail to achieve what I hoped, I cannot immediately conclude that GOD has abandoned me. The verse makes no such judgment.

Preparing a piece of music makes this distinction tangible. Composing, arranging, listening repeatedly, correcting mistakes, and finishing the recording are things I must do. Yet I cannot determine with certainty how the work will be received. Effort belongs to my responsibility; controlling every result lies beyond my power.

The verse also begins with compassion, pardon, and consultation. I should therefore not use the language of trust to excuse harshness toward others. Trust may be more than a feeling about the future. It can be a moral direction I try to preserve while making decisions and dealing with people.

**Verse references:** 3:159.

## 11. Bringing Grief to GOD Instead of Hiding It

### Verse

> **12:86** — He said, “I simply complain to GOD about my dilemma and grief, for I know from GOD what you do not know.

### Reflection on the Verse

Verse 12:86 opens a very human door for me. Faith and grief are sometimes treated as opposites: if you are sad, you do not trust enough; if you are afraid, you have not submitted enough; if you cry, you are weak. Yet this verse places grief and turning toward GOD in the same sentence.

That gives me reason not to deny what I am experiencing. I may genuinely be sad. I may have lost something, faced a prolonged difficulty, or become worn down by a problem I cannot resolve. Telling myself repeatedly that I am strong may not make those feelings disappear.

The words of the verse bring a dilemma and sorrow before GOD. To me, this is another dimension of seeking refuge: turning to Him without disguising my experience, while refusing to forget His knowledge and mercy.

There is no guarantee in the verse that grief will end immediately. But I can see that a person may remain directed toward GOD even when deeply troubled. Perhaps what exhausts me at times is not only the event itself, but the effort to carry it as though it were a burden known to no one.

**Verse references:** 12:86.

## 12. Despair Can Become a Harsh Judgment Against Oneself

### Verse

> **39:53** — Proclaim: “O My servants who exceeded the limits, never despair of GOD’s mercy. For GOD forgives all sins. He is the Forgiver, Most Merciful.”

### Reflection on the Verse

People can spend years judging themselves over a past mistake. A wrong decision, a hurtful sentence, or a missed opportunity returns to the mind again and again. Regret can lead to correction. But declaring that no change is possible for oneself is another matter.

That is why the call not to despair of GOD’s mercy in 39:53 matters so deeply to me. The verse addresses servants who have exceeded the limits. It reminds me that having done wrong does not eliminate the possibility of turning toward GOD’s mercy.

I must not turn this hope into permission to avoid accountability. If I violated someone’s rights, I should try to make amends. If I lied, I should return to truthfulness. If I made an error, I should work to change my conduct. Hope in mercy does not make actions unimportant.

What troubles me is the way a person can transform his past into a final and unchangeable verdict on his future. I do not possess the authority to pronounce over myself a judgment that GOD has not given me. I want to look at my past honestly, neither declaring myself innocent nor shutting the door to mercy.

**Verse references:** 39:53.

## 13. Steadfastness and Prayer When the Mind Feels Scattered

### Verse

> **2:153** — O  you  who  believe,  seek  help through  steadfastness  and  the  Contact  Prayers  (Salat).  GOD  is  with those who steadfastly persevere.

### Reflection on the Verse

People usually want their suffering to end at once. We want the problem solved today, the fear gone now, and uncertainty removed without delay. Life does not always move at that speed. Verse 2:153 joins the search for help with steadfastness and the Contact Prayers.

I do not want to mistake steadfastness for emotional numbness. Sometimes it means refusing a hasty and harmful decision. Sometimes it is continuing to do what is right while tired. At other times it means carrying out one’s responsibility before any result becomes visible.

When I reflect on the Contact Prayers, I see an opportunity to stop making the concerns of the day the unquestioned center of everything. Turning to GOD can be more than rehearsing my problems; it can help me reconsider my position before them. Yet having prayed does not mean I have completed the work that remains mine to do.

I see neither an instruction to stop acting nor a claim that personal willpower solves everything. Perseverance and asking GOD for help stand together. Continuing to do what is right while difficult thoughts remain may sometimes be one of the most concrete expressions of trust.

**Verse references:** 2:153.

## 14. Noticing Gain Alongside Hardship

### Verse

> **94:5** — With pain there is gain. 
>
> **94:6** — Indeed, with pain there is gain. 

### Reflection on the Verse

I want to read 94:5-6 carefully. I cannot draw from these verses a guarantee that every pain will end immediately, every loss will be replaced by greater material wealth, or every difficulty will resolve exactly as I want. What the verses actually do is place gain alongside pain.

In my own work, difficulties have sometimes exposed what I needed to learn. A method that failed pushed me toward a better one; a project that stalled forced me to discover another approach. But I cannot claim that every person will find the same benefit in every kind of suffering. Some difficulties last a long time, and someone living through them may see no gain at all.

Still, negative thoughts can turn the present into a permanent prediction. The mind says that because the problem remains today, nothing will ever change. But I do not possess the full details of tomorrow. Declaring that an unresolved problem must remain unresolved forever exceeds my knowledge.

Verses 94:5-6 leave me with room for hope that does not deny pain. I need not minimize hardship, but neither must I declare that life contains no other possibilities. Hope can mean refusing to pass a final judgment on outcomes I do not know.

**Verse references:** 94:5; 94:6.

## 15. Knowledge, Justice, and Testimony: Not Mistaking Fear for Truth

### Verse

> **3:18** — GOD  bears  witness  that  there  is  no god except He, and so do the angels and  those  who  possess  knowledge. Truthfully  and  equitably,  He  is  the absolute  god;  there  is  no  god  but He, the Almighty, Most Wise.
>
> **17:36** — You  shall  not  accept  any  information,  unless  you  verify  it  for  yourself. I  have  given  you  the  hearing,  the eyesight,  and  the  brain,  and  you  are responsible for using them.

### Reflection on the Verse

Negative thoughts are not only about emotion; they also concern knowledge. A person may treat a feared possibility as certain even when there is not enough evidence. He assumes someone is angry with him, that a project must fail, or that the future will inevitably deteriorate. Soon, these assumptions begin directing his actions as though they were confirmed reports.

Verse 17:36 reminds me that information must be verified for myself. I want to apply that responsibility to the claims my own mind generates. Why has this thought appeared? What observation supports it? Does it describe something that happened, or merely one of several possibilities?

In music production, I do not assume every sound I hear was an intentional creative choice. I examine its source and correct an error when necessary. I can give my thoughts similar attention without claiming that this makes me infallible. The purpose is not to become suspicious of everything, but to avoid accepting falsehood as knowledge.

Verse 3:18 joins testimony to GOD’s oneness with knowledge and justice. That makes me think monotheism is not only a sentence I pronounce, but a conviction that bears upon my responsibility for truth. An unsupported judgment about another person may be unjust; I also need care when judging myself. **Having a thought is not the same as verifying it.**

**Verse references:** 3:18; 17:36.

## 16. Making People into Lords and Losing the Center of Trust

### Verse

> **9:31** — They  have  set  up  their  religious leaders  and  scholars  as  lords,*  instead of GOD. Others deified the Messiah, son of Mary. They were all commanded  to  worship  only  one god. There is no god except He. Be He glorified, high above having any partners.

### Reflection on the Verse

When my mind becomes confused, I may long for someone who can provide absolute certainty. I want another person to know the future, settle my problems, and end every doubt. Yet seeking information and support is not the same as treating a human being as an unquestionable final authority.

Verse 9:31 warns against setting up religious leaders and scholars as lords instead of GOD. I should not mistake that warning for a prohibition on learning. I may learn from a teacher, listen to a researcher, or consult someone with experience. But I must not place that person’s words on the same level as the word of GOD.

This matters when dealing with negative thoughts as well. If someone claims that only he can give me spiritual peace, or that overcoming fear requires absolute obedience to him, I need to examine the claim. Receiving help is one matter; surrendering the final judgment of my inner life to another person is another.

For me, seeking refuge in GOD does not require rejecting every human voice. It requires respecting the limits of each voice. My desire for security should not become a reason to grant someone else unlimited authority over me.

**Verse references:** 9:31.

## 17. Seeking Refuge Directly in GOD: A Direction Beyond the Crowd

### Verse

> **23:97** — Say,  “My  Lord,  I  seek  refuge  in  You from the whispers of the devils.
>
> **23:98** — “And  I  seek  refuge  in  You,  my Lord, lest they come near me.”

### Reflection on the Verse

In 23:97-98, refuge appears as a direct appeal to GOD. The speaker seeks protection from the whispers of the devils and from their coming near. Read beside 41:36 and 7:200, these verses remind me that seeking refuge does not depend on another person’s permission or approval.

Sometimes I want to relieve inner distress by speaking with someone. That is a human need. Yet there may also be matters I cannot easily share, cannot explain clearly, or must face in solitude. Even then, I need not make my turning toward GOD conditional upon another person granting me access.

I should not misuse these verses to declare that seeking practical or professional support is unnecessary when someone faces a genuine health problem. Their explicit subject is refuge in GOD from the whispers of devils. Respecting that subject is part of both spiritual seriousness and intellectual honesty.

I find a simple measure here. When fear rises within me, I can choose to turn toward GOD instead of offering fear all my attention. Refuge does not prove that my mind is completely silent. It helps me remember which voice must never become the final authority over my life.

**Verse references:** 23:97; 23:98.

## 18. The Boundary of Trust: Refusing to Give Fear Absolute Power

### Verse

> **16:99** — He  has  no  power  over  those  who believe and trust in their Lord.

### Reflection on the Verse

Verse 16:99 speaks about a limit on the devil’s power. It says he has no power over those who believe and trust in their Lord. That makes the call to trust GOD more meaningful when confronting devilish whispers, rather than imagining the whisper itself as an irresistible force.

But I do not want to turn this verse into an automatic formula promising the disappearance of every psychological difficulty. The presence of an unwanted thought does not authorize me to draw a final conclusion about a person’s faith or relationship with GOD. Verse 16:99 does not make that kind of personal diagnosis.

I ask whether I have magnified my own fears beyond their place. Does a troubling idea truly possess all the power I give it, or have I made it the manager of my entire day? Fear may alert me to something worth examining. It does not follow that I must obey every instruction fear gives me.

Trust does not invite me to declare myself spiritually superior. It directs me back to the source of my confidence. Rather than claiming that a person who seeks refuge must always feel strong, I want to continue turning to GOD even when I feel weak.

**Verse references:** 16:99.

## 19. Arabic Roots: Mercy, Refuge, Remembrance, and Trust

### Verse

> **73:9** — Lord  of  the  east  and  the  west;  there is  no  other  god  besides  Him.  You should  choose  Him  as  your  advocate.
>
> **41:36** — When  the  devil  whispers  an  idea  to you,  you shall  seek  refuge  in  GOD. He is the Hearer, the Omniscient.
>
> **13:28** — They  are  the  ones  whose  hearts rejoice  in  remembering  GOD.  Absolutely,  by  remembering  GOD,  the hearts rejoice.
>
> **65:3** — And  will  provide  for  him  whence  he never  expected.  Anyone  who  trusts in GOD, He suffices him. GOD’s commands  are  done.  GOD  has decreed for everything its fate.

### Reflection on the Verse

When I examine the Arabic wording of these verses, I notice connections among mercy, refuge, remembrance, and trust. Yet I do not want to substitute word roots for the verses as a whole. A root has a range of meaning, and its actual sense must be read in context.

The words **الرَّحْمَٰنِ** and **الرَّحِيمِ** in 1:1 are connected with **ر ح م (r-ḥ-m)**, the field of mercy. This language stands at the opening of the Quran. The imperative **فَٱسْتَعِذْ** in 41:36 and 7:200 is related to **ع و ذ (ʿ-w-dh)**, seeking protection and refuge. What interests me is the direction of the appeal: the person turns toward GOD.

The field of the devilish prompting in 41:36 involves **ن ز غ (n-z-gh)**, while remembrance in 13:28 is connected with **ذ ك ر (dh-k-r)**. One brings a disruptive prompting into view; the other recalls GOD. This contrast makes me question why I sometimes give a frightening whisper all my attention.

The verb of trusting in 65:3 is related to **و ك ل (w-k-l)**. The language of sufficiency in that verse involves **ح س ب (ḥ-s-b)**. When I also read the instruction to choose GOD as advocate in 73:9, I see trust as a question of whom I ultimately depend upon, not merely a passing feeling of reassurance.

In 3:18, testimony connects with **ش ه د (sh-h-d)**, knowledge with **ع ل م (ʿ-l-m)**, and equity with **ق س ط (q-s-ṭ)**. In 9:31, **أَرْبَابًا** is related to **ر ب ب (r-b-b)**, the field of lordship. These connections help preserve an ethical boundary: refuge does not require abandoning verification, justice, or personal responsibility to a human authority.

I do not use these roots to establish new rules absent from the verses. Their value is more modest and perhaps more lasting. Remember mercy, seek refuge in GOD from disruptive prompting, examine what you believe, uphold justice, and preserve the direction of your trust.

**Verse references:** 73:9; 41:36; 13:28; 65:3.

## Other Claims

There are different religious and psychological approaches to negative thoughts, whispering, refuge, and submission. They should not all be reduced to a single explanation. Some traditional interpretations attribute nearly every disturbing thought directly to the devil. Yet 41:36 and 7:200 speak specifically of devilish whispering. They do not state that every episode of anxiety or grief has the same cause.

Other approaches may present fixed repetitions of particular prayers or phrases as a necessary method for every kind of distress. Personal experiences can differ. The verses considered here, however, do not establish one compulsory repetition count as a universal cure for negative thoughts.

The desire for spiritual reassurance can also be joined to demands for unquestioning obedience to religious figures. Learning from others and making them ultimate authorities in place of GOD are distinct matters. The warning in 9:31 remains relevant to that boundary.

Psychological explanations may discuss stress, past experiences, and mental processes. It would be inaccurate to claim that all such explanations are stated directly in these verses. Nor do the cited verses justify automatically treating prolonged anxiety as proof of deficient faith. Seeking refuge in GOD and receiving appropriate professional support need not exclude one another.

I want to preserve the clear wording of 16:99 concerning trust. But turning it into the claim that no believer can experience an unwanted thought would go beyond the verse. Traditions and personal experiences may vary; the standard I want to keep is the distinction between what the verses explicitly say and what I infer from them.

## Conclusion

### Verse

> **42:10** — If you dispute any part of this message,  the  judgment  for  doing  this rests with GOD. Such is GOD my Lord. In Him I trust, and to Him I submit.

### Reflection on the Verse

Read together, these verses make me see that freedom from negative thoughts is not merely a matter of removing every disturbing idea from my mind. Beneath that desire lie questions about trust, knowledge, justice, and submission. What do I believe? What do I regard as verified? What frightens me? And toward whom do I actually turn for refuge?

Verse 1:1 begins with mercy. Verse 1:5 establishes the direction of help. Verses 41:36 and 7:200 direct a person confronted by devilish whispering toward refuge in GOD. Verse 13:28 connects the heart’s joy with remembrance. Verses 39:36 and 3:173 make me consider GOD’s sufficiency; 65:3 joins trust with His decree over outcomes.

Verse 3:159 reminds me not to set trust against action. Grief is brought before GOD in 12:86, and 39:53 forbids despair of His mercy. Verse 17:36 offers a standard for examining information, including claims generated by my own mind. Verse 3:18 places testimony beside knowledge and equity.

**Trusting GOD does not mean that I will never have another negative thought. It means trying not to forget GOD when such a thought arises.** Feeling afraid is not the same as appointing fear the lord of my life. Being sad is not the same as despairing of GOD’s mercy.

There may be times when I am frightened, grieving, or facing the collapse of a plan. I do not want to deny such experiences. A concrete danger should be assessed. A wrong should be corrected. A question I cannot answer should be investigated. Trust in GOD cannot be an excuse to ignore reality.

Yet I am not required to transform my lack of knowledge about tomorrow into certainty of disaster. I may not choose every thought that appears in my mind. I remain responsible for which thoughts I verify, which ones I act upon, and toward whom I seek refuge.

At the end, I return to the opening verse. I can turn toward GOD while my thoughts are unsettled. I can remember Him while sad. I can work diligently without pretending that every result is under my control. And I can resist handing to another person, a fear, or my own ego the authority that belongs to GOD.

The trust and submission expressed in 42:10 become questions I put to my own life. Do I profess these words only when writing about them, or can I remember them when the result I expected does not come?

Perhaps the central spiritual struggle for me is this: not to lose the truthfulness of today because I fear an unknown tomorrow. To do what I can, investigate what is true, preserve justice, remember mercy, and trust GOD.

**For there is much that I do not know. But GOD’s knowledge is not limited by mine.**
