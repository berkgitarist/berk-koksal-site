# Deep Reverence: Remembering, Faithfulness to the Covenant, and Turning to GOD Alone

*A reflection on 2:40 and verses connected with deep reverence*

**Author:** Berk KÖKSAL

**Date:** 12.08.2026

**Location:** Mersin

### Verse

> **1:1** — In the name of GOD, Most Gracious, Most Merciful.*

**Greetings. Peace be upon you.**

The primary aim of this article is to consider the verses together with other related verses and to express, in a sincere and profound way, what bearing witness to the oneness of GOD means in human life in terms of knowledge, justice, truthfulness, and submission.

## Introduction

At first glance, the expression “deep reverence” can be read as an emotion. A person may tremble before greatness, feel apprehensive, fall silent, or become aware of their own smallness. But when I enter into Al-Baqarah 2:40, the phrase does not stand alone. Before it comes a blessing; then comes the call to fulfill the human side of the covenant. At the end comes a direct instruction: **“Reverence Me.”**

For this reason, I do not want to explain deep reverence in this article only through fear, only through humility, or only through emotional intensity. When the verses in the document are placed side by side, a broader map emerges: deep reverence becomes visible sometimes as faithfulness to the covenant, sometimes as truthful speech, sometimes as fearing GOD rather than people, sometimes as preserving one’s orientation in privacy, sometimes as justice, sometimes as worship, and sometimes as restraining the self from sinful desires.

Previously, in *The Weight of Testimony*, I reflected on the relationship between monotheism, knowledge, and justice; in *Not Accepting Without Verification*, on the responsibility of knowledge; and in *How Can Tawhid Be Distorted by a Single Word?*, on how carefully language must be used when speaking about GOD. “Deep reverence” seems like a quieter current running beneath all these themes. Because whoever a person truly exalts is also where they anchor their deepest fear, hope, obedience, and accountability.

At the center of this reflection is one question: **What does deep reverence for GOD concretely change in my life?**

## 2. The Softening of the Heart: What Does Deep Reverence Do Within?

### Verse

> **2:45** — You shall seek help through steadfastness and the Contact Prayers (Salat). This is difficult indeed, but not so for those who are reverent,

> **2:74** — Yet your hearts became hardened like rocks, or even harder. For there are rocks from which rivers gush out. Some crack open and gentle streams flow from them, and other rocks shrink out of reverence for GOD. GOD is never unaware of anything you do.

> **7:154** — When Moses' anger subsided, he picked up the tablets, which contained guidance and mercy for those who reverence their Lord.

> **17:109** — They fall on their chins, prostrating and weeping. For it increases their reverence.

### Reflection on the Verse

If deep reverence were only an outwardly visible behavior, the comparison in 2:74 between hearts and even rocks that shrink out of reverence for GOD would not be so striking. The verse says that the heart can harden; it even presents the possibility that a human heart may fall behind stone itself. This calls me to look for deep reverence first not in the appearance of others, but in my own inner hardness.

In 2:45, seeking help through steadfastness and the Contact Prayers takes on a different meaning for those who are reverent, making me think about the relationship between inward orientation and the capacity to bear burdens. In 7:154, the tablets contain guidance and mercy for those who reverence their Lord; in 17:109, the verses increase reverence. These illuminate the same point from different directions: before revelation, the heart either softens or hardens in order to protect itself.

In 13:13, the thunder praising His glory and the angels being mentioned with reverence break the human measure that places the self at the center. My emotion, my fear, or my enthusiasm is not the measure of deep reverence. When I remember the place of all creation before GOD, I begin to see my own place more accurately as well.

The trace of deep reverence in the heart may not be constant tension, but rather not becoming hardened before truth. Can a person turn back when they see that they were mistaken? Can they listen to a verse without bending it when it touches their interests? This is where the heart reveals itself.


**Verse references:** 2:45, 2:74, 7:154, 13:13, 17:109.

## 3. Fearing GOD, Not People

### Verse

> **3:28** — The believers shall never ally themselves with disbelievers in place of believers. Whoever does this is exiled from GOD, except those who are forced to do so to avoid persecution. GOD warns you to reverence Him alone. The ultimate destiny is to GOD.

> **3:30** — The day will come when every soul will find all the good it has done brought before it. As for its evil works, it will wish that they were far, far away. GOD warns you to reverence Him alone. GOD is Compassionate toward the people.

> **5:44** — We sent down the Torah,* containing guidance and light. The Jewish prophets, as well as the rabbis and priests, ruled by it as commanded in GOD's scripture and as they themselves witnessed. Therefore, do not reverence the people; instead, reverence Me. And do not trade away My revelations for a cheap price. Those who do not rule in accordance with GOD's revelations are disbelievers.

> **33:37** — Recall that you said to the one who was blessed by GOD and blessed by you, “Keep your wife and reverence GOD,” while you concealed within yourself what GOD wished to proclaim. Thus, you feared the people when you should have feared GOD alone. When Zaid was completely through with his wife, we had you marry her, in order to establish the precedent that a man may marry the divorced wife of his adopted son. GOD's commands shall be carried out.

> **33:39** — Those who deliver GOD's messages and reverence Him alone should never fear anyone but GOD. GOD is the most efficient reckoner.

### Reflection on the Verse

5:44 draws one of the distinctions at the center of this article very clearly: “do not reverence the people; instead, reverence Me.” When I read this sentence, I do not think only of explicit pressures. The desire to be liked, fear of exclusion, anxiety about losing the approval of a community, apprehension about the reaction of a religious authority, or silencing one's conscience under the shadow of a powerful person all bring the same question before me: Whom am I truly magnifying?

33:37 directly makes visible the tension between fearing people and fearing GOD. 33:39 states that those who deliver the messages reverence Him alone and should fear no one but GOD. Here, the source of courage is not a person's self-confidence; it is knowing who carries out the reckoning.

3:28 and 3:30 connect this orientation with the final return and awareness of accountability. There may be caution, coercion, and real dangers in human relationships; the verse itself treats coercion separately. But surrendering the heart's ultimate center of fear and allegiance to another power is something else. Deep reverence should not turn into a coarse fearlessness toward every person; it should clarify to whom I give ultimate weight.

This distinction is also decisive for me regarding religious authority. A person may be knowledgeable, experienced, and may say something true. But no human being can take GOD's place. Deep reverence does not teach contempt for people; it teaches not placing a human being in GOD's place.


**Verse references:** 3:28, 3:30, 5:44, 33:37, 33:39.

### Reflection on the Verse

In 3:199, deep reverence and not trading away GOD's revelations for a cheap price meet in the same sentence. This shows that one's approach to revelation is not merely an intellectual matter, but a moral one. A person can read, transmit, and teach a text; but when their relationship with it begins to be shaped by interest, prestige, or the demands of the crowd, deep reverence is tested.

The message of 16:2 places monotheism and deep reverence side by side: proclaiming that there is no other god beside GOD is given together with deep reverence directed to Him alone. 50:45 explicitly identifies the Quran as the means of reminding and says that the person delivering the message has no power over people. Together, these establish a very important boundary for me: the message is powerful; the task of the person who conveys it is not to bind people to their own authority.

While 20:3 says that the reminder finds a response in the reverent person, 65:10 says, after calling believers and those who possess intelligence to reverence, that GOD has sent down a message. For this reason, encountering the Quran is not merely acquiring information. The message establishes a measure before a person and leads to a question heavier than “Do you know this?”: “What do you do in response to it?”

Earlier, in *Not Accepting Without Verification* and *Finding Is One Thing, Understanding Is Another*, I reflected on how knowledge does not remove human responsibility. Here the same line appears before me through another expression: deep reverence. Not placing knowledge above revelation, not placing the tool in the place of the source, and not imprisoning what I read outside my life.


**Verse references:** 3:199, 16:2, 20:3, 50:45, 65:10.

### Reflection on the Verse

The simplicity of 16:51 is strong enough to carry the entire section: the call not to worship two gods, to turn to only one god, and to reverence GOD alone all meet in the same sentence. 6:51 states that beside GOD there is no Lord and Master and no intercessor. 23:52 says, “I am your Lord; reverence Me,” while 29:16 directly places worshiping GOD and reverencing Him side by side. 30:31 makes submission, deep reverence, the Contact Prayers, and staying away from idol worship parts of the same orientation.

Alongside these verses, I cannot pass over 3:18 without reflection: bearing witness to the oneness of GOD is mentioned together with knowledge and justice. In 6:19, while GOD's testimony is declared the greatest, the human being is told not to bear witness that there are other gods. Testimony is not merely saying “one” with the tongue; it reveals which being life grants absolute authority to.

In 48:9, belief, reverence, observance, and glorification appear together. For me, the daily test of monotheism begins here: Am I moving a person, institution, tradition, system, my own self, or my fear into a place that belongs to GOD? Deep reverence is not monotheism's emotional shadow; it is monotheism's direction in life.

## 6. Obedience: The Boundary Between Following the Messenger and Taking a Human as Lord

### Reflection on the Verse

16:50 says that the angels reverence their Lord and do what they are commanded. This is one of the clearest examples of the relationship between deep reverence and obedience. Yet because the word obedience can so easily be abused in the human world, it is necessary to distinguish whom the verses call people to obey and in what context.

In 26:108, 26:110, and 26:179, the messengers repeatedly call, “Reverence GOD and obey me.” In 43:63, Jesus brings wisdom and clarification, then points in the same direction. In 71:3, worshiping GOD, reverencing Him, and obeying the messenger are mentioned together. Obedience to the messenger gains meaning not because the messenger establishes an independent sovereignty of his own, but because he carries GOD's message.

Here 9:31 is a protective boundary for me: people are criticized for taking their religious leaders and scholars as lords instead of GOD. This verse does not forbid listening to a knowledgeable person; rather, it exposes the danger of giving a knowledgeable person the place that belongs to GOD. In 24:52, obedience to GOD and His messenger meets deep reverence for GOD on the same line of success.

20:44 also makes me think that obedience and preaching should not turn into a coarse display of superiority. Moses and Aaron are told to speak gently even to Pharaoh. The truth of the message does not give a person the right to be harsh. If, while claiming deep reverence, I crush other people, I may turn my own ego into the center of the message.

The issue I reflected on in *The Weight of Testimony* joins deep reverence here. Just as whom I exalt, before whom I bow, and whose word I grant absoluteness are part of my monotheism, so is the truthfulness of my own speech. Deep reverence for GOD turns the ethics of language into a daily act of worship.

## 8. Knowledge: Deep Reverence as Awareness, Not Blindness

35:28 says that “the people who truly reverence GOD are those who possess knowledge.” This makes it difficult to view deep reverence as an ignorant trembling. A person can learn to look good in a crowd. In 50:33, those who reverenced the Most Gracious in their privacy are said to have come “wholeheartedly.” This expression is important to me: deep reverence is not merely a behavior imposed from outside; it is the willing return of the heart. If a person preserves the same orientation when alone, the foundation of display becomes weaker.

Previously, when writing about privacy in religious applications, I reflected on the moral weight of not entering another person's private space without permission. Here privacy opens from another direction: my relationship with GOD where no one else can see it. When the phone is off, the stage is over, no one applauds, and no one monitors me, what do I choose?

The quietest test of deep reverence may be here. A person can appear serious in public yet apply a completely different measure to themselves when alone. The verses present solitude not as a place where responsibility disappears, but as a place where orientation becomes clearer.

## 12. Moral Boundaries: Anger, Provision, Chastity, and the Self

This shows the boundary that deep reverence places in a moment of anger. Even when a person thinks they are right, they are not authorized to do anything whatsoever.

5:88 and 5:96 remind us of deep reverence in the context of lawful provision and established limits. In 11:78, the protection of guests and a moral boundary, and in 33:55, the ordering of private life, again close with a call to reverence GOD. The fields of these verses differ, but their common direction is the same: deep reverence becomes visible not only during worship, but in the boundaries of body, provision, anger, and relationships.

64:16, with the expression “as much as you can,” reminds me both of responsibility and of human capacity; then come listening, obedience, and giving. 79:40 joins deep reverence with restraining the self from sinful desires. This may be the summary of the whole section: deep reverence means not accepting every desire of the self as a right.

The self can produce very powerful reasons to legitimize whatever it wants. Deep reverence does not erase the voice of the self; it puts a boundary around it. Preserving the difference between “I want it” and “it is right” is the foundation of inner discipline.


**Verse references:** 5:28, 5:88, 5:96, 11:78, 33:55, 64:16, 79:40.

5:57 reminds us to reverence GOD while addressing the pressure of those who mock belief and the boundaries of friendship. 31:33 says that this life and insignificant illusions must not divert a person from GOD. One points to social pressure, the other to the deceptive attraction of the world.

## 14. Awareness of Accountability: What Have I Sent Ahead for Tomorrow?

### Verse

### Reflection on the Verse

59:18 is one of the most personal verses in this article for me: every soul is asked to look at what it has sent ahead “for tomorrow.” Deep reverence is repeated twice, and GOD is declared fully aware of everything that is done. This shows how narrow it is to measure life only by the reaction received today.

In 13:21, deep reverence and fear of the dreadful reckoning stand side by side. 22:1 reminds us of the quaking of the Hour. 70:27 describes those who carry the seriousness of their Lord's retribution. 39:16 makes the warning personal with the words, “O My servants, reverence Me.”

Awareness of accountability does not have to produce despair. On the contrary, it makes the possibility of changing today serious. If I can examine today what I am sending ahead for tomorrow, I still have the possibility of correcting my direction. Deep reverence brings the future reckoning into today's conduct.

My question to myself is simple but difficult to escape: Which thing I do today would I want to see when it is placed before me tomorrow? Which word, earning, silence, or anger of mine would I wish to be very far away? The scene drawn by 3:30 does not leave this question abstract.


**Verse references:** 13:21, 22:1, 39:16, 59:18, 70:27.

## 19. The End of the Promise: The Fruit of Deep Reverence

### Verse

> **14:14** — “And after them, we will let you settle in their land. This is the reward for those who reverence My majesty and reverence My promise.”

### Reflection on the Verse

14:14 announces an outcome for those who reverence GOD's majesty and His promise. In 39:73, those who reverence their Lord are ushered into Paradise and hear, “Peace be upon you; you have won.” This prevents me from reading the end of deep reverence only through the language of fear. At the end of the path there is peace.

98:8 describes a reciprocity in which GOD is pleased with them and they are pleased with Him. This verse takes me back to 2:40. The article began with a sentence about covenant; at the end there is approval and recompense. The human being is not bargaining with GOD, but fulfills their responsibility by trusting His promise.

For this reason, reducing deep reverence only to fear of punishment would be incomplete. The verses in the document show deep reverence together with covenant, monotheism, obedience, truthful speech, justice, privacy, knowledge, worship, striving, restraint of the self, accountability, and hope. This breadth teaches me not to confine the expression to a single emotion.

Where I arrive is this: deep reverence is recognizing GOD's true weight in the heart and in life. It diminishes the pressure of people, limits the claims of the self, calls speech toward truthfulness, and carries the future reckoning into today's decision. Most importantly, it makes the direction one: GOD alone.


**Verse references:** 14:14, 39:73, 98:8.

## On the Arabic Roots of the Words

When I look at the Arabic sentence of 2:40 in terms of roots, I see that the flow of meaning in the verse is also connected within the words themselves. Rather than making roots an independent source of judgment, it seems safer to consider them together with the verse's own Turkish and English renderings.

The root **ذ ك ر (Dhal-Kaf-Ra)** appears in the verse in the expression **ٱذْكُرُوا۟ (udhkurū)**. The Authorized English Text says “remember,” and the Turkish text says “hatırlayın.” The first movement of deep reverence begins by breaking forgetfulness. When the blessing is forgotten, the covenant can easily look like a burden; when it is remembered, responsibility becomes a response to goodness received.

The root **ن ع م (Nun-Ayn-Mim)** is connected with **نِعْمَتِىَ (ni'matī)** and the idea of favor in the verse. The Turkish translation says, “Remember My favor, which I bestowed upon you.” Thus deep reverence is nourished not only by the possibility of punishment, but also by the remembrance of goodness and grace.

The root **ع ه د (Ayn-Ha-Dal)** carries the covenant in the expressions **بِعَهْدِىٓ (bi-ahdī) / بِعَهْدِكُمْ (bi-ahdikum)**. The central structure of the verse establishes a language of reciprocity: “Fulfill your part of the covenant, that I may fulfill My part of the covenant.” This reciprocity does not make GOD and the human being equal; it makes human responsibility and the reliability of GOD's promise visible in the same sentence.

The root **و ف ي (Waw-Fa-Ya)** carries the idea of fulfillment through the forms **أَوْفُوا۟ (awfū) / أُوفِ (ūfi)**. Remembering is not left here as a passive mental activity; it becomes faithfulness. The doorway through which deep reverence opens into conduct is very clear in these verbs.

The root **ر ه ب (Ra-Ha-Ba)** appears at the end of the verse in the form **فَٱرْهَبُونِ (farhabūnī)**. The Authorized English Text renders it as “reverence Me,” while the Turkish text renders it as “Bana derin saygı duyun.” Reading this choice together with the other verses in the article makes me think that I should not understand the word merely as panic or paralyzing fear. The same deep reverence is mentioned together with truthful speech, obedience, hope, inward orientation in privacy, justice, knowledge, and restraining the self.

The roots do not establish a new religion for me; they help me notice more carefully the connection already visible in the verse: **remember → recognize the blessing → remain faithful to the covenant → reverence GOD alone.**

## Other Claims

### Verse

> **2:40** — O Children of Israel, remember My favor, which I bestowed upon you. Fulfill your part of the covenant, that I may fulfill My part of the covenant. Reverence Me.

> **5:44** — We sent down the Torah,* containing guidance and light. The Jewish prophets, as well as the rabbis and priests, ruled by it as commanded in GOD's scripture and as they themselves witnessed. Therefore, do not reverence the people; instead, reverence Me. And do not trade away My revelations for a cheap price. Those who do not rule in accordance with GOD's revelations are disbelievers.

> **16:51** — GOD has proclaimed: “Do not worship two gods; there is only one god. Reverence Me alone.”

> **35:28** — Also, people, animals, and livestock come in various colors. Thus, the people who truly reverence GOD are those who possess knowledge. GOD is Almighty, Forgiving.

> **3:18** — GOD bears witness that there is no god except Him, and so do the angels and those who possess knowledge. Truthfully and justly, He is the absolute god; there is no god except Him, the Almighty, Most Wise.

### Reflection on the Verse

Different words may be preferred for the expression “deep reverence” in different translation and interpretive traditions. Some approaches may explain the expression at the end of the verse with an emphasis on fear, some through the language of caution or awe, and others through a semantic field that carries reverence and trembling together. The existence of these differences is not, by itself, sufficient to pass judgment on a person's intention or faith.

The main line of this article does not rest on those external interpretations. The text I rely on here is the Authorized English Translation together with the Turkish rendering given in the document: **“reverence Me / Bana derin saygı duyun.”** I have tried to consider this rendering together with the other verses in the same document where the expression “deep reverence” appears.

Another claim may be that deep reverence is only fear and should not be connected with love, hope, or awareness. Yet the verses used in this article show deep reverence together with hope in 7:56, knowledge in 35:28, truthful speech in 9:119 and 33:70, self-examination in 59:18, and justice in 65:2. For this reason, a single emotional definition may narrow the broad relationship established by the verses.

There are also different approaches regarding religious authority. While some traditions grant religious leaders and scholars broad interpretive authority, the warning in 9:31 against taking religious leaders and scholars as lords instead of GOD remains a boundary for me. I read this verse not as a reason to reject every knowledgeable person, but as a reason not to absolutize any human being in GOD's place. The statement in 5:44, “do not reverence the people; instead, reverence Me,” strengthens this boundary as well.

The mention of those who possess knowledge within the testimony of 3:18 also prevents devaluing the knowledgeable person. Value and absolute authority are not the same thing. A knowledgeable person may bear true testimony, but does not become GOD. The messenger delivers the message, but the owner of the message is GOD. A person may listen to an intermediary, but cannot change the center of worship and ultimate deep reverence.

Mentioning these different claims does not change the main conclusion of the article. For me, the shared direction of the verses is simpler: the ultimate address of deep reverence is GOD, and this orientation is tested by truthfulness, justice, knowledge, submission, and responsibility.

## Conclusion

When I return to Al-Baqarah 2:40, I no longer hear the final sentence of the verse on its own. The words “Reverence Me” echo together with the two movements that precede them: remember the blessing and fulfill your part of the covenant.

When I consider together all 99 verses listed under “Verse Analysis” on www.kuranteyit.com in this article, under 19 headings, I see that deep reverence is a measure of life too broad to be reduced to a single emotion. The verses connect it with monotheism, truthful speech, knowledge, justice, worship, privacy, trust, striving, accountability, hope, and restraining the self.

The clearest line is the matter of direction. 5:44 says to reverence GOD, not people. 16:51 says, “Reverence Me alone.” 33:39 states that those who deliver the messages reverence Him alone. For me, these three emphases make clear the emotional and moral center of monotheism: a person can respect many people, learn from many people, and love many people; but they cannot give another being the ultimate weight that belongs to GOD.

Deep reverence also does not appear to be a language constructed to diminish the human being. 8:29 speaks of enlightenment and forgiveness, 57:28 of light and mercy, 39:20 and 39:73 of reward and peace, and 98:8 of servants with whom GOD is pleased. The seriousness of accountability and the openness of hope stand on the same map.

For this reason, I want to test deep reverence in my own life with these questions: Do I remember the blessings? Do I keep my promises? Do I place people's approval before GOD? Am I truthful? Do I preserve the same measure when I am alone? Does my knowledge make me more humble and more responsible? Do I know a boundary in my anger? Do I look today at what I am sending ahead for tomorrow?

2:40 does more than command an emotion. It reminds me of my direction.

**Remember the blessing. Remain faithful to the covenant. Reverence GOD alone.**
