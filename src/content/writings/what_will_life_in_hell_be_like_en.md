# What Will Life in Hell Be Like? An Existence Death Does Not End and Escape Does Not Open

**Author:** Berk KÖKSAL  
**Date:** October 21, 2026  
**Place:** Mersin

The main purpose of this article is to contemplate the verses together with other relevant verses and to explore, sincerely and deeply, what bearing witness to the oneness of GOD means for human life in terms of knowledge, justice, truthfulness, and submission.

## 1. An Existence Death Does Not End: What Does “Life” in Hell Mean?

### Verse

**35:36**

**Arabic:** وَٱلَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا۟ لَهُمْ نَارُ جَهَنَّمَ لَا يُقْضَىٰ عَلَيْهِمْ فَيَمُوتُوا۟ وَلَا يُخَفَّفُ عَنْهُم مِّنْ عَذَابِهَا كَذَٰلِكَ نَجْزِى كُلَّ كَفُورٍ

**English:** As  for  those  who  disbelieve,  they have  incurred  the  fire  of  Hell,  where they  are  never  finished  by  death,  nor is  the  retribution  ever  commuted  for them.  We  thus  requite  the  unappreciative.

**Turkish:** İnkâr edenlere gelince, Cehennemin ateşini üzerlerine çekmişlerdir; orada ne ölüm onları sona erdirir, ne de azap onlar için hafifletilir. Biz nankörlere işte böyle karşılık veririz.

### Reflection on the Verse

35:36 establishes a disturbing boundary: death does not terminate the people there, and the retribution is not lightened. In this world, death is the final boundary of bodily pain. In the description of Hell, that escape is closed.

This means “What will life in Hell be like?” cannot be answered by simply extending ordinary worldly life. The verse emphasizes continuation without relief. Duration remains, but no movement toward recovery is described.

The thought is morally heavy: here we often comfort ourselves with “everything eventually ends.” The verse warns that some consequences cannot be treated as temporary simply because we wish them to be.

## 2. “Neither Dies Nor Lives”: When Existence Itself Loses the Meaning of Life

### Verse

> **20:74** — Anyone  who  comes  to  his  Lord guilty  will  incur  Hell,  wherein  he never dies, nor stays alive.

### Reflection on the Verse

20:74 sharpens the paradox: the guilty person who enters Hell “neither dies nor lives.” This sounds less like a biological formula than a collapse of what makes life worth calling life. Existence continues, but peace, growth, hope, and escape do not.

In this world, to be alive includes the possibility of changing direction. One can repent, learn, repair, and return. In the scenes of Hell, people still remember, speak, regret, and ask to be sent back, but awareness no longer becomes a new opportunity.

Hell is therefore not only intensity of pain; it is awareness after the period in which awareness could change the outcome has ended.

## 3. The Body Continues to Feel

### Verse

> **4:56** — Surely,  those  who  disbelieve  in  our revelations,  we  will  condemn  them to  the  hellfire.  Whenever  their  skins are  burnt,  we  will  give  them  new skins.  Thus,  they  will  suffer  continuously.  GOD  is  Almighty,  Most Wise.

### Reflection on the Verse

4:56 speaks of burned skins being replaced so that suffering continues. The verse makes it difficult to reduce Hell to psychological regret alone; bodily language is explicit.

What weighs most heavily is the continuity of suffering. In worldly life pain may be interrupted by unconsciousness, healing, or death. The verse removes those familiar endings.

It also changes how I see the body now. Today pain can warn and protect. In Hell, the capacity to feel is no longer a warning before danger; it is part of a consequence already reached.

## 4. Trying to Escape and Being Forced Back

### Verse

> **22:19** — Here  are  two  parties  feuding  with regard  to  their  Lord.  As  for  those who  disbelieve,  they  will  have clothes  of  fire  tailored  for  them. Hellish  liquid  will  be  poured  on  top of their heads.

> **22:20** — It  will  cause  their  insides  to  melt,  as well as their skins.

> **22:21** — They will be confined in iron pots. 

> **22:22** — Whenever  they  try  to  exit  such  misery,  they  will  be  forced  back  in: “Taste the agony of burning.”

### Reflection on the Verse

22:19-22 describes garments of fire, hellish liquid, melting interiors and skins, and then the repeated attempt to escape. Whenever they try to leave, they are forced back.

The phrase “whenever” is striking. The desire to leave remains, but the time in which changing direction could alter destiny has passed.

In this life a wrong path can still be abandoned. Hell makes the value of repentance visible by contrast: returning today is radically different from merely wanting escape after judgment.

## 5. Thirst Remains, but Water Does Not Rescue

### Verse

> **7:50** — The dwellers of Hell will call on the dwellers of Paradise: “Let some of your water, or some of GOD’s provisions to you flow towards us.” They will say, “GOD has forbidden them for the disbelievers.”

### Reflection on the Verse

7:50 shows the people of Hell asking the people of Paradise for water or some of GOD’s provision. Desire and need have not disappeared, but access has.

In Paradise water is provision. Here it becomes something seen and requested but unavailable.

One of the hardest dimensions of Hell may be precisely this: knowing what would bring relief while being unable to reach it. Recognition no longer equals participation.

## 6. Food That Does Not Nourish

### Verse

> **44:43** — Surely, the tree of bitterness—

> **44:44** — will provide the food for the sinful.

> **44:45** — Like lye, it will boil in the stomachs.

> **44:46** — Like the boiling of hellish drinks.

### Reflection on the Verse

44:43-46 speaks of the tree of bitterness as food for the sinner, boiling in the stomach like caustic material.

Food normally sustains life. In Paradise it is delight. In Hell, the same field of human need becomes part of suffering.

The verses do not authorize a technical theory of how bodies there function. What they clearly establish is that the language of food and drink is no longer the language of relief.

## 7. Drinking Without Relief

### Verse

> **47:15** — The  allegory  of  Paradise  that  is promised for the righteous is this: it has rivers of unpolluted water, and rivers of fresh milk, and rivers of wine—delicious  for  the  drinkers— and rivers of strained honey. They have all kinds of fruits therein, and forgiveness from their Lord. (Are they better) or those who abide forever in the hellfire, and drink hellish water that tears up their intestines?

### Reflection on the Verse

47:15 contrasts the rivers of Paradise with hellish water that tears the intestines.

The ordinary relationship between thirst, water, and relief is broken. Need does not lead to healing.

This also creates a moral image that reaches back into present life: human beings can seek relief in what deepens their suffering. Hell presents that structure in its most severe form.

## 8. The Moment Money, Power, and Friendship Fail

### Verse

> **69:25** — As for him who is given his record in his left hand, he will say, “Oh, I wish I never received my record.

> **69:26** — “I wish I never knew my account.

> **69:27** — “I wish my death was eternal.

> **69:28** — “My money cannot help me.

> **69:29** — “All my power is gone.” 

> **69:30** — Take him and shackle him.

> **69:31** — Burn him in Hell.

> **69:32** — In  a  chain  that  is  seventy  arms  long, tie him up.

> **69:33** — For he did not believe in GOD, Most Great.

> **69:34** — Nor  did  he  advocate  the  feeding  of the poor.

> **69:35** — Consequently,  he  has  no  friend here.

> **69:36** — Nor  any  food,  except  the  bitter variety.

> **69:37** — Food for the sinners.

### Reflection on the Verse

69:25-37 portrays a collapse of every worldly support. The person wishes he had never received the record, wishes death had been final, and admits that money cannot help and power is gone.

Then comes the statement that he has no friend there. Status and social capital cannot rescue him.

The passage also joins disbelief with failure to advocate feeding the poor. The warning is therefore not merely abstract theology; it reaches into how we respond to another person’s hunger.

## 9. Leaders and Followers in the Same Fire

### Verse

> **40:47** — As  they  argue  in  Hell,  the  followers will  say  to  their  leaders,  “We  used  to be  your  followers,  can  you  spare  us any part of this Hell?”

> **40:48** — The  leaders  will  say,  “We  are  all  in this  together.  GOD  has  judged among the people.”

> **40:49** — Those  in  the  hellfire  will  say  to  the guardians  of  Hell,  “Call  upon  your Lord  to  reduce  the  retribution  for us, for even one day.”

> **40:50** — They  will  say,  “Did  you  not  receive your  messengers  who  delivered  to you  clear  messages?”  They  will reply,  “Yes  we  did.”  They  will  say, “Then implore (as much as you wish);  the  imploring  of  the  disbelievers is always in vain.”

### Reflection on the Verse

40:47-50 shows followers asking leaders to spare them part of the fire. The leaders answer that they are all in it together.

Leadership can create the illusion that responsibility can be transferred. The Hereafter destroys that illusion. Leaders cannot carry followers out; followers cannot erase agency by saying that they only obeyed.

Then the people ask the keepers for even one day of relief. In an eternal setting, the request for a single day is one of the most revealing descriptions of how the punishment is experienced.

## 10. “If Only We Had Obeyed”: Recognition Too Late

### Verse

> **33:65** — Eternally  they  abide  therein.  They will find no lord, nor a supporter.

> **33:66** — The  day  they  are  thrown  into  Hell, they  will  say,  “Oh,  we  wish  we obeyed  GOD,  and  obeyed  the  messenger.”

> **33:67** — They  will  also  say,  “Our  Lord,  we have  obeyed  our  masters  and  leaders, but they led us astray.

> **33:68** — “Our  Lord,  give  them  double  the retribution,  and  curse  them  a  tremendous curse.”

### Reflection on the Verse

33:66-68 records a direct confession: if only we had obeyed GOD and the messenger. They then blame the masters and leaders they had followed.

The problem is no longer lack of information. It is late evaluation.

The passage makes present religious authority morally serious. Following a leader, scholar, family, or community does not cancel personal responsibility before GOD.

## 11. Friendships Turning into Curses

### Verse

> **29:25** — He said, “You worship beside GOD powerless idols due to peer pressure, just to preserve some friendship among you in this worldly life. But then, on the Day of Resurrection, you will disown one another, and curse one another. Your destiny is Hell, wherein you cannot help one another.”

### Reflection on the Verse

29:25 says that worldly bonds built around idols become rejection and mutual cursing on the Day of Resurrection. Togetherness in Hell does not become comfort.

A crowd can make a wrong path feel safe in this world. In the Hereafter, numbers do not dilute responsibility.

The verse therefore gives a severe test for relationships: does this bond help me move toward GOD and truth, or does it merely make shared error feel normal?

## 12. “Had We Listened or Understood”: The Mind Wakes Too Late

### Verse

> **67:6** — For those who disbelieved in their Lord, the retribution of Gehenna. What a miserable destiny. 

> **67:7** — When they get thrown therein, they hear its furor as it fumes.

> **67:8** — It  almost  explodes  from  rage. Whenever a group is thrown therein, its guards would ask them, “Did you not receive a warner?” 

> **67:9** — They would answer, “Yes indeed; a warner did come to us, but we disbelieved  and  said,  ‘GOD  did  not reveal  anything.  You are totally astray.’ ”

> **67:10** — They also say, “If we heard or understood, we would not be among the dwellers of Hell!”

> **67:11** — Thus, they confessed their sins. Woe to the dwellers of Hell.

### Reflection on the Verse

67:10 gives one of the most powerful confessions in the Quran: had we listened or understood, we would not be among the dwellers of Hell.

Hell is therefore connected not only with fear but with the ethics of knowledge. Hearing is not merely receiving sound; one must take truth seriously. Understanding is not merely intelligence; one must allow truth to change direction.

The mind awakens there, but too late. Reflection today is more valuable than regret tomorrow.

## 13. “Take Us Out”—Until Even Speech Is Closed

### Verse

> **23:103** — Those  whose  weights  are  light  are the  ones  who  lost  their  souls;  they abide in Hell forever.

> **23:104** — Fire  will  overwhelm  their  faces,  and they last miserably therein.

> **23:105** — Were  not  My  revelations  recited  to you,  and  you  kept  on  rejecting them?

> **23:106** — They  will  say,  “Our  Lord,  our  wickedness  overwhelmed  us,  and  we were people gone astray.

> **23:107** — “Our  Lord,  take  us  out  of  this;  if  we return  (to  our  old  behavior),  then  we are really wicked.”

> **23:108** — He  will  say,  “Abide  therein,  humiliated, and do not speak to Me.

### Reflection on the Verse

23:106-108 shows the people admitting their error and asking to be taken out. The answer is severe: remain humiliated there and do not speak to Me.

In this world prayer, repentance, and return are open. The scene portrays a time when the same words no longer change the outcome because the period of choosing has been exhausted.

This does not make mercy meaningless. It makes the present opening of mercy more precious.

## 14. Asking for a Second Life

### Verse

> **35:36** — As  for  those  who  disbelieve,  they have  incurred  the  fire  of  Hell,  where they  are  never  finished  by  death,  nor is  the  retribution  ever  commuted  for them.  We  thus  requite  the  unappreciative.

> **35:37** — They  will  scream  therein,  “Our Lord,  if  you  get  us  out  of  here,  we will  work  righteousness,  instead  of the  works  we  used  to  do.”  Did  we not  give  you  a  life-long  chance,  with continuous  reminders  for  those  who would  take  heed?  Did  you  not  receive  the  warner?  Therefore,  taste (the  consequences).  The  transgressors will have no one to help them.

### Reflection on the Verse

35:37 records a specific promise: if they are taken out, they will work righteousness instead of what they used to do. The response reminds them that they were given a lifetime in which anyone willing to take heed could do so, and that a warner came.

Time itself is revealed as a moral gift. A day we treat as ordinary may later be understood as an irretrievable opportunity.

One of the needs in Hell seems to be “one more chance,” but that chance belongs to the present life.

## 15. Confinement, Chains, and Many Regrets

### Verse

> **25:12** — When  it  sees  them  from  afar,  they will hear its rage and fuming.

> **25:13** — And  when  they  are  thrown  into  it, through  a  narrow  place,  all  shackled, they will declare their remorse.

> **25:14** — You  will  not  declare  just  a  single remorse,  on  that  day;  you  will  suffer through  a  great  number  of  remorses.

### Reflection on the Verse

25:12-14 describes Hell’s sound from afar, confinement in a narrow place while chained, and not one regret but many regrets.

Freedom is a gift we often use without noticing: the ability to move, leave, reconsider, and change direction. Here space itself is constricted, and consciousness is filled with regret.

The verses do not explain the exact psychology of those many regrets, but they clearly make regret a defining part of the experience.

## 16. Repeated Attempts to Leave

### Verse

> **32:20** — As for the wicked, their destiny is Hell. Every time they try to leave it, they will be forced back. They will be told, “Taste the agony of Hell which you used to disbelieve in.”

### Reflection on the Verse

32:20 is brief and severe: whenever they try to leave the fire, they are forced back.

The desire to escape remains. Desire by itself, however, is no longer enough.

The verse therefore magnifies present agency. Before the outcome is fixed, wanting to change can still become actual change.

## 17. Groaning and Isolation from News

### Verse

> **21:98** — You  and  the  idols  you  worship  besides GOD will be fuel  for Hell; this is your inevitable destiny.

> **21:99** — If  those  were  gods,  they  would  not have  ended  up  in  Hell.  All  its  inhabitants abide in it forever.

> **21:100** — They  will  sigh  and  groan  therein, and they will have no access to any news.

### Reflection on the Verse

21:98-100 speaks of groaning and of having no access to news. The suffering is not only bodily; the horizon itself appears closed.

In this world, news often means possibility: perhaps help is coming, perhaps something has changed. Lack of access to any news evokes a state in which expectation itself has narrowed.

We should not invent details beyond the verse. What is clear is the portrayal of Hell as a closed and heavy condition rather than an open-ended life of possibility.

## 18. The Moral Summary of the Road to Hell

### Verse

> **74:38** — Every soul is trapped by its sins.

> **74:39** — Except for those on the right.

> **74:40** — While in Paradise, they will ask.

> **74:41** — About the guilty.

> **74:42** — “What brought you to this retribution?”

> **74:43** — They will say, “We did not observe the contact prayers (Salat).

> **74:44** — “We did not feed the poor.

> **74:45** — “We blundered with the blunderers.

> **74:46** — “We disbelieved in the Day of Judgment.

> **74:47** — “Until certainty came to us now.”

### Reflection on the Verse

The question is direct: what brought you here? The answers bring the subject of Hell back into present ethics.

They did not observe the Contact Prayers, did not feed the poor, joined in vain talk, and denied the Day of Judgment until certainty arrived.

This list is crucial. Hell is not presented merely as the consequence of holding one wrong abstract idea. Relationship with GOD, care for the poor, the quality of speech, and awareness of accountability appear together.

The healthiest contemplation of Hell therefore turns “What will happen there?” into “What am I doing here?”

## 19. Arabic Word Roots: The Language in Which Death and Relief Are Closed

### Verse

> **35:36** — As  for  those  who  disbelieve,  they have  incurred  the  fire  of  Hell,  where they  are  never  finished  by  death,  nor is  the  retribution  ever  commuted  for them.  We  thus  requite  the  unappreciative.

### Reflection on the Verse

The Arabic structure of 35:36 concentrates the continuity and closure of Hell into several strong root fields.

**كَفَرُوا — k-f-r (ك ف ر):** the field of covering, rejecting, and ingratitude. The verse connects the outcome with rejection of truth.

**نَار — n-w-r / n-y-r field (ن و ر / ن ي ر):** fire and burning. Fire is among the Quran’s most basic sensory images for Hell, here joined to continuing punishment.

**يُقْضَىٰ — q-ḍ-y (ق ض ي):** to judge, complete, conclude, settle. The clause indicates that their condition is not concluded through death.

**يَمُوتُوا — m-w-t (م و ت):** to die. Death, which ends mortal bodily life, no longer functions as an exit.

**يُخَفَّفُ — kh-f-f (خ ف ف):** to become light or be reduced. The punishment is not lightened.

**عَذَاب — ʿ-dh-b (ع ذ ب):** punishment, torment, painful consequence. Across the article, bodily pain, regret, isolation, and deprivation appear as different dimensions of the same judged condition.

**نَجْزِي — j-z-y (ج ز ي):** to recompense or repay. This frames the descriptions morally: the scenes are presented as consequences and recompense, not random cruelty.

Together the movement is: **rejection → recompense → fire → no termination through death → no reduction of punishment.** The weight of 35:36 lies not only in pain, but in the closing of the doors that would normally end pain.

## Other Claims

### Verse

> **22:22** — Whenever  they  try  to  exit  such  misery,  they  will  be  forced  back  in: “Taste the agony of burning.”

### Reflection on the Verse

Across history, interpreters have differed over how literally or symbolically the descriptions of Hell should be read. The note attached to 22:19-22 in the authorized translation itself remarks that the horrors of Hell may be expressed symbolically while still being presented in the most graphic terms. This allows room for caution about assuming that every image operates according to the physics of our present world.

At the same time, the verse texts themselves repeatedly use concrete language: fire, skin, food, drink, chains, thirst, regret, speech, requests for help, and attempts to escape. It would therefore also go beyond the verses to dismiss all of this as “only metaphor.”

Claims about the precise location, physical mechanism, experience of time, or detailed metaphysics of punishment should remain secondary. The secure ground of the verses is that Hell is a real outcome of judgment associated with pain, deprivation, regret, failed help, and the closing of escape.

## Conclusion

### Verse

> **67:10** — They also say, “If we heard or understood, we would not be among the dwellers of Hell!”

### Reflection on the Verse

When we reflected on Paradise, the natural question was, “What is there?” With Hell, the question increasingly becomes, “Which exits are no longer there?”

Death does not end the condition. Escape attempts fail. Even one day of relief is not granted. Water is requested but the water of Paradise is inaccessible. Food does not comfort. Money cannot help. Power disappears. Friends cannot rescue. Leaders cannot carry followers. Followers cannot transfer responsibility to leaders. People ask to be sent back, but the opportunity of worldly life does not return.

The most terrifying feature of Hell may therefore not be fire alone. It is **the end of the period in which recognizing the truth can still change one’s direction.**

In this world a person can doubt, delay, deceive himself, follow the wrong authority, neglect the poor, or join empty speech—and still turn back. That is what makes 67:10 so severe: “Had we listened or understood...” The capacity was there; it was not used.

I do not want these verses to produce fear without transformation. Their purpose in contemplation should be to wake a person while return is still possible. If I do not want to ask tomorrow for one day of relief, I should not delay repentance today. If I do not want to blame leaders tomorrow, I should not place any human being above GOD’s word today. If I do not want to beg for water tomorrow, I should not ignore the gifts given to me or the hunger of others today.

Paradise opens with phrases such as “whatever they wish” and “even more.” Hell closes with phrases such as “they cannot get out,” “it will not be lightened,” “there is no helper,” and death does not end it.

That contrast reveals the value of present freedom. The doors are not closed yet. I can still pray, return, act justly, feed, listen, understand, and change.

Perhaps the healthiest contemplation of Hell is not fascination with future suffering, but awakening to present possibility.
