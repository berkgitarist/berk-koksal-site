# [6:138] How Is a Religious Innovation Invented? When Human Rules Are Attributed to GOD

**Author:** Berk KÖKSAL  
**Date:** November 17, 2026  
**Location:** Mersin

Peace. Peace be upon you.

How does a person invent a religious ruling? The first image that comes to mind might be a ceremony, a new behavior, or an unfamiliar practice. Yet when I stand before 6:138, the problem begins somewhere quieter: with the claim that only certain people have permission to use what has been provided. A human being draws a boundary and then places GOD's name behind it. Those are not the same act.

My own life requires choices and limits. I leave some habits behind and adopt others. I form opinions and defend them. But I must notice the moment at which my decision starts being presented as a religious command binding other people. What seems sensible to me may not be decreed by GOD. What seems unfamiliar to me is not automatically forbidden either.

When I read 6:138 beside 57:27, 42:21, and 16:116, different expressions bring me back to the same responsibility: how truthful am I about the source of a rule I have invented, inherited, defended, or taught? Could I make another human being's words sacred merely because I admire the person? Could I do the same with my own words because I admire my own conclusion?

I am not writing this to find an easy label for other people. I want to examine **the limit of speaking in GOD's name, beginning with my own language**. The connection between monotheism, knowledge, justice, truthfulness, and submission becomes difficult to ignore here. I need to remember the difference between trying to understand a verse and speaking as if I owned it.

## 1. How Does an Innovation Begin? Presenting a Human Claim as GOD’s Law

### Verse

> **6:138 — العربية**
> وَقَالُوا۟ هَٰذِهِۦٓ أَنْعَٰمٌ وَحَرْثٌ حِجْرٌ لَّا يَطْعَمُهَآ إِلَّا مَن نَّشَآءُ بِزَعْمِهِمْ وَأَنْعَٰمٌ حُرِّمَتْ ظُهُورُهَا وَأَنْعَٰمٌ لَّا يَذْكُرُونَ ٱسْمَ ٱللَّهِ عَلَيْهَا ٱفْتِرَآءً عَلَيْهِ سَيَجْزِيهِم بِمَا كَانُوا۟ يَفْتَرُونَ

> **6:138 — English**
> They  said,  “These  are  livestock  and crops  that  are  prohibited;  no  one shall  eat  them  except  whomever  we permit,”  so  they  claimed. ### Reflection on the Verse

Sometimes the clearest way to understand a wrong is to look at the moment it first becomes a rule. In 6:138 people decide who may consume certain provisions, forbid riding particular animals, and make their own restrictions binding. These are not presented merely as personal preferences. They become religious prohibitions, and the verse calls them innovations attributed to GOD. What troubles me is not only the content of the restrictions but **the source of the authority claimed for them**.

A person can make choices about personal conduct. I may avoid a food, change a habit, or decline a practice for reasons of conscience or health. But when I announce my choice as a command from GOD and impose it on other people, the nature of my claim changes. The people described in 6:138 were doing more than choosing for themselves. They decided who had permission, then clothed that decision in a sacred authority it did not possess.

This makes me examine my own speech. When I tell another person not to do something, what am I really saying? 'I do not think this is wise' is different from 'GOD has prohibited this.' The first sentence admits that I am speaking from my understanding. The second speaks in the name of revelation. I ought to feel the weight of that difference before I use it.

Perhaps an innovation does not always begin with an impressive ceremony. It may begin with a modest sentence in which a human preference is quietly presented as a divine decree. Verse reference: **6:138**.

## 2. The Cost of Invented Rules: Exclusion and Human Lives

### Verse

> **6:139** — They  also  said,  “What  is  in  the  bellies  of  these  livestock  is  reserved exclusively  for  the  males  among  us, and  prohibited  for  our  wives.”  But  if it  was  a  stillbirth,  they  permitted their  wives  to  share  therein.  He  will certainly  requite  them  for  their  innovations.  He  is  Most  Wise,  Omniscient.

> **6:140** — Losers  indeed  are  those  who  killed their  children  foolishly,  due  to  their lack  of  knowledge,  and  prohibited what  GOD  has  provided  for  them, and  followed  innovations  attributed to  GOD.  They  have  gone  astray; they are not guided. 

### Reflection on the Verse

Verse 6:139 shows how these rules operate among people. Certain provisions are reserved for men and withheld from women, yet a different outcome changes who may share them. An apparently strict rule sits beside an arbitrary exception. I see how easily a human arrangement can start posing as a measure of righteousness: first a group is excluded, then the exclusion begins to feel self-evidently right.

The next verse is more severe. In 6:140, ignorance, the foolish killing of children, and the prohibition of provisions granted by GOD appear together. I do not read these as interchangeable acts. Rather, I see the real-world consequences that can accompany false authority and a failure to understand. The problem is not simply an abstract disagreement over terminology. Human beings are affected by what others claim GOD demands.

Whenever I hear a confident religious ruling, there is a question I must not neglect: who carries the burden of this ruling? It matters how a custom began, but it also matters whose rights and lives are diminished by its enforcement. Invoking GOD's name does not lessen my responsibility toward the person standing in front of me. It heightens it.

A practice can become common without becoming true. Familiarity does not erase its cost. These verses make the consequences of invented religious restrictions impossible for me to treat as a minor vocabulary dispute. Verse references: **6:139; 6:140**.

## 3. Who Invented Hermitism? Is Good Intention Enough?

### Verse

> **57:27** — Subsequent to them, we sent our messengers. We sent Jesus the son of Mary, and we gave him the Injeel (Gospel),  and  we  placed  in  the hearts of his followers kindness and mercy. But they invented hermitism which we never decreed for them. All we asked them to do was to uphold  the  commandments  approved by GOD. But they did not uphold the message as they should have. Consequently, we gave those who  believed  among  them  their recompense, while  many of them were wicked.

### Reflection on the Verse

Verse 57:27 approaches the issue from a direction I might easily overlook. It first speaks of the kindness and mercy placed in the hearts of Jesus' followers. Then it says that they invented hermitism, which had not been decreed for them. That sequence matters. Genuine compassion among people does not automatically make every new religious discipline they devise a divine command. Affection and authorization are not the same thing.

I may want to draw nearer to GOD. I may adopt a discipline, arrange my day around remembrance, or devote more time to an act of care. Yet this verse does not grant me permission to turn a personal discipline into a compulsory religious law for everyone. Nor does it say that every new method, tool, or voluntary practice is necessarily prohibited. It speaks of a particular invented hermitism and a failure to uphold the message properly.

Its ending is equally important. The verse does not assign every member of that community the same final standing. It says believers among them received their reward while many others were wicked. I would be distorting its distinction if I used the account to judge every person's inner intentions with absolute certainty.

The question becomes personal: **At what point do I mistake an expression of devotion that I have chosen for a command GOD has issued?** Good intentions do not remove my need to answer honestly. Verse reference: **57:27**.

## 4. Who May Decree Religious Law? The Danger of Shared Authority

### Verse

> **42:21** — They  follow  idols  who  decree  for them  religious  laws  never  authorized  by  GOD.  If  it  were  not  for the  predetermined  decision,  they would  have  been  judged  immediately.  Indeed,  the  transgressors have  incurred  a  painful  retribution.*

> **12:40** — “You  do  not  worship  beside  Him except  innovations  that  you  have made  up,  you  and  your  parents. GOD  has  never  authorized  such idols.  All  ruling  belongs  to  GOD, and  He  has  ruled  that  you  shall  not worship  except  Him.  This  is  the  perfect  religion,  but  most  people  do  not know.

### Reflection on the Verse

The issue in 42:21 is direct: religious laws are decreed without GOD's authorization. Verse 12:40 likewise confronts invented objects of devotion and places all ruling with GOD. Read together, they suggest that an innovation can be more than a new custom. It can become **a claim to relocate religious authority**.

A ruling may sound attractive. The person presenting it may be intelligent, eloquent, and sincerely admired. Sometimes I accept a sentence simply because I have grown used to the voice that speaks it. Yet personal credibility does not establish divine authorization. Listening carefully to another human being and elevating that human being's words to the status of revelation are very different acts.

Verse 12:40 brings the question of authority into the ordinary decisions of my life. Who is entitled to make a prohibition binding upon my conscience in GOD's name? I can express a reasoned concern about something. I can argue that it seems unwise. But establishing a new religious obligation is a greater claim, one I cannot justify by admiration, habit, or emotional force.

These verses also keep my understanding of monotheism from remaining merely a slogan. To say that there is one GOD while assigning independent divine legislative power to other voices would deserve serious self-examination. I cannot use my interpretation as proof of its own authority. Verse references: **42:21; 12:40**.

## 5. A Prohibition Made with Words: The Tongue and Lawful or Unlawful

### Verse

> **16:116** — You shall not utter lies with your own tongues stating: “This is lawful, and this is unlawful,” to fabricate lies and attribute them to GOD. Surely, those who fabricate lies and attribute them to GOD will never succeed.

> **10:59** — Say, “Did you note how GOD sends down to you all kinds of provisions, then you render some of them unlawful, and some lawful?” Say, “Did GOD give you permission to do this? Or, do you fabricate lies and attribute them to GOD?”

> **16:117** — They enjoy briefly, then suffer painful retribution.

### Reflection on the Verse

Verse 16:116 addresses the tongue itself. It warns against declaring things lawful or unlawful in order to fabricate a lie and attribute it to GOD. Verse 10:59 then asks a searching question: what permission did GOD give for the restriction you are announcing? I want to place that question at the entrance to every conversation in which someone speaks as though a human opinion were divine legislation.

Short words can carry long consequences. 'Unlawful.' 'Required.' 'Sinful.' Such language may change another person's conscience, family life, and choices for years. The aim is not to make all moral discussion impossible. It is to refuse the specific falsehood of claiming divine authorization where it has not been established.

I notice how easily a loud voice or the approval of a crowd can replace the work of verification. Yet 10:59 does not ask whether the rule sounds impressive or whether an admired person has defended it. It asks whether GOD gave permission. That question is quieter, and far more demanding. If I have added a conclusion that the verse does not explicitly state, I need to distinguish my reasoning from the revelation itself.

Verse 16:117 reminds me that there may be a fleeting benefit in a fabricated claim but not lasting success. Speaking with absolute certainty can make me feel powerful; it does not make me informed. Verse references: **16:116; 10:59; 16:117**.

## 6. Prohibiting What Is Good: Mistaking Restriction for Reverence

### Verse

> **7:32** — Say,  “Who  prohibited  the  nice things GOD has created for His creatures, and the good provisions?” Say, “Such provisions are to be enjoyed in this life by those who believe. Moreover, the good provisions will be exclusively theirs on the Day of Resurrection.” We thus explain the revelations for people who know.

> **5:87** — O you who believe, do not prohibit good things that are made lawful by GOD, and do not aggress; GOD dislikes the aggressors.

> **66:1** — O you prophet, why do you prohibit what GOD has made lawful for you, just to please your wives? GOD is Forgiver, Merciful.*

### Reflection on the Verse

Verse 7:32 asks who has prohibited the good things created by GOD. Verse 5:87 directly tells believers not to prohibit what GOD has made lawful. These words challenge a habit of mind I recognize: the assumption that the more restrictions I impose, the closer I must be to righteousness. A voluntary discipline and a divine prohibition are not the same kind of statement.

Verse 66:1 is particularly striking. The prophet is questioned for prohibiting something GOD had made lawful in an effort to please his wives. This is not a license for me to invent the event's unmentioned details. It is a reason to notice the direction of the question: why turn what is allowed into what is forbidden, even when pleasing others is part of the motivation?

I may decide that a certain pleasure, purchase, or habit is unhelpful in my circumstances. It may be too costly, distracting, or physically unsuitable for me. I should be able to explain that decision honestly. 'I choose not to do this' is a much more modest statement than 'GOD has forbidden everyone to do this.' The second requires evidence that the first does not.

These verses do not command unrestricted indulgence; 5:87 also warns against aggression. They ask me to distinguish the limits GOD has set from the additional boundaries I might wish to impose. Verse references: **7:32; 5:87; 66:1**.

## 7. When a Custom Becomes Sacred: Rules Imposed on Livestock

### Verse

> **5:103** — GOD  did  not  prohibit  livestock  that begets  certain  combinations  of males  and  females,  nor  livestock liberated  by  an  oath,  nor  the  one that  begets  two  males  in  a  row,  nor the  bull  that  fathers  ten.  It  is  the disbelievers  who  invented  such  lies about  GOD.  Most  of  them  do  not understand.

> **6:143** — Eight kinds of livestock: regarding the two kinds of sheep, and the two kinds of goats, say, “Is it the two males that He prohibited, or the two females,  or  the  contents  of  the wombs of the two females? Tell me what you know, if you are truthful.”

> **6:119** — Why should you not eat from that upon which GOD’s name has been mentioned? He has detailed for you what is prohibited for you, unless you are forced. Indeed, many people mislead others with their personal  opinions,  without knowledge. Your Lord is fully aware of the transgressors.

### Reflection on the Verse

The livestock distinctions in 5:103 may feel remote from modern life: categories based on breeding patterns, special vows, and inherited rules about animals. What makes the passage relevant is not that the customs seem strange to me. Strange is not a reliable test of falsehood. The actual issue is that prohibitions were attributed to GOD which He had not decreed.

Verse 6:143 approaches the claim almost like an investigation. Which male, which female, what is in the womb? The person asserting a prohibition is asked for knowledge. I find this method more demanding than mockery, and much fairer. It forces an argument to confront the details that it is supposed to explain. A complicated tradition does not escape the simple question: where is your evidence?

Verse 6:119 brings personal opinion into view. It describes people who mislead others without knowledge. I can think carefully about a subject and still be mistaken. Reflection is not revelation. The fact that I have worked hard on a conclusion gives me a responsibility to test it, not the right to burden everyone else with it in GOD's name.

These passages move the emphasis away from the impressiveness of a rule toward the truthfulness of its justification. A long list of prohibitions may make me look disciplined. It cannot, by itself, establish that I have understood GOD's command. Verse references: **5:103; 6:143; 6:119**.

## 8. We Were Taught This Way: Making Wrongdoing Look Commanded

### Verse

> **7:28** — They commit a gross sin, then say, “We found our parents doing this, and GOD has commanded us to do it.’’ Say, “GOD never advocates sin. Are you saying about GOD what you do not know?” 

> **7:33** — Say, “My Lord prohibits only evil deeds, be they obvious or hidden, and sins, and unjustifiable aggression, and to set up beside GOD powerless idols, and to say about GOD what you do not know.”

> **2:169** — He  only  commands  you  to  commit evil and vice,  and  to say about GOD what you do not know.

### Reflection on the Verse

In 7:28, two defenses follow each other. People first appeal to what their parents did, then claim that GOD commanded it. The origin of a practice is being mistaken for evidence of its legitimacy. That transition is what unsettles me. A sentence inherited yesterday may be announced as a divine command today. Without verification, its authority has not actually increased; only its presentation has changed.

Verse 7:33 includes speaking about GOD without knowledge among the things prohibited. Verse 2:169 joins wrongdoing with saying what one does not know about Him. Truthfulness, then, is not a polite accessory to religious life. A claim about GOD carries a particular burden, even when its speaker feels certain or belongs to a respected community.

I think about the people who taught me as I grew up. They need not have been wrong about everything. Much of what is good in my life may have come through their care and example. Yet love for a person is not proof that every claim the person makes is true. Refusing to imagine that someone I love could be mistaken would not be a respectful way of treating either that person or the truth.

I do not need to despise what I have inherited. I need the freedom and honesty to examine it. The issue is not whether a practice is old; it is whether its claimed divine authority can be justified. Verse references: **7:28; 7:33; 2:169**.

## 9. Imitating Scripture: Concealing Where the Words Came From

### Verse

> **2:79** — Therefore, woe to those who distort the  scripture  with  their  own  hands, then say, “This is what GOD has revealed,”  seeking  a  cheap  material gain. Woe to them for such distortion, and woe to them for their illicit gains.

> **3:78** — Among them are those who twist their tongues to imitate the scripture, that you may think it is from the scripture, when it is not from the scripture, and they claim that it is from GOD, when it is not from GOD. Thus, they utter lies and attribute them to GOD, knowingly.

### Reflection on the Verse

Verse 2:79 arrests me because the wrong is not confined to inventing a particular prohibition. People distort scripture and present the result as something revealed by GOD, with material gain in view. Verse 3:78 speaks of twisting words so that what is not scripture is mistaken for scripture. The shared concern is **a deliberate misrepresentation of a statement's source**.

A person may write beautifully or offer a compelling explanation. I write, too. But nothing I write becomes revelation merely because it appears beside a verse. The danger in 2:79 therefore reaches beyond the story of an old manuscript. It asks every writer who handles sacred texts what belongs to the source and what has been supplied by the interpreter.

If I place my comments immediately after a verse, readers should still be able to tell them apart. If I silently omit words, alter punctuation, or merge my inference into a quotation, I blur a boundary they have a right to see. Not every accidental error is the deliberate corruption described in these verses; I cannot claim to know a person's intention from a typographical mistake. But that does not lessen my duty to preserve the text faithfully.

A human being may compose an excellent sentence. The human being has no right to lie about who said it. Verse references: **2:79; 3:78**.

## 10. Making Religious Leaders into Lords: Where Does Trust Become Submission?

### Verse

> **9:31** — They  have  set  up  their  religious leaders  and  scholars  as  lords,*  instead of GOD. Others deified the Messiah, son of Mary. They were all commanded  to  worship  only  one god. There is no god except He. Be He glorified, high above having any partners.

> **39:3** — Absolutely,  the  religion  shall  be devoted to GOD alone. Those who set up idols beside Him say, “We idolize them only to bring us closer to GOD; for they are in a better position!” GOD will judge them regarding their disputes. GOD does not guide such liars, disbelievers.

### Reflection on the Verse

I do not want to hurry through 9:31. It speaks of making religious leaders and scholars into lords instead of GOD. I cannot honestly conclude that learning from another human being is itself condemned. But a human interpretation can be elevated into an independent authority that demands submission. Learning from a teacher and treating that teacher as the owner of truth are not the same action.

Verse 39:3 describes intermediaries justified as bringing people nearer to GOD. The explanation might sound sincere. I may genuinely admire someone's learning, character, or dedication. Yet admiration must not erase the question of whom I ultimately worship and whose authority I regard as final. A good-sounding justification does not establish that an intermediary has the status I assign to them.

I ask myself when I stop examining what a respected person says. Do I become defensive as soon as someone suggests that the person may have misunderstood a verse? Can I imagine an admired teacher being right about one thing and mistaken about another? If I cannot, then my attachment may be protecting an image rather than pursuing truth.

I can be grateful to those from whom I learn. Gratitude does not require surrendering the distinction between human explanation and GOD's word. Verse references: **9:31; 39:3**.

## 11. Our Parents Did This: How an Invented Rule Outlives Its Author

### Verse

> **2:170** — When  they  are  told,  “Follow  what GOD  has  revealed  herein,”  they  say, “We  follow  only  what  we  found  our parents  doing.”  What  if  their  parents  did  not  understand,  and  were not guided?

> **31:21** — When  they  are  told,  “Follow  these revelations  of  GOD,”  they  say,  “No, we  follow  only  what  we  found  our parents  doing.”  What  if  the  devil  is leading them to the agony of Hell?

> **43:22** — The  fact  is:  they  said,  “We  found our  parents  carrying  on  certain practices,  and  we  are  following  in their footsteps.”

> **43:23** — Invariably,  when  we  sent  a  warner to  any  community,  the  leaders  of that  community  would  say,  “We found  our  parents  following  certain practices,  and  we  will  continue  in their footsteps.”

### Reflection on the Verse

Verse 2:170 gives a simple defense for inherited practice: we found our parents doing it. Verses 31:21 and 43:22–23 develop the pattern. First a behavior is repeated. Then the fact that it has been repeated becomes the reason it must continue. Eventually the question of who began it, and on what grounds, may disappear. Repetition can acquire the appearance of authority without gaining evidence.

I do not respond to this with anger toward my parents or anyone else's. We are not born choosing the language, habits, fears, and hopes that surround us. We first receive; only later do we gain the ability to examine. The verses challenge the promotion of inheritance into a substitute for revelation. They do not require me to assume that everything my family passed down was false.

In my earlier reflection <a href="https://berkkoksal.com/writings/we_found_our_parents_doing_this_en/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">“We Found Our Parents Doing This”</a>, I explored inherited belief and personal verification. Here I face the next question: could I become the person who passes an unsupported religious claim to another generation? A child listening to me may not recognize where the verse ends and my interpretation begins.

To question an inherited rule need not mean despising the people who handed it to me. It may mean respecting them, and GOD's word, enough to stop a mistaken claim from being repeated as certainty. Verse references: **2:170; 31:21; 43:22; 43:23**.

## 12. Conjecture, Desire, and Excusing Ourselves with Destiny

### Verse

> **6:148** — The idol worshipers say, “Had GOD willed, we would not practice idolatry, nor would our parents, nor would we prohibit anything.” Thus did those before them disbelieve, until they incurred our retribution. Say,  “Do  you  have  any  proven knowledge that you can show us? You follow nothing but conjecture; you only guess.” 

> **53:23** — These  are  but  names  that  you  made up,  you  and  your  forefathers.  GOD never  authorized  such  a  blasphemy. They  follow  conjecture,  and  personal  desire,  when  the  true  guidance  has  come  to  them  herein  from their Lord.

> **53:28** — They had no knowledge about this; they only conjectured. Conjecture is no substitute for the truth.

### Reflection on the Verse

In 6:148, people defend their conduct by appealing to what GOD must have willed. The argument can become an escape from accountability: if He had wanted otherwise, we would not have done this. The reply returns the discussion to evidence. Can they produce established knowledge? A claim about destiny is not a substitute for proof of divine permission.

Verse 53:23 connects invented names with conjecture and personal desire, while 53:28 says conjecture cannot replace truth. The challenge reaches into my motives. I may defend a rule not because I know it is correct, but because it makes the world seem controllable. I may choose an explanation because it preserves a role, a reputation, or a familiar structure in which I feel safe.

It is easy to agree that my wishes do not determine GOD's command. Living by that distinction is harder. A conviction that I have defended for years can begin to feel like part of my identity. Discovering an error may then feel like losing myself. But correction does not have to destroy my dignity. It can simply return my opinion to its proper status as an opinion.

These verses do not authorize me to announce what every other person's hidden intention must be. They do give me a searching question to ask about myself: am I looking for evidence, or merely for words that will protect a conclusion I have already chosen? Verse references: **6:148; 53:23; 53:28**.

## 13. Show the Proof: What Makes a Claim Religiously Binding?

### Verse

> **17:36** — You  shall  not  accept  any  information,  unless  you  verify  it  for  yourself. I  have  given  you  the  hearing,  the eyesight,  and  the  brain,  and  you  are responsible for using them.

> **2:111** — Some  have  said,  “No  one  will  enter Paradise  except  Jews  or  Christians!”  Such  is  their  wishful  thinking. Say, “Show us your proof, if you are right.”

> **39:18** — They  are  the  ones  who  examine  all words,  then  follow  the  best.  These are  the  ones  whom  GOD  has  guided;  these  are  the  ones  who  possess intelligence.

### Reflection on the Verse

Verse 17:36 makes me responsible for how I use hearing, eyesight, and understanding. It calls me to verify rather than passively accept information. Verse 2:111 demands proof for an extravagant claim about who will enter Paradise. Verse 39:18 describes people who examine words and follow the best. Together they make investigation appear not as an enemy of faith but as a duty of truthful speech.

If someone announces a religious obligation, I want to learn to ask a calm question: which part of the verse supports the obligation you have stated? I need not embarrass the person or begin a contest of status. In fact, the more confident I feel about a religious claim, the more important it is for me to show its basis, because others may trust my certainty without examining it for themselves.

But inquiry has a discipline of its own. Investigation does not mean ignoring every passage that challenges my desires. Examining words honestly includes attending to what I would rather not hear. A person seeking truth must be willing to lose an argument. Otherwise questions become only another strategy for defending a preferred answer.

This is also where the ethics of conversation matters. I can challenge a claim while treating the speaker fairly. I can be firm about the need for evidence without pretending to know the speaker's whole heart. Verse references: **17:36; 2:111; 39:18**.

## 14. Returning to the Source: Keeping Revelation Apart from Human Speech

### Verse

> **6:114** — Shall I seek other than GOD as a source of law, when He has revealed to you this book fully detailed?* Those who received the scripture recognize that it has been revealed from  your  Lord,  truthfully.  You shall not harbor any doubt.

> **6:115** — The  word  of  your  Lord  is  complete,* in truth and justice. Nothing shall abrogate His words. He is the Hearer, the Omniscient.

> **18:27** — You  shall  recite  what  is  revealed  to you  of  your  Lord’s  scripture.  Nothing  shall  abrogate  His  words,  and you  shall  not  find  any  other  source beside it.

> **6:19** — Say,  “Whose  testimony  is  the greatest?” Say, “GOD’s. He is the witness between me and you that this Quran* has been inspired to me, to preach it to you and whomever it reaches. Indeed, you bear witness that there are other gods* beside GOD.” Say, “I do not testify as you do; there is only one god, and I disown your idolatry.”

### Reflection on the Verse

Verse 6:114 questions seeking a source of religious law other than GOD. Verse 6:115 describes the word of the Lord as complete in truth and justice. Verse 18:27 directs us to recite what has been revealed and insists that His words cannot be abrogated. Verse 6:19 speaks of the Quran reaching others. Together they give me a direction: when a claim is made in GOD's name, return to the revelation whose authority is being invoked.

Human beings still need to think and explain. I may read a passage many times, examine its Arabic words, compare related verses, and write questions that remain unanswered. None of these activities produces a new revelation. The value of an article like this cannot be that readers surrender their judgment to its author. It should be that they can see the verse clearly enough to examine what the author has said.

There is a subtle danger even in a good principle. If I announce that GOD's word is complete and then treat my own interpretation as beyond correction, I have confused the source with myself. Taking scripture seriously should increase my willingness to distinguish what is stated from what I infer. It should not make my conclusions immune to criticism.

The more I speak about divine words, the more I need to let those words remain visible rather than hiding them behind my voice. Verse references: **6:114; 6:115; 18:27; 6:19**.

## 15. If the Religion Is Complete, What Can Human Beings Add?

### Verse

> **5:3** — Prohibited  for  you  are  animals  that die  of  themselves,  blood,  the  meat  of pigs,*  and  animals  dedicated  to other  than  GOD.  (Animals  that  die of  themselves  include  those)  strangled,  struck  with  an  object,  fallen from  a  height,  gored,  attacked  by  a wild  animal—unless  you  save  your animal  before  it  dies—and  animals sacrificed  on  altars.  Also  prohibited is  dividing  the  meat  through  a  game of  chance;  this  is  an  abomination. Today, the disbelievers have given up  concerning  (the  eradication  of) your  religion;  do  not  fear  them  and fear  Me  instead.  Today,  I  have  completed  your  religion,  perfected  My blessing  upon  you,  and  I  have decreed  Submission  as  the  religion for  you.  If  one  is  forced  by  famine (to  eat  prohibited  food),  without being deliberately sinful, then GOD is Forgiver, Merciful.

> **16:89** — The day will come when we will raise from every community a witness from among them, and bring you as the witness of these people. We have revealed to you this book to provide explanations for everything, and guidance, and mercy, and good news for the submitters.

> **5:48** — Then  we  revealed  to  you  this  scripture,  truthfully,  confirming  previous scriptures,  and  superseding  them. You  shall  rule  among  them  in  accordance  with  GOD’s  revelations, and do not follow their wishes if they differ  from  the  truth  that  came  to you. For each of you, we have decreed  laws  and  different  rites.  Had GOD willed, He could have  made you  one  congregation.  But  He  thus puts  you  to  the  test  through  the revelations  He  has  given  each  of you.  You  shall  compete  in  righteousness. To GOD is your final destiny—all  of  you—then  He  will  inform you of everything you had disputed.

### Reflection on the Verse

Verse 5:3 speaks of the completion of the religion. Verse 16:89 describes the book's role in providing explanations, guidance, mercy, and good news. Together they make me ask what status I am claiming for anything I 'add.' An interpretation, a teaching method, a practical arrangement, and a new religious obligation announced in GOD's name are not interchangeable. Treating them all as one thing would hide the central question of authority.

Verse 5:48 adds an important qualification. It says that laws and different rites were decreed for different communities, and that people are tested through what was revealed to them. I therefore cannot assume that every religious difference is automatically an unauthorized human invention. At the same time, the existence of divinely decreed differences does not authorize anyone to manufacture new rulings and attribute them to GOD. The question remains: who decreed this, and on what evidence?

I might adopt a new tool for reading scripture, improve an explanation, or change how I organize my personal studies. These actions do not, simply by being new, produce a new verse or a binding list of prohibitions. But if I begin telling others that my method is an indispensable divine command, I am making a very different claim. Recognizing the difference can protect sincere work without granting unlimited religious authority to human invention.

The verses do not hand me a ready-made verdict on every unfamiliar custom. They make me responsible for distinguishing what GOD has actually said from what I have concluded. Verse references: **5:3; 16:89; 5:48**.

## 16. Innovation and Division: When Every Group Defends Its Own Rules

### Verse

> **42:13** — He decreed for you the same religion decreed for Noah, and what we inspired to you, and what we decreed for Abraham, Moses, and Jesus: “You shall uphold this one religion, and do not divide it.”
> 
> Monotheists  vs.  Idol Worshipers
> The  idol  worshipers  will  greatly resent what you invite them to do. GOD  redeems  to  Himself  whomever He wills; He guides to Himself only those who totally submit.

> **42:14** — Ironically, they broke up into sects only after the knowledge had come to them, due to jealousy and resentment among themselves. If it were not  for  a  predetermined  decision from your Lord to respite them for a definite interim, they would have been  judged  immediately.  Indeed, the later generations who inherited the scripture are full of doubts.

> **6:159** — Those  who  divide  themselves  into sects do not belong with you. Their judgment  rests  with  GOD,  then  He will  inform  them  of  everything  they had done.

> **30:32** — (Do  not  fall  in  idol  worship,)  like those  who  divide  their  religion  into sects;  each  party  rejoicing  with  what they have.

### Reflection on the Verse

Verse 42:13 calls for upholding one religion without dividing it. The next verse describes division arising after knowledge had come, driven by jealousy and resentment. That order disturbs me. Sometimes people separate because they do not know; sometimes they resist what they know because a group identity has become too precious to surrender. In the second case, more information alone may not repair the problem. An attachment to being right is involved.

Verses 6:159 and 30:32 warn about dividing religion into sects, with each party pleased by what it possesses. I recognize the appeal of belonging. Shared conviction can give people courage and help them act together. But when the group's interpretation becomes more important than the truth it claims to serve, belonging starts to replace investigation. Neither numbers nor sincerity make every group position correct.

This connects with my earlier reflection <a href="https://berkkoksal.com/writings/dividing_the_religion_en/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">“Dividing the Religion”</a>. There is nothing inherently wrong with saying 'we.' The danger appears when 'we' becomes an excuse to treat an unsupported human rule as the one measure by which everyone else must be judged. A practice proclaimed for the sake of unity can itself create division.

These verses do not appoint me to despise people outside my circle. They ask me to examine which claims inside my own circle I have stopped testing. Verse references: **42:13; 42:14; 6:159; 30:32**.

## 17. Who Is Testifying? Justice in Speaking on GOD’s Behalf

### Verse

> **6:150** — Say,  “Bring  your  witnesses  who would testify that GOD has prohibited this or that.” If they testify, do not testify with them. Nor shall you follow the opinions of those who reject  our  revelations,  and  those who disbelieve in the Hereafter, and those who stray away from their Lord.

> **4:135** — O  you who  believe,  you  shall  be absolutely  equitable,  and  observe GOD,  when  you  serve  as  witnesses, even  against  yourselves,  or  your parents,  or  your  relatives.  Whether the  accused  is  rich  or  poor,  GOD takes  care  of  both.  Therefore,  do not  be  biased  by  your  personal wishes.  If  you  deviate  or  disregard (this  commandment),  then  GOD  is fully  Cognizant  of  everything  you do.

> **6:152** — You  shall  not  touch  the  orphans’ money  except  in  the  most  righteous manner,  until  they  reach  maturity. You shall give full weight and full measure  when  you  trade,  equitably. We  do  not  burden  any  soul  beyond its  means.  You  shall  be  absolutely just  when  you  bear  witness,  even against  your  relatives.  You  shall  fulfill your covenant with GOD. These are  His  commandments  to  you,  that you may take heed.

### Reflection on the Verse

Verse 6:150 asks that witnesses be brought for claims about what GOD has prohibited. It also warns against joining false testimony. I hear in this a responsibility that extends beyond the person who first invented a rule. I may never have uttered the original claim. Yet by endorsing it without grounds, I can help it gain the appearance of truth.

Verse 4:135 calls for justice even against ourselves, our parents, and our closest relatives. Verse 6:152 likewise demands fairness in speech. These instructions change how I understand disagreement in religion. A claim is not protected from scrutiny because it comes from my family, from an admired teacher, or from an institution that has helped me. Loyalty cannot substitute for truthfulness.

Perhaps the hardest testimony is the one I must give about my own error. Could I admit that I have repeated a prohibition for years without finding a verse that authorizes it? Could I correct myself even if doing so disappoints people who trust me? Reputation is a powerful temptation. It can quietly turn the protection of my image into a reason to burden others with what GOD did not require.

I also need a limit on my criticism. Correcting a claim is not the same as knowing the whole moral condition of its speaker. I can refuse an unsupported ruling and remain fair to the person who expressed it. Verse references: **6:150; 4:135; 6:152**.

## 18. Turning Back from a False Claim: Warning, Wisdom, and Accountability

### Verse

> **69:44** — Had  he  uttered  any  other  teachings.

> **69:45** — We would have punished him.

> **69:46** — We  would  have  stopped  the  revelations to him.

> **69:47** — None  of  you  could  have  helped him.

> **16:125** — You shall invite to the path of your Lord  with  wisdom  and  kind  enlightenment, and debate with them in the best possible manner. Your Lord knows best who has strayed from His path, and He knows best who are the guided ones.

> **41:46** — Whoever  works  righteousness,  does so  for  his  own  good,  and  whoever works  evil  does  so  to  his  own  detriment.  Your  Lord  is  never  unjust towards the people.

### Reflection on the Verse

Verses 69:44–47 show how seriously the possibility of attributing other teachings to GOD is treated. I do not want to turn that warning into a performance of fear directed at other people. Yet it would be equally wrong to make the misattribution of revelation sound trivial. Honesty about a statement's source is more than an academic courtesy. It respects the consciences of those who may shape their lives around what I say.

Verse 16:125 calls for wisdom, kind enlightenment, and the best manner of debate. It prevents me from using the seriousness of the earlier warning as permission to treat people harshly. I may object to a rule, ask for its evidence, and argue that it should not be attributed to GOD. None of those actions requires humiliation, contempt, or a claim to possess another person's final judgment. A sound aim does not automatically make every method sound.

Verse 41:46 brings this discussion back to my own life. Working righteousness is for my own good, too. If I recognize today that I repeated an unsupported religious claim yesterday, I can correct it. I need not experience correction as a defeat unless I have made my reputation for infallibility more important than being truthful.

I may still be wrong after writing this article. The vital thing is not to hide future errors inside a sacred vocabulary. The authority I want protected is not my own. Verse references: **69:44; 69:45; 69:46; 69:47; 16:125; 41:46**.

## 19. Arabic Roots: Invention, Fabrication, Ruling, and Permission

### Verse

> **6:138** — They  said,  “These  are  livestock  and crops  that  are  prohibited;  no  one shall  eat  them  except  whomever  we permit,”  so  they  claimed.  They  also prohibited  the  riding  of  certain  livestock.  Even  the  livestock  they ate,  they  never  pronounced  GOD’s name  as  they  sacrificed  them.  Such are innovations attributed to Him. He  will  surely  requite  them  for  their innovations.

> **6:139** — They  also  said,  “What  is  in  the  bellies  of  these  livestock  is  reserved exclusively  for  the  males  among  us, and  prohibited  for  our  wives.”  But  if it  was  a  stillbirth,  they  permitted their  wives  to  share  therein.  He  will certainly  requite  them  for  their  innovations.  He  is  Most  Wise,  Omniscient.

> **57:27** — Subsequent to them, we sent our messengers. We sent Jesus the son of Mary, and we gave him the Injeel (Gospel),  and  we  placed  in  the hearts of his followers kindness and mercy. But they invented hermitism which we never decreed for them. All we asked them to do was to uphold  the  commandments  approved by GOD. But they did not uphold the message as they should have. Consequently, we gave those who  believed  among  them  their recompense, while  many of them were wicked.

> **42:21** — They  follow  idols  who  decree  for them  religious  laws  never  authorized  by  GOD.  If  it  were  not  for the  predetermined  decision,  they would  have  been  judged  immediately.  Indeed,  the  transgressors have  incurred  a  painful  retribution.*

> **16:116** — You shall not utter lies with your own tongues stating: “This is lawful, and this is unlawful,” to fabricate lies and attribute them to GOD. Surely, those who fabricate lies and attribute them to GOD will never succeed.

> **10:59** — Say, “Did you note how GOD sends down to you all kinds of provisions, then you render some of them unlawful, and some lawful?” Say, “Did GOD give you permission to do this? Or, do you fabricate lies and attribute them to GOD?”

> **12:40** — “You  do  not  worship  beside  Him except  innovations  that  you  have made  up,  you  and  your  parents. GOD  has  never  authorized  such idols.  All  ruling  belongs  to  GOD, and  He  has  ruled  that  you  shall  not worship  except  Him.  This  is  the  perfect  religion,  but  most  people  do  not know.

### Reflection on the Verse

I use the English words *innovation* and *fabrication*, but they do not always correspond to the same Arabic root. If I flatten every verse into a single slogan about 'new things,' I may fail to notice what each passage actually says. Studying the original words is useful not because it makes me the final authority on language, but because it slows down premature conclusions.

In 57:27, **ٱبْتَدَعُوهَا** (*ibtadaʿūhā*) belongs to the **ب-د-ع (b-d-ʿ)** root family, in the account of hermitism being invented. That is directly related to the familiar term *bidʿah*. Yet 6:138, rendered in Turkish with the word *bidatler* and in English with *innovations*, uses **ٱفْتِرَآءً** (*iftirāʾan*), from **ف-ر-ي (f-r-y)**, in the context of falsely attributing a restriction to GOD. The form **لِّتَفْتَرُوا۟** (*li-taftarū*) in 16:116 comes from the same latter root. These verses are related in subject, but they are not simply repetitions of one Arabic word.

The **وَصْفَهُمْ** (*waṣfahum*) of 6:139 belongs to **و-ص-ف (w-ṣ-f)** and occurs where the Turkish rendering again describes the consequences of invented rulings. The verb **شَرَعُوا۟** (*sharaʿū*) in 42:21 belongs to **ش-ر-ع (sh-r-ʿ)** and concerns the decreeing of religious laws. Verse 10:59 asks about permission with **أَذِنَ** (*adhina*), from **أ-ذ-ن (ʾ-dh-n)**. Verse 12:40 uses **ٱلْحُكْمُ** (*al-ḥukmu*), from **ح-ك-م (ḥ-k-m)**, when speaking of who holds the authority to rule.

These words help me preserve a basic distinction: making something new, inventing a lie about GOD, issuing a religious ruling, and receiving authorization are not the same act. The passages connect them in specific circumstances. They do not allow me to declare everything unfamiliar a forbidden religious invention by word association alone. My reference is **Rashad Khalifa’s Authorized English Translation**, with **the Turkish translation of the Authorized English Translation** used alongside the Arabic text. The roots must discipline my interpretation, not become ornaments for it. Verse references: **6:138; 6:139; 57:27; 42:21; 16:116; 10:59; 12:40**.

## Other Claims

One approach treats every practice that emerges later in religious life as suspect in itself. Another divides innovations into acceptable and unacceptable kinds. Such classifications have been used in historical religious debate, but I cannot present any one of them as a technical system explicitly laid out in the Quran. Verse 57:27 discusses a particular invented hermitism; 6:138–140 addresses falsely attributed prohibitions; 42:21 addresses unauthorized religious laws. Connecting these texts is legitimate reflection. Inventing a fresh, universal list of prohibitions on their behalf is not a necessary conclusion.

Some inherited interpretations defend a practice because it has been preserved for generations. Others mistrust something precisely because it appeared later. Neither age nor novelty, by itself, is the question these passages place at the center. Verses 2:170 and 17:36 draw attention to knowledge and verification. A new educational method does not automatically claim to be a new law from GOD; nor does an old religious practice automatically prove that GOD decreed it. Where the verses do not give an exhaustive classification, I should not speak as if they do.

There are also historical arguments over the forms of ritual, collective customs, devotional disciplines, and methods of teaching. This article does not pretend to settle those disputes in detail. It keeps to the textual responsibility that can be identified here: do not attribute an unauthorized religious law to GOD, do not conceal the distinction between revelation and human speech, and do not abandon knowledge and justice.

Finally, there is a danger in the label 'innovator' itself. Verse 6:150 rejects false testimony, 16:125 calls for wise and kind discussion, and 4:135 insists upon justice. I can dispute a person's religious assertion without pretending that my label establishes the final judgment on the person's entire life. My accusation also must answer to evidence.

## Conclusion

I began with the question, 'How is a religious innovation invented?' At first, I imagined someone introducing a new religious practice. But 6:138 revealed a more serious possibility: people may make their own restrictions, build a religious system around them, and then attribute the system to GOD. When a person refuses to sign a human opinion with a human name, sacred authority can become a way of escaping responsibility for that opinion.

Verses 6:139–140 show that the consequences are not merely theoretical. A fabricated rule can shape who receives provisions, who is excluded, and who is harmed. Verse 57:27 reminds me that an unauthorized religious discipline may arise even in a setting where kindness and mercy are present. Verses 42:21 and 12:40 bring the issue to the heart of monotheism: who is being given the power to decree the religion?

I do not want these passages to make me imagine myself standing above everyone else as a judge. There are intentions I do not know and lives I have not seen. The first place for correction is my own speech. If I offer an interpretation, I should name it as an interpretation. If I make a personal choice, I should not disguise it as a divine prohibition. If I claim that a verse establishes a rule, I should show where the verse supports that claim. When I cannot, certainty would be dishonest.

The uncomfortable truth is that invention is not only a problem belonging to people in the distant past. I can approach the same boundary while writing, researching, defending a cherished belief, or advising someone who trusts me. However eloquent my sentence may be, it does not become GOD's sentence. My anger at disagreement cannot change what the revelation actually states.

Verse 17:36 demands verification. Verse 39:18 commends examining words. Verse 4:135 requires justice, and 16:125 requires wisdom and kind argument. Without these disciplines, even criticism of invented religious authority may turn into another exercise in human superiority. If I condemn the replacement of GOD's authority while seeking that authority for myself, what have I really corrected?

The question I want to carry away is simple: **Have I kept an honest boundary between what GOD said and what I said?** Returning to that question before issuing a religious claim may protect me not only from inventing a new error but from confidently repeating an old one.
