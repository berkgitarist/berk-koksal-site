# The Steps of Satan: Does a Human Being Fall Into Major Deviation All at Once?

**Author:** Berk KÖKSAL  
**Date:** September 11, 2026  
**Place:** Mersin

The central purpose of this article is to contemplate the verses together with other related verses, and to consider sincerely and deeply what bearing witness to the oneness of GOD means in human life in terms of knowledge, justice, truthfulness, and submission.

Peace. Peace be upon you.

We often imagine evil as one large and obvious decision. Yet 2:168 uses the language of “steps.” It does not describe one dramatic leap by Satan, but warns people not to follow his steps. That language makes me think about moral direction through small permissions, repeated habits, accepted claims, and justifications.

The next verse, 2:169, names evil, vice, and speaking about GOD without knowledge. Then 2:170 criticizes inherited practice, and 2:171 describes repetition without understanding. The idea of “steps” therefore extends beyond behavior into knowledge, speech, and religious authority.

The question of this article is: **Does a human being fall into major deviation all at once, or can distance from truth grow through small, justified steps?**

## 1. Why Are Satan's “Steps” Plural?

### Verse

> **2:168**
>
> يَٰٓأَيُّهَا ٱلنَّاسُ كُلُوا۟ مِمَّا فِى ٱلْأَرْضِ حَلَٰلًا طَيِّبًا وَلَا تَتَّبِعُوا۟ خُطُوَٰتِ ٱلشَّيْطَٰنِ إِنَّهُۥ لَكُمْ عَدُوٌّ مُّبِينٌ
>
> O  people,  eat  from  the  earth’s  products  all  that  is  lawful  and  good,  and do  not  follow  the  steps  of  Satan;  he is your most ardent enemy.
>
> Ey insanlar, yeryüzünün helal ve iyi olan ürünlerinin hepsini yiyin ve Şeytan’ın adımlarını takip etmeyin; o sizin en azılı düşmanınızdır.

Source: <a href="https://kuranteyit.com/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">kuranteyit.com</a>

### Reflection on the Verse

The word that most arrests me in 2:168 is “steps.” The plural form does not authorize a complete psychological theory, but it clearly conveys direction and repeated movement.

It may therefore be inadequate to look for moral danger only in the final dramatic result. Direction is already visible in smaller choices.

My first question becomes: what small thing I permit today could make a larger wrong look normal tomorrow?

**Verse references:** 2:168.

## 2. Speaking About GOD Without Knowledge

### Verse

> **2:169** — He  only  commands  you  to  commit evil and vice,  and  to say about GOD what you do not know.

### Reflection on the Verse

2:169 names three commands associated with Satan: evil, vice, and saying about GOD what one does not know.

The third is especially serious for religious thought. A person can go astray not only through behavior, but through claims of knowledge. Saying “GOD commanded this” or “GOD prohibited this” is not an ordinary opinion.

Resistance to Satan's steps therefore begins in speech as well. Remaining silent where I do not know may be an act of truthfulness, not weakness.

**Verse references:** 2:169.

## 3. Can Inherited Habit Become One of the Steps?

### Verse

> **2:170** — When  they  are  told,  “Follow  what GOD  has  revealed  herein,”  they  say, “We  follow  only  what  we  found  our parents  doing.”  What  if  their  parents  did  not  understand,  and  were not guided?

### Reflection on the Verse

2:170 records the response to the command to follow revelation: “We follow only what we found our parents doing.” This directly meets the question explored in *We Found Our Parents Doing This*.

Inheritance is not automatically evil. The problem begins when inheritance becomes evidence in place of revelation. A practice seen for years can begin to feel like a command from GOD.

I do not need to hate inherited religion; I need to verify it before revelation.

**Verse references:** 2:170.

## 4. Repeating Without Understanding

### Verse

> **2:171** — The  example  of  such  disbelievers  is that  of  parrots  who  repeat  what  they hear  of  sounds  and  calls,  without understanding.  Deaf,  dumb,  and blind; they cannot understand.

### Reflection on the Verse

2:171 uses the image of parrots repeating sounds without understanding. Saying correct words is not the same as understanding what one says.

A sentence repeated often enough can become untouchable. The words remain while the connection to meaning weakens. Religious language can stay active while the mind becomes passive.

Resisting Satan's steps therefore means more than rejecting false statements. It also means refusing to repeat even true statements without understanding.

**Verse references:** 2:171.

## 5. The Contrast Between Complete Submission and the Steps

### Verse

> **2:208** — O  you  who  believe,  you  shall  embrace  total  submission;  do  not  follow the steps of Satan, for he is your most ardent enemy.

### Reflection on the Verse

2:208 commands complete submission and immediately warns against following Satan's steps. On one side stands comprehensive submission; on the other, a sequence of followed steps.

A person can divide life into zones, surrendering some to GOD while reserving others for the self. The verse calls beyond that divided allegiance.

This is not a claim of perfection. It means returning direction to GOD's measure instead of defending the failure.

**Verse references:** 2:208.

## 6. Is Purification Our Own Achievement?

### Verse

> **24:21** — O you who believe, do not follow the steps of Satan. Anyone who follows the steps of Satan, should know that he advocates evil and vice. If it were not for GOD’s grace towards you, and His mercy, none of you would have been purified. But GOD purifies whomever He wills. GOD is Hearer, Knower.

### Reflection on the Verse

24:21 warns that following Satan's path leads toward evil and vice, then says that without GOD's grace and mercy none of us would be purified.

This prevents the struggle from becoming a hero story about the self. “I am aware; nothing can happen to me” can itself produce arrogance.

Responsibility is mine, but I cannot claim purification as an achievement independent of GOD.

**Verse references:** 24:21.

## 7. The Steps Even in the Field of Lawful Provision

### Verse

> **6:142** — Some  livestock  supply  you  with transportation,  as  well  as  bedding materials.  Eat  from  GOD’s  provisions  to  you,  and  do  not  follow  the steps  of  Satan;  he  is  your  most  ardent enemy.

### Reflection on the Verse

6:142 repeats the warning in the context of provision and livestock: eat from what GOD has provided and do not follow Satan's steps.

This makes the field of lawful and forbidden especially sensitive. A person can forbid what GOD made lawful through self-created religious authority, or turn personal preference into a rule attributed to GOD.

Piety is not the invention of more prohibitions. Knowing the limit of one's authority is also part of submission.

**Verse references:** 6:142.

## 8. The Responsibility of Saying “This Is Lawful, This Is Forbidden”

### Verse

> **16:116** — You shall not utter lies with your own tongues stating: “This is lawful, and this is unlawful,” to fabricate lies and attribute them to GOD. Surely, those who fabricate lies and attribute them to GOD will never succeed.

### Reflection on the Verse

16:116 establishes a concrete boundary: do not invent lies by saying “This is lawful, this is forbidden” and attribute them to GOD.

This becomes a basic criterion for evaluating religious leaders and scholars. Knowledge, fame, or followers do not authorize a human being to legislate in GOD's name beyond revelation.

The same boundary applies to me. Personal judgment should not be presented as divine law.

**Verse references:** 16:116.

## 9. Ancestors, Sin, and False Attribution to GOD

### Verse

> **7:28** — They commit a gross sin, then say, “We found our parents doing this, and GOD has commanded us to do it.’’ Say, “GOD never advocates sin. Are you saying about GOD what you do not know?” 

### Reflection on the Verse

7:28 joins two movements: people appeal to their parents and then claim that GOD commanded what they are doing. The answer is direct: GOD never advocates sin; are you saying about GOD what you do not know?

Inherited behavior and divine legitimization can reinforce each other. A person preserves a tradition and then creates a claim about GOD to protect it.

I need to ask: am I truly deriving a practice from revelation, or making revelation defend a practice I already prefer?

**Verse references:** 7:28.

## 10. Treating the Enemy as an Enemy

### Verse

> **35:6** — The  devil  is  your  enemy,  so  treat him as an enemy. He only invites his party to be the dwellers of Hell.

### Reflection on the Verse

35:6 is simple: Satan is your enemy, so treat him as an enemy. Satan is therefore not merely a theoretical idea.

Yet the verse does not authorize us to label other human beings as “Satan.” The named enemy is Satan; human beings remain responsible for their choices.

In religious conflict I should resist demonizing another person and become more alert to pride, falsehood, rage, and unsupported claims within myself.

**Verse references:** 35:6.

## 11. The Fork Between Worshiping Satan and Worshiping GOD

### Verse

> **36:60** — Did  I  not  covenant  with  you,  O Children of Adam, that you shall not worship the devil? That he is your most ardent enemy?
> 
> **36:61** — And  that  you  shall  worship  Me alone? This is the right path.
> 
> **36:62** — He has misled multitudes of you. Did you not possess any understanding? 

### Reflection on the Verse

36:60-62 takes the issue to its root: do not worship Satan; worship GOD alone. The “steps” ultimately connect to authority and servitude.

A person may never think of himself as consciously worshiping Satan, yet repeated obedience to invitations that lead away from GOD forms another pattern of allegiance.

Tawhid is more than refusing visible idols. It requires attention to which call actually directs one's life.

**Verse references:** 36:60; 36:61; 36:62.

## 12. Producing Hatred and Distracting from Remembrance

### Verse

> **5:90** — O you who believe, intoxicants, and gambling, and the altars of idols, and the games of chance are abominations of the devil; you shall avoid them, that you may succeed.
> 
> **5:91** — The devil wants to provoke animosity and hatred among you through intoxicants  and  gambling,  and  to distract  you  from  remembering GOD, and from observing the Contact Prayers (Salat). Will you then refrain?

### Reflection on the Verse

5:90-91 does not leave Satan's influence abstract. Through intoxicants and gambling, hostility and hatred are produced and people are distracted from remembering GOD and observing prayer.

The result appears on two levels: human relationships are damaged and the relationship with GOD is weakened.

If a habit steadily makes me angrier, more distracted, more forgetful, and less responsible, calling it merely a “personal preference” may not be enough.

**Verse references:** 5:90; 5:91.

## 13. Can Speech Become a Step That Divides People?

### Verse

> **17:53** — Tell  My  servants  to  treat  each  other in  the  best  possible  manner,  for  the devil will always try to drive a  wedge among  them.  Surely,  the  devil  is man’s most ardent enemy.

### Reflection on the Verse

17:53 tells GOD's servants to deal with one another in the best possible manner because Satan tries to create division between them.

Large conflicts can begin in speech. A correct criticism delivered as humiliation, or true information spoken with pride, can damage relationships even when some content is correct.

Interrupting Satan's steps can begin with forming one sentence better.

**Verse references:** 17:53.

## 14. Breaking the Chain When the Whisper Comes

### Verse

> **7:200** — When  the  devil  whispers  to  you  any whisper,  seek  refuge  in  GOD;  He  is Hearer, Omniscient.
> 
> **7:201** — Those  who  are  righteous,  whenever the  devil  approaches  them  with  an idea,  they  remember,  whereupon they become seers.
> 
> **41:36** — When  the  devil  whispers  an  idea  to you,  you shall  seek  refuge  in  GOD. He is the Hearer, the Omniscient.

### Reflection on the Verse

7:200-201 and 41:36 show a way out: when a satanic idea or whisper appears, seek refuge in GOD, remember, and become able to see again.

The arrival of a step does not force me to follow it. Remembrance can interrupt the sequence. Seeking refuge in GOD can create distance between the thought and the self.

Spiritual vigilance may begin by refusing to treat the first impulse as my identity and choosing direction again.

**Verse references:** 7:200; 7:201; 41:36.

## 15. Does Satan Compel, or Only Invite?

### Verse

> **14:22** — And  the  devil  will  say, after  the judgment had been issued, “GOD has promised you the truthful promise, and I promised you, but I broke my promise. I had no power over you; I simply invited you, and you accepted  my  invitation.  Therefore, do not blame me, and blame only yourselves. My complaining cannot help you, nor can your complaining help me. I have disbelieved in your idolizing  me.  The  transgressors have  incurred  a  painful  retribution.”

### Reflection on the Verse

14:22 establishes a clear boundary of responsibility. Satan will say that he had no power over people; he only invited them and they accepted the invitation.

The verse prevents us from blaming Satan in order to escape personal responsibility. There is an enemy and an invitation, but there is also a response.

Speaking about Satan's steps should not create fatalistic excuses. It should remind us that choice reappears before every step.

**Verse references:** 14:22.

## 16. Unverified Information Can Also Become a Step

### Verse

> **17:36** — You  shall  not  accept  any  information,  unless  you  verify  it  for  yourself. I  have  given  you  the  hearing,  the eyesight,  and  the  brain,  and  you  are responsible for using them.

### Reflection on the Verse

17:36 commands us not to accept information we have not verified for ourselves and makes hearing, sight, and the mind matters of responsibility.

It does not use the phrase “Satan's steps,” but it forms a strong ethical connection with 2:169's warning against speaking about GOD without knowledge.

Small permissions such as “everyone says so” or “our scholar said it” can harden into religious conviction. Verification interrupts that chain.

**Verse references:** 17:36.

## 17. Not Dismissing Small Wrongs as Small

### Verse

> **2:168** — O  people,  eat  from  the  earth’s  products  all  that  is  lawful  and  good,  and do  not  follow  the  steps  of  Satan;  he is your most ardent enemy.
> 
> **17:53** — Tell  My  servants  to  treat  each  other in  the  best  possible  manner,  for  the devil will always try to drive a  wedge among  them.  Surely,  the  devil  is man’s most ardent enemy.
> 
> **7:201** — Those  who  are  righteous,  whenever the  devil  approaches  them  with  an idea,  they  remember,  whereupon they become seers.

### Reflection on the Verse

Read together, these verses make me stop looking for Satan's steps only in extraordinary forms of evil.

Passing on an unverified claim, justifying a cruel sentence in anger, preferring tradition to a verse, or speaking with certainty about GOD where I do not know may all affect direction.

None may look like major deviation by itself. That is precisely what a step is: a small movement that changes where I am walking.

**Verse references:** 2:168; 17:53; 7:201.

## 18. Can Silence Where I Do Not Know Be Protection?

### Verse

> **2:169** — He  only  commands  you  to  commit evil and vice,  and  to say about GOD what you do not know.
> 
> **16:116** — You shall not utter lies with your own tongues stating: “This is lawful, and this is unlawful,” to fabricate lies and attribute them to GOD. Surely, those who fabricate lies and attribute them to GOD will never succeed.

### Reflection on the Verse

Read together, 2:169 and 16:116 show that religious speech carries serious responsibility. Attributing to GOD what I do not know is not merely an intellectual mistake; it crosses a moral boundary.

Sometimes the most truthful religious answer may therefore be “I do not know.” A person can refuse to fill the gap with personal authority.

Protection from Satan's steps may lie not in speaking more, but in knowing which claims I have no right to make.

**Verse references:** 2:169; 16:116.

## 19. Arabic Word Roots: Step, Following, and Enmity

### Verse

> **2:168** — O  people,  eat  from  the  earth’s  products  all  that  is  lawful  and  good,  and do  not  follow  the  steps  of  Satan;  he is your most ardent enemy.

### Reflection on the Verse

The word **خُطُوَات (khutuwat)** in 2:168 is connected with **خ-ط-و (kh-ṭ-w)** and carries the idea of stepping or walking. Its plural form clearly speaks of multiple “steps.”

**تَتَّبِعُوا (tattabi'u)** comes from **ت-ب-ع (t-b-')**, the field of following and going after.

**حَلَالًا (halalan)** is connected with **ح-ل-ل (h-l-l)**, while **طَيِّبًا (tayyiban)** comes from **ط-ي-ب (ṭ-y-b)**. The warning appears in the context of lawful and good provision.

**عَدُوّ (aduww)** is connected with **ع-د-و ('-d-w)** and carries the sense of enmity. The direction is clear: the path not to be followed belongs to an evident enemy.

**Verse references:** 2:168.

## Other Claims

Traditional interpretations sometimes explain “Satan's steps” through gradual involvement in particular sins, religious innovations, or human additions in the field of lawful and forbidden.

Some examples may fit the verses, but it would be unwarranted to make one later historical practice the exclusive meaning. The main line comes directly from the verses: evil, vice, speaking about GOD without knowledge, placing inheritance above revelation, repetition without understanding, producing hostility, and human responsibility in responding to invitation.

## Conclusion

When I return to 2:168, the word “steps” feels much more serious. Major wrongs may become visible only at the end, while direction has already been changing.

2:169 warns against speaking about GOD without knowledge; 2:170 against placing inheritance before revelation; 2:171 against repetition without understanding; 24:21 connects the path with evil and vice; 17:53 shows the divisive power of speech; and 5:91 gives concrete mechanisms of hostility and distraction from remembrance. 7:200-201 and 41:36 show that the sequence can be interrupted. Finally, 14:22 returns responsibility to the human being: Satan invites but does not compel.

The struggle therefore begins for me not with “Where is Satan?” but with “Which invitation am I accepting?”

One way to avoid great deviation may be refusing to dismiss small wrongs as small.

GOD knows best.
